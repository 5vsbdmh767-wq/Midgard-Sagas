Between Iron & Oak is a campaign about two places that were never meant to have much to do with each other: the Free City of [[Zobeck]], all brass and steam and argument, and the Old [[The Margreve|Margreve]], a forest that was already old when the gods were young. You will spend roughly equal time in both, and the longer you play, the harder it gets to treat them as separate stories.

---
## The City
Ninety-two years ago the people of Zobeck rose up against [[House Stross]], the nobles who had ruled them for centuries through a bargain with the shadow fey. Dwarven smiths, human artisans, and kobold miners fought side by side. [[Castle Shadowcrag]] burned, the Stross family fell, and Zobeck declared itself free. It has been governed by an elected Free City Council ever since.

Today Zobeck is a city of clockwork. Gears drive the public clocks and the water pumps, the [[Arcane Collegium]] teaches magic alongside engineering, and the Gear Goddess [[Rava]] is its patron. The Grand Bazaar fills up every day with merchants from across Midgard.

The revolution didn't fix everything. The [[kobolds]] who bled for the city still live mostly in crowded ghettos, with a single seat on the Council. The [[gearforged]], people whose souls were bound into clockwork bodies, work its factories and fight in its army while scholars and priests argue in print over whether they are people or property. And the ruins of Shadowcrag still stand over the city. Some say they are haunted.

## The Forest
Beyond the walls is the Margreve, and it is not a backdrop. The forest is aware. It knows the people who walk in it by their blood, it remembers what they do there for generations, and it acts on what it remembers, usually in small ways: a trail that runs shorter than it should, game where you need it, or a path that loops you back to where you started for the third time. How you stand with the forest matters more in the Margreve than any title you hold anywhere else.

The forest is full of old magic and older bargains. Fey both lovely and terrible keep their own courts and customs. [[Baba Yaga]]'s hut walks the deep woods on its chicken legs, and it is only found by people she wants to find. Villages along the edge get by on the old ways, the right offerings, and knowing which paths are safe and which places are never spoken of.

## What People Are Saying
In Zobeck, there is talk of noises in the old mining tunnels under the city and of things coming up out of places that were sealed. Old folk who remember the Stross years mutter that the shadows are coming back. The Council says these are isolated incidents.

On the forest's edge, farmers say the trees come back faster every spring and that ground they cleared in their grandparents' day is going back to the woods. Animals that never leave the deep forest have been seen near the road. Druids who keep shrines along the edge talk about rot in places that should be healthy, and about old guardians that have stopped answering.

And deep in the Margreve, at a few very old places, there is a name that nobody seems to be able to explain.

## What to Expect
This is dark fantasy with hope in it. There is horror here: corruption, curses, and things in the deep woods that don't sit right in the mind. But the darkness can be fought. It will cost something.

Expect city politics and forest politics, urban dungeons and wilderness groves, and factions on both sides that remember what you did. Social encounters matter as much as combat, and survival skills matter as much as knowing the right people. Not every enemy is evil and not every ally is trustworthy, and some choices won't have a right answer.

The campaign is meant to run from 1st level into the teens. Early choices come back later. NPCs remember. Factions react. The forest remembers everything.

### Themes
The campaign keeps coming back to a few questions. What is freedom worth, and is it real without equality? Can a clockwork city and a living forest share the same ground, or does one have to give way? Not all shadows are evil and not all light is good, and the shadow fey are victims as well as villains. And kobolds and gearforged are still waiting to find out whether the city they helped build means what it says.

## Making a Character
Your character needs a reason to care about Zobeck and a reason to care about the Margreve. This is the one firm requirement. The story moves back and forth between them for the whole campaign, and a character who only cares about one will spend half of it on the sidelines. A ranger who guides Zobeck merchants through the forest, a kobold miner whose family still keeps the old rites, a Collegium student who studies living magic: any of those work.

Some lineages fit especially well.

- [[Kobolds]] carry the Revolt and its unfinished business with them, and know the old tunnels under the city.
- [[Gearforged]] live the city's biggest open question.
- [[Dwarves]] often have grandparents who fought in the Revolt, and many dwarven families now run guilds.
- [[Humans]] are the most common folk in Zobeck. The [[Kariv]], wandering humans who travel between forest and city, understand both.
- [[Elves]] and [[Elfmarked]] usually have old ties to the forest and to the fey, and the oldest may remember the Stross years.
- [[Gnomes]] tinker in Zobeck, while the gnomes of [[Niemheim]] keep closer to the forest's magic.
- [[Bearfolk]], [[Alseid]], and [[centaurs]] belong to the Margreve and start out known to it.
- [[Ravenfolk]] serve the forest as messengers and are trusted with its secrets.
- [[Erina]], the hedgehog folk, live in burrows near the forest and under the city alike, and are good at noticing when something is wrong.

Other lineages are welcome with a backstory that ties them to both places. When you build your character, think about where your family stood in the Revolt, what you think of the forest, where you stand on the kobolds and the gearforged, and what you would be willing to give up for the thing you care about most. The campaign will ask.

## The Wider World
Zobeck sits in the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]] of [[Midgard]]. You don't need to know the setting to play, but these are the neighbors.

- [[The Ironcrags]], dwarven cantons to the west and old allies of the city
- The [[Magdar Kingdom]], human lands to the south
- The [[Blood Kingdom]], ruled by vampires, to the north
- [[The Darakhul Imperium]], the ghoul empire under the ground
- [[The Shadow Realm]], home of the shadow fey, never as far away as it should be
