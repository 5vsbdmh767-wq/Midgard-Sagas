*An old elvish court on a crag in the Black Hills, above [[The Margreve]].*

By the standards of the elvish nobility the Griffon Court was always a backwater: a summer camp for learning to ride griffons, hunting deer, and pondering the mysteries of the deep forest. It was also a martial court from the start. It bred and trained griffons, hippogriffs, wyverns, and two-headed eagles for the elvish armies, and it trained their knights and captains, the practical officers who kept rebellions down and borders moving. Its flying couriers once carried messages and goods by air to Thorn, Gennecka, Liadmura, and Velersh.

The Court is built on a stony crag above most of the canopy, with three large clearings for training, flying, and a small orchard and vineyard. Today it looks more like a woodland village than a seat of the old legions. It is ringed by marsh and by briar so old it has to be cut with axes, and most visitors come by ley road or on griffon wing, if they come at all.

The elves are gone, but the Court is still kept. A handful of elves, a score of elfmarked, and a few gnomes and halflings keep it running and keep a stable of trained griffons ready for the Imperatrix of [[Grand Duchy of Dornig|Dornig]]. Since her health failed, the best of the birds have gone to her favorites instead. The old tunnels, armories, and barracks of the Fifth Legion, the Flying Fifth, are said to be sealed. It is also said that the Court's armor, weapons, and training are still available to elves who prove worthy, but nobody would call the place welcoming.

The Court is commanded by [[Alluvar of Rothenheim]], Marshall of the Margreve.

##### With the Party
Alluvar met the party out in the [[Rushfens]] and walked them in himself. The briar opened for him without a sound and closed behind them. In the yard, a young elf was saddling a griffon. In the hall, Alluvar fed them and brought out the ledger: every surfacing of [[The Crumbling Tomb]] since the elves left Midgard. Only once before had anyone walked out of the tomb: Qeldar, something of shadow that the garrison took for an elf and was never sure of, who stayed at the Court, preached that the bright fey and the shadow fey were two sides of one coin, and then stole something and left.

That night the party split up. Lesshy and Rozen took their harvested materials to the forge and its halfling smith. Sidious watched from an upstairs window as nine pilgrims, who had come up the old road at dusk, began a rite on the eastern clearing. The garrison paid them no attention.
