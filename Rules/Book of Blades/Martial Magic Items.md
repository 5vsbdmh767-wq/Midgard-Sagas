## Magic Items for Martial Wielders

Magic items serve as a brilliant way to give martial characters options to get involved in fantastical combat while staying true to their class.

### Helm of Enriching Aura

*Wondrous Item, Very Rare 120,000 gp (Requires Attunement by a [[Paladin]])*

This brilliant helm radiates a commanding aura. Each friendly creature that starts its turn within 10 feet of you while you wear this helm gains a bonus on its weapon attack and damage rolls until the end of its turn. The bonus it gains depends on the weapon you are wielding. If you are wielding a magical weapon, the creature’s weapon attacks are magical. If you are wielding a weapon that gives you a bonus to attack and damage rolls, such as a *+2 longsword*, the creature gains that same bonus on weapon attacks. If you are wielding a weapon that deals extra damage, such as a *flame tongue longsword*, and that extra damage doesn’t require a special condition to activate, such as a critical hit, the creature’s weapon attacks deal that same extra damage on a hit. The helm can share only the passive, inherent traits of your weapon. It doesn’t share extra features you might add to the weapon, such as the bonus damage from the Divine Smite or Sneak Attack class features or the bonuses from a mechanist’s Augment class feature.

### Hidden Scabbard

*Wondrous Item, Uncommon 1,000 gp*

This scabbard is a plain and unassuming scabbard for holding a specific type of weapon, offering no detail or decoration to distinguish it from an ordinary one designed to hold that type of weapon. When a weapon appropriate for the scabbard is sheathed in it, the scabbard creates an illusion on itself and the weapon to disguise the pair as something inconspicuous, such as a belt pouch or other accessory. While wearing the scabbard, you can see through the illusion.

Physical interaction with the scabbard reveals the illusion. A creature other than you can use its action to examine the scabbard and determine it is covered by an illusion with a successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) check. Magic that can dispel illusions can end this illusion for 10 minutes.

### Magic Weights

*Wondrous Item, Rare 8,000 gp (Requires Attunement)*

These weights allow warriors to train all their waking hours. While wearing these weights, your movement speed is reduced by 10 feet, and you have disadvantage on all ability checks and saves made using DEX. Once you have worn them for at least 5 days without removing them, your body acclimates to them and these penalties end.

When you remove the magic weights after acclimating to them, your speed increases dramatically as your body feels lighter without its magical constraint. You act as though under the power of the *haste* spell for up to 5 minutes, at which point your body goes back to normal. If you wish to take advantage of this effect again, you must spend another five days reacclimating to the weights.

### Portable Palisade

*Wondrous Item, Uncommon 2,000 gp*

You place this small box onto the ground as a bonus action, expanding it into a short wall, 5 feet high, 6 inches thick, and 10 feet long. The wall cannot be moved or repositioned while it is active. The wall has 20 hit points and a 10 AC. If the wall runs out of hit points, it is knocked down and reverts to its box state.

If not knocked down, the wall remains until you use a bonus action to speak its command word again while touching it. The *portable palisade* regains any hit points that it lost every morning at dawn.

### Sheltering Cape

*Wondrous Item, Rare 4,000 gp (Requires Attunement)*

While you wear this cape, you gain a +1 bonus to AC. When a friendly creature you can see within 10 feet of you takes damage, you can use a reaction to flare this cape in that creature’s direction. The damage dealt to the target is reduced by 1d6. Once the cape has prevented 24 damage, it can’t be used in this way again until the next dawn.

### Speartag

*Wondrous Item, Uncommon 1,100 gp (Requires Attunement)*

This tiny tag is made from the feathers of a magical bird that can always find its way home. The speartag has 5 charges and regains 1d4 + 1 expended charges daily at dawn. When attached to a weapon with the Thrown property, you can use a bonus action to expend 1 charge and magically teleport that weapon into your empty hand, provided it is on the same plane of existence as you.

## Fabled Magic Items

The arms and armor in this section have epic stories that began long before your adventurer found them. Perhaps your characters knew or are related to past holders of these objects, or maybe the PCs have heard these stories for years and come across the very object in their travels. Maybe they simply found one lying in a dungeon, and the stories are waiting to be uncovered.

### Bash And Havoc

*This pair of massive brass gauntlets dwarfs the hands put inside them. They have knobby knuckles and flat, squared-off fingers, with an arcane light emitting from the metal seams. Each gauntlet sports an eldritch dial on the wrist, which can be turned to several settings. The settings are written in draconic runes. Bash*

and *Havoc* were the signature weapon of the Brass Mask, a legendary boxer whose identity was never discovered. Some believe, due to the draconic runes on the gauntlets, the boxer was a shapeshifted dragon, competing among humans in a sport the rest of his kind deemed vulgar and unsatisfying. If you saw him in the ring, though, you’d know the Brass Mask loved every second of it.

*Bash* and *Havoc* have elemental magic stored in them, which is released upon contact with an opponent. Though the Brass Mask preferred to use *Bash* with lightning and *Havoc* with fire, the dials on the wrists allow the wearer to adjust the elemental settings.

### Bash and Havoc

*Weapon (Gauntlets), Fabled*

*Priceless
 (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)* These gauntlets are a simple melee weapon that deal 1d6 bludgeoning damage plus your STR modifier. In addition, you have a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this weapon.

***Elemental Tuning (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while attuned to this weapon.

**5th Level.** Choose one element for each gauntlet from the following: acid, cold, fire, lightning, or poison. The gauntlets now deal 1d4 damage of that type in addition to the bludgeoning damage of the gauntlets. You can swap the type of elemental damage a gauntlet deals by turning its dial and letting it reset for 8 hours (which you can do during a long rest). During the reset time, it deals no elemental damage.

**9th Level.** The gauntlets now deal 1d8 bludgeoning damage plus 1d8 elemental damage.

**13th Level.** You are resistant to the two damage types that *Bash* and *Havoc* are set to.

**17th Level.** The gauntlets now deal 2d6 bludgeoning damage plus 2d6 elemental damage.

### Deadfall And Fetch

*These paired handaxes have well-worn, coppery wooden handles and dull gray blades that show signs of heavy use. A fallen tree is engraved upon the head of Deadfall, and a thunderhead is engraved on the head of Fetch. Deadfall*

and *Fetch* are a set of paired handaxes first owned by the dwarven ranger Deblim Flintcoat. Deblim came from a long line of lumberjacks and coal miners in the Red Hills but was filled with a wanderlust uncommon in his people. Shortly after leaving home with a pair of axes given to him by his parents, he met and became a traveling companion of the wizard Anishan Fateshaper who is believed to have enchanted the axes for his friend.

### Deadfall and Fetch

*Weapon (Paired Handaxes), Fabled*

*Priceless
 (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)* You have a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with both *Deadfall* and *Fetch*. When holding both handaxes, you can activate them as an action to gain advantage on WIS (Survival) checks for 1 hour. Once used, this property can’t be used again until the next dawn.

***Swiftly Hewn (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while wielding both axes. You cannot use any of these abilities unless wielding both axes.

**5th Level.** When you score a critical hit with *Deadfall*, the target must succeed on a DC 13 STR save or be knocked prone. When throwing *Fetch*, you can make a ranged attack without incurring disadvantage for throwing at long range.

**9th Level.** After you take an action to make a melee weapon attack with one of these handaxes and a bonus action to make a melee weapon attack with the other one, you can make an additional attack with either handaxe (no action required).

**13th Level.** *Deadfall* gains 3 charges. As an action, you can spend 1 charge and slam *Deadfall* to the ground, causing violent vibrations to spread out from you in a 30-foot cone. Creatures standing within the area of effect take 6d6 thunder damage and must make a DC 15 CON save. On a failure, affected creatures fall prone. On a success, a creature takes half as much damage and does not fall prone. *Deadfall* regains all expended charges daily at dawn. *Fetch* returns to your hand immediately after it is used to make a ranged weapon attack.

**17th Level.** Melee weapon attacks made with *Deadfall* deal an additional 2d6 thunder damage to Constructs and Plants. Ranged weapon attacks with *Fetch* do not incur disadvantage when made within 5 feet of a hostile creature.

### Featherwing

*This magical longbow is crafted from colorful feathers, each arm resembling a bird’s wing with the string tied between their tips.*

In times of legend, the wood elven ranger Syllania, sometimes called the Silverstar among her people, rescued a giant owl from ravenous harpies. Unbeknownst to Syllania, that owl was Prince Amylix of the Feathered Kingdoms. Grateful for his rescue by the young elf, he ordered a magical longbow created for her using the feathers of his avian subjects.

Sullania would use her magical longbow, called *Featherwing*, in the defense of woodland realms until her death at the hands of the dragon Gadirthex. *Featherwing* then passed into the dragon’s hoard where it is assumed to still lie, though there are rumors the dragon has bestowed the weapon upon one of its minions in exchange for loyal service.

### Featherwing

*Weapon (Longbow), Fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement by an Elf)*

While holding *Featherwing*, gentle breezes rustle your hair and stir your clothes. Should you fall, comforting winds buoy your descent, and you take no damage from falls of 60 feet or less. For falls of greater than 60 feet, subtract 60 from the total fall height to determine the bludgeoning damage you take from the fall.

***Favored Winds (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while wielding or carrying this longbow.

**5th Level.** You have a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this magic longbow. When you score a critical hit with this weapon, the target must succeed on a DC 13 STR save or be pushed up to 5 feet away from you by violent winds.

**9th Level.** When you attack with this longbow, you gain a flying speed equal to your walking speed until the end of your turn. If you are not on a solid surface that can hold your weight at the end of your move, you float down gently and land safely.

**13th Level.** While carrying *Featherwing*, you have advantage on WIS (Perception) checks that rely on hearing or sight. In addition, when you hit with an attack using this longbow, the target takes an extra 1d6 force damage as invisible claws tear at them.

**17th Level.** You now gain a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this longbow, instead of +1. While carrying this longbow, you always have a flying speed equal to your walking speed and you never provoke opportunity attacks while flying.

### Groundbyrne’s Greaves

*Made from fine dwarven steel, these greaves always sparkle as though they were polished yesterday. Decorative, downward- pointing arrows are engraved at their base.* The item’s story revolves around Saga Groundbyrne, a legendary dwarven soldier and adventurer. Born to a miner and an inventor, she left her underground home when she came of age. She longed for the skies. She longed to scale mountains, hunt dragons, and glide on the back of a gryphon. Her father, Gravus Groundbyrne, designed these greaves as a going-away present.

The downward-pointing arrows at the base of the greaves are more than just a reminder of where Saga came from; they’re dwarven runes that stabilize the greaves and make sure the wearer always lands on their feet.

### Groundbyrne’s Greaves

*Armor (Greaves), Fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)*

While wearing these greaves, you gain a +1 bonus to AC. If you fall while wearing them, you always land on your feet rather than falling prone.

***Grounding Power (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while wearing this armor.

**5th Level.** Bludgeoning damage you take from falling is halved.

**9th Level.** You take no bludgeoning damage from falling.

**13th Level.** When you fall from a height that would normally cause you to take damage, you can redirect it to creatures near where you land. All creatures within 10 feet of your landing point must succeed on a STR save with a DC equal to 8 + your PB + your STR modifier. On a failure, a creature takes bludgeoning damage equal to what they would have taken had they fallen from the same distance you fell.

**17th Level.** You gain the ability to redirect damage from your fall more precisely. You can now choose which creatures within 20 feet of your landing point take bludgeoning damage based on your fall distance.

### Laceros, The Godsplinter

*This light and delicate-looking lance features a gorgeous cream- colored handle accented by blood-red paint. Its multi-pronged head resembles a briar. Hefting it reveals it has a surprising amount of weight to it. Surgically precise, Laceros can bypass even the thickest of armors.* Many believe Laceros to be a weapon of the gods. Others believe it to be a godslayer. Like most magic items, it does not show signs of aging, but over the years it has been observed to grow heavier. Original accounts of this fabled lance describe it as it appears: a light and delicate spear. Anyone who hefts it now would find it difficult to wield without proper training. Perhaps this is the weight of the lives the Godsplinter has claimed across the ages.

### Laceros, the Godsplinter

*Weapon (Lance), Fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)*

You have a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this weapon. In addition, this lance radiates bright light within 30 feet of you and dim light for another 30 feet.

***Armor Piercing (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while wielding this weapon.

**5th Level.** When your attack roll succeeds by 12 or more, your target suffers a cumulative −1 to their AC. Their AC returns to normal at dawn.

**9th Level.** You now gain a +2 bonus to attack and damage rolls with this weapon, instead of +1.

**13th Level.** When you reduce a target’s AC, you also inflict a blessed wound on that target. At the beginning of each of its turns, a wounded target takes 1d4 radiant damage for each wound it has sustained from Laceros.

**17th Level.** You now gain a +3 bonus to attack and damage rolls with this weapon, instead of +2.

### The Patchwork Protector

*This studded leather armor is made using the hides of many different monsters and sewn together with faintly glowing threads. The whole piece vibrates softly when touched, as though it is alive.* They say Hearde the Huntress prowled the forest for so long she forgot what it was like to live among humans. Mundane game was of no interest to her. She sought magical creatures. Each time she killed a new one, she collected parts of it, cleaned them, and tanned their skins. With all the hides, pelts, and scales, she made herself a set of patchwork leather armor.

Visually repulsive, the armor is itself repulsed by contact with humanoid forms. Somewhere in the skins, the memories of being hunted by Hearde remain. The armor much prefers to fit the form of a beast, and its magical properties allow it to change shape to accommodate one willing to go through with such a transformation.

### The Patchwork Protector

*Armor (Studded Leather), Fabled Priceless (5th-level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)*

While wearing this armor, you gain a +1 bonus to AC. In addition, you can sense when a Beast or creature with the Animal tag is within 100 feet of you.

***Adaptive Armor (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while wearing this armor.

**5th Level.** Choose one Beast or creature with the Animal tag with a CR equal to or less than your character level. While wearing the armor, you can transform into this creature as an action. Your game statistics, except for INT, WIS, and CHA, are replaced by those of the chosen creature. You have the maximum hit points of the new form. When you revert to your normal form, you return to the number of hit points you had before you transformed. If you drop to 0 HP, you revert to your normal form. You retain all benefits of wearing this armor, even while transformed.

**9th Level.** You can choose an additional creature to transform into.

**13th Level.** You can choose two additional creatures to transform into.

**17th Level.** You now gain a +2 bonus to AC, instead of +1.You can transform into any Beast or creature with the Animal tag.

### Planner’s Piece

*This silver pocket watch has several hands used to denote time, direction, and weather conditions. Only one attuned to it can innately tell which hand refers to what. Across the interior is etched the phrase, “Never late again.” The exterior, if kept clean, has a mirror sheen and can be used as a mirror.* The *planner’s piece* is a pocket watch originally designed by the gnome hunter Davish Quickstep. Davish hunted prey of all kinds, including bounties. He relished the cerebral thrills of a well-executed trap. His schemes and captures eventually crossed the wrong criminal— after Davish turned a man in and he was hanged, the man’s the twin sister set a trap of her own. Davish escaped without a scratch, but he was a moment too late to save his family from the devastating fire that consumed their home. Afterward, he developed an obsession with flawless timing that outlasted his long years in the form of this magical timepiece.

### Planner’s Piece

*Wondrous Item, Fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement by a [[Rogue]])*

The *planner’s piece* has 3 charges. By spending 1 minute studying your surroundings and the *planner’s piece,* and expending 1 charge, you can cast the *augury* spell from it. The *planner’s piece* regains all expended charges daily at dawn.

***Time Keeper (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while holding this timepiece.

**5th Level.** You gain a bonus to initiative rolls equal to your INT modifier.

**9th Level.** As an action, you can expend 1 charge and click the top button of this timepiece. At any point in the next minute, you can click the button again as a bonus action to teleport to the space where you previously clicked it, as long as it is on the same plane.

**13th Level.** When you or a creature you can see makes an ability check, attack roll, or save, you can use your reaction and expend 1 charge. Roll a d20 and choose which of the rolls is used for that attack roll, ability check, or save.

**17th Level.** You can expend 3 charges to stop time for 3 rounds. No time passes for other creatures while you take 3 turns in a row, during which you can use actions and move as normal. This effect ends immediately if one of your actions affects a creature other than you or an object being worn or carried by someone other than you. In addition, the effect ends if you move more than 1,000 feet from the location where you activated it. When time returns to normal, roll a d6. On a roll of 1, the *planner’s piece* breaks and becomes inoperable.

### Reaver, The Endless Hunger

*The wood on the handle of this long scythe is dark ebony. The blade is permanently stained with blood and never needs sharpening. It seems to nick you and people around you easily, even if you are careful.* A lich requires souls to extend its accursed lifespan, but a soul harvested in the proper way can also be a delicacy. A soul is at its most potent at the moment it escapes the body. To that end, the scythe Reaver was forged. When a powerful warrior attunes to it and uses it to kill a foe, Reaver can directly transform the soul into energy for the warrior.

But dark magic such as this always comes with a price. Reaver is alive, many say, and shares its creator’s hunger. As the lich used Reaver to kill enemies and extract their souls, Reaver siphoned off bits of those souls for itself all the while. By the time the lich realized what was happening, it was too late. Reaver consumed its creator. Now the Endless Hunger waits in the lowest depth of a lich’s dungeon grave for its next accomplice—and eventually, its next meal.

### Reaver, the Endless Hunger

*Weapon (Scythe), Fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)*

You have a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this weapon. In addition, while you wield it, you have advantage on CHA (Intimidation) checks.

***Absorb Essence (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while wielding this weapon.

**5th Level.** When you deal the killing blow to a creature, you gain a Reaver die: a d8. When you deal damage with this scythe, as a reaction you can expend any number of Reaver dice you have gained as additional necrotic damage. All unexpended Reaver dice are lost at dawn.

**9th Level.** The Reaver die changes from a d8 to a d10. As an alternative to using Reaver dice to deal damage, you can take an action to expend a Reaver die to regain hit points equal to the die roll.

**13th Level.** The Reaver die changes from a d10 to a d12. If you use a Reaver die to regain hit points, you can do it as a bonus action instead of an action.

**17th Level.** When you deal necrotic damage with this scythe, you ignore a target’s resistance to necrotic damage.

### Shield Of Soaring Promise

*This perfectly round shield sports an intricate metalwork design of wings and feathers. There is no handle on the back, although it appears as though one was broken off.* Long ago, a pair of halfling warriors became feared mercenaries across the land. They moved and fought as one unit, providing perfect cover and support for each other. Shillian and Lanco, they were called. Sister and Brother. Shield and Lance. There was no chink in their rock-solid defense when together.

A rival mercenary group conspired to destroy them, slipping poison into both Shillian and Lanco’s drinks at a tavern one night. While Shillian succumbed, Lanco managed to hang on—barely. As he fought off the effects of the poison, an assassin arrived to finish him off. The assassin brandished a dagger and struck. At that moment, Shillian’s shield rose from its place on the ground and blocked the assassin’s attack. It was her promise to protect her brother even from beyond the grave.

### Shield of Soaring Promise

*Armor (Shield), Fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)*

While wielding this shield, you gain a +1 bonus to AC, on top of the normal AC bonus a shield gives you. In addition, the shield hovers near your arm, and you can use both hands as if you weren’t wielding it. Any effects, such as the *heat metal* spell, that might harm you due to wielding it have no effect on you.

***Hands-Free (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while attuned to this shield.

**5th Level.** As a bonus action, you speak a command word, and the shield floats near you instead of staying near your arm. It reacts to incoming damage, providing half cover. The shield moves with you, but if it is forcibly moved more than 5 feet away from you, the effect ends and the shield falls to the ground. You concentrate on this ability as if concentrating on a spell, and you can end it as a bonus action. When this effect ends, you may not use it again until you have finished a short rest.

**9th Level.** You can now move your shield up to 15 feet away from you, and it can provide half cover to another creature of your choice within range.

**13th Level.** The shield now provides a +2 bonus to your AC when activated, instead of +1.

**17th Level.** The shield now provides a +3 bonus to your AC when activated, instead of +2. In addition, you have advantage on CON saves to maintain concentration on your shield.

### Trainer’s Whip

*The wood-bound handle of this braided whip ends in a small animal paw. The leather has embossed impressions of claws, hooves, beaks, and pointed teeth.* The *trainer’s whip* was the property of Cruinna Finnthighearn, a ranger known for her menagerie of animal friends. Unfortunately, the ship carrying Cruinna sank in a violent storm. While the ranger and her companions survived the ordeal by working together, she was forced to leave behind much of her gear. When the ship went down, a trunk containing the *trainer’s whip*, among other things, was lost. Whether her magical whip lies at the bottom of the sea or the trunk washed up on some beach remains to be seen.

### Trainer’s Whip

*Weapon (Whip), Fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement)*

This whip has 3 charges. You can choose one Beast or creature with the Animal tag within 60 feet of you that has a friendly attitude toward you and that you can see. It must be a single creature, not a swarm.

While wielding the whip, as an action, you can expend a charge to teach that creature one trick. It performs this trick flawlessly for 1 week or until you use the whip to teach another trick. You can have only one creature under the whip’s control at a time. If you teach a trick to another creature, the effect ends on the first creature. The list of tricks includes:

- **Defend.** The creature protects a person or place that you designate, attacking creatures that approach that creature or location.

- **Fetch.** The creature retrieves an object or takes an object somewhere or to someone.

- **Heel.** The creature follows you closely, even into places it might not want to go. It withdraws from combat when ordered.

- **Help.** Unless given a command otherwise, the creature uses its action to assist your next attack against an enemy that you have already attacked.

- **Hunt.** The creature uses its natural talents to hunt for game and brings it back to you.

- **Perform.** The creature performs for an audience with entertaining tricks such as playing dead or walking on its hind legs.

- **Stay.** The creature waits somewhere for you. It avoids combat and hides if possible.

- **Track.** A creature with the Heightened Smell trait can attempt to follow a trail.

Once you have a taught a creature a trick, you do not have to continue wielding the whip for the effect to continue. In all cases, a creature affected by the whip defends itself if attacked. If the week time period expires, the effect ends and the creature remains friendly to you unless the GM decides the creature’s attitude changes toward you. All used charges return the following dawn.

***Beast Friend (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while wielding or carrying this whip. All references to Beast include creatures with the Animal tag, but not swarms.

**5th Level.** While within 5 feet of your chosen Beast, you each gain a +1 bonus to AC.

**9th Level.** Your chosen Beast’s attacks become magical. When you make a weapon attack with this whip, you can use your reaction to allow your Beast to make a single attack against the same target.

**13th Level.** Your chosen Beast gains a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls. When your Beast hits a creature with an opportunity attack, that creature’s speed becomes 0 until the start of your next turn.

**17th Level.** If you have spell slots, you can store a single spell of 1st through 5th circle in your Beast by touching the whip to the Beast as you cast the spell. If you attempt to store another spell in your Beast, your first stored spell is lost. The spell uses the slot, spell save DC, spell attack bonus, and spellcasting ability of the original caster, but when cast, it is otherwise treated as though the Beast is the caster. The Beast does not choose when to cast the spell, but rather it takes effect when a certain circumstance occurs that you choose when you store the spell in it. The Beast casts the spell, within any parameters set by the original caster, requiring no components, immediately after the circumstance is met for the first time.

### Wyrm-Scored Coin

*This gold piece is deeply marred by the breath of a dragon— permafrost, acid pitting, partially molten—to the point of obscuring its origin. However, its edges gleam brightly, and it flips wonderfully. A close inspection might reveal the scarring on the coin resembles a sharp, watching eye. . . .* It is said a dragon, upon defeat or death, may invoke a terrible curse upon all of their wealth. Other stories speak of a curse befalling a traitor who slays an ally to secure more of a dragon’s wealth. Regardless of the truth, every single coin and treasure from that dragon’s hoard shares the curse and brings doom, betrayal, and torment to all who hold them until the entire hoard is returned or destroyed. This coin was the first prized piece of a dragon’s hoard. It draws those attuned to it to acquire wealth, perhaps in the hope of retrieving the hoard and bringing peace to the dragon. In fact, if one were to properly cleanse such a hoard, the dragon’s ill will might expire, leaving a fortune behind.

### Wyrm-Scored Coin

*Wondrous Item, fabled Priceless (5th-Level and Higher Properties Require Attunement by a Rogue)*

The coin has a number of charges equal to your PB. When you damage an enemy with your Sneak Attack while carrying the coin, you can expend 1 charge to gain temporary hit points equal to half the value rolled on the Sneak Attack dice (rounded down). You regain all expended uses at dawn.

***Piece of the Hoard (Requires Attunement).*** As your level increases, you gain the following benefits while carrying this coin.

**5th Level.** As an action, you can expend 1 charge and cast the *locate* ritual spell from the coin at 2nd circle. If two or more objects within range meet the description you provide, this leads you to the most expensive one.

**9th Level.** You can now use your Uncanny Dodge feature to reduce the damage from any source of acid, cold, fire, lighting, or poison damage by half as a reaction, even if it isn’t an attack. When using your Evasion feature, you can use your reaction to succeed automatically on the DEX save if you roll and fail it.

**13th Level.** You can expend 1 charge to sprout draconic wings, gaining a flying speed equal to your walking speed. While flying in this manner, you do not provoke opportunity attacks.

In addition, as an action, you can expend 2 charges to cast *commune with nature* from the coin. In addition to the list of facts you can know from this spell, you can also know about dragons or the presence of prevalent wealth or treasures within range. As an action, you can expend 2 charges to cast *contact other plane* from the coin to contact a powerful dragon on another plane.

**17th Level.** You are immune to the frightened condition. In addition, at dawn you can choose one damage type: acid, cold, fire, lightning, or poison. You are immune to that damage type until the next dawn. While thus immune, parts of your skin are covered in a rough, ragged patchwork of scales.
