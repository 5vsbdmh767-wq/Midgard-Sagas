%% source: Book of Blades %%

Any dumb ogre can deal damage with a club. Superior combatants wield their tools inventively, shifting the tides of battle with a flick of the wrist or a swing of the sword arm. For players, weapon options are fantastic ways to differentiate existing weapons and give a wielder some different ways to think about using their regular tools.

> [!note] Optional Class Feature: Additional Monk Weapon Proficiencies
> At the GM’s discretion, a monk character can be considered proficient with the following weapons in addition to the normal simple weapons and shortswords: butterfly swords, ji, hook swords, and rope dart. These are considered monk weapons regardless of their properties.

Unless specified otherwise, a weapon attack used to perform a weapon option has only the option’s listed effect and doesn’t deal normal weapon damage. Weapon options can be used only when a wielder takes the Attack action on their turn, unless a feature like the fighter’s Martial Action allows a weapon option attack to be performed as a bonus action. Characters with the Multiattack feature can perform a weapon option in place of one of the attacks granted by Multiattack.

At the GM’s discretion, some weapon options might not work against certain creatures. For example, the trip weapon option might not work against a creature without discernable legs, such as an ooze, or that is anchored or attached to the ground in some way, such as a tree with animated limbs.

Not every weapon option presented here is appropriate for every campaign. Rather than allowing a weapon option to be broadly used by anyone who is proficient with a weapon, a GM might allow some weapon options to be gained only by training with a martial mentor (see **Martial Mentors** in **Chapter 2**).

As always, if a weapon option calls for a creature to make an ability check or save, the DC equals 8 + the attacker’s PB + attacker’s STR or DEX modifier (attacker’s choice).

## New Weapon Options

Monks have even more nuance with their distinct weapons, using their lightweight armaments to trip, distract, stun, and entangle foes. This section offers maneuvers inspired by the martial techniques employed by monks.

Remember that unarmed strikes are melee weapon attacks that use an attacker’s body, but these attacks don’t use weapons. Since all PCs are proficient with unarmed strikes, anyone can try unarmed strike weapon options. However, a GM might rule that unarmed strike weapon options are only available to characters with the Martial Art class feature.

## Weapon Options for Martial Arts Weapons

These weapon options are usable with the martial arts weapons detailed in this chapter. Unlike regular weapon options, these use your reaction instead of an action or bonus action.

### Disarming Parry

When a creature that you can see attempts to hit you with a melee weapon attack, you can use your reaction to make a STR save with a DC equal to the creature’s attack roll.

On a success, its attack misses, and it must succeed on a STR save or drop their weapon. The dropped item lands in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of the target. If no unoccupied space is within range, the item lands at the target’s feet.

### Disarming Twist

You attempt to disarm an opponent whose weapon you have trapped using your weapon’s hook (see **Weapon Trap** weapon option). As a reaction, make a DEX or STR check (your choice) contested by the target’s STR check. If you succeed, the target drops the trapped weapon. The dropped item lands in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of the target. If no unoccupied space is within range, the item lands at the target’s feet.

### Forearm Block

If you are wielding a tonfa in each hand and a creature you can see hits you with a melee weapon attack, you can use your reaction to make a DEX check with a DC equal to the creature’s attack roll. If you succeed, its attack misses.

### Hook and Pivot

When a creature your size or smaller that you can see misses you with a melee weapon attack, you can use your reaction to force it to make a DEX save. On a failure, you change places with the creature and can immediately shove it 5 feet.

### Weapon Trap

When a creature you can see attempts to hit you with a melee weapon attack, you can use your reaction to make a STR or DEX save (your choice) with a DC equal to the creature’s attack roll. You have advantage if you are wielding a hooked weapon in each hand. If you succeed, its attack misses and you trap its weapon until the end of your next turn or until you release it, whichever comes first. When a creature’s weapon is trapped in this way, it has disadvantage on attack rolls with that weapon, and you have disadvantage on attacks using your trapping weapon.

## New Weapon Options for Existing Weapons

These weapon options are usable by some weapons on the **Weapons** table in **Chapter 5** of *Player’s Guide* in addition to martial art weapons in this chapter.

### Ensnare

*Prerequisite: Any Whip*

When a creature you can see attempts to move out of your reach, you can keep it in place. Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, you ensnare the target with your weapon. It can’t move until the start of your next turn or until you release it, whichever comes first.

***Special.*** A rope dart can be used to perform this weapon option.

### Misdirect

*Prerequisite: Any Thrown Weapon*

You attempt to distract your target by intentionally missing. Make a ranged weapon attack with a thrown weapon. On a hit, the target must succeed on a WIS save or the next attack roll against it is made with advantage.

### Twine

*Prerequisite: Any Whip*

You attempt to grapple a creature by entwining them in your weapon. Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, the weapon tangles around the target, and you can immediately pull them 5 feet directly toward you. If this movement would pull a creature into damaging terrain, such as lava or a pit, it must succeed on a STR or DEX save (target’s choice) to avoid the pull.

***Special.*** A rope dart (see **New Weapons** in this chapter) can be used to perform this weapon option.

### Whirling Distraction

*Prerequisite: Any Whip*

As a bonus action, you whirl your weapon through an intricate set of spins to confuse a creature who can see you. The creature must succeed on a WIS save or you have advantage on melee weapon attacks against that creature until the start of your next turn.

***Special.*** A quarterstaff, nunchaku, or rope dart can also be used to perform this weapon option.

## Magic Item Weapon Options

These weapon options are beyond the boundaries of normal weapon functioning. They can only performed by enchanted weapons or permanent magic item weapons. These weapon options might be a little over the top for some campaigns, so a GM should check and make sure they are appropriate to the setting before allowing them.

### Arcane Breakage

*Prerequisite: A Magic Melee Weapon*

You attempt to dispel a simple magical effect holding a portal shut, such as an *arcane lock*. Make an attack roll, treating the spell effect’s DC as its AC for this attack. On a hit, the effect is dispelled and the weapon deals damage to the object the effect was cast on.

### Batter Up

*Prerequisite: A Magic Melee Weapon You Could Feasibly Use like a Bat, Such as a Bastard Sword or Greataxe*

You swing your weapon and attempt to launch a target far away from you. Make an attack roll. On a hit, the target must succeed on a STR or DEX save (target’s choice) or be launched in an arc a number of feet equal to your STR score. When the target lands, it falls prone.

### Break Resistance

*Prerequisite: A Magic Weapon*

You exploit a weak spot in your opponent’s resistances, temporarily bypassing its defenses. Make an attack roll. On a hit, your target must succeed on a CON save. On a failure, choose one damage type. All attacks on that target ignore any resistance to that damage type until the beginning of your next turn.

### Charge Up

*Prerequisite: A Magic Melee Weapon*

You channel all your energy into your weapon, transforming it into pure power. As an action, begin channeling. Then, as a bonus action, begin chanting. You must concentrate on this channeling as though you were concentrating on a spell. Provided you maintain concentration and chanting, your next attack with your magical weapon deals an extra amount of force damage equal to 5 × your PB.

### Deflect Spell

*Prerequisite: A Magic Melee Weapon*

When you are targeted by a spell or magical effect you can see, you can attempt to deflect it with the magic in the weapon you wield. As a reaction, choose a number (up to 5) to add to your save roll or AC that you use to resist the spell. Your magic melee weapon loses its magical properties for that same number of rounds. You can’t use this effect again until you finish a long rest.

### Power Vault

*Prerequisite: A Magic Polearm, Axe, or Other Weapon with a Long Handle*

You use your weapon to launch yourself high into the air. You must move at least 10 feet in a straight line. Then, as an action, you leap to a height of 10 feet + your STR modifier. The magic from your weapon keeps you suspended at that height until the beginning of your next turn, when you fall back down. You can choose to land safely in an empty space within 15 feet of where you left the ground and can safely change elevation (up or down) an amount equal to the height you leaped.

If an enemy is in range when you land, you can make a melee attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, you deal 2d6 bludgeoning damage in addition to the weapon’s regular damage. On a miss, you fall prone in an adjacent space.

### Self-Destruct

*Prerequisite: A Magic Weapon or Shield to Which You Are Attuned*

You set your attuned magic item to self-destruct. As an action, you begin a countdown that expires in 1d4 rounds at the beginning of your turn. When the item self-destructs, it creates a blast of radiant damage as detailed in the **Damage and Area of Effect Radius** table. The item is destroyed in the process.

### Transfer Power

*Prerequisite: A Magic Weapon to Which You Are Attuned*

You redirect the magical energy in your weapon to another source of magic. As an action, you can restore either a spell slot to an ally you can see or charges to a magic item you are holding. The circle of the spell slot or number of charges that can be restored is determined by your weapon’s rarity according to the **Spell Slot Circle or Charges Restored** table.

You can’t give a new spell slot or additional charges with this weapon option. You can only restore resources that have been depleted. Once you use this weapon option, any magical properties of your weapon are nonfunctional until the following dawn, although you remain attuned to it for the duration. The weapon continues to work as a mundane specimen during this time.

### Wall Waster

*Prerequisite: A Magic Melee Weapon That Deals Bludgeoning Damage*

You can bash a hole in a magical wall, such as one created with the *wall of stone* spell or similar. Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, you create a break in a magical wall that is 5 feet wide, 5 feet thick, and 10 feet tall. If any of the wall’s duration remains, then once your weapon is removed from this break, the wall reforms in that space.

## Rogue Tricks

This section provides additional options useful to rogues and others who take on their roles in a game.

### Dead to Rights

It can be disappointing to hold a foe at knifepoint or spellpoint, only for them to slip away with a Disengage trait or by sucking up the opportunity attack. Worse, your foe might ignore your threat completely and attack anyway.

If you are wielding a melee weapon and your opponent is surprised, you can use your action to declare a target dead to rights. You can also declare a single creature dead to rights if it’s in range of your attack for a full round and neither of you moves, takes a Dodge action, or attacks any creature during that time. The target must have no intention or means to defend itself, such as a pleading negotiator.

Dead to rights is a special Ready action (see **Chapter 6** of *Player’s Guide*). If the trigger conditions you set for attacking the target are met and you hit, you score an automatic critical hit.

If two or more instances of dead to rights occur, such as when a hidden enemy reveals itself and puts you dead to rights while you were staring down a foe, then a standoff occurs. If only one creature in initiative opts to attack, its dead to rights Ready action triggers first. If two or more parties meet their attacking trigger conditions on the same initiative count, they must make contested DEX checks. The winner can use its reaction to benefit from dead to rights.

A GM might consider dealing the attack’s maximum damage instead of normal critical damage. This could especially apply if the target is restrained, paralyzed, or otherwise unable to avoid the worst.

### Setup

Due to their typically high DEX modifiers, rogues often go early in initiative, when they would rather wait for an ally to get into position first. The Setup option lets you address this hurry-up-and-wait situation.

If you are not surprised and none of your enemies have acted in this encounter, as a bonus action, you can swap your place in the initiative order with one ally you can see. The ally takes its turn on your initiative count. Then you take your turn (without a bonus action) on their former initiative count.

### Cunning Tricks

A cunning rogue has a toolkit of tricks to play in a fight, from a barroom tussle to a dragon’s lair. You can perform these tricks as extensions of your Cunning Action class feature at 2nd level. If the trick requires a save, the difficulty class is equal to 8 + your DEX modifier + your PB. All cunning tricks are bonus actions in addition to other requirements included in the trick.

Once you’ve used a trick, any creature that witnessed it has advantage on saves to resist the same trick for 24 hours.

**Blind.** This trick requires a handful of sand, dust, ash, embers, ink, or similar substance. You toss a substance at the eyes of a creature (or its ocular equivalent). It must succeed on a DEX save or be blinded until it spends a bonus action clearing the grime.

**Grab.** A creature within 5 feet of you and also within 5 feet of an ally must make a DEX save. On a failure, you or the ally grapple it as a reaction.

**Graze.** This trick requires a weapon with the Finesse property. Make an attack roll. On a hit, you do not add your ability modifier to damage. Your next attack against the same creature before the end of your next turn can be a Sneak Attack, as if you met the normal requirements.

**Mug.** After attacking a creature with a melee weapon attack or a shove, you can attempt to snatch a small, easily concealed object carried by the target without being noticed. Make a DEX (Sleight of Hand) check with a DC equal to the creature’s Perception score. On a success, you steal the item without being noticed, and you can stow it among your possessions without being seen. You can also plant a small object using the same rules. In the case of volatile items like alchemist’s fire, an acid flask, or holy water, you can use this trick instead of a ranged attack and douse the target on a success.

**Press.** You use nimble leverage to reposition a foe. Make a melee weapon attack against a creature. Regardless of whether you hit, the press follows. The creature must make a STR or INT save (your choice). On a failure, it’s pushed 5 feet.

**Startle.** If a creature is surprised or has not acted in combat yet, you can frighten it with loud noise and sudden movement. A creature within 30 feet of you must succeed on a WIS save or be frightened of you until the end of your next turn. If you deal damage to it before the frightened condition expires, it is extended for another turn. The creature can make a new WIS save at the end of each of its turns to end the effect. Afterward, it can’t be affected by this trick again.

### Set Pitfalls

With clever use of thieves’ tools and some useful materials, you can avoid figurative pitfalls of combat by setting literal pitfalls for your foes. A rogue can set up a pitfall where, should the target fail a save, they’ll be subject to a rogue’s Sneak Attack damage and potentially suffer a debilitating condition. To set a pitfall, you need at least 1 minute, you need to have a set of thieves’ tools and be proficient with them, and you must have any other materials as decided by your GM. Examples include rocks hanging from a string, oil to create a slick, ball bearings, or acid flasks. The save DC of a pitfall is equal to 8 + your PB + your INT modifier.

A pitfall affects 5 square feet of space. Compare a creature’s Perception score to the pitfall’s save DC to determine whether the creature notices it.

The base damage of a pitfall is 1d4 + your INT modifier bludgeoning damage. Certain materials can change the damage type, as appropriate. In addition, you add your Sneak Attack damage to the final result.
