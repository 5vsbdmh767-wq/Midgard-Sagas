%% source: Book of Blades %%

Throughout history—both real and imagined—terrain and weather have affected the outcomes of battles, which in turn affect the outcomes of wars. At the fabled Battle of Thermopylae, the Spartans used the narrow mountain passages to limit the number of Persians they were fighting at a time. In 1274, a typhoon struck the Mongol fleet attempting to invade Japan, leading to a third of the ships sinking, the rest damaged, with around 13,000 casualties. In wars as far-ranging as the American Revolution, the Peninsular War, and the Vietnam War, military forces at a significant numerical disadvantage used terrain and weather to turn the tide of battle. Indeed, terrain is so important that Sun Tzu devoted an entire chapter to it in *The Art of War*.

Terrain has played an equal role in fictional combat. A long time ago in a galaxy far, far away, the lava on a certain planet had significant consequences for one combatant— after the other used the high ground to his advantage. Or in Kurosawa’s *Seven Samurai* (and any number of the many films and shows based on that classic), the eponymous heroes get the town ready for a much larger, stronger invading force by setting traps, reinforcing key defensive positions, and establishing low walls for cover. And, of course, in the *Lord of the Rings* trilogy, which inspired much of modern roleplaying, terrain plays a key role in many scenes: Fangorn Forest, the Mines of Moria, the Black Gate, and more.

## Minor Mechanics

Each terrain feature in this section presents a snippet of mechanics that can keep the battleground lively. Some are damage-causing effects, while others might incur a condition or affect mobility in unusual ways. In all cases, both PCs and NPCs can have equal access to them. Many are similar to traps or hazards and can be used in conjunction with any of those features.

It’s a big ask to expect GMs to come up with brand new terrain and environmental conditions for every fight. However, there’s a lot to pull from in here. Mix and match the effects listed here to spice up a battlefield, and use the list of weather effects (see **Weather Effects** in this section) to make your own.

## Tips For Using Terrain Features

Keep the following in mind:

- Make sure players know there are mechanical impacts for the terrain and they can use terrain to their advantage. If a tree can get knocked over in the woods, but no one knows it, can it really get knocked over? Of course, you can still spring surprises mid-fight! But if you want PCs to use an environmental feature, tell the players it is there.

- Describe both the terrain and its effect on the fight.

What’s it like when the ship suddenly lurches to port and the cargo starts going overboard? If you only say that “something” happens, you’re missing an opportunity to add flavor.

- Let NPCs and monsters use these features too. Baddies are scary based on their CR, but they’re even scarier when they’re smart. How will your players react when their enemies snare them in a giant spider’s web?

## Terrain Features By Environment

To expedite your ability to create battlefields ahead of time or improvise interesting combat on the fly, the following terrain features and their effects are organized by the types of environment they are typically found in. In addition, each environment references types of weather that can impact combat for good or ill (see **Weather Effects**).

## Desert

If it weren’t for the violent swings in temperature, the ever‑shifting topography, the dangerous animals, the lack of readily available food and water, and a host of unpredictable and deadly elements, the desert would be lovely.

***Suggested Weather:*** Gusting, lightning storm, sandstorm, scorching heat

### Mirage

*In the deep desert, anything that disrupts the endless sea of dunes can trick exhausted minds.* When a creature spends a short rest in a 10-foot-square patch of desert terrain that interrupts the endless dunes, they can set up the conditions for a mirage. A player must describe the mirage to the GM and what effect it has on a fooled viewer. Afterward, a creature within 50 feet of the mirage that sees it and does not realize its illusory nature must succeed on a DC 16 WIS save or be subject to the command of the creature that made it. It acts like the *command* spell, but the command must relate to the mirage. For instance, if the mirage is a pond of water, then the one‑word command can be “Drink!”

### Cactus Patch

*The needles of these plants range from tiny, incessant irritants to piercing spines as long as daggers. All of them have a way of getting in the openings of armor.* These cacti grow in a 15-foot-radius patch. The area is difficult terrain, and a creature moving through it takes 1d4 piercing damage for each 5 feet of movement. A creature moving through can make a DC 14 DEX (Sleight of Hand) or STR (Athletics) check to artfully hack and slash the cacti, leaving a trail of difficult terrain that deals no damage to anyone moving in the creature’s path.

### Sand Squid Trap

*Stories tell of predatory creatures that shun all forms of light and live deep beneath the surface of the desert. They are drawn by the steady rhythm of creatures walking.* As a bonus action, a creature can make a DC 15 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check. On a success, it can identify a hidden sand squid and intuit how to entice it to attack another target—by creating rhythmic sound on the ground near the target.

The targeted creature must succeed on a DC 17 DEX save or be knocked prone and become grappled by one of the sand squid’s tentacles. As an action on its turn, a grappled creature can make a DC 17 STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check (target chooses) to escape. If it fails, it can repeat the save at the end of its turn. On a second failed save, the creature remains prone and becomes restrained. On a third failed save, the creature is dragged beneath the sands and begins suffocating.

### Bleached Skull of the Tyrant

*The empty eye sockets of this massive skull hold your gaze, penetrating your soul with ancient animus.* This Large skull retains residual power from whatever came before. As a bonus action, a creature can make a DC 19 INT (Religion) or WIS (Insight) check to identify the nature of the skull. On a success, while adjacent to the skull, it can activate the skull’s gaze as an action. The gaze radiates in a 25-foot cone for 1 minute. Each creature in the cone must succeed on a DC 18 WIS save or be frightened until the end of its next turn. A creature that fails by 10 or more is stunned until the end of its next turn.

### Acid Ponds

*Yellow ponds of roiling sulfur dot the landscape. Any debris that falls in immediately bubbles and hisses.* A creature that falls (or gets shoved) into this sulfurous liquid must succeed on a DC 15 CON save for its nonmagical weapons and armor. Wooden weapons roll the save with disadvantage. On a failure, the weapons take a cumulative −1 to attack and damage rolls and armor takes a cumulative −1 to AC. Any piece of equipment that is reduced to −5 is destroyed. A creature that starts a round in the acid pond takes 2 (1d4) acid damage, and its weapons and armor must repeat the previous save.

Spells that affect water (e.g. *shape water*, *destroy water*, *control water*) function on the acid ponds and puddles as they would on normal water except sulfurous water can’t be created or replicated using magic.

### Gas Pocket

*Winds belch up from beneath the sands, leading to light- headedness in some. Unprotected flame can ignite the gas, flaring into a blaze for a moment before fading away.* As a bonus action, a creature in or adjacent to a space with a gas pocket can produce a source of fire to light it. The gas ignites on initiative count 20. When a gas pocket ignites, all creatures within a 15-foot radius of the gas pocket must make a DC 17 DEX save. On a failure, a creature takes (22) 5d8 fire damage. On a success, it takes half as much damage.

## Forest

In the shadows beneath the leafy canopy, the forest is an ever-changing vertical environment. Those adept at navigating the towering trees and verdant undergrowth can find a tactical advantage.

***Suggested Weather:*** Light rain, windy

### Dead Tree Hollow

*Not all the trees in the forest are flourishing. A dead tree with
 a large hollow and exposed roots leans precariously among the other trees.* The dead tree hollow is a tree trunk that stands upright. A creature adjacent to it can push it over as an action. The tree falls away from the creature in a 15-foot line that is 5 feet wide. Creatures in the path of the falling dead tree hollow must succeed on a DC 16 DEX save or take 14 (4d6) bludgeoning damage. On a success, a creature in the line moves to the nearest empty space and takes no damage. The area where the tree falls is difficult terrain.

### Hornet’s Nest

*Though a single sting is merely an annoyance, the fury of an angered nest could prove debilitating. They’re known to be aggressively territorial, and it probably wouldn’t take much to draw them out.* A WIS (Perception) check against a DC 13 reveals the hornet’s nest 10 feet overhead. A creature can use an attack to agitate the hornets. Once attacked, the hornets swarm, creating a lightly obscured area with a 10-foot radius centered on the ground beneath the hive. A creature that enters the area or starts its turn there takes 7 (3d4) piercing damage and must succeed on a DC 14 CON save or take another 7 (3d4) poison damage. Producing fire or smoke disperses the hornets.

### Whipping Thorn Branch

*The tiny nettles and spiky needles on this supple branch are notorious for getting in the eyes of any creature foolish enough to carelessly rush through the bushes.* As an action, a character adjacent to this thorny tree can pull a branch so that it whips another creature that is also adjacent to the tree. The character whipping the branch makes a STR (Athletics), INT (Nature), or WIS (Survival) check (attacker’s choice) contested by the target creature’s DEX (Acrobatics) check. On a success, the target takes 2 (1d4) damage. In addition, the target is blinded and can move at only half speed until the end of its next turn.

### Climbing Tree

*Low, thick vines wrap around tree trunks and intertwine with limbs. The bark and sap grips the hands and feet like a parent guiding a young child. Some trees are just better for climbing.* When climbing this tree, all creatures have a climbing speed equal to half their normal speed.

Once a climber is 10–20 feet off the ground in the tree, a creature has half cover from those below. Once it is higher than 20 feet off the ground in the tree, it has three-quarters cover. However, anyone higher than 20 feet into the tree makes all melee and ranged attacks as though their target has half cover.

A creature in the tree at least 10 feet above another creature can drop down onto it as part of a melee weapon attack. On a hit, the attacker can add an additional die of their weapon’s type to damage. On a miss, the attacker takes 1d6 fall damage per 10 feet dropped and must succeed on a DC 14 DEX (Acrobatics) check or fall prone on a limb.

### Gnarled Roots

*Large tangles of roots create an uneven forest floor. Some protrude far enough from the earth to create loops capable of ensnaring even the deftest feet.* The roots fill a 10-foot-radius area that is difficult terrain. A creature that succeeds on a melee attack against a target within the root area can take a bonus action to make a DC 15 STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check. On a success, the target is knocked prone. On a failure, the attacker falls prone instead.

While among the roots, any DEX checks involving footwork are made with disadvantage. Any normal actions that rely on footwork, such as Dodge or Uncanny Dodge, require a DC 15 DEX (Acrobatics) check to succeed.

### Tangle of Hanging Vines

*Ropey vines hang down from the canopy to the forest floor. They run the gamut from thick to thin, but all seem to pulse with the desire to wrap around anything they touch. Some are tangled upon themselves, making them nearly impossible to climb, and yet there may still be a use to them.* This tangle of hanging vines forms curtain-like lines in the forest, providing half cover to those obscured by them. As a bonus action, a creature within 5 feet of the vines can attempt to entangle another adjacent creature in them. The attacker makes an INT (Nature), DEX (Sleight of Hand), or WIS (Survival) check (attacker’s choice) contested by the target’s STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check. If the target loses, they are restrained. A restrained creature or an adjacent ally can spend an action getting free of the vines.

## Mountains

Mountainous terrain threatens danger wherever you look—false footing, looming avalanches, or falling boulders. The weather is equally unpredictable. A clear, sunny peak can be overwhelmed by a towering blizzard with little warning.

***Suggested Weather:*** Blizzard, eclipse, foggy, gusting, hail, heavy rain, hurricane, lightning storm, light rain, windy

### Whistling Stones

*A strange rock formation with horizontal holes creates a
 strangled sound. Perhaps adjusting them by removing built up debris or simply repositioning them could change the nature of the sounds created.* As an action, a creature can make a DC 15 INT (Nature) or a WIS (Survival) check to adjust the stones so that they whistle. If the creature is proficient in any musical instrument, they can roll the check with advantage. On a success, when the wind howls, the stones whistle and vibrate so loudly that all creatures within a 20-foot radius of the stones are deafened and can’t cast spells with verbal components.

Once the stones are whistling, a creature can make a successful DC 15 INT (Nature) or a WIS (Survival) check to stop them from making noise.

### Cliff Ledge

*A narrow cliff ledge juts out over a sheer rock wall. Tiny plants and mosses fill the hairline fractures that built up over years of freezes and thaws, leaving the ledge barely hanging on the rest of the cliff.* This cliff ledge is 25 feet long and 5 feet wide. As a bonus action, a creature can make a successful DC 17 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check to identify a network of fractures in the ledge. As an action, it can then strike the network of fractures, causing the cliff ledge to crumble and collapse.

A creature on the ledge when it collapses must succeed on a DC 17 DEX save or fall prone in an adjacent space. If the creature fails by 5 or more, it slides into a trench and is buried by dirt and debris, taking 14 (4d6) bludgeoning damage. Creatures buried in the trench can use an action to dig out from the debris and use all of its walking speed to move to the nearest open space.

### Surfing Slate

*Several flat stones lie on the slick mountain slope. The naturally smooth terrain makes it seem possible to slide from one end of the mountain to the other. Though the ground is wet from moisture, the tops of the stones have dried in the sun and seem to invite the feet of the daring.* A creature that moves 10 or more feet in a straight line on this slate can make a DC 14 STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check to slide an additional 40 feet in the same direction. This movement does not provoke attacks of opportunity and is not limited by the creature’s speed. If the creature still has movement left over at the end of the slide, it can still use it.

### Draconic Burial Mound

*Enormous moldering bones protrude from the uneven ground. Though the dragons buried here are long dead, their malicious intent and elemental affinities remain.* The burial mound covers a 10-foot-square area. A creature standing on it that makes an attack can make a DC 14 WIS (Insight) check as part of the attack to get aid from the malevolent dragon spirits. On a success, the attack is a critical hit on a roll of 17–20 and the damage becomes one of the following types at random: acid, cold, fire, lightning, poison, or thunder.

The burial mound can be used 1d4 + 2 times. It fully recharges during a short rest.

### Giant Boulder

*A boulder larger than a wagon teeters on a ledge high above.* A boulder sits on a 50-foot ledge. To send it falling, a creature can attack the debris surrounding its base (12 AC, 15 HP). If an attack reduces the debris’s hit points to 0, the boulder falls on a 10-foot-square area below. Creatures in the area must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or take 27 (5d10) bludgeoning damage.

The giant boulder continues to roll along any slope. If the area is flat, it rolls in a random direction for 30 feet, dealing an additional 11 (2d10) bludgeoning damage to a creature that fails a DC 15 DEX save.

### Permanent Storm

*Whether from an ancient battle or ages of erosion, a depression has formed on part of the mountain. Winds continuously swirl the dense vapor that covers the ground despite the icy rain falling here.* A permanent storm swirls in a 20-foot radius. The area within the storm is difficult terrain. For those outside the storm, the area is heavily obscured. For those inside the storm, everything outside is lightly obscured. All DEX (Stealth) checks within the storm are made with advantage and creatures may move while remaining hidden.

## Sailing Ship

There’s nothing like the open sea. It’s not all sharks, pirates, and scurvy; there’s ropes and grog too! You’ll get used to all the climbing and the constant swaying soon enough.

***Suggested Weather:*** Eclipse, foggy, gusting, heavy rain, hurricane, light rain, scorching, windy

### Drop Anchor

*Generally speaking, dropping anchor at full speed is not advisable.* As an action, a creature adjacent to the lever of the anchor winch may throw it, dropping the anchor. Roll on the **Drop Anchor** table to determine what happens:

**Drop Anchor**

| d6 | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | The anchor catches hard and the ship pitches, submerging the bow. All creatures on the ship must make a DC 15 DEX save or be pulled 20 feet toward the front of the ship and be knocked prone. On a success, a creature gets pulled only 10 feet and is not prone. |
| 3–4 | The anchor lands on the starboard side and the ship moves in a circle. All creatures on a circling ship must succeed on a DC 15 STR save or be pulled 10 feet toward the turn, due to the pitch of the ship. If the creature reaches the edge of the ship, it must succeed on a DC 14 DEX save or fall into the water. |
| 5–6 | The effects are the same as a 3–4 result, but the anchor lands on the port side and the direction of movement is adjusted accordingly. |

A creature can attempt to break the anchor chain (16 AC, 20 HP, resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical weapons) or make a successful DC 19 STR (Athletics) check to pull the anchor up. Once the chain is broken or the anchor is pulled up, the ship rights itself.

### Faulty Hatchway

*A latticed wooden hatch, set in the floor and warped by the ocean air, presents a danger to sailors who don’t have their sea legs.* A creature that enters the 10-foot square of a faulty hatchway must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or fall through the hatch and take 7 (3d4) bludgeoning damage from rolling down the stairs. The creature ends up prone at the bottom of the stairs.

### Ship Wheel

*Many new sailors haven’t seen how far a ship can lean before capsizing, but just because the ship stays up doesn’t mean the sailors stay on.* As an action, a creature within 5 feet of the steering wheel can turn the wheel hard to one side. All creatures on the main deck must make a DC 15 STR save or be pushed 15 feet in the opposite direction the ship is turning. If this movement pushes a creature over the side of a ship, it must make a DC 15 DEX save. On a success, it stays on its feet and remains at the edge of the ship. On a failure, it’s thrown from the ship and falls into the water. Unsecured objects are pushed 15 feet automatically.

### Almost Friendly Dolphins

*Dolphins are among the most intelligent animals found in the seas, and these particular dolphins have a sense of humor. When a creature falls from a ship, they dance on the surface, clapping and cackling. In exchange for making them laugh, they happily help anyone back onto the ship.* As an action, a creature in the water and in view of a ship can make a DC 13 WIS (Animal Handling) check to convince friendly dolphins to help them back onto the ship. On a success, the dolphins use their heads to push the creature toward the ship, up to 60 feet per turn, and then launch them into the air when close. At the top of the launch, the creature must succeed on a DC 12 DEX save or fall prone on the deck of the ship. If the creature fails by 5 or more, it falls back into the water, and all the dolphins laugh at them. On a failure, the dolphins mock the creature with whistles and clicks (most creatures don’t understand what’s being said).

### Drop the Mainsail

*Though they look like big sheets from a distance, up close the sails are made of heavy, durable cloth. Despite this, it would only take a couple of cuts to the right ropes to drop it down.* As an action, a creature within 5 feet of the mast can drop the sail. The sail is 50 feet long and falls in a 10-foot-wide line across the deck of the ship. Creatures under the sail when it falls must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or be knocked prone. Those knocked prone are in difficult terrain and are heavily obscured until they can get out from under the sail. A creature under the sail can spend their entire movement to get out from the sail and stand up.

### Skysails

*Above the mainsails are the topsails, and above those are the skysails. Wind whips through the sheets up here and it’s very difficult to hear, but from this great perch, the rest of the ship is easily accessible—provided you have a long enough rope.* Using all of its speed, a creature on the mast can make a DC 13 STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check to swing to any open space on the ship. On a failure, the creature lands prone at the base of the mast.

Alternately, as an action, the creature can swing into another creature on the ship, making a STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check contested by the target’s STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics). On a success, the target takes 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage and is pushed up to 15 feet. If this moves the creature over the edge of the ship, it must make a DC 16 DEX save or fall into the water.

## Tundra

The deep cold of the tundra impedes thought. But pain means you can still feel your body, and that’s good. Out here, the snow can blind or bury you, or you can turn it into your ally.

***Suggested Weather:*** Blizzard, eclipse, gusting, hail, windy

### Subzero Gusts

*A faint whistling of the wind going through the trees and across the rime-covered landscape always precedes the terrible icy blasts, capable of freezing the blood in one’s veins.* A 10-foot-wide line of subzero air howls across the tundra. Creatures that enter the path of the subzero gusts must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or lose 15 feet of speed, which is cumulative with other effects. If the creature is wet or has taken cold damage this turn, the save is made with disadvantage. If a creature’s speed is reduced to 0 and they fail a CON save against the wind, they receive one level of exhaustion. The creature’s speed returns to normal once it has completed a short rest in a warm place.

### Ice Geyser

*All at once, a mix of boiling water blasts into the air, spraying scalding water and shards of icy shrapnel everywhere. The water thrown overhead comes down as coin-sized clumps of hail, pelting anyone still standing nearby.* The ice geyser acts on initiative count 20. On every third turn, the geyser erupts. A creature within 15 feet of it must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or take 5 (2d4) fire damage, 5 (2d4) cold damage, and 5 (2d4) bludgeoning damage.

A creature can perturb the geyser (for example by throwing something into it) to cause it to erupt immediately.

### Layered Snow

*The crust of the snow cracks with every step, making travel far more troublesome. However, with a little understanding or some light footwork, one could move across it and gain the upper hand on a foe.* Areas covered in layered snow are difficult terrain. As part of movement, a creature can make a successful DC 14 INT (Nature) check to ignore the difficult terrain this round. If that creature moves more than 10 feet and makes an attack, it gets advantage on its first attack. On a failure, the creature stumbles and loses half its movement.

### Icicle Lens

*Hanging sheets of preternaturally clear ice can focus light. Even the light of a torch could become dazzlingly bright if used in conjunction with one of these ice sheets.* As an action, a creature with a light source (such as a torch or a *light* spell) can attempt to angle the light and/or the ice sheet to blind another creature within 30 feet of it. The target must succeed on a DC 16 CON save or be blinded until the end of its next turn.

### Powder Snow Drift

*Between the winds and heavy snowfalls, drifts have formed the size of battlements. Only the lightest of the snow seems to be moved so easily. Anything that hits the drifts causes a powdery explosion, creating a localized white-out.* The snow drift is 10 feet tall and at least 40 feet long. A creature can run through the powder snow drift, as long as they’ve moved at least 10 feet in a straight line toward it. When a creature hits the snow drift, it blows apart, leaving a heavily obscured area in a 10-foot radius for 1d4 rounds. A creature that falls into the snow drift from a height of less than 100 feet takes no fall damage but lands prone and is blinded until it leaves the drift.

### Frozen Tree

*Cruel winter has allowed the snow and ice to permeate even the heartwood of this ancient tree. The ice has spread to every limb, branch, and leaf. Even a small cut could result in sharing the fate of this frozen sentinel of the forest.* As an action, a creature can strike the tree. The tree shatters in a 15-foot cone in the direction of the blow. A creature in the cone must make a successful DC 15 CON save or take 4 (1d8) cold damage and 4 (1d8) slashing damage and reduce their speed by half. A creature that fails the save must make another DC 15 CON save at the end of its turn. On a failure, its speed is reduced to 0 and it must make another save at the end of its next turn. On a success, its speed returns to normal. If the creature’s speed is 0 and it fails the subsequent CON save, it is petrified in ice.

## Cavern

More civilizations have come and gone down here than can be named. Their crumbling remains are covered in flora and fauna adapted to a world without light.

***Suggested Weather:*** Foggy, light rain (in the form of dripping ceilings)

### Giant Spider Web

*A spider web, large enough to ensnare a half-dozen adventurers, hangs above the entrance. Signs of neglect suggest it hasn’t been mended or tended in some time. With only a little effort, one could release it from its anchors.* As an action, a creature can use a bladed weapon or similar effect to free a giant spider web from its anchor. The web then drops onto a 15-foot-radius area below it. Creatures inside the web’s radius must succeed on a DC 15 DEX save or become restrained.

The web area is considered difficult terrain, and a creature that steps onto the web for the first time must succeed on a DC 14 STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check or become restrained. Unlike other webs, the giant spider web does not burn. However, any cold damage done to the webs destroys their natural adhesive permanently.

Creatures restrained by the web can use an action to make a DC 14 STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check to escape.

### Stalactites and Stalagmites

*A formation of stalactites and stalagmites form what looks to be a grinning maw. Navigating the space is difficult, and the jagged stones threaten to crush and pierce anyone passing through.* A Large or larger creature must squeeze through spaces with the stalactites and stalagmites. When an enemy is adjacent to either stone type, a creature can make a DC 15 CHA (Deception) check to feint as a part of their attack. On a success, the enemy takes an additional 12 (5d4) piercing damage from the stalactite or bludgeoning damage from the stalagmite. If this damage is enough to kill the creature, it is either smashed by the falling stalactite or impaled on the stalagmite.

### Solana’s Moss

*Legends tell of flora beloved and blessed by the gods. Aside from its silver color, what a celestial being found so alluring about such a humble piece of moss is far from obvious.* As an action, a creature can ignite the roots of [[Solana]]’s moss. Once ignited, it sheds bright light in a 5-foot radius and dim light for an additional 5 feet for 1 round. A creature that starts its turn within the bright light must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be blinded until the start of its next turn. The light is considered sunlight, and creatures with sunlight sensitivity have disadvantage on the CON save. The moss is fully consumed by the flame at the start of the igniting creature’s next turn.

### Chimerical Fungus

*A three-“headed” fungus blooms out of cracks in the stone. Wispy spore clusters roll off the fungus even from the languid air currents down here.* As a bonus action, a creature can smash a 5-foot patch of fungus to discharge its spores on a target within 5 feet, including itself. The triggering creature chooses one effect of the spores: *calm emotions* (as the 2nd-circle Divine spell) with a DC 16 CON save, *faerie fire* (as the 1st-circle Wyrd spell) with a DC 16 DEX save, or *hideous laughter* (as the 1st‑circle Arcane spell) with a DC 16 WIS save. Each of these effects lasts for 1 minute, or until the creature either makes the save at the end of its turn or uses an action to remove the spores.

### Confounding Tunnels

*The winds of the caverns move through a system of channels that produce sounds ranging from a flute to a howl.* A creature adjacent to a confounding tunnel can use an action to make a DC 14 CHA (Deception) or CHA (Performance) check to throw its voice through the tunnels at a specific creature. On a success, the target creature is distracted and the next attack against it has advantage. Alternately, a creature can use a reaction with the same ability checks and DC against a creature making an attack. On a success, the triggering attack has disadvantage.

### Crumbling Pillars

*All that remains of this ancient shrine to a dead god are the ruins of pillars that once reached to the ceiling of this vast chamber.* There are six crumbling pillars, each at least 30 feet tall. Each pillar leans in one of the four cardinal directions, chosen at random or as the GM sees fit. As a bonus action, a creature can change the direction a pillar is leaning by pushing on it and succeeding on a DC 18 STR (Athletics) check. As an action, a creature can push over a pillar in any direction with the same check, regardless of its leaning. The pillar falls in a line (at least 30 feet long and 5 feet wide). A creature in that line must succeed on a DC 16 DEX save or take 11 (2d10) damage and be knocked prone. If the line of the pillar includes another pillar, then that pillar falls in the direction it is leaning. If two or more pillars fall, then the areas within 10 feet of the pillars are heavily obscured by plumes of dust for 1d4 rounds.

## Weather Effects

The core rules cover [[Encounter Gameplay#Hazards|weather-related hazards]] you can apply to a scene. To augment those rules, this section contains a set of standard weather types and mechanical effects that can accompany them.

Each terrain type in this section has a set of suggested weather effects. In addition to adding to the ambiance of the scene, use the **Weather Types** table to add ready-made in-game effects based on a weather type.

**Weather Types**

| Type | Effects |
|---|---|
| Acidic rain | Damaging (1d4 acid), concentration check (DC 12), unstable ground. In addition, all plant-based magical effects last for half their normal duration. |

The **Glossary of Effects** section can also act like a grab bag for any weather pattern you dream up, or even unrelated to weather.

## Glossary Of Effects

*These effects describe states listed on the* **Weather Types** *table.*

***Blinding.*** A creature starting its turn in the area of effect must succeed on a CON save at the indicated DC or suffer the blinded condition until the start of its next turn.

***Concentration Check.*** A creature in the area of effect concentrating on a spell must succeed on a concentration check at the indicated DC at the end of its turn while the weather effect persists.

***Cooling.*** All fire-based damage (magical and nonmagical) in the area of effect is halved.

***Damaging.*** A creature starting its turn in the area of effect suffers a specific kind of damage each round, as indicated parenthetically. If there is a potential save, that is included.

***Darkness.*** The area of effect is heavily obscured. Vision abilities that pierce darkness work as normal.

***Deafening.*** All creatures in the area of effect suffer from the deafened condition.

***Dim Light.*** The area of effect is lightly obscured. Vision abilities that pierce darkness work as normal.

***Dispersing Winds.*** Gasses and other effects in the area, including magical ones that are dispersed by wind, are eliminated.

***Exhausting Effect.*** A creature remaining in the area of effect for the indicated number of rounds must make a CON save at the indicated DC or suffer one level of exhaustion.

***Heavily Obscured.*** All creatures in the area of effect suffer the blinded condition when trying to see something. Attack rolls against the creature have advantage, and the creature’s attack rolls have disadvantage.

***Lightly Obscured.*** All creatures in the area of effect suffer disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks that rely on sight.

***Overwhelmingly Bright.*** WIS (Perception) checks relying on sight automatically fail.

***Ranged Detriment (Major).*** All nonmagical ranged weapon attacks in the area of effect suffer a −5 penalty to attack rolls. All magical ranged weapon attacks in the area of effect suffer a −2 penalty to attack rolls.

***Ranged Detriment (Minor).*** All nonmagical ranged weapon attacks in the area of effect suffer a −2 penalty to attack rolls.

***Slippery.*** On a successful forced movement within range (such as a shove), the attacker chooses which one of the following two effects happens: either the target is moved an additional 5 feet, or the target must make a DC 15 DEX or STR save or be knocked prone.

***Sunny.*** A creature in the area of effect experiences sunlight. It lets most creatures see normally and has special effects on creatures with the Sunlight Sensitivity trait or similar features.

***Unstable Ground.*** All creatures in the area of effect have disadvantage on saves against being knocked prone or other checks to maintain balance.
