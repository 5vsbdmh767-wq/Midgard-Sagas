*Optional, from Book of Blades.*

Starting with the story of your character is great way to guide the mechanical choices you make. A theme can help inform the build of your character.

A theme provides a big-picture scheme of your character. If you aren’t sure what you want to play, you’re short on time, or just want somewhere to start, browse the themes below for some common fantasy archetypes.

Each theme gives a brief description along with examples from fiction, myth, or history. Where applicable, author, game, or publication are listed next to inspiration. For historical figures, the label “(historical)” appears next to the name.

This is followed by suggested choices for appropriate class, subclass, heritage, background, and talents. At the end of each theme is a section for appropriate skills or tools to consider taking proficiency with, items you might want to use, or other strategy or role-playing tips.

Some themes give you choices so you can tailor to a specific vision. Nothing is set in stone. These are all thoughtful suggestions, and you should modify them as you like to fit your character’s concept.

## Agile Assassin

The agile assassin is more than a simple rogue. They are smarter, sneakier, and faster than common cutpurses. These silent killers are trained to eliminate targets quickly and silently, sometimes with poison, and almost always with surprise. They might look like the greengrocer who slips poison into their target’s food, or they might leap from the shadows and stab an unsuspecting target before melting into the shadows and escaping.

***Examples.*** Artemis Entreri (character by R. A. Salvatore), Arya Stark (*A Song of Ice and Fire* novels by George R.R. Martin), Hassan-i Sabbah (historical), John Wick (*John Wick* movie series), the Vishkanya (historical)

***Class (Subclass).*** [[Ranger]] (Blade Dancer) or [[Rogue]] (Enforcer)

***Heritage.*** Cosmopolitan, Supplicant, or Vexed ***Background.*** Artist, Criminal, or Outcast ***Talents.*** Covert or Quick, depending on whether you would be sneaky or fast.

***Suggestions.*** You need DEX, but CHA and STR are also helpful. For skill proficiencies, Deception, Perception, Sleight of Hand, and Stealth are good choices. Consider learning herbalist tools so you have a knowledge of poisons or a disguise kit to blend into any locale. As you level up, look to acquire magical weapons and objects that help with stealth or change your appearance. If your class is ranger, take Quick Strike for your Martial Action class feature.

## Beastmaster

Whether their best friend is a dog, horse, hawk, or something else, the beastmaster specializes in working with animals. These heroes have a deep bond with one or more animals and rely on their aid in both combat and exploration. Beastmasters are frequently not strong in social situations with Humanoids but have the advantage when it comes to Beasts.

***Examples.*** Dar (*The Beast Master* by Andre Norton), FitzChivalry (The Farseer novel trilogy by Robin Hobb), Mowgli (*The Jungle Book* by Rudyard Kipling), Princess Mononoke (movie by Studio Ghibli)

***Class (Subclass).*** Ranger (Pack Master)

***Heritage.*** Wildlands ***Background.*** Rustic ***Talents.*** Since you bring your own animal allies with you, Comrade is an excellent talent choice to generate Luck for you and you can use the Help action as a bonus action.

***Suggestions.*** The features you receive from the Rustic background and your ranger spells all use WIS. So after DEX, make WIS your second highest ability score. In addition to your animal spirit companion, consider other animal friends you might bring along, like a warhorse or trained dog. You have advantage on Animal Handling checks to interact with animals and can cast *animal friendship*, so you are likely to be able to convince extra animal friends to help in dangerous situations. A beastmaster character rarely takes social skills.

## Dark Dabbler

While not spellcasters, some characters delve into forbidden knowledge or are willing to make deals with creatures from beyond for a taste of magical power to augment their martial prowess. Dark dabblers might have a book from which they draw this knowledge, or be trained by a morally flexible guild or hermit with questionable sanity. Dark dabblers might be mistaken for warlocks, but their connection is not a true pact.

***Examples.*** Victor Frankenstein (*Frankenstein* by Mary Shelley), John Constantine (DC Comics), Percy de Rolo (from the *Critical Role* actual play)

***Class (Subclass).*** [[Mechanist]] (Fleshsmith), Mechanist (Spellwright), [[Fighter]] (Spellblade)

***Heritage.*** Anointed, Cloud, Salvager, or Supplicant ***Background.*** Adherent, Maker, or Scholar ***Talents.*** If you have access, Ritualist is your optimal choice, though Trade Skills can be used to focus on a related skill such as Arcana.

***Suggestions.*** As a dark dabbler, you will never be a true spellcaster like a cleric or wizard, but you emphasize advantages that your foes might not expect. Make choices to enhance the features that you gain from your subclass. Gaining access to ritual spells is a prime choice. Also make sure you pick up a magic weapon. While gathering dark knowledge, you are sure to attract unwanted attention of the kind frequently immune to normal weapons.

## Dueling Bravo

The witty duelist has a flamboyant style that attracts attention. They spout poetry to a prospective paramour or sing a drinking song in the inn; they are cocksure, quick to banter, and swagger through life. When the time comes, they are always ready to draw steel.

Bravos typically operate in bustling cities where their reputation as a skilled duelist allows them a life of ease, or at least more ease than they would find traveling through the wilderness.

***Examples.*** Cyrano de Bergerac (historical), Giacomo Casanova (historical), Inigo Montoya (*The Princess Bride* by William Goldman), Miyamoto Musashi (historical), Zorro (Zorro stories by Johnston McCulley)

***Class (Subclass).*** Fighter (Weapon Master)

***Heritage.*** Cosmopolitan or Diaspora ***Background.*** Duelist is the obvious choice, but Artist or Outcast may also work.

***Talents.*** Quick is a great choice since it lets you use a bonus action to move 5 feet away from an opponent without provoking an opportunity attack. Opportunist is a convenient choice to get in extra attacks if you can knock your rival prone.

***Suggestions.*** You want to focus on DEX over STR, especially to make sure you go win initiative to go first in combat. Consider using two weapons with the Finesse and Light weapon properties such as daggers, scimitars, or shortswords. For your Martial Action, choose Quick Strike to maximize your dueling attacks. When combined with Action Surge, this makes you especially effective at quickly blooding foes. Proficiency in Performance helps complete the Bravo picture. As you gain levels, consider adding a level or two of rogue class to gain access to the Sneak Attack and Cunning Action class features.

## Horse Archer

Horse archers are known for their mobility, skill, and devastating effectiveness on the battlefield. These warriors combine speed and precision using hit-and-run tactics or overwhelming volleys of arrows. In addition to being deadly archers, these heroes are knowledgeable about horses and other animals, as well as the natural world.

***Examples.*** Dothraki (*A Song of Ice and Fire* novels by George R.R. Martin), Halt O’Carrick (The Ranger’s Apprentice series by John Flanagan), Mongols (historical), Scythians (historical), Temujin (*The Mongoliad* by Neal Stephenson and Greg Bear)

***Class (Subclass).*** [[Monk]] (Equine Soul)

***Heritage.*** Nomadic or Wildlands ***Background.*** Homesteader or Soldier ***Talents.*** Far Traveler if you want to emphasize your traveling abilities, or Combat Conditioning if you want to be more resilient.

***Suggestions.*** Animal Handling and Survival are important skills, as they help you with your mount and in knowing the ground over which you travel. Gaining proficiency with trapper tools will also help you care for your horse companion. As you level up, look for a magic bow and items that enhance or protect your horse. Many of your abilities require your mount, so make sure it’s protected or that you have a way to quickly replace it. Being able to cast *find steed* or *phantom steed* from a magic item or using the Ritualist talent is a great way to maintain full effectiveness should the unthinkable happen to your mount. At even higher levels, consider unusual mounts that might grant additional modes of movement like flying or burrowing.

## Mad Reaver

The mad reaver is an unstoppable force of destruction, immune to fear and pain when they are overcome with rage. Appearing out of nowhere, the mad reaver relies on violence, reckless behavior, and obliviousness to pain or injury. Fighting on despite wounds that would cripple ordinary folk, the mad reaver is a rage-filled, terrifying opponent that mows through enemies with brutal efficiency. The mad reaver enters a state akin to madness that is without fear or pain and makes them seem unstoppable. Biting their shields and howling like an animal, they charge into the thick of battle.

***Examples.*** Conan the [[Barbarian]] (Conan stories by Robert E. Howard), Guts (*Berserk* manga series by Kentaro Miura), Hulk (Marvel Comics), Logen Ninefingers (The First Law novel trilogy by Joe Abercrombie), Vikings, Wulfgar (fictional character created by R.A. Salvatore)

***Class (Subclass).*** Barbarian (Berserker) or Barbarian (Frostborn Raider); Berserker seems the likely pick, but Frostborn Raider gives you additional tricks.

***Heritage.*** Nomadic or Slayer ***Background.*** Soldier ***Talents.*** You are going to be in a lot of melee combat, so Combat Conditioning helps you stand up to all the damage coming your way.

***Suggestions.*** Your role relies on your STR and CON. Your class features and the Combat Conditioning talent make you very resilient and an impressive melee combatant. As you gain experience, Furious Charge and Physical Fortitude talents are worth considering, based on whether you want to emphasize attack or defense. You also want to pick up proficiency in Athletics and Intimidation skills. If you want to be a traditional Viking raider, pick proficiency with water vehicles too. For weapons, consider a twohanded melee weapon like greataxe or greatsword.

## Mercenary Sellsword

A professional fighter for hire, mercenary sellswords are loyal to coin rather than country. These heroes have a pragmatic outlook and shift alliances based on the highest bidder, though some remain loyal once they accept a contract. Most mercenary sellswords realize winning is everything if they want to see tomorrow. Making sure the enemy doesn’t have the ability or motivation to fight is key to their long-term health. Dirty tricks keep you alive more than any honorable code.

***Examples.*** Bronn (*A Song of Ice and Fire* novels by George R.R. Martin), The Black Company (set of novels by Glen Cook), The Free Companies (historical French and Italian mercenaries).

***Class (Subclass).*** Fighter (Weapon Master)

***Heritage.*** Nomadic or Slayer ***Background.*** Soldier ***Talents.*** Combat Conditioning if you want to be in the thick of things, or Field Medic if you see yourself as a leader or helper of fellow soldiers. Eventually, Weapon Option Specialist (see **Martial Talents** in **Chapter 1**) highlights the dirty tricks you’ve learned from a lifetime of combat.

***Suggestions.*** Anyone can swing a sword, but a smart warrior works to eliminate their enemy before the swinging starts. Consider options to lay ambushes with Stealth. Attack before your enemy can, especially from hiding. If you do end up in a standup fight, make sure you are armored enough to be standing at the end. Talents like Armor Expert or Armor Training are especially useful in that regard, and the Covert and [[Martial Talents#Vanguard|Vanguard]] talents are useful for taking more attacks than your enemy. Stealth and Deception are useful proficiencies to have. Your armor and weapons are key to your role as a sellsword, so look to upgrade them as you level. When you can afford it, invest in equipment-shaped dirty tricks like caltrops and oil and use them liberally.

## Monster Hunter

Monster hunters are heroes that specialize in hunting and destroying the enemies of humanoid societies. Some monster hunters choose to pursue their mission after a traumatic past encounter, while others want to protect their home or loved ones from encroaching harm. These heroes wander in search of the next threat, rarely staying in one place for long. They are capable in combat and stealth but also use brainpower to research their foes and outwit enemies that might overpower them. They study the lore of various monsters so they can mitigate the strengths and exploit the weaknesses their targets assuredly have. They also need constantly to be aware of their surroundings, as well as the intentions of people around them, for not all monsters hide in the shadows.

***Examples.*** Blade (Marvel Comics), Geralt of Rivia (*The Witcher* novel series by Andrzej Sapkowski), Trevor Belmont (*Castlevania* video game), Solomon Kane (fictional character created by Robert E. Howard)

***Class (Subclass).*** Ranger (Hunter) or Ranger (Peerless Scout)

***Heritage.*** Anointed, Diaspora, Grove, or Slayer ***Background.*** Outcast ***Talents.*** Start with Aware. It allows you to avoid being surprised by the monsters you hunt and increases your chance of striking first.

***Suggestions.*** STR, DEX, WIS, and INT are all going to be useful, so monster hunters frequently end up spreading the capacity around rather than focusing heavily on one ability score. You need an edge against your foes, so abilities like being able to track your foe or set up ambushes and traps are key, all while avoiding the same from them. You want to grab disposable tricks like acid, oil, or alchemist’s fire for creatures with immunities to other weapons. Owning one or more magic weapons as you level up is also helpful, as many monsters have resistance or immunity to non-magic weapons.

## Pugilist

Where other heroes carry a wide selection of weapons, the pugilist beats their foes with their bare hands. While all these pugilist heroes are close-quarters combatants, they might be street fighters, monastic martial artists, gladiators trained for the arena, or barbarian brawlers. Some fighters are lean and dodge the blows of their enemies, wearing them out. Others take hits on the chin, outlasting and eventually dealing more damage than their enemy.

***Examples.*** Hercules (mythological), Jackie Chan (actor in dozens of martial arts films), James “Gentleman Jim” Corbett (American heavyweight boxing champion), Tormund Giantsbane (*A Song of Ice and Fire* novels by George R. R. Martin)

***Class (Subclass).*** Monk (Open Hand) works best, but Barbarian (Frostborn Raider) works for more of a brute.

***Heritage.*** Cosmopolitan, Cottage, or Wildlands ***Background.*** Rustic ***Talents.*** Hand to Hand if you are not a monk or Physical Fortitude if you are.

***Suggestions.*** STR is your most important ability, and secondarily, you want either DEX or CON. As you gain levels, pick up the Wrestling Mastery talent. When making choices about your character, look for ways to mitigate your two biggest weaknesses. As a primarily melee character, anything that helps you get into the fight quicker is to your advantage. Both the barbarian and monk classes increase your speed, but dealing with flying foes or enemies at a distant range may still take one or more rounds to close with them, if at all. Additional modes of movement will help you get to your target. Items like *winged boots*, *boots of striding and springing*, or a *cape of the mountebank* help get you to your enemy. Your other challenge is, as a lightly armored melee character, you will take damage. Look for anything that lets you resist damage (the barbarian’s Rage feature), increases your hit points (such as the Combat Conditioning talent), or improves your CON (magic items).

## Ruler in Exile

The ruler in exile is a noble figure forced into exile, hiding their true identity while rallying allies and seeking to reclaim their throne. Whatever their actual noble title, the ruler in exile is now separated from their lands or noble house for some reason. No matter how much they run from their responsibilities or how many trials pile up between them and their title, eventually they find their way back. Inevitably, more difficulties present themselves before the ruler in exile can regain their former status. These heroes are frequently charismatic and find men and women flocking to their side. A good-aligned ruler in exile always feels at least some responsibility for these people and strives to unite and protect them.

***Examples.*** Aragorn (*The Lord of the Rings* trilogy by J.R.R. Tolkien), Daenerys Targaryen (*A Song of Ice and Fire* novels by George R.R. Martin), Odysseus (mythological), Prince Caspian (*Prince Caspian* by C.S. Lewis)

***Class (Subclass).*** Fighter (Knight)

***Heritage.*** Diaspora or Privileged ***Background.*** Courtier or Soldier ***Talents.*** Combat Conditioning if you want to be more hands-on, or Polyglot if you want to be more of a leader.

***Suggestions.*** Unlike many fighters, consider putting an above-average score into CHA and picking up proficiency with Persuasion, Deception, or Insight. As a potential leader, you probably will make speeches and negotiate deals. History is also a useful proficiency for recognizing heraldry from your society, its neighbors, and your homeland’s enemies. Gain allies by helping others. Look to purchase or find alchemical items and potions that you can hand out to them.

## Siege Engineer

The siege engineer is a mastermind of war machines and siegecraft, skilled in designing engines of destruction and overcoming fortifications. This hero blends intellect with martial prowess to out-think their enemies. They make a plan, a backup plan, and a plan for when those things fail. Siege engineers try to fight from advantageous ground and deny the enemy that ground, especially when their enemy thinks they hold it.

***Examples.*** Archimedes (historical), Qyburn (*A Song of Ice and Fire* novels by George R.R. Martin), Leo Fitz (*Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.* TV series), Tony Stark (Marvel Comics)

***Class (Subclass).*** Mechanist (Metallurgist) or Mechanist (Spellwright)

***Heritage.*** Fireforge, Salvager, or Stone ***Background.*** Maker ***Talents.*** Starting out with Artillerist makes you better with siege engines and ranged weapons.

***Suggestions.*** Consider if you are more of a powered-armor character or someone throwing explosive devices from a large distance. Armor Training is useful if you are the former, while Return Fire might come in handy for the latter. Both History and Investigation are proficiencies that come in handy for knowing things about fortifications you might want to bring down. Proficiency with alchemist tools helps you create nasty explosions, and construction tools helps you create defensive works or bring down walls. A *portable hole* or *bag of holding* is useful to carry tools and raw materials for building defenses.

## Staunch Bulwark

The staunch bulwark is the rock upon which the party’s enemies break. Wearing heavy armor and carrying a large shield, these heroes rush towards danger and attempt to become the focus of their foes’ attacks. These characters are often also motivating leaders who look out for their allies. The staunch bulwark makes sure they stand between their comrades and harm and supports their allies on and off the battlefield.

***Examples.*** Ser Brienne of Tarth (*A Song of Ice and Fire* novels by George R.R. Martin), Heloise (*The Sacred Throne* trilogy by Myke Cole), Sturm Brightblade (The Dragonlance Chronicles series by Margaret Weis and Tracy Hickman), William Marshal the first Earl of Pembroke (historical), Richard the Lionheart (historical), Uther the Lightbringer (*World of Warcraft* video game)

***Class (Subclass).*** Fighter (Iron Bastion)

***Heritage.*** Diaspora, Fireforge, or Vexed ***Background.*** Rustic or Soldier ***Talents.*** Combat Conditioning gives you more hit points or Physical Fortitude improves several of your saves, especially those that force you to move.

***Suggestions.*** Focus first on raising your AC, improving your saves, and making yourself resistant to damage. Look for plate armor and a shield, and eventually magic items that improve on that. Second, prioritize ways you can lock down foes or aid and protect your allies, but don’t neglect doing damage in favor of defense. If you can’t be hurt but you don’t hurt back, your enemies ignore you after a round or two. You need to be enough of a threat to keep their attention. Make sure you do some damage, especially if you can add conditions from weapon options to bedevil your foes. The ability to knock foes prone or otherwise deny them movement is key to making sure no one gets past you to your softer allies.

## Untamed Plunderer

Sometimes the most audacious rogues aren’t rogues at all. From difficult beginnings, untamed thieves are characterized by big dreams and taking chances. Relying on brawn, daring plans, and no small amount of luck, these characters attempt schemes that would kill lesser heroes.

***Examples.*** Conan the Barbarian (Conan stories by Robert E. Howard), Fafhrd (Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser stories by Fritz Leiber), Ragnar Lothbrok (historical)

***Class (Subclass).*** Barbarian (Berserker). There is a lot of flexibility in subclass choices, but Berserker gives you Mindless Rage and Intimidating Presence which should come in handy when resisting charm, fear, and exhaustion effects when your plans go awry.

***Heritage.*** Nomadic, Supplicant, or Wildlands. There are a number of overlaps in the skill proficiencies from these heritages and your class, so choose carefully.

***Background.*** Criminal ***Talents.*** Touch of Luck helps you pull through when the unexpected happens. Bottomless Luck or Comrade are worth considering as you gain additional talents to gain more uses for your Luck.

***Suggestions.*** When choosing ability scores, consider having a positive CHA if you plan to use your Deception or Intimidation skills. You might also need DEX if you plan to pick locks and disable traps. Athletics, Intimidation, Perception, Stealth, and Survival are all good proficiencies for this concept. You also get a tool proficiency where thieves’ tools is the likely pick for when things can’t be solved with brawn. As you gain levels, consider the Dungeoneer talent to deal with traps or Physical Fortitude to take advantage of your good saves and emphasize your Luck. A *wand of secrets* can help you find secret doors and traps.

## Warrior of Faith

This is the archetypal religious warrior who believes in a cause or deity bigger than themselves and has devoted their entire life to fighting for that cause. With a strong arm and an unshakable will, the warrior of faith always does the right thing, even when those around them would choose a more expedient path. This hero might have been divinely chosen, followed this path to protect others, or rose to the cause due to their own desire to prove themselves worthy. What makes them different from others is their certainty of right and wrong, and the belief that they must personally do something when things are wrong.

***Examples.*** Anduin Lothar (*World of Warcraft* video game), Bishop Odo of Bayeux (historical), Meristan (*The Lost War* by Justin Lee Anderson), Sir Galahad (Arthurian legend)

***Class (Subclass).*** [[Paladin]] (Devotion)

***Heritage.*** Anointed fits the best, but Cottage also lets you give benefits to others and fits with a protector and caregiver.

***Background.*** Adherent or Soldier.

***Talents.*** Mental Fortitude helps you have the iron will that’s characteristic of this theme, but you might also consider Field Medic if you are looking to help your fellow party members or NPCs in need.

***Suggestions.*** This type of character wants a balanced set of abilities. STR and WIS are key, but CON and CHA are secondarily important, followed by INT and DEX. Don’t neglect Persuasion, as it helps you motivate others and avoid unnecessary combats. You want a good armor class, a decent damage output, and then any abilities that let you support your allies or smite your foes round things out. It sounds like you need everything! To some extent you do. This type of character is mostly melee focused, so good armor and weapons help you most at first. Armor Expert, Comrade, Hard Target, Physical Fortitude, Shield Mastery, and [[Vanguard]] are all talents you might consider. Remember that you get a small number of spells, so choosing those well can help you flex into a role that your party doesn’t have. No healer? Spells that let you heal or remove conditions will be helpful. No other damage dealer? Use your slots on smiting to add to your damage.

## Witty Acrobat

The smiling rogue who dodges between their foes while letting off a steady stream of banter is the iconic witty acrobat. Usually not very large, the witty acrobat wiggles through places that are too small to follow. Whether it’s climbing into second-story windows or dodging under a tavern table, these heroes rely on their wits not only to enrich themselves, but to fight for the underdog. For all they claim to be criminals, witty acrobats frequently also have a heart of gold, somehow ultimately doing the right thing despite claiming to be only self-interested. When this happens, they get themselves into trouble to aid their friends in standing up against true evil.

***Examples.*** Gray Mouser (Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser stories by Fritz Leiber), Locke Lamora (*The Lies of Locke Lamora* by Scott Lynch), Phillipe “The Mouse” Gaston (*Ladyhawke*)

***Class (Subclass).*** Rogue (Acrobat)

***Heritage.*** Cosmopolitan or Cottage ***Background.*** Criminal or Outcast ***Talents.*** Quick focuses on moving around the battlefield and pairs well with a rogue’s ability to Dash as a bonus action. If you want to be especially martial, consider eventually picking up War Dancer (see **Martial Talents** in **Chapter 1**). Polyglot gives you more languages to insult your foes with and advantage on CHA checks to influence others.

***Suggestions.*** While DEX is definitely your most important ability score, CHA and INT are likely to be secondary. You want to be proficient in Acrobatics and Athletics so you can move around. Make sure you pick up at least one CHAbased skill as well. As you level, any magic item that lets you fly or teleport can help you get out of a jam.
