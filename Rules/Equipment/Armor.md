This section lists the types of armor available to PCs and covers special rules regarding their use. The **Armor** table lists the cost, AC bonus, weight, special properties, and proficiencies required for every kind of armor.

## Calculating Armor Class (AC)

Wearing armor increases your armor class (AC), which in turn increases your chance of avoiding enemy attacks. An enemy’s attack roll total must meet or beat your AC to deal damage. So, the higher your AC, the greater your chance of avoiding damage!

A typical PC who isn’t wearing armor has an AC of 10 + their DEX modifier.

When you wear armor, use the AC equation listed with the type of armor you are wearing instead of the typical 10 + DEX modifier equation. See the **Armor** table for an armor type’s AC equation as well as specific bonuses or limitations it imposes when calculating AC.

## Armor Types & Proficiencies

Armor types are categorized into three weights: light, medium, or heavy. You need proficiency in a weight of armor to use it well. For instance, a character with proficiency in light armor can wear any type of armor listed as light armor without penalty.

Your character can wear any kind of armor, regardless of proficiency. However, if you don’t have proficiency, you have disadvantage on all STR and DEX ability checks made while wearing it. You also can’t cast spells while wearing armor you aren’t proficient with.

> [!note] What About Equipment Size?
> To keep things simple, assume that PCs can wear any nonmagical armor or wield any nonmagical equipment they find, within bounds of common sense. For example, it’s unlikely that a smallfolk could wear armor taken from a slain giant. Note that magic items have special sizing rules (see **Magic Items** in this chapter). However, a GM can decide a piece of armor or equipment requires alteration or won’t work for a PC due to its size. The cost for resizing a piece of equipment varies from 10 to 40 percent of the item’s base cost depending on how much alteration is needed and whether the item is made of special materials.
>

### Light Armor

Light armor provides minimal protection while still allowing its wearer to move with relative ease.

***Brigandine.*** This knee-length coat is made of heavy cloth or canvas lined with small metal plates.

***Fashionable Armor.*** This armor provides little protection because it is designed for its striking appearance rather than any practical use in combat. It looks grand, and it does include a few well-placed pieces of polished metal that could turn a blade if the wearer were very lucky. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Leather.*** The breastplate and shoulder protectors of this armor are made of leather that has been stiffened by being boiled in oil. The rest of the armor is made of softer and more flexible materials.

***Padded.*** This full-body outfit consists of quilted layers of cloth and batting.

***Studded Leather.*** Made from tough but flexible leather, studded leather is reinforced with close-set rivets or spikes.

### Medium Armor

Medium armor provides more protection than light armor, but it uses bulkier materials that interfere with range of motion.

***Bone.*** This armor is made from natural materials such as woven reeds, sturdy fabric, or leather with bones used as ridged reinforcement over vital organs and joints. Skulls or antlers are often incorporated for cosmetic effect. While far less flexible and durable than metal, it can still save the wearer’s life. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Breastplate.*** This armor consists of a fitted metal chest lined with supple leather. This armor leaves limbs unprotected but provides good protection for vital organs and allows for easier movement than most medium armor.

***Chain Shirt.*** A chain shirt is made of interlocking metal rings that are worn between layers of clothing or leather. This armor protects the wearer’s upper body and the outer layers muffle the sound of the rings rubbing against one another.

***Half Plate.*** Half plate consists of shaped metal plates that cover most of the wearer's body. It doesn’t include leg protection beyond greaves attached with leather straps.

***Hardened Mail Shirt.*** This chain shirt has rings of adamantine or mithral woven into key areas to provide more protection than normal mail. %% source: Northlands Worldbook %%

***Hide.*** This full-body suit of armor consists of thick furs and pelts.

***Lamellar.*** This armor is made from horizontal strips of overlapping metal plates connected by silk cord or strong fabric. Lamellar is easier to craft than most metal armors and offers significant protection but is less flexible than more sophisticated armors like scale. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Scale Mail.*** This armor consists of a coat and leggings (and perhaps a separate skirt) of leather covered with overlapping pieces of metal, much like the scales of a fish.

***Silk-Backed Coin Mail.*** Made of silver coins from conquered nations and backed by a layer of quilted red silk, these hauberks are typically awarded to soldiers, mercenaries, and generals with conspicuous valor during campaigns in regions where the coins originated. While wearing this armor, you have resistance to piercing damage from nonmagical attacks. If the piercing damage is from a critical hit, the armor loses this quality after the attack until you spend a short rest repairing it (no special tools or check required). %% source: Player's Guide 2 %%

***Walrus Hide.*** These thick, treated walrus hide suits are flexible but quite heavy. In addition to its defensive benefits, walrus hide counts as winter clothing when determining the effects of extreme cold. %% source: Northlands Worldbook %%

### Heavy Armor

Heavy armor provides the most protection of any armor type, but wearing it demands great physical prowess.

***Beinagrind.*** This armor is formed by carving disks or short shingles out of whale or giant bones and lacing them to a backing of flexible leather. %% source: Northlands Worldbook %%

***Chain Mail.*** This is a head-to-toe suit of armor made of interlocking metal rings. Chain mail includes a layer of quilted fabric worn underneath the mail to prevent chafing and to cushion the impact of blows.

***Double Mail.*** Also called tvifold brynja, the rings of this chain mail are doubled, so each pair is passed through by four pairs of other rings. It is very heavy and bulky. %% source: Northlands Worldbook %%

***Plate.*** Plate consists of shaped, interlocking metal plates to cover the entire body. A suit of plate includes gauntlets, heavy leather boots, a visored helmet, and thick layers of padding underneath the armor. Buckles and straps distribute the weight over the body.

***Ring Mail.*** This leather armor has heavy rings sewn into it. The rings help reinforce the armor against attacks.

***Splint.*** This armor is made of narrow vertical strips of metal riveted to a backing of leather that is worn over cloth padding. Flexible chain mail protects the joints.

***Stonesteel.*** Made from a steel and crystal alloy, this unique form of plate armor represents the height of dwarven metallurgical techniques. It consists of overlapping and interlocking laminated plates that cover the entire body, and a suit includes gauntlets, heavy leather boots, and a helmet. Stonesteel is heavy and somewhat unwieldy but offers superior protection to standard plate. Donning and doffing stonesteel armor takes twice as long as standard heavy armor: 20 minutes to don and 10 minutes to doff (or 1 minute to doff with help). %% source: Player's Guide 2 %%

***Wooden Plate.*** This armor is made by using sinew and rope to strap a thick wooden breastplate over the chest and groin while wearing hides or furs underneath for padding. While offering decent protection, such armor is heavy and cumbersome to wear. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

### Special Armor

These are made as light, medium, or heavy armor; the cost and weight are added to the base armor.

***Dragonscale Armor.*** This armor can be light, medium, or heavy, depending on its manufacture. Few sets of armor prove as deadly to produce as dragonscale armor. The reward for such dangerous work is armor that grants resistance to the element corresponding to the dragon it was made from, as shown in the **Damage Resistance by Dragon Type** table.

***Winged Armor.*** This armor can be made light, medium, or heavy and is generally only made for Humanoids. The armor isn’t winged itself but is rather built for warriors who have wings and adjustable for different wing sizes. A non-winged character would feel less protected in winged armor with two large holes in the back.

In addition to allowing winged Humanoids to wear armor, winged armor is built to help fliers stabilize when they’re hit in midair. When an attack or effect would cause you to fall prone in midair, you have advantage on saves against that effect.

### Shields

A shield is handheld armor that is wielded instead of worn. This means their AC bonus can be lost if the shield is destroyed or becomes disarmed. Wielding a shield requires the use of one hand, which means a PC can’t use two-handed weapons while holding a shield. You can only benefit from one shield AC bonus at a time, even if you wield multiple shields.

***Buckler.*** These small metal shields are designed for melee dueling. In addition to blocking, they can knock an opponent’s weapon out of position, creating an opening to attack. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Kite Shield.*** Favored by mounted warriors, this shield’s leaf-like shape protects the rider’s legs and their mount’s flanks. When you use a kite shield while mounted, the shield’s bonus to AC applies to your AC as well as to your mount’s AC. %% source: Player's Guide 2 %%

***Lantern Shield.*** Attached to this buckler is a lightweight lantern. It can be lit like any other lantern by mundane or magical means. The lantern swings from the shield, shedding bright light in a 10-foot radius and dim light for an additional 20 feet. %% source: Book of Blades %%

As a bonus action while the lantern is lit, you can swing your shield toward an opponent within 5 feet of you in an attempt to blind them with the light. Your opponent must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or be blinded until the start of its next turn.

***Leather Shield.*** Shields made of leather are common among groups without access to metal working. Cured leather is stretched over a light wooden frame that includes a simple grip. They are light weight but offer limited protection against metal armaments. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Manica.*** This shield consists of overlapping metal segments fastened to leather straps and wrapped around the upper and lower arm. You can don or doff a manica as an action. Though the manica provides less protection than other shields, it allows you free use of your shield hand. You can’t gain the manica’s bonus to AC while still wielding a weapon in that hand, and you can still only benefit from one shield at a time. %% source: Player's Guide 2 %%

***Musket Shield.*** This device combines a short musket and a rounded metal shield. The musket protrudes though the shield and a metal grate allow the wielder to aim the weapon while still being protected. Unlike most shields it cannot be used effectively in a single hand because it is supported by holding on to the musket built into it. The statistics for the included weapon are identical to a standard musket. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Pavise.*** This is an exceptionally large wooden shield used by archers in battle. The shield is carried in both hands as the archer advances and is then planted on the ground and braced with a fold out stand. Once it stands, that the archer can use it as cover while keeping both hands free to fire their weapon. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Shield.*** This broad piece of wood and metal is held by a handle attached to one side.

***Spike Shield.*** This small metal shield has a sharp and sturdy spike in the boss that can be thrust at an opponent in close combat. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Spiked Shield, Large.*** This shield has the Light weapon property. You can use a spiked shield as a weapon and can use it in two-weapon fighting. On a successful melee attack with it, you deal 1d4 + your STR modifier piercing damage. %% source: Book of Blades %%

## Armor Properties

Many sets of armor have properties that affect their use, as shown in the Properties column of the **Armor** table.

***Buoyant.*** This wooden armor floats in water and can keep its wearer from drowning. The wearer has Advantage on STR (Athletics) checks made to avoid drowning when swimming but cannot effectively dive underwater. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Cover.*** This shield can be propped up as a portable form of cover. When used this way, the shield is stationary and provides a cover bonus rather than its usual bonus to AC. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Cumbersome.*** This armor is heavy and difficult to move in unless you have the required STR score. If your STR score doesn’t equal or exceed the STR score listed in parentheses, your movement speed is reduced by 10 feet while you wear the armor, even if you’re proficient with it.

***Interlocking.*** Interlocking shields can be linked by adjacent combatants to form a shield wall. Doing so provides an additional +1 shield AC bonus to each linked combatant with an interlocking shield. Forming or joining a shield wall is a free action and can be initiated by any allied combatant. Any combatant in a shield wall that moves out of the formation or no longer has an adjacent ally with an interlocking shield, loses the additional AC bonus. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

*Offensive*. At the start of the wielder’s turn, they can choose to lose the shield’s bonus to AC in exchange for a +1 bonus to melee attack rolls.

***Natural Materials.*** This armor is made from materials like wood, leather, or other organic materials. This renders the item immune to the effects of spells like *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Heat Metal|heat metal]]* or features like the rust monster’s Antenna action.

***Noisy.*** This armor imposes disadvantage on any ability checks made to avoid notice, like sneaking, hiding, or otherwise attempting to move silently.

***Spiked.*** This shield has a spike that can be used as a weapon with the same statistics as a dagger, except that it does not have the “Thrown” property. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Two-handed.*** Two-handed shields require two hands to effectively hold and position to gain their AC bonus. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

## Armor Materials

Armor can be made from a special material that gives it additional properties. Note that these properties aren't considered magical.

Armor made from special materials typically costs (and can be sold for) more than the same armor made with standard materials. Some well-known special armor materials are described here. Your GM decides whether armor made from special materials is available for purchase.

***Adamantine.*** Adamantine is an exceptionally hard metal that is difficult to find and costly to mine. While wearing adamantine armor, any critical hit against you becomes a normal hit. Adamantine armor can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 1,000 gp. Only medium or heavy armor without the Natural Materials property can be made from adamantine.

***Carapace.*** Carapace armor is crafted primarily from the chitinous exoskeletons harvested from giant arthropods. The material is incredibly tough yet lightweight, making it an excellent option for warriors who need to stay mobile. Medium carapace armor is considered light armor for the purpose of armor proficiency. In addition, all carapace armor weighs half of what the same armor made from standard materials weighs, plus you ignore the Noisy property. You can’t simply add chitin to existing armor though, so it must be crafted from the start and can only take the form of light or medium armor or shields. Any such armor or shield gains the Natural Materials property. Carapace armor can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 500 gp. %% source: Player's Guide 2 %%

***Decorative.*** This armor is functional but has been decorated by skilled artisans to be a beautiful work of art. Owning such armor marks the wearer as a person of significance and distinction, both on and off the battlefield. Decorative armor helps the wearer make a strong first impression and grants Advantage to the first Charisma Check made to influence a given non-hostile NPC. Decorative armor can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base price + 500 gp. %% source: Trade Secrets %%

***Dragonhide.*** Dragonhide armor is crafted from the scales and bones harvested from dragons—each piece typically being sourced solely from the remains of a single dragon. This material is notoriously tough, as one would expect, and adds significant protection as armor, adding +1 to your AC. Additionally, when you purchase or create dragonhide armor, you must decide which dragon type it came from, and when you take damage of the type associated with that dragon type (see ** table), you reduce that damage by 1d6. You can’t simply add dragonhide to existing armor though, so it must be crafted from the start and can only take the form of medium or heavy armor or shields. Any such armor or shield gains the Natural Materials property. Dragonhide armor can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 5,000 gp. %% source: Player's Guide 2 %%

**Dragonhide Damage**

| Dragon Type | Damage Type |
|---|---|
| Black or Copper | Acid |
| Blue or Bronze | Lightning |
| Brass, Gold, or Red | Fire |
| Green | Poison |
| Silver or White | Cold |
| Void | Necrotic |
| Yellow | Radiant |

***Mithral.*** Mithral is a light yet durable metal that is difficult to find and requires master-level skill to work. Armor made from mithral doesn’t impose disadvantage on DEX (Stealth) checks or have a STR requirement, even if the standard version of the armor has the Cumbersome or Noisy properties. Mithral armor can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 1,000 gp. Only medium or heavy armor without the Natural Materials property can be made from mithral.

***Silvered.*** Silvered armor is simply standard armor plated with silver. While wearing silvered armor, or wielding a silvered shield, foes with the Shapechanger tag have disadvantage on melee attack rolls against you. Silvering armor and shields is a specialty service that skilled blacksmiths with the appropriate resources can provide, costing 100 gp for a shield and 500 gp for a suit of armor. Pre-silvered armor can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 100 gp (for shields) or + 500 gp (for armor). Armor and shields with the Natural Materials property cannot be silvered. %% source: Player's Guide 2 %%

## Getting into and Out of Armor

The time it takes to don (put on) or doff (take off) armor depends on the armor’s weight.

***Don.*** This is the time it takes to put on armor. You benefit from the armor’s AC only if you take the full time to don the suit of armor.

***Doff.*** This is the time it takes to remove armor. If you have help, halve this time, except for shields, which take 1 action regardless of help.

**Donning and Doffing Armor**

| Category | Don | Doff |
|---|---|---|
| Light Armor | 1 minute | 1 minute |
| Medium Armor | 5 minutes | 1 minute |
| Heavy Armor | 10 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Shield | 1 action | 1 action |
