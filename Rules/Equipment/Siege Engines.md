High, thick walls of well-hewn stone and timber protect castles and towns. Thus, siege warfare arose to turn those walls into a coffin.

In the event a prolonged siege is impossible, huge machines of destruction, called siege engines, were devised to harry defenders, fracture battlements, and reduce oncemagnificent walls to rubble. Siege engines are detailed in *Game Master’s Guide*, and here are some additional siege engines for martial characters to operate. In game play, remember the following notes about siege engines:

***Damage Threshold:*** In addition to AC, siege weapons have a damage threshold that represents the damage amount that must be met before the siege weapon loses hit points. If the siege weapon is hit by a single instance of damage that fails to meet its damage threshold, it instead takes no damage. A siege weapon’s damage threshold appears in parenthesis next to its AC.

***Siege Weapon Resilience:*** Like most items, siege weapons are immune to poison and psychic damage and to the following conditions: blinded, charmed, deafened, exhaustion, frightened, incapacitated, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned, prone, stunned, and unconscious. Instead of writing all those immunities in each stat block, a siege weapon that fits this standard has Siege Weapon Resilience in the Immune line of its stat block.

## Cannon

Constructed of iron, packed with explosive powders, loaded with cast iron balls or bundles of stone spheres, these deafening siege engines bellow smoke and fury, laying waste to anything they happen to hit. Though most cannons rely on gunpowder, some variations are powered alchemically or with spellcraft. Medieval cannons were nightmarishly expensive, brittle, and heavy, but if built into a fortification, such weapons could lay waste to even the most steadfast targets.

> [!note] Cannon
> *Large Object*
> **Armor Class** 19 (damage threshold 10)
> **Hit Points** 150
> **Immune** Siege Weapon Resilience

Iron tubes that fire lead balls, cannons cost a minimum of 2,800 gp to create, 600 gp of which is raw iron. Even with access to one or more furnaces, laborers proficient with smithing tools must provide 1,100 hours of work to craft a single cannon. Each firing can cost up to 100 gp in powder and shot.

Cannons come in a variety of sizes and fire a variety of projectiles. Most cannons weigh from 6,000 to 8,000 pounds. Lighter cannons fire 6- to 14-pound projectiles, while the largest cannons can blast 70-pound balls of iron over 3 miles.

Firing a cannon requires two actions to load it, one action to aim it, and one action to use its Blast attack.

***Blast.*** *Ranged Weapon Attack:* +5 hit, range 1/2 mile/1 mile, one target. *Hit:* 44 (8d10) bludgeoning damage.

## Forcewall Projector

These purely magical siege engines operate on a similar principle to the *bead of force* magic item. A magical “boulder” of transparent force energy is loaded into a hopper and fired toward a target. Before impact, the boulder expands into an invisible wall of ten 10-foot-by-10foot panels of force energy, arranged in a predetermined pattern. This is of middling performance against structures but can devastate a massed group of soldiers.

> [!note] Forcewall Projector
> *Large Object*
> **Armor Class** 12 (damage threshold 10)
> **Hit Points** 50
> **Immune** Siege Weapon Resilience

Developed by wizards with precious towers to protect, a forcewall projector resembles a ballista on the business end. The loading area contains a hopper that rolls force projectiles down into the firing area. Loading it is uncharacteristically simple for a siege engine.

Only one action is required to load it and a second action is required to pull the level to fire its Crush attack. The cost of the projector is functionally priceless. If a wizard were to sell the projectiles, they would cost 10,000 gp each.

***Crush.*** The projector flings a boulder of force that expands into ten 10-foot-by-10-foot panels of force energy. Each creature underneath a panel must make a DC 15 DEX save, taking 27 (5d10) bludgeoning damage on a failed save, or half as much damage on a successful one. Structures take half damage from the walls of force falling onto them.

## Scorpion

Identical in form and general function to a ballista, the scorpion is Medium-sized, weighs 150–300 pounds, can be operated by a single artillerist, and is compact enough to fit atop battlements, inside watchtowers, or on the rail of a vehicle. It is not a particularly mobile object, but it is more maneuverable than its big brother ballista and can be carried around and assembled by two people in 1 minute.

> [!note] Scorpion
> *Medium Object*
> **Armor Class** 13 (damage threshold 5)
> **Hit Points** 30
> **Immune** Siege Weapon Resilience

A scorpion resembles a large crossbow on a stout tripod mount. Carried by armies, it is generally an anti-personnel weapon rather than a weapon to use against a structure. Sometimes used in batteries, it flings smaller projectiles (no more than 2 pounds) and is primarily used to kill infantry and mounted units at a distance of 200 feet or to lob bolts down atop enemies within 1,200 feet.

To fire a scorpion, you need one action to load, one action to aim, and one action to use its Bolt attack.

***Bolt.*** *Ranged Weapon Attack:* +3 to hit, range 300/1,200 ft., one target. *Hit:* 22 (5d8) piercing damage.

## Siege Tower

A tall, rectangular construction of wooden-walled staircases covered in fresh or wet animal skins, siege towers protect archers, soldiers, and smaller siege equipment while they overrun a fortification’s curtain wall. Siege towers have wheels and axles to make moving it possible. A siege tower is filled with troops before it reaches enemy fortifications, who must trust the tower’s walls to keep them safe.

> [!note] Siege Tower
> *Gargantuan Object*
> **Armor Class** 15 (damage threshold 10)
> **Hit Points** 250
> **Immune** Siege Weapon Resilience

Heavy planks, thick timbers, iron nails, iron braces, and a huge amount of hides, iron, or non-flammable covering are required to create a siege tower, but it is simple, if time consuming, to build. A siege tower requires 1,500 gp of raw materials and an additional 150 gp of materials for every 10 feet of height beyond 40 feet. Such an engine can take over 580 labor hours to construct.

A fully loaded siege tower can carry hundreds of archers, catapults, and soldiers ready to assault the fortification. Averaging 40 feet tall, such a siege tower could weigh 10,000 pounds while empty or over 50,000 pounds while loaded.

A siege tower has no weapons of its own. It is a mobile fortification used to move troops closer to an objective in relative safety.

## Xorn Engine

Favored by subterranean people and exported for surface warfare, the xorn engine does not employ any actual xorns. Rather, the enchantment on the engine hurls stones gliding through the earth as an elemental might move, in an inverted arc, to explode upward upon contact with air. Although monstrously expensive to build and operate, and relatively short range for a siege weapon, a xorn engine is a fearsome bunker buster, since fortifying toward underground is seldom on an army engineer’s mind.

> [!note] Xorn Engine
> *Large Object*
> **Armor Class** 10 (damage threshold 5)
> **Hit Points** 60
> **Immune** Siege Weapon Resilience

Another magical siege engine, a xorn engine looks like a mangonel built with the arm facing down. A xorn engine requires 8,500 gp to build over a period of several weeks. Most of the cost goes to casting the enchantments that turn boulders into glidestones after loading. A xorn engine is relatively flimsy in construction compared to other siege engines in its class, because it is never exposed to the enemy. By the time it’s in danger, enemies have already breached its location and presumably are moving to capture more valuable prizes.

To fire a xorn engine, one action must be used to load it, and a second action must be spent to use its Glidestone attack. The xorn engine can’t target creatures, objects, vehicles, or structures within 60 feet of it.

***Glidestone.*** *Ranged Weapon Attack:* +6 to hit, range 300/600 ft., one target. *Hit:* 33 (6d10) bludgeoning damage.
