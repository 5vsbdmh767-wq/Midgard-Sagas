Tools help adventurers do complex tasks that they can’t complete with skill alone. For example, you use tools to pick locks, forge documents, cook gourmet meals, and forge weapons. For tasks that require tools, your GM might call for an ability check that uses a tool instead of a skill.

Sometimes a tool is a single item, like a musical instrument. Other times a tool encompasses a kit or set of supplies containing multiple items. When using a tool, you must have all of its component items to make ability checks with it.

Your background, class, heritage, lineage, or talent features can give you proficiency with certain tools. Just like skills, you don’t need to be proficient in a tool to use it, but proficiency with a tool allows you to add your PB to ability checks you make using that tool.

Also, like skills, tools aren’t tied to specific ability scores. The ability score assigned to each ability check using a tool depends on what you are trying to accomplish. For example, when using construction tools, the GM might ask you to make a STR (Construction Tools) check to hammer nails into a hard surface, but ask for a DEX (Construction Tools) check to use the same tools to carve a wooden sculpture.

## Tool Descriptions

The tools available to PCs are described in this section. Entries generally include the following information.

***Associated Abilities.*** Like skills, checks made with tools can involve any ability score. However, the abilities most used with each tool are included for easy reference.

***Components.*** Each description lists all the individual items that comprise the tool set or kit.

***Special Uses.*** Specific uses that require additional explanation are noted individually by name.

***Example Tasks.*** A short table presents example tasks and DCs for each tool.

### Charlatan Tools

Charlatan tools are used to craft disguises and forge documents.

***Associated Abilities:*** DEX, CHA.

***Components.*** This tool kit includes hair dyes, small props, cosmetics, a few choice articles of clothing, a selection of wax seals, gold and silver leaf, inks, a variety of parchments, and sculpting tools to fashion melted wax into seals or prosthetics.

***Create Disguise.*** Over the course of an hour (which can be done as part of a rest) you can create a disguise to make yourself or someone else—including any clothing or equipment—look like a different creature. A disguise can make a creature seem slightly taller, shorter, fatter, or thinner, but can’t conceal true size or basic arrangement of limbs. It takes 1 minute to don such a disguise once created. A set of charlatan tools only has enough materials to create one disguise at a time, but you can disassemble a disguise to create a new disguise as part of the time spent making a disguise. A creature can use its action to inspect a disguised appearance, and must succeed on an INT (Investigation) contest versus your DEX (Charlatan Tools) to discern a disguise.

***Forge Documents.*** Over the course of one hour (which can be done as part of a rest) you can create a simple false document of 5 pages or less—like a personal letter, a party invitation, or a sales permit. With the GM’s permission you can create longer or more complicated documents over the course of several hours or days. A creature can use its action to inspect a forged document, and must succeed on an INT (Investigation) contest versus your DEX (Charlatan Tools) check to discern a forgery.

### Construction Tools

Construction tools are used to build and repair items primarily made of wood or stone.

***Associated Abilities:*** STR, DEX.

***Components.*** This tool kit includes hammers and a mallet, a variety of fasteners, a square and triangle, chisels, a saw, brushes, and spades.

***Fortify.*** You can spend 1 minute fortifying a chest, door, window, or other sealable object or structure. When you do so, the object’s AC increases by an amount equal to your PB or creatures have disadvantage on STR (Athletics) checks made to penetrate the fortification (the GM decides which is more relevant). An item doesn’t receive additional benefits from multiple attempts to fortify it.

***Build Cover.*** You can spend 1 hour (which can be done as part of a long rest) erecting a 5-foot by 5-foot wall panel in an unoccupied space, provided you have the raw materials to do so. The panel provides three-quarters cover (see **Cover** in **Chapter 6**) for one Medium or smaller creature, and it can’t be moved. A panel of cover created with this feature is an object with an AC equal to 10 + your PB and 15 HP if made of wood or 25 HP if made of stone. It is immune to psychic and poison damage. ***Craft Items*.** Construction tools can be used to make items with the crafting downtime activity (see **Downtime Activities** in **Chapter 6**).

## More Tool Uses (Trade Secrets)

*Optional, from Trade Secrets.*

Nearly every profession that involves physical work has specialized tools designed to make the work easier or even tools that are essential to doing that work. Because the **Tales of the Valiant** roleplaying game is largely about fantastic adventures, it doesn’t include as much detail for mundane work as it does with combat and magic. If you are playing an adventure themed around commerce, you may find some of the listed tools or their example uses don’t meet the needs of the story or the action.

### Big Projects

Ambitious PCs may decide to undertake a grand project such as building a roadside inn or constructing a small fortress as their base of operations. The basic crafting rules can be used to cover this kind of downtime project. Each worker makes 10 gp progress per day at the cost of 5 gp in materials and eventually it gets built. That approach works fine if the GM and players aren’t interested in the details of how the fortress was made or the quality of work that was done. On the other hand, a big project like this is a fun opportunity for roleplaying and storytelling. It could even be the focus of a whole campaign.

Big projects are best divided into small pieces. The GM and PCs should both divide the task into meaningful steps and decide what order they should be completed in. Any activities that involve changing tool proficiencies are good places to divide the work. Another is to identify activities dependent on others. For example, to erect a building, you will need a foundation or, at minimum, a clearing for it to stand on, and you will need walls before you can put up a ceiling.

For each step, the two key elements are PC choices and the execution of the work. Good choices usually involve a tradeoff, such as cost vs quality of materials or whether to make building convenient or secure. The tradeoff should matter to the PCs. Execution includes a cost in time, and sometimes an ability check. Ability checks should be used when there is a meaningful risk or reward at stake for good or poor work.

Downtime activities all offer a good structure for the types of outcomes you can make, though in a big PC project, the result will determine how nice the result is rather than how much they can sell it for. Variability in the quality of each aspect can give the result a lot of character. The heroes could create an incredible fortress that can resist a mighty army, but the roof constantly leaks during heavy rains and gremlins infest the cellars.

### New Tool Challenges

Existing tools can do more than the basics. Here are some ideas to expand their range.

#### Alchemist Tools

Alchemist tools are among the most versatile and fun tools to use thanks to the range of useful alchemical concoctions. While many concoctions are inspired by real world chemistry, it’s especially fun to imagine new possibilities.

***Battletape.*** This long strip of tough cloth is treated with a sticky alchemical substance. The inside is stickier than the outside, but both surfaces have adhesive properties. It is most often used to give a weapon or tool a sticky grip but can also temporarily stick objects together, tie up enemies, and even cover a keyhole. Using battletape on a weapon gives it the “secure” weapon property.

***Besty Bond.*** This viscous and colorless liquid can repair broken items or quickly stick items together. The repaired items regain their original strength while improvised bonds have the strength of the weakest bonded material. A one-ounce bottle of besty bond is usually enough to mend 10 tiny items, 4 small items, or 1 medium-sized item. *Disappearing Ink.* This alchemical ink vanishes from sight a few minutes after it is put to paper. Applying heat to the page make the ink visible again for a short time. Disappearing ink is especially popular with spies, cultists, and children.

***Drink and Dye.*** This alchemical liquid is imbibed like a potion and is sold in a range of colors. Over the next hour, it dyes the user’s skin and body fluids the color of the dye. The recommended dosage only adds a shading of the intended color, but some users drink multiple doses to get a more striking shade. The dye fades after 24 hours as the dye is flushed from the body.

***Everink.*** This specially formulated ink lasts much longer than conventional inks as it takes much less of it to leave a bright mark. It is popular with bureaucrats, historians, and anyone else who does a great deal of writing. It is also extremely difficult to remove from surfaces, making it popular for vandals or to simulate a tattoo without the usual discomfort and bleeding.

***Fireworks, Poppers.*** These small fireworks are linked in a chain so that they go off one after another in rapid succession. They create a loud noise, and just a little smoke and spark when they are set off. While not dangerous, they can make for a fine auditory distraction.

***Fireworks, Rockets.*** These types of fireworks launch into the air when they are set off and then explode with dazzling colors. The price listed is for basic rockets, but more exotic and expensive versions can be purchased. While not especially dangerous, they can ignite highly flammable objects like dry grass.

***Fireworks, Whizzers.*** A whizzer is a small firework that spins on the ground and emits colored sparks and a steady whistling-like sound. They are popular for parties and other celebrations.

***Flameout.*** This alchemical powder is sold in fist sized bags. When a handful is thrown at a non-magical fire it instantly extinguishes the flames. Each bag has 4 applications. Using Flameout in combat requires an Action and the user must be within 5 feet of the fire being extinguished.

***Glowdust.*** Also known as sparkle powder, glowdust is an incredibly fine-grained powder that glints and sparkles in nearly any light. It can be used as a cosmetic or decoration, but its most common use among adventurers is to help find invisible objects and identify illusory objects. It is held in the hand and then blown at the area under investigation, sticking to unseen objects and not sticking to unreal objects. Using a dose of glowdust gives all observers advantage on INT (Investigation) checks made to find invisible objects or identify illusory objects. A bag of glowdust has 4 uses for this purpose.

***Gogo Grease.*** Typically sold in a sheepskin bladder, gogo grease is an effective lubricant for gearworks, door hinges, and other mechanical moving parts. It can also make objects and surfaces extremely slippery. Coating an object of 5-foot square floor with gogo grease takes an action in combat. Non-flying creatures entering a square coated with the gogo grease must make a DC 12 DEX saving throw or fall prone. Standing up from prone in the greased square requires a successful DC 12 DEX (Acrobatics) check. Anyone attempting to pick up or use an item coated with the gogo grease must make a successful DC 12 DEX saving throw or they will immediately drop it. The grease can be wiped off with a rag or other cloth as an Action in combat. A skin of gogo grease contains enough grease to cover four 5-foot squares or eight handheld items.

***Quick Clay.*** This modeling putty instantly hardens when water is applied to it. It can quickly make molds but is also sold as a toy and art supply. It is typically sold in a wide mouth bottle with a cork stopper to keep it dry.

***Smoke Bomb.*** Smoke bombs are usually wrapped in wax paper and have a fuse that can be cut to a desired time. Once activated, thick noxious smoke pours out of the bomb for 5 minutes. This creates a 30-foot-radius sphere of thick smoke that reduces visibility to 10 feet within the cloud and prevents sight into or out of the cloud. Any creature that starts their turn in the cloud must make a DC 10 CON saving throw or become Poisoned until the beginning of their next turn. A wind of moderate or greater speed (at least 10 miles per hour) will disperse the cloud while the wind persists.

***Wonder Whiff.*** This alchemical liquid is sold in a small jug. When any scented object is dipped into the jug, the liquid takes on that scent. The wonder whiff can then be applied to nearly any surface or object to give it that scent for the next hour. A jug of wonder whiff contains enough liquid to apply 50 times. Wonder whiff is popular both as a cosmetic and with hunters who want to mask their scent.

***Clothing, winter.*** Winter clothes are meant for freezing temperatures and include layers of clothing with a heavily insulated outer coat. While they offer great protection in cold weather, these outfits are nearly as heavy as light armor.

#### Herbalist Tools

Medicine is one of the most important specializations. Nature often provides medicinal cures, which makes herbalist tools one of the most useful tool sets. Of course, while medicine can cure the body, a herbalist can also corrupt it with poison.

**Herbal Concoctions**

| Concoctions | Cost | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Bandages | 1 cp | ½ lb. |
| Cosmetics | 5 sp | — |
| Dye | 1 sp | — |
| Paint | 1 sp | — |
| Perfume | 5 gp | — |
| Purgative | 5 cp | ½ lb. |
| Poultice | 1 sp | — |
| Tonic | 1 sp | ½ lb. |

***Bandages.*** Little more than cut strips of clean cloth, bandages seal injuries and prevent infection, though they don’t offer a direct game benefit. Nonetheless, every healer carries some.

***Cosmetics.*** Most cosmetics combine botanical and mineral ingredients, so a good herbalist can create make-ups, blushes, and other decorations.

***Dye.*** While some dyes are mineral based, many come from plants or insects, and a good herbalist will know how to produce them.

***Paint.*** Quite a few elements from other tool sets can be made by an herbalist. Many paints for artist tools can be made by an herbalist.

***Perfume.*** Herbalists often produce sweet smelling oils that can be applied to skin or clothing but can also be used in candle making and other activities to keep a home smelling nice.

***Purgative.*** This concoction quickly induces vomiting and purges the stomach of toxic substances. A character poisoned by food or drink within the last hour can drink a purgative to stop that poison’s ongoing effects.

***Poultice.*** A poultice is a mix of herbal ingredients placed on a wound, with a bandage to hold it in place. It both protects against infection and encourages good healing. A character treated with a poultice while resting can roll any spent hit dice twice and take the better result. A poultice must be made fresh and only remains useful for one week at most.

***Tonic.*** Tonics are medicinal drinks that grant the imbiber a feeling of health and energy. Drinking a tonic reduces the creature’s exhaustion by one level and give them advantage on their next CON saving throw. A creature cannot benefit from a tonic again until they complete a long rest.

#### Tinker Tools

Tinker tools are specialized for working on mechanical devices and cross over with jeweler’s tools. Many tinker devices have a good deal of artistic flair and decoration.

***Auto-hammer.*** Most auto-hammers are massive devices that replace the manual swing of a hammer onto an anvil. With an auto-hammer, the strikes hit quickly and with precisely controlled force, allowing the smith to use both hands to manipulate the metal they are working.

***Astrolabe.*** Astrolabes are intricate metal instruments used to track the movement of heavenly bodies and predict celestial events.

#### Trapper Tools

Trapper tools cover a range of related, but differing disciplines. Most examples focus on leatherworking, but trappers also know how to build traps and how to use them to catch prey.

***Bone flute.*** Simple whistles and flutes made from horn or bone are a great way to entertain yourself in the wildlands.

***Kill trophy.*** Trophies made from teeth or bone look intimidating on a shelf or worn around the neck. They can also fetch a good price from city folk who can imagine themselves facing off against wild creatures.

***Satchel.*** Few carrying devices are as versatile and handy as a good leather satchel. They are sturdy and, if well made, can keep your goods dry.

***Taxidermy critters.*** Taxidermy is a trapper’s hobby, and some nobles will pay good money for lifelike animals to decorate their homes.

**Trapper Tools New Example Tasks**

| Task | DC |
|---|---|
| Dig a latrine | 5 |
| Identify animal remains | 15 |
| Set a blind | * |
| Set an animal trap | * |

***Set a blind.*** A blind is a hidden position in a natural setting in which one or more people can remain largely undetected by wildlife. This allows for ambush hunting or discrete wildlife observation. Setting up a blind takes at least 1 hour per person to be hidden. The character making the blind must make a WIS (Trapper Tools) check. The result is the DC needed to see the blind and anything it hides as anything other than part of the natural surroundings. Creatures within 10 feet of the blind have advantage on checks made to detect the blind itself.

***Set an animal trap.*** Trappers are skilled in setting up a range of traps designed to capture or kill animals without doing physical damage that would ruin its value in trade. The type of trap used depends on the prey, the terrain, available materials, and the goals of the trapper. Typically, a trapper will set up a trap and then come back to check on it within 24 hours. Waiting longer means other animals may steal or eat the trapper’s catch.

When a character sets an animal trap, they should name the target type of animal or monster to catch. Most animal traps are only effective against small or smaller sized creatures of animal or lower intelligence. The GM then decides what DC is needed to catch such an animal; 10 for easy prey, 15 for challenging prey, and 20 for especially elusive prey. Before checking on the trap, the character must wait at least 8 hours. When checking on the trap, the character who set the trap makes a WIS (Trapper Tools) check. If they are successful, the trap has caught an animal of the type that the trapper was trying to catch. If they fail, then no animal is caught, though the trap may have been sprung or bait taken.

**Common Animal Traps**

| Name | Cost | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Cage | 5 gp | 10 lb. |
| Deadfall | — | 20 lb. |
| Foothold trap | 20 gp | 10 lb. |
| Pitfall | — | — |
| Snare | 1 cp | — |
| Spring | — | 2 lb. |

***Cage Trap.*** Helped by a latching door, bait is placed inside the cage on a trigger plate. When an animal enters the cage and steps on the plate, the door swings closed and latches shut, trapping the animal inside. The cage must be strong enough to resist the animal’s attempts to escape.

***Deadfall Trap.*** Deadfall traps require a heavy flat stone and a few small sticks. The sticks are cut into a shape that precariously holds up the stone at an angle. Bait is then placed where the sticks meet. When an animal tries to eat the bait, they disturb the sticks and the stone falls, crushing the animal. Deadfall traps are catch only tiny animals that die from the weight of the stone.

***Foothold Trap.*** Made by blacksmiths or tinkerers, foothold traps use spring-loaded iron or steel jaws and a central trigger or a pressure plate, then hidden under leaf litter or other debris. When a creature steps on the trigger, the jaws snap shut and hold the creature’s leg. They need to be strong enough to hold fast, but not so strong as to entirely cut through the limb. These traps are usually placed along animal trails or near fresh water. They must be chained or tethered to an object strong enough to keep the animal from dragging the trap away.

***Pitfall Trap.*** To make a pitfall trap, the trapper only needs to dig a hole large and deep enough for the prey animal to fall into and be unable to escape from. The top of the pit is then disguised with branches, leaf litter, or any other material that will not support the prey animal’s weight. The trap can be baited or placed along a known animal trail.

***Snare Trap.*** Snares require a length of cord or wire with a noose on one end; the other end is anchored to a tree, rock, or stake. Snares are placed along animal paths and in underbrush. When an animal is running along the path and passes through the noose, it tightens around the animal’s neck, holding it fast. The cord or wire needs to be strong enough to prevent an animal chewing through it.

***Spring Trap.*** This trap requires a supple wood branch and a thin tripwire, with a small metal hook to serve as a trigger. The branch is bent back with strong tension, and the tripwire is wound to keep the branch under tension, but any additional pressure on the tripwire will releases the branch to strike and kill the prey.
