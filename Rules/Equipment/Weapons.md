The Weapons table lists the cost, damage dice and type, weight, weapon options, and special properties for common weapons. The table is also split by the proficiencies required (simple or martial) to wield the various weapons most effectively.

## Weapon Types & Proficiencies

Each weapon is categorized into a type associated with a proficiency. For instance, a character with proficiency in martial weapons gets to add their PB to attack rolls made with any weapon listed in the martial weapons section of the **Weapons** table.

Any character can wield any weapon, but only a character with proficiency can add their PB to attack rolls with those weapons.

### Melee Weapons

Melee weapons are used to attack nearby targets within reach. Unless the weapon has the Reach property, melee weapons have a reach of 5 feet.

### Ranged Weapons

Ranged weapons are used to attack targets at a distance.

### Simple Weapons

Simple weapons require little experience to use effectively.

### Martial Weapons

Martial weapons, including swords, axes, and polearms, require training to use effectively.

## Improvised Weapons

When your favorite weapon is across the room, you can attack with whatever’s handy. An improvised weapon includes any object you can wield in one or two hands, such as broken glass, a table leg, a frying pan, a wagon wheel, or a dead goblin.

When in doubt about how much damage an object should deal, find a weapon on the **Weapons** table most like the object and use its damage die. For example, a table leg is a lot like a club. At the GM’s discretion, a character proficient with a weapon can use a similar object as if it were that weapon and add their PB as normal.

If a character uses a ranged weapon to make a melee attack or throws a melee weapon that doesn’t have the Thrown property, those are improvised weapons, and typically deal 1d4 damage of an appropriate damage type. An improvised thrown weapon typically has range of 20 feet and a long range of 60 feet.

## Weapon Options

This section offers options for using weapons in ways other than dealing damage. The Weapon Option column on the **Weapons** table shows which weapons can be used to perform which weapon options. A character can use a weapon option only if the character is wielding and is proficient with an appropriate weapon.

***Weapon Option Saves.*** If an option requires a creature to make an ability check or save, the DC equals 8 + the attacker’s PB + the attacker’s STR or DEX modifier (attacker’s choice). Unless specified otherwise, a weapon attack used to perform a weapon option has only the option’s listed effect and doesn’t deal normal weapon damage. Weapon options can be used only when a wielder takes the Attack action on their turn, unless a feature like the fighter’s Martial Action allows a weapon option attack to be performed as a bonus action. Characters with the Multiattack feature can perform a weapon option in place of one of the attacks granted by Multiattack. At the GM’s discretion, some weapon options might not work against certain creatures. For example, the trip weapon option might not work against a creature without discernable legs, such as an ooze, or that is anchored or attached to the ground in some way, such as a tree with animated limbs.

### Bash

Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, the target has disadvantage on its next attack roll.

### Disarm

Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, the target must succeed on a STR or DEX save (target’s choice) or drop a weapon, shield, or object it is wielding. The dropped item lands in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of the target. If no unoccupied space is within range, the item lands at the target’s feet.

### Hamstring

Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, the target’s base movement speed is reduced by 10 feet for 1 minute. A creature’s speed can’t be reduced by more than 10 feet with this weapon option. A creature within 5 feet of the target can take an action to tend the wound with a successful WIS (Medicine) check (against your weapon option DC), ending the effect on the target. The effect also ends if the target receives any magical healing.

### Pinning Shot

Make an attack roll with this weapon against a Large or smaller creature. On a hit, the target must succeed on a STR or DEX save (target’s choice) or its speed becomes 0 feet until the end of its next turn. A creature, including the target, can use its action to attempt to free the target with a STR (Athletics) check or a DEX (Acrobatics) check (the creature’s choice) versus the attacker’s weapon option DC. On a success, the target is freed and can move as normal. A target must make only one check to free itself, using the highest DC of characters performing this weapon option, regardless of the number of arrows or bolts holding it in place.

### Pull

Make an attack roll with this weapon against a Large or smaller creature. On a hit, the target is pulled up to 5 feet closer to you. If this movement would pull a creature into damaging terrain, such as lava or a pit, it can make a STR or DEX (target’s choice) save to avoid the pull on a success.

### Ricochet Shot

Make an attack roll with this weapon against a target you can see that has half or three-quarters cover. Your chosen target must be within 10 feet of another object or structure that isn’t the same item providing it with cover. When you do so, you can treat the target’s AC as if it wasn’t behind cover. If the attack is successful, the target takes damage from the attack as it would with a standard weapon attack. This weapon option expends the same ammunition as a normal attack with this weapon.

### Trip

Make an attack roll with this weapon against a Large or smaller creature. On a hit, the target must succeed on a STR or DEX save (target’s choice) or fall prone. If the target is mounted, it has advantage on the save.

## Weapon Materials

A weapon can be made from a special material that gives it additional properties. These properties aren't considered magical.

Weapons made from special materials typically cost (and can be sold for) more than the same weapon made with standard materials. Some well-known special weapon materials are described here. Your GM decides whether weapons made from special materials are available for purchase.

***Adamantine.*** Adamantine is an exceptionally hard metal that is difficult to find and costly to mine. On a successful hit, a weapon made of adamantine deals an extra 1d6 damage of the weapon’s type to objects and to creatures with the Golem tag. An adamantine weapon or 10 pieces of ammunition can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 500 gp.

***Silvered.*** Silvered weapons are standard weapons plated with silver. On a successful hit, a silvered weapon deals an extra 1d6 damage of the weapon’s type to creatures with the Shapechanger tag. Silvering weapons is a specialty service that skilled blacksmiths with the appropriate resources can provide. Silvering a weapon or 10 pieces of ammunition costs 100 gp, and a pre-silvered weapon or ammunition can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 100 gp. Note that any magical or nonmagical weapon or ammunition can be silvered, even those made from other special materials.

## Weapon Properties

Many weapons have special properties that affect their use, as shown in the Properties column of the **Weapons** table.

***Ammunition.*** You can use a weapon that has the Ammunition property to make a ranged attack only if you have ammunition to fire from it (see **Adventuring Gear** in this chapter). Each time you attack with the weapon, you expend one piece of ammunition. You need a free hand to load a one-handed weapon, and drawing the ammunition from a quiver, case, or other container is part of the attack. At the end of an encounter, you can recover half your expended ammunition by taking 1 minute to search the battlefield. If you use a weapon with the Ammunition property to make a melee attack, treat it as an improvised weapon (see **Improvised Weapons** in this chapter). A sling must be loaded to deal damage when used as an improvised weapon.

***Finesse.*** When making an attack with a Finesse weapon, use your choice of your STR or DEX modifier for the attack and damage rolls. You must use the same modifier for both rolls.

***Heavy.*** Small creatures have disadvantage on attack rolls with Heavy weapons. Tiny creatures can’t wield Heavy weapons.

***Light.*** A Light weapon is needed for use with two-weapon fighting (see **Melee Attacks** in **Chapter 6**).

***Loading.*** Because of the time required to load this weapon, you can fire only one piece of ammunition from it when you use an action, bonus action, or reaction to fire it, regardless of the number of attacks you can normally make.

***Range.*** A weapon that can be used to make a ranged attack has a range in parentheses after the Ammunition or Thrown property. The range lists two numbers. The first is the weapon’s normal range in feet, and the second indicates the weapon’s long range. When attacking a target beyond normal range and up to its long range, you have disadvantage on the attack roll. You can’t attack a target beyond the weapon’s long range.

***Reach.*** This weapon adds 5 feet to your reach when you make melee attacks with it, as well as when determining your reach for opportunity attacks with it.

***Special (Lance).*** You have disadvantage when you use a lance to attack a target within 5 feet of you. Also, a lance requires two hands to wield when you aren’t mounted (see **Mounted Combat** in **Chapter 6**).

***Thrown.*** You can throw a weapon with the Thrown property to make a ranged attack. If the weapon can also be a melee weapon, use the same ability modifier for that attack roll and damage roll that you would use for a melee attack with the weapon. For example, if you throw a handaxe or javelin, use your STR modifier, but if you throw a dagger, you can use either your STR or DEX modifier, since the dagger has the Finesse property.

***Joining Dirks (Martial Melee).*** Interlocking pommels cap this pair of small blades. As a bonus action, you can interlock the pommels to create a single weapon with a blade on each end (or separate them again into the two blades). ## Weapon Materials (Player's Guide 2)
Weapons can be made from special materials that give them additional qualities. These qualities are simply natural extensions of the material and are not considered magical. Such weapons typically cost, and can be sold for, more than the same weapon made with standard materials. (Weapons made from special materials are available for purchase at GM’s discretion.)

***Cold Iron.*** The timeless creatures of the faerie realms are long inured to the mortal world’s threats—except for weapons of cold-wrought iron. A cold iron weapon is treated as magical when used against any Fey creature, blighting their ageless flesh. Additionally, on a successful hit, a cold iron weapon deals an extra 1d6 damage of the weapon’s type to a Fey creature. You can’t simply add cold iron to an existing weapon though, so it must be crafted from the start. A cold iron weapon or ten pieces of ammunition can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 300 gp.

***Masterwork.*** A masterwork weapon is an exceptionally well-made version of a weapon of the finest materials available, typically crafted by a master. While wielding a masterwork weapon you’re proficient with, you have +1 to attack rolls and damage with that weapon (with the bonus damage matching the weapon’s damage type). Note that magic weapons are already considered to be masterwork quality, so masterwork bonuses don’t stack with bonuses already built into magic weapons. You can’t simply make an existing weapon masterwork though. It must be crafted as a masterwork weapon from the start. A masterwork weapon can be purchased for an amount equal to the weapon’s base cost + 300 gp (or + 600 gp for a weapon with the Double-Headed property). A single masterwork piece of ammunition costs a flat 5 gp (ignore ammunition base cost).

***Mithral.*** Mithral is a light yet durable metal that is difficult to find and requires master-level skill to work. Weapons made from mithral gain the Light property. You can’t simply add mithral to an existing weapon though, so it must be crafted from the start. Any melee weapon that deals piercing or slashing damage and lacks the Heavy or Two-Handed properties can be forged from mithral. Mithral weapons can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 1,000 gp.

***Obsidian.*** Obsidian is volcanic glass that can hold a cutting edge five hundred times sharper than the finest steel blade. On a successful hit, an obsidian weapon deals additional damage equal to the wielder’s PB (with the bonus damage matching the weapon’s damage type). However, obsidian is more fragile than steel, so if you roll a natural 1 on an attack made with an obsidian weapon, the weapon is damaged, and you can’t make attacks with it again until you spend a short rest repairing it (no special tools or check required). Any melee weapon that deals piercing or slashing damage can be made out of obsidian. A weapon forged from obsidian can be purchased for an amount equal to the weapon’s base cost + 1,000 gp, though it is rare to find merchants who carry such weapons, and it is similarly difficult to find crafters capable of working with the material. Where materials such as steel are not common, obsidian weapons can be readily purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost.

***Starforged.*** Though originally believed to be powerful and magical—so-called gifts from the gods—the metals found in meteorites are not actually magical (typically), but they do allow for the crafting of high-quality, extraordinarily sharp weapons. These metals aren’t typically well-suited to being made into weapons on their own and instead are forged as part of an alloy, either with base metals or with extraordinary ones like adamantine. Though not magical (by default), a star-forged weapon is still treated as magical when used against any Aberration or creature with the Outsider tag. Additionally, on a successful hit, it deals an extra 1d6 damage of the weapon’s type to Aberrations and creatures with the Outsider tag. You can’t simply add such metals to an existing weapon though, so it must be crafted from the start. A starforged weapon or ten pieces of ammunition can be purchased for an amount equal to the item’s base cost + 500 gp.

## Weapon Properties (Player's Guide 2)
Many weapons have special properties that affect their use, and this is listed in the Properties column of the **Weapons** table.

***Defensive.*** While wielding this weapon in one hand and a different melee weapon in the other, you gain a +1 bonus to AC. Wielding more than one Defensive weapon doesn’t increase this bonus.

***Double-Headed.*** This weapon has two striking ends, whether that be blades, axe heads, or something else. You can wield it for two-weapon fighting, even though it doesn’t have the Light property, and must use the opposite end for the bonus attack, using the value in parentheses as that attack’s damage. If the other end’s damage type is different from the weapon’s base damage type, this is noted in the weapon’s description.

***Fitted.*** Any attempts to disarm this weapon or otherwise force you to drop it against your will, automatically fail.

***Heavy (STR Requirement).*** In addition to the standard restrictions of the Heavy property, this weapon is impossible to wield effectively unless you have the required STR score. If your STR score doesn’t equal or exceed the STR score listed in parentheses, you have disadvantage on attacks made with it, even if you’re proficient with it.

***Magazine.*** This weapon can be loaded with multiple pieces of ammunition, allowing you to make multiple attacks with it without reloading between shots as long as at least one piece of unspent ammunition remains in the magazine. Each time you make an attack roll with the weapon, a piece of ammunition is expended from the magazine. Weapons with this property have a number listed with it, specifying the maximum pieces of ammunition the magazine can hold. For example, a property listed as “Magazine (8)” indicates that weapon’s magazine can hold up to eight pieces of ammunition at one time. When the ammunition from the magazine is depleted, it takes a Use an Object action to replace an empty magazine with a new full one. Refilling an empty magazine with ammunition takes 1 minute.

### Puncture

Make an attack roll with this weapon against a target wearing manufactured armor. On a hit, the target must succeed on a DEX save or the armor takes a −1 penalty to the AC it offers. On a critical hit, this penalty increases to −2.

## More Weapons (Trade Secrets)

*Optional, from Trade Secrets.*

### Weapon Types (Trade Secrets)

Each weapon is categorized into a type associated with a proficiency, either simple or martial.

***Aruval (Martial Melee).*** This is a broad, single-edged blade, two to three feet long. At the end of the blade, opposite the cutting edge, it has a backswept spike used to defeat heavy armor or catch an opponent’s limbs.

***Bayonet (Simple Melee).*** A short blade attached to the end of a musket makes it much like a short spear in close quarters combat.

***Bolo (Simple Ranged).*** A typical bolo has three weighted lines connected to a central ring or knot. When thrown correctly the lines spread out evenly and spin. A bolo can trip up a moving target wrapping around the runner’s legs.

***Broadsword (Martial Melee).*** This large family of single- handed, double-edged swords are commonly paired with a shield on the battlefield. It is the classic knight’s blade. Most have a sturdy enough pommel to deliver a solid strike to opponents in the clinch.

***Cat o’ Nine Tails (Simple Melee).*** Also known as a scourge, this flail-like weapon combines small weights at the end of multiple short chains connected to a wooden handle. While commonly used in corporal punishments, it can also be harass opponents in battle.

***Fire Lance (Martial Melee).*** This spear has a bundle of fireworks attached to the shaft just behind the tip of the spear. Once lit, it spews flames, smoke, and noxious smells. This can be disorient and terrify opponents, and it sets materials on fire when the spear is stuck in them. The fireworks need to be replaced after they are used, but they last for a good minute before sputtering out.

***Gaff (Simple Melee).*** A sturdy metal hook mounted on a long pole often serves as a rough-and-ready weapons. They are used by fishermen to pull fish up from a net and by sailors to hook ropes and cargo. They also can knock someone down in a pinch.

***Guisarme (Martial Melee).*** One of many polearm types, the guisarme features a stabbing point for thrusting, a hooked blade for grabbing and cutting, and a stout metal spike for bashing away at armor.

***Hairpin (Simple Melee).*** Disguised as a regular hairpin, this holdout weapon is made of sturdy steel and can be used as a deadly weapon in the right hands.

***Hot Iron (Simple Melee).*** Any long piece of iron heated to a red-hot glow can make a menacing weapon to bludgeon, burn, and scar an opponent. Fireplace pokers and smith’s heated tools are classic examples.

***Jitte (Simple Melee).*** Jitte are sturdy metal batons with a forward-facing hook mounted just above the grip. The hook us used to catch enemy weapons and to catch an opponent’s wrist or joints when grappling.

***Katar (Martial Melee).*** Also known as push daggers or punching daggers, a katar features a thick and wide blade with a tapered point that is mounted on a guarded handle perpendicular to the blade. This allows it to make powerful punching strikes.

***Lucerne Hammer (Martial Melee).*** This polearm has a stabbing pike point at either end and a beaked hammer head set just below the forward-facing point. This gives you choices in exactly how you want to stab your enemies.

***Monkey’s Fist (Simple Melee).*** The monkey’s fist is sailor’s knot combining a thick, round ball of rope with a leading “tail” to hold it by. When tied around a stone or lead weight, it makes an effective flail like weapon.

***Pistol Sword (Martial Melee and Ranged).*** These hybrid weapons are created more as a test of skill than for practical use, but they are still deadly. The single-shot pistol is aimed down the back of the single edged blade and fired with a trigger built into the handle.

***Pitchfork (Simple Melee).*** When not moving hay, pitchforks can fend off bandits or overthrow tyrants.

***Poleaxe (Martial Melee).*** A typical poleax has a six-foot- long, square wooden shaft reinforced with long metal plates. The business end combines three weapons: a bladed spear point, an axe-head, and a hammerhead, making it one of the most complex and versatile knightly weapons.

***Throwing Stick (Simple Melee).*** Much like a boomerang, a throwing stick has a wing shape to be thrown a good distance and to deliver a hard strike. Unlike its trickier cousins, it does not return to the thrower.

***Tiger Claws (Simple Melee).*** These metal claws are held like brass knuckles but used to make slashing attacks at very close range. It’s common to wield one in each hand.

***War Fan (Simple Melee).*** War fans resemble normal folding paper fans at first blush, but they are made of iron or steel and can strike a heavy blow or block incoming attacks.

### Weapon Options (Trade Secrets)

This section offers options for using weapons in ways other than dealing damage. The Weapon Option column on the Weapons table shows which weapons can perform which weapon options. A character can use a weapon option only if the character is wielding and is proficient with an appropriate weapon.

***Brand.*** Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit, the target takes 1d4 fire damage and the target must succeed on a CON Saving Throw or be scared by the weapon.

***Distract.*** Make an attack roll with this weapon. On a hit the target must succeed on a WIS Saving Throw or be unable to use a reaction or take bonus action until the end of their next turn.

***Grappling.*** You can use this weapon to grapple. While wielding this weapon, you do not need a free hand to grapple with. While you have an opponent grappled, attacks made with this weapon against the opponent cannot miss unless a natural 1 is rolled.

### Weapon Materials (Trade Secrets)

A weapon’s material can give it additional properties. These properties aren't considered magical.

Weapons made from special materials often cost (and can be sold for) more than the same weapon made with standard materials. Some well-known special weapon materials are described here. Your GM decides whether weapons made from special materials are available for purchase.

***Shoddy.*** Not every weapon is made with quality materials. Some smiths and craftspeople either make do with what they have or try to cut costs to maximize profits. Either way, these are not weapons you want to bet your life on. A shoddy weapon breaks after any Critical Hit or Critical Miss is rolled when attacking with it. In the case of a Critical Hit, damage is still dealt. If the seller is honest, a shoddy weapon costs three-quarter the weapon’s base cost, otherwise they are full price.

### Weapon Properties (Trade Secrets)

Many weapons have special properties, as shown in the Properties column of the Weapons table.

***Disguised.*** While this is a weapon, it looks mundane and harmless unless closely inspected.

***Hot.*** This weapon deals an additional 1 Fire damage on a successful attack.

***Secure.*** You have Advantage on Ability Checks and Saving Throws made to avoid being disarmed.
