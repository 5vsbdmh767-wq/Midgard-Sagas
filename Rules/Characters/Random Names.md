Typically, naming customs and conventions are specific to the cultures and regions within a particular game world. However, if you need a name for a PC/NPC quickly, you can use the following tables to either randomly determine or choose one from the options presented. The first batch of tables are divided into examples that follow trends typical of the most widespread languages in a general fantasy world: Common, Dwarvish, Elvish, Gnomish/ [[Halflings|Halfling]], and Undercommon. When using these, choose the table that best represents the heritage and cultural background of the character you wish to name. The rest of the tables are more generic, suitable for use with a wide variety of game world elements.

The options in the **Common Names** table are ideal for any character raised in a society that uses Common as its primary language. In a typical fantasy setting, the most prevalent lineages in this category are often human and beastkin. It includes the widest diversity of names, often varying widely by geographic region.

## Dwarvish Names

The options in the **Dwarvish Names** table are ideal for any character raised in a society that uses Dwarvish as its primary language. In a typical fantasy setting, the most prevalent lineage in this category is often dwarf. It is also ideal for creatures that speak Orcish since both languages rely heavily on words with hard consonants and on descriptive surnames that speak to personal or family history.

It is also ideal for creatures that speak Sylvan since both languages rely heavily on words with multiple syllables and soft, lilting sounds.

## Gnomish & Halfling Names

The options in the **Gnomish & Halfling Names** table are ideal for any character raised in a society that uses either Gnomish or Halfling as its primary language. In a typical fantasy setting, the most prevalent lineage in this category is often smallfolk. Both languages being dialects of the same root language, they rely on whimsical combinations of syllables and plentiful doubled letters.

## Undercommon Names

The options in the **Undercommon Names** table are ideal for any character raised in a society that uses Undercommon as its primary language. In a typical fantasy setting, the most prevalent lineages in this category are often drow and duergar. It is also ideal for creatures that speak [[Goblins|Goblin]] since both languages rely heavily on words with sharp sounds and on syllables segmented by apostrophes.

## Margrevian Names

%% source: Tales of the Old Margreve %%

The names of the people of the [[The Margreve|Margreve Forest]] are as varied as the people themselves, but some names are particularly popular. Most of these names originated from specific peoples within the Margreve Forest but have since spread to those who have traded with, been raised by, or otherwise interacted with such people. Use the Margrevian Names table when choosing a name for a character from the Margreve Forest or as inspiration for creating a name for such a character.

***Alseid Names.*** [[Alseid]] tend to name those in their community using a combination of Elvish and Sylvan syllables. The names are often extremely long, and many youth choose shorter nicknames for themselves when they come of age.

***Bearfolk Names.*** [[Bearfolk]] tend to give those in their community names heavy with consonants or growling sounds.

***Erina Names.*** [[Erina]] tend to bestow names with long, open vowel sounds, and they often extend the sounds of vowels in names when speaking affectionately to or about someone.

***Piney Names.*** Those in piney communities often declare new names for themselves each Spring. The names typically evoke the sounds, colors, flora, and fauna of the forest and often avoid relying on distinct words.

***Deed Names.*** Many raised within the bounds of the Margreve Forest receive a second name from their community—often known as a deed name—as part of the passage into adulthood. This name typically describes some honorable or prominent trait of the individual or some great deed they accomplished when coming of age. Those who carry deed names typically keep the names throughout their lives to remind them of their past, but many change their deed name as they accomplish greater deeds in their lives.
