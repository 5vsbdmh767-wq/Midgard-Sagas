The gearforged are an artificial lineage of living minds inside mechanical bodies of metal and wood. Though they come from a vast array of backgrounds, gearforged are created one at a time in arcane workshops and foundries.

All gearforged were once other creatures with bodies of flesh and blood. For various reasons, their conscious minds were transplanted into bodies of iron, steel, brass, and wood, driven by pistons and springs. Each is as distinctive in appearance as any person. Some gearforged spend a fortune on acquiring a new body while others scrape together anything that works, especially if the subject is aging or ill.

All gearforged are made in a humanoid shape, often in the likeness of the person’s original lineage. The vast majority of gearforged are shaped like humans, who were the original creators of gearforged, though many other variations exist.

[[Gearforged]] have articulated joints, hands, feet, and crystalline eyes. However, gearforged mechanisms are more than mechanical, for gearforged are machines with souls. Their arms and legs are driven by everwound springs. Their minds are actuated by memory gears, transverse cognition gearing, and the marvel of a soul gem connected directly to a maze of silver and mithral conduits for steam, spark, and magic. These elements reside in a shell of iron, brass, and steel.

For who they are in Midgard, see [[Gearforged]]. Common heritages: [[Heritages#Cosmopolitan Heritage|Cosmopolitan]], [[Heritages#Ironwrought Heritage|Ironwrought]], [[Heritages#Steam-and-Brass Heritage|Steam-and-Brass]], [[Heritages#Stygian Heritage|Stygian]].

## Gearforged Lineage Traits

Your gearforged character has the following traits.

***Age.*** The soul inhabiting a gearforged can be any age. If its new body is kept in good repair, there is no known limit to how long it can function.

***Size.*** Your size is Medium or Small. When you create your gearforged character, choose a different lineage option (such as human or kobold) to inform the basic shape and size of your constructed body (also called your chassis). Your size is determined by the lineage you pick. If you choose a lineage with multiple size options, you can choose between them. For example, if you choose to have a kobold chassis, you can choose whether your size is Small or Medium. Your size and height are determined by the lineage design you choose. On average, gearforged weigh twenty to thirty percent more than a typical creature of the lineage their chassis is modelled after.

***Speed.*** Your base walking speed is 30 feet.

***Constructed Vitality.*** You don’t need to eat, drink, or breathe, and you don’t sleep the way most creatures do. Instead, you enter a dormant state for 6 hours a day, where you resemble a statue and remain semiconscious. While in this state, you have disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks. After resting in this way, you gain the same benefit that other creatures do from 8 hours of sleep.

***Hybrid Humanoid.*** Your Humanoid soul inhabits a machine, making you primarily a Humanoid. However, your constructed body gives you traits in common with Constructs. You are immune to disease, and you are resistant to poison damage. In addition, you have advantage on saves against being paralyzed, petrified, or poisoned.

***Machine Speech.*** You can speak and understand Machine Speech, a mechanical language of clicks, beeps, and clacks spoken primarily by gearforged and mechadrons.

> [!note] Gearforged Components
> The range of gearforged anatomy in all its variants is remarkable, but all gearforged share some common parts.
>
> ***Everwound Springs.*** These magical springs provide energy over long periods, acting as the power source for most of a gearforged’s moving parts. A broken everwound spring results in the loss of function in its attached digit or limb, much like a damaged joint or ligaments in a flesh‑and-blood creature.
>
> ***Memory Gears.*** These delicate constructions are scroll‑like ribbons pierced with thousands of pin holes and wound about with tiny enchantments of great complexity. The memory of a gearforged for all the days after its creation lives in the memory gears. Older gearforged have many such gears, and the material component for the magic to create them requires an additional gear for every 10 years of life. Installing a new memory gear requires 1 day’s work, tinker tools, and 2,000 gp.
>
> ***Soul Gem.*** The mind of a gearforged is as sharp as that of any flesh-and-blood creature, but it is more portable. The animating, vital principle of a gearforged—its will, its personality, its mind—is retained in a soul gem. The destruction of this gem means the death of that gearforged.
>

***Upgrade.*** Choose one of the following traits to represent a specialized function built into your chassis:

- **Always Armed.** You can integrate a melee weapon into each of your arms (maximum of two weapons, one per arm). You can’t integrate weapons with the Two- Handed property. If you integrate a weapon with the Ammunition property, you must still provide a separate supply of ammunition for firing and loading. You can integrate or replace a suitable weapon over the course of 10 minutes (which can be done as part of a short or long rest). While a weapon is integrated, it can’t be disarmed, though you can use an action to remove a weapon from your arm, so it can be thrown or dropped. You can draw or sheathe an integrated weapon as normal, which causes the weapon to fold into or spring from your arm as a sheath. While an integrated weapon is drawn, it is considered held in that arm’s hand. You automatically succeed on ability checks made to conceal an integrated weapon when it is sheathed.

- **Bulk Up.** As a bonus action, you can activate a set of special gears inside your body to extend your limbs and temporarily increase your size to Large. This effect lasts for a number of rounds equal to your PB. While enlarged, your weapon attacks deal an extra 1d4 damage of the appropriate type and you have advantage on STR checks and saves. If you lack the room to become Large, you retain your normal size and lose this bonus action. After you use this trait, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

- **Quick Fix.** When you are below half your hit point maximum, you can use a bonus action to apply a quick patch-up to your damaged body. You gain temporary hit points equal to your twice your PB. You can’t use this trait again until you finish a short or long rest.
