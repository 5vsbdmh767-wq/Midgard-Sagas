*Optional, from Northlands Worldbook.*

This page covers two kinds of prophecy, greater prophecies and prophetic blessings or warnings, along with advice on building them, a way to reward players for using them, tables of omens, and some sample prophecies.

## Prophecies

Prophecies are predictions of events to come. They are usually cryptic and open to many readings, because by their nature they are vague. Unless handed down by a god or other powerful being, the prophecies most Northerners hear come from seers. As mortals, seers can only grasp at the complex weaving of [[The Norns|the Norns]], so they speak in vague terms rather than names, dates, and events. A seer's prophecies are called prophetic blessings or prophetic warnings.

***Greater Prophecies.*** Greater prophecies are found mostly in myths, legends, and tales, and usually come straight from the Norns, revealing great changes in a life or the world. They speak cryptically of fixed, unchangeable future events. In the North they are exceedingly rare, since the Norns seldom reveal their loom to mortals. Characters who visit a seer never receive one. They are entirely the GM's to give or withhold, and they should have a major impact on the story. They don't usually carry the benefits and consequences of prophetic readings, because fulfilling one changes nations rather than granting a bonus to one person.

### Why Use Prophecies?

Prophecies can serve many purposes. They can be the focus of an entire campaign, though sparingly, since the characters and not a cryptic omen should drive the story. They can deliver information the GM wants to release in-world. They can foreshadow events, giving players a wonderful "Oh! That's what 'Void Monarch' meant!" moment. They can be a through-line that gets a drifting campaign back on track. And they can drive the villains: the evil wizard hunting the relic of an ancient prophecy, or the horde of fanatics invading because their bloodthirsty god's prophecy says so. Above all, remember that a prophecy is not fate.

> [!note] Prophecies and Omens Are Vague
> An omen or prophecy must be open to different readings, a sign that means different things to different people, in hindsight, or as circumstances change.
>
> For the GM, vagueness keeps options open if the campaign changes. A specific prophecy becomes useless if a character dies unexpectedly. Instead of naming the trollkin druid, a good prophecy might speak of the "Child of Two Worlds" or "Seeker of Balance," titles a new dwarven cleric might also fit. If events unfold differently than expected, the prophecy wasn't wrong; the interpretation was.
>
> Some suggestions for creating omens and prophecies:
>
> - Couch prophecies in symbolism and mysteriously vague titles.
> - Make omens seem out of the ordinary but not immediately threatening.
> - Keep dates and locations vague or unclear.
> - Use [[Flyting and Kennings#Kennings|kennings]] or allusions rather than naming the exact creature, site, or phenomenon you plan to use later.
> - Use metaphors or pseudonyms instead of specific names (especially the characters' names).
> - Make sure prophecies can unfold in a few different ways, and that omens can be read in different ways.
> - Limit a prophecy's scope to a single event, not someone's whole life.
> - Use omens to foreshadow and to set the mood you want at the table.

## Prophetic Readings

Prophetic blessings and warnings are brief glimpses of the loom of fate, given to characters by seers or supernatural creatures.

- ***Prophetic Blessing.*** A prophecy suggesting an action in a certain kind of circumstance.
- ***Prophetic Warning.*** A prophecy warning the character to avoid a certain action in a certain kind of circumstance.

Each blessing or warning has three parts: a trigger, an action, and an outcome. The trigger is a short, cryptic circumstance or description the character can recognize in play. The action is what the character does in response. The outcome is the result of taking that action, used more often in greater prophecies but sometimes added for flavor. All three are deliberately vague so they can apply to many situations. When a player (or the GM) decides a trigger applies to something happening in the game, the character can act to fulfill the rest, gaining a magical benefit or avoiding a consequence.

***Overview.*** In general, it works like this:

- The character approaches a seer and asks a specific question.
- The player makes a prophecy roll to find the reading's power and whether it is a blessing or a warning.
- A blessing comes with a benefit, and a warning with a consequence, chosen by the player, rolled on a table, or chosen by the GM.
- The blessing or warning is generated from the tables below and contains at least one trigger.
- Later, the player or GM can decide the trigger applies to the current situation. For a blessing, the character can perform the described action and gain the benefit. For a warning, if the character acts in the forbidden way, they suffer the consequence.

## Creating a Prophecy

To create a greater prophecy, skip to Step 3.

### Step 1: Prophecy Roll

When a seer or other being gives a character a prophetic blessing or warning, the player makes a prophecy roll to find its power and whether it is a blessing or a warning: a d20 plus the character's [[Reputation|reputation]] modifier or half their PB, whichever is higher.

**Prophecy Roll**

| Roll Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 4 or less | Dire Warning |
| 5–7 | Greater Warning |
| 8–9 | Least Warning |
| 10 | GM’s Choice |
| 11–16 | Least Blessing |
| 17–19 | Greater Blessing |
| 20 or more | Legendary Blessing |

### Step 2: Benefits and Consequences

Next, determine the reading's benefit or consequence. Players can choose a benefit with the GM's approval, roll a d8 on the appropriate table, or let the GM choose. Consequences are best chosen by the GM or rolled randomly.

Each blessing or warning gives the character a game effect when the reading is fulfilled. They come in three levels, least, greater, and legendary or dire, each more powerful than the last. Each lists a duration, which the GM can change to fit the situation, and the GM can always extend or end an effect if that better serves the prophecy.

**Least Benefits and Consequences**

| d8 | Benefit | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add 1d6 to your next attack roll, or add extra force damage on your next damage roll. | You have disadvantage on your next attack roll. |
| 2 | As a reaction, halve the damage from an attack against you. | You have vulnerability to the damage type of the next successful attack against you. |
| 3 | Your AC increases by +2 until the start of your next turn. | Reduce your AC by 2 until the start of your next turn. |
| 4 | You ignore difficult terrain for 1 hour. | For 1 hour, every foot you travel in difficult terrain costs you 2 extra feet of movement (instead of 1). |
| 5 | You gain temporary HP equal to your PB plus your character level. | A nearby foe gains temporary HP equal to your PB plus your character level. |
| 6 | You cast *[[1st-Circle Spells#Shield\|shield]]* without expending a spell slot. | A nearby foe casts *shield* as a reaction. |
| 7 | You can immediately reroll your next failed skill check, but you must use the new result. | You must immediately reroll your next successful skill check and use the new result. |
| 8 | You have advantage on your next initiative check. | You have disadvantage on your next initiative check. |

**Greater Benefits and Consequences**

| d8 | Benefit | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | For 1 minute, you score a critical hit when you roll a natural 19 or 20. | A nearby foe has advantage on weapon attacks made against you for 1d4 rounds. |
| 2 | Add your PB as an additional bonus to any ability or skill check you make for 8 hours. | You lose your proficiency in all ability and skill checks for 8 hours. |
| 3 | You gain resistance to a damage type of your choice for 8 hours. | You have vulnerability to a damage type appropriate to the prophecy for 8 hours. |
| 4 | You can take the Disengage or Dodge action without using an action for 1d4 rounds. | For 1d4 rounds, if you use a bonus action on your turn, you can’t use a reaction until the start of your next turn. |
| 5 | For 1d4 rounds, you have the Evasion class feature, as per the rogue class. | A nearby foe recovers hit points equal to half its maximum HP. |
| 6 | For 1d4 rounds, as a bonus action, you cast *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Misty Step\|misty step]]* without expending a spell slot. | For 1d4 rounds, there is a 40 percent chance at the start of each creature’s turn that you magically teleport to a random, unoccupied space within 60 feet of you. |
| 7 | Choose INT, WIS, or CHA. You have advantage on all skill checks using that ability score for 8 hours. | Roll a d6 (1–2 INT, 3–4 WIS, 5–6 CHA). You have disadvantage on all skill checks using that ability score for 8 hours. |
| 8 | For 8 hours, monsters and NPCs never feel unwilling when you use the Influence action so long as they don’t begin with a hostile attitude. | For 8 hours, monsters and NPCs never feel willing when you use the Influence action. |

| 7 | For 24 hours, you can use the Help action as a bonus action. You can help other creatures from up to 30 feet away. | For 1 hour, if two enemies are within 5 feet of each other, and both are within 30 feet of you, they have advantage on attack rolls, saving throws, and skill checks. |
| 8 | You can choose one of the following: regain hit points equal to half your maximum hit points; regain a number of spell slots equal to your PB; or remove a condition, disease, or curse. | You suffer one of the following (chosen by the GM): lose half your current hit points; lose a number of spell slots equal to half your proficiency bonus (starting with the highest circle); or gain a condition, disease, or curse (GM’s choice) that can only be removed by powerful magic. |

### Step 3: Generating the Prophecy

The GM selects or rolls the trigger, the action, and optionally the outcome. The same tables can build a greater prophecy. Prophecies, especially blessings and warnings, should almost always begin with "When" or "If," and the trigger and action together should form a complete sentence.

***Trigger.*** Roll a d20 for each column of the **Trigger** table and combine the results into a vague description, with a little poetic license. For example, "Dry/Wet," "Guardian," "Bellows," and "Holy Place" might become "When the arid guardian bellows in the holy temple."

**Trigger**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fleeting | Shadow | Bellows | Ground |
| 2 | Brave | King | Tangles | Power |
| 3 | Dark | Light | Awakens | Holy Place |
| 4 | Hidden | Child | Beguiles | Rift |
| 5 | Dry/Wet | Beast | Moves | Wilderness |
| 6 | Forgotten | Overlord | Unravels | Wicked |
| 7 | Great | Statue | Kills | Path |
| 8 | Flickering | Hero | Fades | Day/Night |
| 9 | Cursed | Flame | Murmurs | Time |
| 10 | Crimson | Mirror | Shatters | Secret |
| 11 | Silent | Relic/Artifact | Unleashes | Weapon |
| 12 | Cold/Hot/Warm | Figure | Burns | Town/Village |
| 13 | Pure | Guardian | Injures | Cavern |
| 14 | Dead | Heart | Resists | Innocent |
| 15 | Fractured | Book | Summons | Malediction |
| 16 | Holy/Unholy | Serpent | Opens | Vault |
| 17 | Whispering | Storm | Illuminates | Allies/Foes |
| 18 | Shifting | Weapon | Distorts | Stone |

| 20 | Fallen | Rune | Rends | Reality |

***Action.*** Roll a d20 for each column of the **Action** table and combine the results into a vague description, rearranging or adding words as needed. For example, "Run," "Relic," "Behind," and "Concealment" might become "Run from behind the concealed relic," with "from" added for clarity.

If the reading is a warning, the action must be phrased as something to avoid. For example, "Hold," "Relic," "Inside," and "Light" might become "Don't hold the relic inside the light." The GM decides when a trigger is active and might not tell the players.

**Action**

| d20 | Verb | Subject | Preposition | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Strike | Rune/Glyph | Under | Concealment |
| 2 | Ignite | Mountain | Into | Wind |
| 3 | Push | Oath | To | Moon |
| 4 | Challenge | Weapon | Past | Blood |
| 5 | Charge | Courage | For | Steel |
| 6 | Seize | Beast | By | Gap |
| 7 | Halt | Water/Liquid | Near | Damage Type (cold, fire, etc.) |
| 8 | Run | Relic | Across | Shadow |
| 9 | Destroy | Ally | Upon | Honor |
| 10 | Charm | Site | With | Depth/Abyss |
| 11 | Betray | Foe | Out | Love |
| 12 | Command | Stone | Amidst | Elemental Power |
| 13 | Break | Speed | As | Vengeance |
| 14 | Study | Stranger | Through | Quiet |
| 15 | Rush | Treasure | Inside | Ground |
| 16 | Hold | Magic | Off | Light |
| 17 | Guard | Victim | Toward | Magic |
| 18 | Follow | Door/Doorway | Behind | Tide |
| 19 | Cast | Air | On | North/South/East/West |
| 20 | Bind | Friendship | Over | Gods |

***Outcomes.*** An outcome is a change to the world, for good or ill, that may happen when a greater prophecy is fulfilled. Blessings and warnings don't usually have one, since the benefit or consequence fills that role. If the GM adds one to a reading, it should begin with "to," such as "To derive new knowledge . . . ," and suit the benefit or consequence; "the gods bestow a blessing" might go with attacks having disadvantage against you.

To create an outcome, choose a column (or roll a d4) and roll a d20 in it, then reword the result to fit the trigger and action and form a complete sentence. Usually a prophecy has one outcome, though a particularly momentous one might have two, and greater prophecies always have one. Phrase it as something that hasn't happened yet, to fit the opening "When" or "If," using words like "will," "shall," and "lest." For example, "Sleeping god awakens" gives a prophecy that, if triggered, will wake a sleeping god.

**Outcome**

| d20 | Individual Change | Societal Change | Environmental Change | Material Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Someone imprisoned is freed | Evil vanquishes good | Unnaturally long winter | An artifact becomes cursed |
| 2 | Songs are sung about the hero | Sleeping god awakens | Bountiful harvests | Riches found in the hills |
| 3 | A curse falls upon someone | Civilization falls | Earthquakes shake the land | Recovery of the stolen treasure |
| 4 | Inherit a powerful legacy | Cults rise up across the land | Dawn pierces the night | Wellspring of power blooms |
| 5 | Unlikely love blooms | An ancient foe returns | Stars fall from the sky | Crafting a powerful artifact |
| 6 | Falls to inner demons | Hope is restored | A new land is found | Deriving new knowledge |
| 7 | A great beast emerges | Interplanar invasion | Magic becomes unpredictable | A crown heralds the new heir |
| 8 | Hero becomes a symbol | Good rises to defeat evil | Disaster strikes a distant land | A potent relic is shattered |
| 9 | Faith is rekindled | A city rises from the ashes | Spirits inhabit the forest | Wealth of the land is restored |
| 10 | An old enemy returns | The sickness is cured | A new moon rises | Forgotten vault found |
| 11 | Unexpected allies arrive to aid | Ragnarok or the end times | Land is covered in shadow | Fantastic vessel lands nearby |
| 12 | Vengeance is sworn | A charismatic ruler is crowned | Fey summer lasts twice as long | Fell lore is lost |
| 13 | The great foe falls | Balance returned to the land | Flora and fauna turn deadly | Wealth is discovered |
| 14 | Rescue a loved one | People rise against an evil king | Portals open | A sacred relic is stolen |
| 15 | Overcomes inner obstacles | A new belief spreads | Nature’s power is restored | A castle crumbles |
| 16 | The gods bestow a blessing | The dead return | A volcano erupts | Trade resumes between allies |
| 17 | Enemies become allies | Alliance against the darkness | Gods grant healing rains | A cloak bridges two people |
| 18 | Lost relations are found | A forbidden rite is unearthed | Oceans rise and flood the land | Charts to distant lands found |
| 19 | Old friends reunited | Plague or sickness | Eclipse or planetary alignment | Guilds become powerful |
| 20 | Allies are betrayed | The people flock to a new god | Barren lands become fertile | A treasured possession becomes a relic |

*The wording of these outcomes should be changed to fit each prophecy.*

***Final Prophecy.*** Together, the trigger, action, and benefit or consequence (or outcome) form a complete sentence. From the examples above, a prophetic reading with a greater blessing might be: "When the arid destroyer bellows in the holy temple, run from behind the concealed relic to gain [[Volund]]'s protection" (resistance to fire damage). A greater prophecy might be: "When the arid destroyer bellows in the holy temple, run from behind the concealed relic, and the sleeping god will awaken."

## Sample Prophetic Blessings and Warnings

These samples show how players can read triggers in different ways. Some of their elements aren't on the tables, to show how GMs can invent their own.

### Least Prophetic Blessing

*When the storm casts its shadow, seek shelter within the ancient to sip from Mimir's well.*

***Benefit.*** If you do so, you gain advantage on INT- and WIS-based ability checks for 1 hour.

***Possible Interpretations.***

- The shadow of the storm could be a literal storm overhead, but it could also be a creature or situation. A giant's fury could be called a "storm," so the giant's shadow might be the trigger.
- Moving into an old structure could be "seeking shelter within the ancient," but "ancient" could also mean a mountain, so stepping into a cave would count. So could standing beneath the branches of a mighty tree.

### Legendary Prophetic Blessing

*When the stars hide their faces, quench the wound-brand to strike like Sleipnir.*

***Benefit.*** If you do so, once in the next 24 hours you can use an action to move up to twice your speed and make a melee attack. This movement doesn't provoke opportunity attacks. If you hit a creature, it is pushed 10 feet away from you and has the prone condition.

***Possible Interpretations.***

- Stars hiding their faces could be a kenning for darkness, or more literally, people looking away from a situation.
- The obvious meaning of "quenching the wound-brand" is putting out something on fire. But wound-brand could be a kenning for a sword, and quenching is a smithing term for plunging hot metal into water, so a player might simply stick their weapon into water.

### Greater Prophetic Warning

*When the light speaks in screams, don't answer the call with wyrd-craft, or suffer [[Thor]]'s fate.*

***Consequence.*** If you cast a spell before the end of your next turn, you have the poisoned condition for 1d4 rounds. While poisoned this way, your thoughts are muddled, and you can cast only cantrips.

***Possible Interpretations.***

- The light speaking in screams could be something like a *fireball*, bright and causing screams of pain, or a glowing foe who is yelling.
- The warning against answering with wyrd-craft plainly means spellcasting, but a player could decide it means an enchanted weapon, so the warning would be not to attack the "screaming light."

### Dire Prophetic Warning

*When an ancient roar fractures the land, don't spill the dew of slaughter, or your hand will betray your allies.*

***Consequence.*** The first time you deal damage with an attack in the next 8 hours, you are cursed for 1 minute. While cursed, roll a d6 at the start of each of your turns. On a 1 or 2, you must move and make a melee attack against a random creature. If you can't reach one, you can instead take the Dash action toward a random creature.

***Possible Interpretations.***

- An ancient roar fracturing the land could be an earthquake, a yelling giant striking the ground with her club, or an erupting volcano.
- "Don't spill the dew" could mean don't spill a nearby container. But "dew of slaughter" is a kenning for blood, so it could also warn against spilling blood, your own or (more likely here) a foe's.

## Sample Greater Prophecy

GMs can roll several times on the tables to build longer prophecies, like this one:

*When a great king withholds secrets, and the statue of the brave awakens in the village square, bind well the rune-carved blade with blessed steel, or the forbidden rite shall unearth the sleeping god. Should this come to pass, hope can yet be restored if the fractured binding is cast anew with bloody stone.*

***Construction.*** This prophecy follows this format, with a few words added for clarity:

- Trigger (great king withholds secrets)
- Trigger (statue awakes in village)
- Action (bind blade with steel)
- Outcome (forbidden rite awakens a god)
- Outcome (hope is restored)
- Trigger (fractured binding cast anew)
- Action (with bloody stone)

## Omens

Small signs, portents, and sightings work hand in hand with prophecies to foreshadow events. They tell less than a prophecy but add flavor, mystery, and tension. The right balance differs for every game and table: too many omens blunt their impact, and too few leave the table without the tone the GM wants.

The tables below give three kinds of omen: hopeful, foreboding, and mysterious. GMs can use them as a base and invent their own. Usually the GM chooses the kind of omen and adapts it to the campaign, but for a random result, roll a d4 on the **Omen Types** table.

**Omen Types**

| d4 | Type |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hopeful |
| 2 | Foreboding |
| 3 | Mysterious |
| 4 | GM’s Choice |

|---|---|
| 1 | A bear with pure white fur stands upon a rise, staring up at the stars before wandering away. |
| 2 | The sun, warm and bright, pierces through the overcast skies to illuminate the area around you. |
| 3 | Two ravens wait until you notice them on a nearby perch before croaking and flying away. |
| 4 | A grey wolf appears for a moment, seems to nod to you, then withdraws into shadow. |
| 5 | The sparks rising from the fire twist into familiar runes before scattering into the night. |
| 6 | Nine hand-sized stones form a perfect circle ahead of you. |
| 7 | A single flower blooms amidst the snow, a splash of color against the white. |
| 8 | For a moment, you hear the clicking of the weaver’s loom in the wind. |
| 9 | A stranger in a tattered cloak stands in the shadows watching you with a single gleaming eye. |
| 10 | You find a small stone with a pattern not unlike a rune scratched upon it by an unknown hand. |
| 11 | A bright, fiery light burns itself across the sky, heading north. |
