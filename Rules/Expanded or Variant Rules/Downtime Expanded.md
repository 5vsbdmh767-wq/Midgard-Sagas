This section introduces additional downtime activity options for PCs. See the *Player’s Guide for the general* *downtime rules.*

## Creature Training

You can spend downtime bonding with and training a creature in your care. This activity requires access to a properly attended stable or similar facility, specifically built to house and train Beasts (and creatures with the Animal tag). Only a mature creature stabled in an adequate facility can receive the benefits of this activity. A PC can use this activity to train additional creature types (GM’s discretion), but stabling and working with such creatures might incur additional difficulties and expenses. For example, a GM might allow a PC to train a blink dog or pseudodragon but stabling such unique or intelligent creatures might add complications, needs, and expenses, such as anti-teleportation wards for the blink dog or a small library to keep the pseudodragon entertained.

***Time.*** The creature training activity is measured in weeks of downtime. Performing this activity requires a PC to spend a minimum of 1 week of downtime to train, handle, and personally care for a creature.

***Cost.*** Creature training requires access to a creature and a stabling facility equipped to care for the creature. Consult the **Creature Care Expenses** table to determine the weekly cost of feeding and stabling a creature in your care. Note that due to the potential special nature and needs of creatures with the Animal tag, they always cost more to feed and stable than standard Beasts.

***Resolution.*** At the end of each week spent engaging in creature training, a PC makes a WIS (Animal Handling) check, or a similarly relevant ability check to determine if they can attempt an additional training check. The result also determines how difficult a training check is, as shown on the **Creature Training Resolution table and**

**described in the Training Check section.**

### Training Check

Meeting a creature’s basic needs is relatively simple, but training a creature requires significantly more time and effort. This investment is represented by a training check. A PC must first succeed on their creature training resolution check before they can even attempt a training check, and multiple weeks performing the creature training increase the likelihood of succeeding on a successful training check.

***Training DC.*** Every creature has a starting training DC, which represents how receptive it is to training. A creature’s base training DC is equal to 10 + the creature’s highest ability score modifier. The creature’s type, CR, or intelligence might increase or decrease its starting DC by up to 3 (GM’s discretion). For example, a highly intelligent creature, like an ape, might easily pick up on training cues (potentially decreasing the DC) while attempts to train a naturally stubborn creature like an owlbear might prove more difficult (potentially increasing the DC). Note that repeated creature training resolution attempts can permanently raise or lower this DC, making an individual creature’s DC potentially different from other creatures of its kind.

***Training Check.*** Once you’ve determined a creature’s starting training DC and succeeded on your creature training resolution check, you can attempt a training check. Make a WIS (Animal Handling) check against the creature’s current training DC. On a success, the creature is successfully trained, and it gains one benefit of your choice from the **Creature Training Benefits** table. While a single creature can receive multiple training benefits, it can only receive a particular benefit once (unless stated otherwise). A particular creature can have a maximum number of training benefits equal to its PB (minimum of two benefits) at any one time.

## Criminal Enterprise

If allowed by the GM, you can spend downtime engaging in criminal enterprise—a sophisticated variety of crime that is organized, complex, and requires the combined talents of many individuals. Running a successful criminal enterprise requires gathering information, forging connections with criminal contacts, and ensuring the necessary wheels of business stay greased.

This activity requires access to a thieves’ guild or similarly well-organized criminal network. Work with your GM to determine what organizations of this type exist in the world of your game and how the PCs can get involved in their particular brand of illicit business. In most cases, arranging an introduction with a criminal organization requires you to use some combination of earned contacts and favors (see carousing downtime activity in *Player’s Guide*).

> [!note] What Is a Thieves’ Guild?
> Thieves’ guilds are clandestine organizations of criminals commonly found in fantasy worlds, typically in large cities also possessing a variety of government-sanctioned trade guilds. Like standard guilds, a thieves’ guild offers benefits like training, support, and protection to its members in exchange for a share of their profits. While members of such a guild operate outside the confines of local law, they are strictly bound to follow the unique rules codified by the guild, which it strictly enforces to maintain secrecy and prevent individuals from getting too greedy. Petty thieves operating in settlements under the dominion of prolific and powerful thieves’ guilds are pressured to join their ranks or face the consequences— which might very well mean a knife in the back when next you turn down a dark alley.
>

A major component of this activity is engaging in criminal operations. The nature of these operations depends on the type of crimes performed by your criminal network, but common examples include pulling off a heist, smuggling cargo, breaking someone out of prison, or assassinating a prominent individual. Whatever the specifics, you must have your GM’s approval before you attempt such an operation to ensure it falls within the bounds of the story and the overall themes and restrictions agreed upon by all the players at the table (see **Safety Tools** in the *Player’s Guide).*

***Time.*** The criminal enterprise activity is measured in weeks of downtime. It requires a PC to spend a minimum of 1 week of downtime for the planning and actioning of a single criminal operation.

***Cost.*** Criminal enterprise requires a PC to spend 50 gp per week to cover expenses, representing cuts paid to criminal partners, bribes for information, and the purchase of specialized gear and required services. Only one PC is required to pay, but if all of the PCs participating in the same operation each pay the full weekly cost, then the week’s resolution roll is at advantage.

***Resolution.*** At the end of each week spent engaging in criminal enterprise, a PC makes an ability check to determine the results of the criminal operation, which are measured in Infamy points, as shown on the **Criminal Enterprise Resolution** table. There is only a single resolution roll per week for a given operation, and the following ability checks are recommended for it, depending on the role of the PC making the roll:

- **The Brain.** The brain is the mastermind behind the operation. They are rarely present when the operation goes down but do the heavy lifting on logistical planning, often bearing the upfront costs of each job. If you are filling this roll, use INT (Investigation) to make your criminal operation resolution roll.

- **The Face.** The face is the inside agent that uses charm to their advantage. They gather valuable information, deploy resources, and keep marks distracted while the job goes down. If you are filling this roll, use CHA (Deception) to make your criminal operation resolution roll.

- **The Lookout.** The lookout is the eyes and ears of the operation. They stand guard while the rest of the team completes the job. If you are filling this roll, use WIS (Perception) to make your criminal operation resolution roll.

- **The Muscle.** The muscle is the bruiser that backs up the team. They are the team member that starts swinging if the operation goes sideways. If you are filling this roll, use STR (Athletics) to make your criminal operation resolution roll.

- **The Talent.** The talent is a catchall term for the ones who get their hands dirty during the operation, such as the burglar, lockpick, pickpocket, or assassin. They are the team member that quietly slips past whatever bars access to the operation’s target. If you are filling this roll, use DEX (Stealth) to make your criminal operation resolution roll.

*Criminal Enterprise Resolution*

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | The operation was a disaster. You lose 1 Infamy. If you have no Infamy when you roll this result, you have disadvantage on the next criminal enterprise resolution roll you make. |
| 6–10 | The operation fell apart. No one got caught, but no one got paid. You gain no Infamy this week. |
| 11–15 | You hit a few snags but pulled off the operation in the end. You gain 1 Infamy. |
| 16–20 | The operation was a success. Gain 2 Infamy. |
| 21+ | You pulled off the perfect crime. Gain 3 Infamy. |

### Using Infamy

Infamy represents a PC’s successful crime streak, respect, and influence within their associated criminal network. You can spend Infamy immediately upon receiving it or bank it to spend at a later time. Infamy can be spent in the following ways.

***Acquire Contact (Costs 1 Infamy).*** You can spend 1 Infamy to make a new criminal contact. Each new contact automatically owes the PC one favor. For more information on contacts, favors, and their uses, see carousing downtime activity in the Player’s Guide.

***Get Paid (Costs 1 or More Infamy).*** You can spend 1 Infamy to fence stolen goods or otherwise collect your payout for successfully completing criminal operations. You can choose to spend one or more Infamy in this way, and when you do, you receive 250 gp × the number of Infamy spent. You must be in a populated settlement or in a location otherwise populated by one or more criminal networks to spend Infamy in this way.

***Legendary Caper (Costs 5 Infamy).*** You can spend 5 Infamy to pull off a legendary caper, provided you haven’t performed a legendary caper in the last year. A legendary caper is the crown jewel of operations, and you receive a significant payout from it: choose either to receive 10,000 gp or a single uncommon or rarer magic item (GM’s discretion).
