*Optional, from Player's Guide 2.*

Experience and treasure are fine rewards for valiant heroes, but there is a plethora of additional ways to reward PCs for a job well done. While it is important for a GM to incorporate a hearty mix of reward types in their game planning, it is equally important that all players advocate for the reward types they are most interested in receiving over the course of their adventures. For additional guidance on incorporating rewards in your games, see the *Game Master’s Guide.*

The following sections cover unique new rewards to offer in addition to experience and treasure.

## Downtime

Rewarding periods of downtime is an excellent way to signal the end of a major adventure or story arc. Downtime is essentially free time that allows PCs to take a breather and pursue more mundane interests when the action is light. Periods of downtime are also critical for building, expanding, and enjoying the benefits of the base building rules presented in this chapter. Since most downtime activities require at least a full week of downtime to make progress, handing out downtime rewards of one or more weeks is recommended, as opposed to handing out single days. For more information on how downtime works and a selection of downtime activities (in addition to those presented in this chapter), see the *Player’s Guide*. For additional optional downtime activities, see the *Game* *Master’s Guide.*

## Luck

Luck is a unique fail-forward resource that PCs can generate on their own, so it isn’t strictly necessary for GMs to hand out. However, rewarding Luck is an easy and immediate way to highlight great roleplaying or other forms of player participation. Giving every PC 1 Luck immediately after surviving a difficult encounter is a special way to reward them during gameplay. PCs should also make sure to spend accumulated Luck regularly, instead of hording it. Using Luck regularly ensures the GM will feel more comfortable handing it out.

## Mystic Gifts

Mystic gifts are magical bestowments that can temporarily or permanently alter a PC’s statistics or grant special features to use. These mystic gifts come in two varieties. The lesser, called charms, offer limited powers that confer spells or special features when used. Greater than these are blessings, providing more wondrous, lasting changes to worthy recipients.

If a charm or blessing allows you to cast a spell, you can do so even if you can’t normally cast spells or can’t normally access the spell’s associated source. Spells cast via charms or blessings are always cast at the lowest possible spell circle, and they neither expend any of your spell slots nor require any components. If a spell cast from a charm or blessing requires concentration, you must still concentrate on it, following normal concentration rules (unless stated otherwise). Any deviations to a spell’s casting time or duration are listed in the charm or blessing description but otherwise follow the standard requirements listed in the spell’s description. If a spellcasting ability is required, it will either be set by the GM when the charm or blessing is bestowed or it will be your choice (GM’s discretion).

### Charms

Charms are limited in use and most commonly replicate the effects of potions or spells. Each charm explains how long it remains once granted, after which it disappears, removing your access to it. Otherwise, only divine intervention or the *[[9th-Circle Spells#Wish|wish]]* spell (see *Player’s Guide*) can remove a charm from a creature.

Use the charms listed here (see *Player’s Guide* for all listed spells), or simply use them as examples when creating new ones better tailored to the story and setting of your game:

- **Charm of Abjuring.** This charm has 3 charges. As an action, you can expend 1 charge to cast *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]*. Alternatively, if you would be affected by a curse effect or otherwise cursed against your will, it expends 1 charge and instantly triggers, preventing you from being affected. Once all its charges have been expended, the charm disappears.

- **Charm of Elemental Warding.** This charm has 3 charges. As an action, you can expend 1 charge to cast the *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Protection from Energy|protection from energy]]* spell on yourself. When you cast the spell in this way, you don’t need to concentrate, and it stays active for the full duration. Once all its charges have been expended, the charm disappears.

- **Charm of Ethereal Visitation.** This charm has 6 charges. As an action, you can expend one of the following spells at its lowest circle: *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Blink|blink]]* (3 charges) or *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Misty Step|misty step]]* (1 charge). Once all its charges have been expended, the charm disappears.

- **Charm of Second Chances.** This charm grants you the benefit of the *[[4th-Circle Spells#Death Ward|death ward]]* spell. Once the spell is triggered, the charm disappears.

- **Charm of Swiftness.** This charm grants you the benefits of the *[[1st-Circle Spells#Longstrider|longstrider]]* spell. In addition, you have advantage on DEX (Acrobatics) checks. These benefits last for 10 days, after which the charm disappears.

- **Charm of the Labyrinth.** This charm has 2 charges. As an action, you can expend 1 charge to cast the *plane* *shift* spell. Once all its charges have been expended, the charm disappears.

- **Charm of Wild Speech.** This charm grants you the benefits of the *[[1st-Circle Spells#Speak with Animals|speak with animals]]* spell. In addition, you have advantage on CHA (Persuasion) checks made to influence Beasts. These benefits last for 10 days, after which the charm disappears.

### Blessings

A blessing is a potent magical effect that grants a permanent benefit to a PC and most commonly replicates the effects of a wondrous magic item. The entity that bestowed the blessing can choose to revoke it, but otherwise, nothing short of divine intervention or a *[[9th-Circle Spells#Wish|wish]]* spell *(see Player’s Guide)* can remove it.

A PC can only have a number of blessings equal to their PB at any one time—though it is unlikely for a single person to ever receive more than one blessing in a single lifetime. Additionally, a PC can’t benefit from the same blessing twice.

Use the blessings listed here, or simply use them as examples when creating new ones better tailored to the story and setting of your game:

- **Blessing of Battle Foresight.** You add your WIS modifier to Initiative checks.

- **Blessing of Noble Felicity.** You immediately gain 3 Luck, and the maximum amount of Luck you can have increases to 8.

- **Blessing of Primal Vigor.** You gain a number of additional hit dice equal to your PB. Whenever your PB increases, you gain an additional hit die.

- **Blessing of Relentless Soul.** You add your PB to your checks when you make death saves.

- **Blessing of Sound Mind.** Your INT, WIS, or CHA (GM’s choice) score increases by 2, up to a maximum of 22.

- **Blessing of Strong Body.** Your STR, DEX, or CON (GM’s choice) score increases by 2, up to a maximum of 22.

- **Blessing of Turnblade Resilience.** You have resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical attacks.

## Prestige Awards

Prestige awards provide narrative benefits to help further stories and enhance social standing. Letters of recommendation, medals of commendation, and titles can grant PCs social recognition that can open doors and curry favor with important NPCs or factions.

The following rewards are typically offered in addition to more standard treasure. However, any of the following might stand alone or serve as the impetus for a quest or adventure arc.

### Estate Ownership

Parcels of land, borderland keeps, and recently vacated or repossessed estates are frequent rewards gifted by royalty and by political leaders. A well-appointed vassal is presumably a loyal one, though the gift of estate or property ownership is often a mixed blessing. While many can only dream of owning a large swath of land or a quaint mountain chateau, the reality is that the government or sovereign who presides over the domain will inevitably seek tithes or service from the owners of the property. Failure to capitulate when called upon in this manner could mean revocation of the property.

***Inheritances.*** Death is inevitable, and you can’t take what you own to the afterlife. Those who see their ends coming, whether in old age, by divination, or otherwise, might have the forethought to write a will of inheritance to grant their estate to their next of kin or a beneficiary. Many a classic story and adventure starts with an unexpectedly inherited windfall. Exploring an eccentric late relative’s enigmatic household can make for an entertaining—and devious—mystery.

***Parcels of Land.*** A gift of open land is frequently given to knights, court mages, and persons who have distinguished themselves through toil and labor. Sometimes the parcel of land will be entirely uninhabited and without limitation for what to build on it. In other instances, the land might already be occupied by commoners, farms, and such. Either way, the newly appointed owner is expected to preside over and protect the land and its inhabitants.

***Strongholds.*** A stronghold, castle, keep, outpost, tower, or some other fortified building makes for an ideal base both for mercenaries and for heroes with patrons. A stronghold might come fully furnished and staffed, or it might be in disrepair, requiring new management and upkeep. In either case, an appointee to a stronghold is expected to maintain its battle readiness, patrol the surrounding realm, and defend itself and neighboring communities. If the PCs are gifted with a stronghold, you can use the base building rules in this chapter to construct a base from scratch or to model a base that reflects the stronghold’s current condition.

### Letters of Recommendation

Letters of recommendation are formal documents that ease complications during travel and when interacting with high society—the same way gold greases palms for the general masses. A signed letter in a sealed folio, tube, or envelope and marked with the signet of someone highly respected can grant the PCs access to places and persons they would usually be turned away from. A letter of recommendation from someone whose reputation has been damaged, however, can have an opposite and endangering effect, complicating an already tense situation.

Here are a few alternatives to a letter of recommendation that still serve a similar purpose.

***Decrees of Clemency.*** A decree of clemency exonerates the convicted from all accused crimes. Such a document is an act of mercy from a lenient governing body or sovereign. Ironically, many career criminals would kill to get their hands on such a legally protective document.

***Letters of Marque.*** This type of formal document is a signed license that absolves the listed persons from crimes of piracy and formally enlists them as privateers in employ to the governing body or sovereign who drafted the document. It is a highly desirable document for a ne’er-do-well to have. Forgeries are common, especially along coastlines where criminals look for any excuse to evade the law. Masters of disguise will sometimes carry on long deceptions, posing as their peers, in order to operate under the protection of a letter of marque—which just gets messy once the questions start building.

***Royal Writ.*** This alternative to a letter of recommendation is a formal order drafted on behalf of and signed by a sovereign. These hold great sway over the lawful and dutybound but incur derision and animosity from those who do not recognize such high authority.

### Medals of Commendation

Medals are awarded to deserving PCs for noble deeds, for exceptional heroism in battle, and for self-sacrifice on behalf of the nation. Someone who possesses and displays an earned medal of commendation makes a statement that garners notice and respect. Unless a medal is also a magic item, it doesn’t confer any special benefit to a PC, though it can affect how everyone else responds to them and open new directions for social encounters to go in.

Different medals symbolize different things and typically incorporate a variety of iconography, coloration, and metals and other materials that are significant to both the task and to the political or royal personage who awarded them. Some examples are presented here.

***Medals for Self-Sacrifice.*** These medals are meant to console the bereaved after their loved ones perished while defending their countries and political leaders. With resurrection and reincarnation magic possible, sometimes the owner of the medal returns alive and well to receive the honor. This can cause all manner of legal confusion regarding identity. These medals are usually cast in silver with red, purple, or black fabric accents, and they often depict hearts, religious symbolism, flowers, and angels.

***Medals for Valiant Service.*** These medals commend free agents, such as adventuring heroes, for their defense of nations and regions from supernatural forces without commission. Saving a community from an undead army and a goblin horde, slaying a dragon, or quelling a volcanic eruption are all worthy deeds, deserving of one of these medals. Typically, these medals are cast in gold with fabric accents in blue or the colors of the awarding nation, and they often depict the national animal or symbol.

***Medals for Valor.*** These medals commend conscripted and commissioned soldiers and officers who perform their duties with distinction. Whether just barely surviving an unfortunate onslaught or leading a successful siege upon a neighboring nation, these medals aim to recognize soldiers for their bravery. However, not everyone who receives such a medal regards it with the same esteem as those outside of service might. These medals are typically cast in silver, pewter, or bronze with blue or green fabric accents or the awarding nation’s flag colors, and they often depict lions, eagles, crossed blades, stars, and other nationally recognized symbols.

### Titles

Often accompanying estate ownership and occasionally medals of commendation, titles are honorifics that declare a high social standing. Governments and sovereigns have the power to appoint titles as well as to strip them, if the receiver later proves unworthy. A PC can hold multiple titles, and in fact, many nobles and members of royal families do, according to tradition and matters of inheritance.

Occasionally kings, queens, and emperors are chosen by the people or, even more rarely, by higher powers of divine origin. Such titles carry with them a solemn and heavy station, and the stakes are great in meeting the expectations of the masses and the assaults of opposing forces.

***Academic Titles.*** Archmage, barrister, chancellor, doctor, magister, master, and professor are all titles utilized by collegiums and universities, reflecting the degree of study and commitment required. Many guilds adopt their own titles for members who hold seats of authority.

***Heroic Titles.*** While not governmentally recognized, heroes often complete valiant deeds worthy of naming that the common masses love to exalt, like Dame Juliene the Brave or Ameliorax the Fiery. The best way to earn a heroic title and make it stick is to have a bard witness your deeds and sing your praises until you are famous. It is important that you know who to trust, however, when it comes to bearing a heroic title. A malicious or inept bard could saddle you forever with a name that inspires derision rather than awe, like Brandall the Craven.

***Noble Titles.*** These titles are appointed when a PC rises in social standing, either through knighthood or birth or by marriage into a noble or royal family. Typical European-centric noble title peerages might be structured from least to greatest as in the **Noble Titles** table.

***Religious Titles.*** Churches and religious institutions vary widely as do the nomenclature they use to award and anoint their fellows. Many religious hierarchies appointed their low priests with reverend, cantor, or brother/sister before ascending the ranks.
