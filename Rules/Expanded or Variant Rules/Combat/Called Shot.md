When a creature wants to attack a specific location on another creature, you can apply the called shot rule to the situation. Only an attack can be a called shot, and a spell can’t be a called shot unless the spell requires an attack roll.

When a creature makes an attack a called shot, the creature designates a specific location on the target’s body, dealing extra damage if the attack hits. The location must be a small, vulnerable section of the target’s body and can’t be a section containing the bulk of the target’s body mass. For example, a creature can make a called shot against another creature’s eyes or limbs but not its torso.

To make a called shot, the creature makes an attack roll as normal, but the target gains a bonus to its AC based on the type of location being targeted: +2 for a limb, +3 for a head, +4 for eyes or a specific organ. At the GM’s discretion, this AC bonus might be higher or lower, depending on the specific location being targeted. If the creature hits this new AC, the attack deals extra damage equal to twice the creature’s PB. If the creature fails to hit this new AC, the attack misses, even if the attack roll would ordinarily hit the target’s standard AC. If the attack is a critical hit, the creature rolls damage dice three times, instead of twice. For an even more dynamic combat encounter, consider using the optional injuries rule (see **[[Injuries]]**) alongside the called shot rule. If combining these two optional rules, the attacker can choose an injury based on the called shot’s location instead of dealing extra damage.
