%% house rule: Bryant's rulings, 22 Nov 2026. Wording is mine. %%

The price on a magic item's page is its list price: what it sells for in an ordinary market, with normal supply and normal demand. What you actually pay depends on where you are and who is selling.

## Supply and Demand

Magic items use the same market rules as trade goods. Roll or set the local supply and demand from [[Trade Goods & Money#Market Prices|Market Prices]] and apply the modifier to the list price. A magic item that is scarce in town can cost twice its list price. In a glutted market it can go for much less.

Magic items skip the Seller Type table. The list price is already a merchant's price, so a shopkeeper doesn't add a markup on top of it.

How often an item turns up, how much gold a settlement can handle, bargaining and the black market all work as described in [[Shopping & Markets]].

## Cursed Items

A cursed item's list price is half the price of whatever it pretends to be. A seller who knows about the curse wants it gone and won't say why. A price that seems too good is the warning sign.

Once a curse is known, only the [[Shopping & Markets#The Black Market|black market]] will buy the item, at 25% of its list price.

## Regional Prices

Some places in Midgard have far more magic than others. In the [[Magocracy of Allain]] and in [[Nuria Natal]], magic is everyday, and magic items cost 10% less than their list price. Elsewhere, use the list price.
