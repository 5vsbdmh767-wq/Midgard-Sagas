*Optional, from Northlands Worldbook.*

These naval combat rules from the *Northlands Worldbook* are meant to make the ship a dynamic, exciting part of any fight. They cover much the same ground as the other [[Nautical Rules]] pages, written for the [[Vehicles#Longships|longships]] of the [[Northlands]]; use whichever set your table prefers.

## Ship Siege Weapons

In the real-world cultures that inspired the Northlands, longships didn't usually carry siege weapons; their warriors fought with the melee and ranged weapons they could carry. Depending on your setting, ballistae or mangonels can be loaded onto a longship if they suit your style of play, and the longship stat blocks include them. Remove them if they don't make sense for your game.

A ship-mounted weapon's attack bonus equals the ship's STR or DEX modifier, whichever is higher, and so does its damage bonus. A character firing a ship-mounted weapon can use their own ranged weapon attack and damage bonus instead, using the higher of their STR or DEX.

***Ballista.*** A massive crossbow that fires weighted bolts. The crew can load and aim a ship-mounted ballista with the Operate Ship Weapon action.

***Naval Ram.*** A metal and wood ram mounted on the ship's prow at or below the waterline, used to smash a hole in another vessel's side. If the ship moves more than 30 feet first, a ram deals an extra 1d10 piercing damage.

***Ship Catapult.*** A small ship-mounted catapult that throws stones at enemy vessels. Loading and aiming it takes a Utilize action, and firing it takes another. Several crew can work the same weapon to do these in the same round.

## Movement (Sail and Rowing)

Use the vehicle's speed. If you use a ship from another source that lists only miles per hour, a handy approximation of its speed in feet per round is its miles per hour times 10, assuming someone is piloting it. A galley moving at 4 mph in average wind, for example, moves 40 feet per round. On a grid, round to the nearest 5 feet. A longship's listed speed covers both sailing and rowing.

### Changing Direction

Sailing ships don't turn quickly. A ship changing direction without taking a shipboard action moves the first half of its speed in the direction it faces, turns 45°, and moves the second half. A turning galley, for example, moves 20 feet straight ahead and then 20 feet on a 45° diagonal.

### Direction of Wind and Drifting

Wind direction matters greatly in sailing, deciding whether a ship can sail straight or must tack back and forth. These rules simplify it by assuming the crew is tacking and adjusting the sails as needed. A ship no one controls drifts with the wind at the start of each of its turns: 5 feet in light wind, 10 feet in average wind, and 15 feet in strong wind. A ship being rowed generally ignores wind and drift, except in storms.

### Turning Into the Wind

Ships never want to sail straight into the wind. A vessel that turns into the wind and moves that way has its speed halved. If its speed is greater than 0 feet while moving into the wind, the crew member at the helm must succeed on a DC 12 STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check to turn the ship. If they fail, or the ship's speed was reduced to 0 feet, it drifts with the wind and is stuck until the wind changes.

### Wind Speed

Travel distances assume average wind, which over days is likely. In a combat round it needn't be. With no wind, sailing ships don't move; in light wind, halve the ship's speed; in strong wind, double it. Longships can also be rowed.

## Sea Weather

At sea in a fantasy world, strange weather joins the ordinary changes of wind and visibility. Use the **Wind** and **Precipitation** tables to set conditions. The movement effects assume a sail-powered ship; for a rowed ship or one with some other power, an effect might not apply.

| 7–14 | Average | Sail-powered ships move at normal speed. |
| 15–19 | Strong | Sail-powered ships move at double speed. |
| 20 | Unusual | Roll on the **Unusual Weather** table. Average wind unless the entry says otherwise. |

## Naval Combat

A ship-to-ship battle can be a thrilling part of an adventure. Without these rules, GMs can run a ship encounter like any other combat, with every ship maneuvering as the players or GM wish at the end of each round. That is certainly the quickest way. For more involved ship encounters, use the optional rules below.

### Crew Member Stat Blocks

The average sailor has the statistics of a bandit or pirate. An officer could be a pirate or a viking, and the captain a wolf reaver dwarf or pirate captain.

### Initiative

Ships move and act last in initiative, and their crews are assumed to do their actions. Player characters and unique foes not busy running a ship roll initiative as normal. Each round, determine initiative between rival ships: the person at the helm of each ship makes a STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check, and the winner chooses whether to act first or delay. A ship with no one at the helm automatically fails.

### Using Ship-Mounted Weapons

Unlike an ordinary siege weapon, a large shipboard weapon needs separate Utilize actions to load, aim, and fire. A raiding party is assumed to have hired enough crew to work all its ship-mounted weapons. The Operate Ship Weapon action reloads and aims a weapon, but firing it still takes another action. If an enemy is in a weapon's firing arc, usually 90° from the direction it is mounted, its crew can fire on it.

### Ship Damage

A ship damaged in combat, whether by weapons, spells, or collisions, can suffer the effects on the **Damage to the Ship** table.

**Damage to the Ship**

| Percent of Ship's HP Lost | Effect |
|---|---|
| 25% | The ship has a rupture in its hull and is taking on water. Its speed decreases by 5 feet. |
| 50% | A second rupture lets in even more water. Its speed decreases by another 5 feet. |
| 75% | A third rupture, and the ship is slowly sinking. Its speed decreases by another 5 feet. Unless repaired, it sinks in 2d6 rounds. |
| 100% | The ship is holed and can no longer move or maneuver. It is sinking. Unless repaired, it sinks in 1d4 rounds. |

### Targeting Crew and Casualties

Ship weapons aren't usually aimed at enemy sailors, since the ship is a much easier target, but sailors can be hurt as collateral damage. When an attack scores a critical hit, besides rolling the damage dice twice, use the Disable Station shipboard action to find where the ship was hit. Roll a d6 for the number of crew members in that area who are unconscious and dying. Unless a character or NPC with medical skill or healing magic acts to save them, 75 percent of them die in 1d4 rounds. A character in the area takes half the attack's damage from flying shrapnel, or none with a successful DEX save against a DC equal to the enemy gunner's attack roll.

## Actions During Ship Combat

During a naval battle, the characters can act independently of the ship and crew or work with them, as the players decide.

***Characters During Combat.*** Characters can act as in any other combat. Throwing *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Fireball|fireballs]]* and raining arrows are still good tactics, though most engagements begin at (2d6 + 2) × 100 feet, so characters' attacks usually aren't in range at first. A character can also spend their turn on a shipboard action by taking the Ready action; once the ship has moved at the end of the round, they use their reaction to do the readied shipboard action.

***Ship and Crew During Combat.*** Ships and crews act at the end of the initiative round. On its turn, a vehicle can move up to its speed and take one shipboard action, which the players choose together. Characters who readied shipboard actions can add more. Unless noted otherwise, each shipboard action can be taken only once per turn.

## Shipboard Actions

The ship's crew, and potentially the characters, can take these actions in ship-to-ship combat.

***All Stop.*** The crew attempts a DC 12 STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check to strike the sails and drop anchor, bringing the ship's speed to 0 feet after it finishes moving this round. On a failure, the ship keeps moving for another round before the anchor catches. This can't be attempted in deep water.

***Come About.*** The crew attempts a sharp change of direction with a DC 15 STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check. On a success, the ship turns 90° instead of the normal 45°. On a failure, it doesn't turn at all. The check has disadvantage if the ship is crossing against the wind, unless it is being rowed.

***Disable Station.*** The crew fires to disable a station on the enemy ship, with disadvantage on the attack. On a hit, instead of damaging the hull, one station is damaged as shown on the **Disable Station** table. Roll a d6 for the number of crew members in that area who are unconscious and dying.

***Escape a Grapple.*** A crew member leads others in cutting the lines tying their ship to another. A successful DC 13 STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check cuts them.

***Evasive Maneuver.*** The crew makes a STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check contested by the enemy crew. On a success, attackers have disadvantage on ranged attacks against the ship.

***Force Position.*** The crew makes a STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check contested by the enemy crew to force the enemy into a bad position. On a success, the enemy ship has disadvantage on its check for initiative next round. On a failure, the enemy has advantage on it.

***Full Speed Ahead.*** The crew trims the sails for a burst of speed, and the ship can Dash this turn. If it is powered by oars, the crew must succeed on a DC 12 CON save or gain one level of exhaustion.

***Grapple.*** A crew member leads others in throwing grappling hooks onto an enemy ship within 5 feet and tying the ships together, which takes a successful DC 13 STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check. While tied together, neither ship can move, and neither crew can use Come About, Evasive Maneuver, Force Position, Full Speed Ahead, Hold Her Steady, Ram, or Sheer.

***Hold Her Steady.*** The crew holds the ship steady, giving its weapon crews advantage to hit one enemy ship. All enemy ships have advantage to hit the ship holding steady.

***Motivate (Characters Only).*** As a reaction, a character shouts encouragement to the crew during a shipboard action. On a successful DC 13 CHA (Intimidation or Persuasion) check, all crew rolls for that shipboard action have advantage.

***Operate Ship Weapon.*** As an action, the crew loads and aims a ship-mounted weapon. Firing it still takes a separate action.

***Patch.*** A crew member leads a repair team to put a temporary patch on the hull or fix a damaged station, with a +4 bonus to the check. This can be used only once per hole or damaged station. The DC is 10 + 2 per hole the ship has suffered to restore hit points, or 15 to repair a station. A success restores 2d6 hit points to the ship or puts a station back in working order.

***Ram.*** If the ship is moving, the crew can try to ram a vessel within half the ship's speed by making a STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check against the other ship's AC and moving that far. On a success, both ships take 2d10 damage for every size category of the ramming ship; a ship with a ram takes only half. The ships are hopelessly locked together and can't be separated without several minutes of work. On a miss, the ships graze each other and each takes one-quarter damage. A ship can't ram against the wind unless it is being rowed.

***Sheer.*** Only against ships with oars. The crew brings their ship within 5 feet of the enemy and shears off all the oars on one side, reducing its speed to 0 feet for 3 rounds until new oars can be put out. The crew member at the helm must succeed on a STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check with a DC equal to the other ship's AC.

**Disable Station**

| d6 | Station | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rigging | Sails are torn and the rigging ripped down. The ship's speed decreases by 10 feet. |
| 2 | Mast | The ship loses a mast. If it has more than one, its speed is halved. If it has only one, its speed becomes 0 feet unless it is rowed. |
| 3 | Rudder | The rudder is damaged. A 45° turn requires the crew member at the helm to succeed on a STR (Athletics) or WIS (Survival) check, and Come About is no longer available. If the rudder is hit twice, it is destroyed, and the ship can no longer turn. |
| 4 | Helm | The helm is hit, and the ship can't turn until the Patch action fixes it. |
| 5 | Weapon | The weapon takes the attack's normal damage. If damaged but not destroyed, it can't reload or fire for 1 round. |
| 6 | Deck | A brutal shot hits the main deck, reducing 2d6 crew members to 0 HP. |
