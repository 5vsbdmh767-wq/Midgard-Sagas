## Building Backstories

Though the GM is responsible for crafting a rich story and appropriate atmosphere for a campaign of horror, players can significantly contribute by creating PCs suited to engage with its dark themes. Though multiple options appear below for things like subclasses, lineages, heritages, and backgrounds, a solid backstory grounded in horror tropes can reinforce the campaign and provide storytelling fodder for everyone at the table.

### Genre Tropes and Expectations

One of the many joys of the game is the freedom to portray a PC of your choosing. But creating a PC with specific archetypes, tropes, and expectations in mind also allows you to contribute to the mood of the campaign.

### Player Best Practices

Players should keep certain things in mind—both when creating their PC and during the course of play at the table—in order to help maintain a fun horror environment for everyone. This includes the PCs’ actions as well as the players’ and might require extra attention to handle well.

#### Tone

Though a horror game doesn’t have to be all doom and gloom, consider the PC you wish to portray, their typical behavior, and how that meshes with the more serious tone of horror. There is a place for comedy in horror, but you should consider your actions before breaking the atmosphere being created in the game. Pay attention to when the tension needs to be broken and when it needs to be increased, both in and out of character. Limit distractions and look for opportunities to reinforce the tropes and tone of the game.

#### Helplessness and Heroics

A major element of the horror genre involves PCs in terrifying circumstances beyond their control. In many games, you play heroes, which might seem antithetical to the idea of helplessness. However, struggle lies at the heart of a good adventure, and not every PC springs to life as a perfect, all-powerful protagonist.

Consider how both you and your PC will deal with the inevitable moments of failure and helplessness throughout the game. Look for opportunities to lean into those moments in a way that’s both fun and interesting for you and the other players. This doesn’t mean you should constantly sabotage your group’s efforts but rather that you accept that this genre itself means sometimes you lose. And those losses can provide moments for reflection and growth. Even when PCs survive their ordeals, they typically come away with scars, both physical and emotional, that shape the core of their being.

#### Lines and Consent

When leaning into horror tropes with your PC, be sure you and your fellow players are aware and respectful of one another’s limits. Pulling back from a particular topic, taking a break after an intense scene, or stepping out of character to discuss a decision or storyline are all acceptable.

The use of safety tools in horror is covered in [[Safety Tools]]. If your GM doesn’t introduce these tools at your table, there’s nothing wrong with starting the discussion yourself. Though safety tools are most effective when introduced at session zero, don’t be afraid to request and implement them later should the need arise.

### Character Archetypes

When creating a PC for a horror game, players might find it helpful to consider the tropes of the genre and the archetypical characters found in these stories. Here are several of those archetypes.

Consider which, if any, of these your PC fits into and how their actions and reactions reinforce the narrative, and they might very well fall into more than one. These archetypes can vary depending on the subgenre of horror you’re playing in, but the overall themes remain the same.

#### The Cursed

The cursed has a secret they guard with their very life—a secret even they are too terrified to talk about until forced. This secret might be the key to the party’s survival or the monster’s downfall, or it might be something the PC believes will turn everyone else against them if it is known.

#### The Innocent

The innocent has likely been sheltered for much of their life. They might be gullible, or they might simply see the best in people and are naive to the darker ways of the world.

#### The Penitent

The penitent has done things they are no longer proud of—and might even regret. They typically don’t talk about it and might be running from the consequences of their previous actions. A tragic event might have forced them away from their old life. If so, they harbor immense guilt and might be directly to blame for the event. The PC might be an antihero, or they might be trying to change their old ways.

#### The Skeptic

The skeptic doesn’t believe in the supernatural, remaining convinced that strange experiences can be rationalized away. This skepticism might be a true blind spot, or it might be a defense mechanism they use to keep their fear at bay. Either way, a refusal to accept the truth of their situation is often the PC’s downfall.

#### The Veteran

The veteran has seen things like this in the past and possesses the knowledge and experience to help others weather the terrors. They are often the first to see the threat for what it is and typically believe others when they recount their own strange tales. The PC can provide information and insights that can help others survive their ordeals—assuming the advice is taken.

### Taste of Terror

Sometimes, characters in horror stories have lived normal lives up the point that they are suddenly faced with unimaginable terrors. Other times, they might have had a taste of terror in their past, something that foreshadows the horror to come, that marks them as different, or that left a strange scar on their otherwise normal life.

Players have the option of adding a taste of terror to their PCs’ backstories. This element can inform roleplaying and how a player approaches their PC’s build and progression. It can also help the GM craft a story that resonates with each PC. A player can roll on one of the tables in this section or choose the option that most inspires them. The tables are divided into tropes to reinforce the atmosphere of a horror campaign.

#### Brush with Darkness

In a single moment, everything changed. When you were younger, you glimpsed a horror beyond reckoning and were forever marked by it. To determine this pivotal moment, roll a d6 and consult the **Brush with Darkness** table.

**Brush with Darkness**

| d6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | A family member secretly dabbled in dark and forbidden magics. One night, you accidentally interrupted a delicate ritual, causing it to go awry. You barely survived the backlash, and they vanished, leaving only the echo of their screams in your ears. Others presumed they died in the event, but you’ve always wondered about their fate. |
| 2 | The door to your basement or attic was always locked—until one day, it wasn’t. Curiosity got the better of you, and you ventured in. What you saw there will live forever in your memories . . . and your nightmares. |
| 3 | What started as a prank spiraled out of control. Someone innocent died. Everyone involved pledged a vow of silence, but the guilt still haunts you. |
| 4 | What was supposed to be a night of frivolity and parlor games with friends ended in terror when a séance went wrong. None of you can forget what happened, and none of you will ever be the same. |
| 5 | You’ve always loved to read, and the more fantastical, strange, and esoteric the better. One day, you read the wrong book. Before you realized what happened, you released something terrifying into the world. |
| 6 | Your best friend came to you one night, terrified. They’d stumbled upon a horrible secret, something that made them a target of a mysterious entity, and they claimed the shadows were coming for them. They never told you what the secret was, but when they disappeared, only you knew the truth: they hadn’t run away, they had been taken. |

#### Inauspicious Beginnings

Something unusual occurred on the day of your birth or shortly thereafter, heralding things to come. To determine the nature of this occurrence, roll a d6 and consult the **Inauspicious Beginnings** table.

**Inauspicious Beginnings**

| d6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | No one knows who your parents are or where you come from. You were left on a doorstep in the dark of night with a strange trinket tucked into the basket beside you. You’ve always wondered about your past and the meaning of the trinket that you still carry with you. |
| 2 | The town’s wise woman fainted the first time she saw you. When she awoke, she had been stricken blind and had no memory of what she’d seen in you. |
| 3 | There was a solar eclipse on the day of your birth. You were born at the moment of totality, beneath the silvery corona of the black sun. |
| 4 | You have a birthmark. It terrified the elders and caused your parents to flee town with you when you were still very young. You were taught to hide it, lest others know you for what you are. |
| 5 | You never knew your father, and your mother died before you could ask her about him. There are signs, however, that cause you to suspect he was not a mortal being but something far more sinister. |
| 6 | You were born during the worst storm of the century. Your town barely survived the wind, rain, and flooding, and several folks claim the tempest ceased the moment you drew your first breath. |

#### Sole Survivor

A tragedy occurred, and you were the only one to make it out alive. To determine the nature of the tragedy, roll a d6 and consult the **Sole Survivor** table.

**Sole Survivor**

| d6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | It came in the night. The first sign was the scratching in the walls. Then came the screams. When help finally arrived, you were the only one left alive. |
| 2 | It was a fire. Nothing more, nothing less. But you could never bring yourself to talk about how the flames seemed to reach out for you like grasping hands—and how you heard your name whispered over and over in the roar of the inferno. |
| 3 | The hiking trip was meant to be a chance for you and your friends to relax in the peace and quiet of nature. Instead, you found yourselves hunted. When you crawled out of the woods two days later, injured but alive, you knew you would never forget the harrowing experience. |
| 4 | The people of your village were friendly, offering hospitality to any stranger who stopped by. One evening, something came to your village disguised as a weary traveler. By dawn, everyone else was dead. |
| 5 | The rumors had said the house was haunted, but none of you believed such nonsense. You believe now. The spirits of your loved ones have joined the ghostly chorus within its walls. |
| 6 | You’d made that voyage safely at least half a dozen times before, but this time was different. It was a miracle that they found you at all, floating atop your makeshift raft, seemingly delirious with terrible tales on your lips. |
