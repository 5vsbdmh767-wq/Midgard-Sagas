Normally, backgrounds reflect a character’s training before they became adventurers and provide various bonuses, such as skills, proficiencies, and equipment. For many characters, a background remains an element of their past. It occasionally impacts a campaign, but it mostly serves an important function as roleplaying fodder and thematic color. For example, a character might sometimes use their nobility as an adventurer, but this background doesn’t change or grow.

On the other hand, what if an element similar to a background could continue to develop and even change over the course of a character’s adventuring career?

Character threads are positions, occupations, and situations a character might pursue while they continue to go on quests or battle foes. Though threads are similar to backgrounds, they are less about what your character did before becoming an adventurer and more about what your character does in the “background” while they are adventuring.

Perhaps the PC is appointed as an ambassador to a neighboring country or elevated to the position of minister in a royal court. Or perhaps a character with a noble background learns their family has been cheated out of their fortune and is now impoverished.

Threads are aspects added to characters at any time during their career that have specific short or long-term goals. They reward characters with new features and abilities as they advance along milestones toward that goal. Once that endpoint of a thread is achieved, new threads can be applied as they occur during the course of the campaign. You can add threads to your character by choosing one of the ones presented below, or you can use the options presented in this section to create your own tiered threads.

| Thread | In Brief |
|---|---|
| [[#Bloodsworn]] | You have been wronged. Now your fate is painted with blood. |
| [[#Cursemarked]] | You bear the brunt of a curse of ill luck, and it’ll get worse before the end. |
| [[#Explorer]] | Glory lies just over the horizon. Time to find it. |
| [[#Fatebound]] | Your fate is written. Make it glorious. |
| [[#Herald]] | Your stories don’t just recount events . . . they shape history. |
| [[#Legend Hunter]] | Neither storm nor night nor bloody fang shall slow your relentless pursuit. |
| [[#Sworn Huskarl]] | You carry out the will of your king, no matter the risk. |

## Elements of a Character Thread

All character threads establish a specific goal as well as thematic milestones that the character must reach in order to achieve that goal, and each new milestone unlocks a new benefit or feature. When the specific goal is ultimately accomplished, the character can change their thread to a new one (see [[#Adding or Abandoning New Threads]]). A character can have only one thread at a time.

The use of Threads in your campaign can lead to a balancing issue between characters taking on threads and those who do not. The easiest way to handle this is to just acknowledge that threads may make some characters slightly more powerful than others. GMs uncomfortable with this can also hand out some magic items or other rewards to non-thread using characters.

When adopting a thread, a certain amount of personalization is required to fit the milestones to the goal at hand and to the overall direction of the campaign. Players should work with their GMs to adjust the goal and milestones for their thread as described below.

***Goal.*** All threads begin with a goal determined by the player. The goal helps to guide the creation of the thread’s milestones and should thematically fit with the character and with the nature of the campaign.

More importantly, the goal should be a specific, achievable objective, something the character hopes to accomplish in the future. The more specific this goal, the better, for this will ease the creation of its milestones. For example, a character picking up the Bloodsworn thread might choose a goal of defeating the giants who destroyed their village.

***Milestones.*** With the goal in hand, the milestones must be decided. These are events of personal achievement or significance. They act as obstacles or thresholds on the path to reaching the thread’s goal. A thread isn’t restricted in its number of milestones, but four is recommended. On achieving each milestone, a benefit is unlocked, depending on its ranking—least, lesser, greater, or superior (see ***Milestone Benefits***, below).

**Milestone Recommended Levels**

| Milestone Rank | Class Level | Types of Benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Least | 1–4 | Tool, skill, or language proficiency; useful contact or a deal with a merchant; common magic item; or minor magic ability or charm that is usable once. |
| Lesser | 5–10 | Expertise in a skill; gifted a house or owed a favor; uncommon magic item; or spell of 2nd circle or lower (usable once per long rest). |
| Greater | 11–16 | Fear; gifted a castle, knighthood, or noble title; rare magic item; or spell of 5th circle or lower (usable once per Long Rest). |
| Superior | 17–20 | A powerful Mark of Prestige or Supernatural Gift; married into royal family or appointed important court position; very rare magic item; or spell of 8th circle or lower (usable once per Long Rest). |

While the goal or motivation of a thread is specific, milestones usually are not. This allows the GM some flexibility in applying them to individual campaigns. Each thread description includes a few example milestones, but there are no strict rules about what a milestone might be or when it can occur.

Milestones can even change as the path toward the character’s goal shifts. The player and GM might even retroactively determine that an unexpected event counts as a milestone to that thread’s goal. Ideally, the player should work this out with the GM, agreeing on applicable milestones and determining how (and when) they are achieved.

***Spacing Milestones.*** When creating or adding a thread to a character, decide with the GM how long (in theory) it should take to reach the goal. This might depend on the scope or length of the campaign and on the nature of the thread. An Impoverished Noble thread, for example, might be quite short, ending when a certain wealth threshold is reached. Conversely, the Illegitimate Scion thread might only end when the character is adopted into a new family, an event which could take 20 levels.

Once the length of the thread is determined, add in the milestones, spacing them out evenly in terms of levels.

***Milestone Benefits.*** The four ranks of milestones—least, lesser, greater, superior—have recommended level ranges that serve as guides for determining the types of benefits a character receives. These correspond to the level range the character is expected to be within when they reach that milestone. Threads can have multiple milestones of the same rank. Sample benefits are listed in the **Milestone Recommended Levels** table.

Characters should generally earn only one benefit for each milestone unlocked, but GMs can always choose to reward additional benefits.

### A Sample Thread Progression

Halbera Gap-Tooth, a bearfolk Viking, begins her career with the [[Backgrounds#Northlands Reaver|Northlands Reaver]] background and the Bloodsworn thread. Halbera’s player chooses “I must defeat the storm giant who slew my jarl” as a goal.

Working with the GM, the player determines that this thread should only go to 10th level, or thereabouts, because after that the campaign might shift away from the area where the storm giant operates. They create four milestones that can lead Halbera to that final confrontation with her foe, plus benefits for each of those milestones.

- Uncover the identity of the storm giant. (Least: Cunning feature.)
- Learn the location of the storm giant’s hidden lair. (Least: a seeking charm usable once per day.)
- Defeat one of the storm giant’s minions who has important information. (Lesser: Wrath feature.)
- Defeat the storm giant. (Lesser: Gain the lair as a new base of operations.)

This progression assumes a 10-level arc, but threads can be as long or as short as the GM wishes. Shorter-range goals merely require fewer milestones.

## Adding or Abandoning New Threads

Threads give characters another way to show how they change and grow over the course of a campaign. When a thread is completed, or even abandoned, a character can adopt a new thread to reflect the altering events of the story. Having proven themselves to their estranged parent, for example, a character with the Illegitimate Scion thread now looks to rebuild the family fortune. That Illegitimate Scion thread might shift then to that of the Impoverished Noble thread. The player and GM should agree when circumstances are correct for a new thread, but the GM has the final say.

When adding new threads, the character retains all milestone benefits of previous threads. When abandoning a thread (that is, relinquishing it before achieving its stated goal), the character loses all of the thread’s milestone benefits already gained. Those features were temporary benefits that didn’t become permanent because the character’s focus changed, leaving the source of those features unresolved.

## Thread Descriptions

This section describes seven sample threads from which to choose. Though these threads presume a long arc of milestones, reaching from 1st level all the way to 20th level, they can be adjusted for shorter arcs by reducing the number of milestones or by reducing their spacing.

If a more personalized approach is preferred, simply use the structure of the provided threads as inspiration for creating new threads.

## Bloodsworn

*You have been wronged. Now your fate is painted with blood.*

Among the [[Northlanders]], no oath is more serious than an oath of vengeance. But when justice cannot be achieved by any other means, a sworn promise to mete out revenge for wrongdoing is the only answer that remains. Those who make such oaths are called Bloodsworn, and their vengeance becomes a tale told by skalds for generations.

As a Bloodsworn, your ultimate goal is revenge upon the one (or ones) who wronged you. Unfortunately, the wrongdoer is beyond your reach, at least for now. This means your plan must be carried out over weeks, months, or even longer. During this time, you’ll no doubt be distracted by other adventures, but your focus demands that you always return to your oath. Bloodsworn must have been wronged or harmed in some way before the gods will accept their blood oath. In most cases, your vengeance will be fatal to your target, but in some situations, other punishments may be more fitting, especially if the wrong you’re avenging doesn’t itself involve a death.

***Goals.*** Work with your GM to create a goal specific to your campaign or use one from the **Bloodsworn Goals** table. The specific nature of each milestone is dependent on the goal selected and should be agreed upon by both player and GM. Use the suggested milestone benefits described in this section or create new ones based on the rules for creating a thread.

**Bloodsworn Goals**

| d6 | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | I must kill the one who murdered the one I love. |
| 2 | I cannot rest until the usurper who killed my jarl is dead by my hands. |
| 3 | A crew of scheming charlatans ruined my family’s business. I vow to punish them in ways the law cannot. |
| 4 | The power-mad jarl of our lands has destroyed my community. I have a cunning plan to ensure he is overthrown. |
| 5 | My trusted shieldmate betrayed me and left me to die. Now I shall return the favor. |
| 6 | I was shamed by the false words of another. I will deliver vengeance tenfold upon my accuser. |

### Least Milestone Feature

When you achieve the first milestone of this thread, you gain your choice of one of the following benefits.

***Cunning.*** You gain proficiency in your choice of one of the following skills: Deception, Insight, or Intimidation.

***Tool of Vengeance.*** You gain a common magic item of your choice.

### Lesser Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain your choice of one of the following benefits.

***Enemy of My Enemy.*** You’ve made an ally, a person who is also sworn to oppose your enemy. They have granted you an underground hideout from which to conduct your campaign of vengeance.

***Wrath.*** You can cast *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Vengeful Smite|vengeful smite]]*, using your choice of INT, WIS, or CHA as your spellcasting ability score. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

### Greater Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Tenacity.*** You can resist effects that would slow your oath from being carried out. If you begin your turn with the frightened, incapacitated, paralyzed, or stunned condition, you may choose to end one of those conditions. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Superior Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Dire Oath.*** When you complete a long rest, name a specific creature you swore your oath against or who stands in the way of you completing your oath. For the next 24 hours, that creature is marked by your dire oath. While the dire oath lasts, you have advantage on all attack rolls made against that creature, and advantage on skill checks made to locate or learn about that creature.

After you use this feature, you must wait 7 days before you can use it again.

## Cursemarked

*You bear the brunt of a curse of ill luck, and it’ll get worse before the end.*

Northlanders place great faith in the existence of luck. The gods can bestow good fortune upon the faithful, and honorable actions can directly lead to successful endeavors. Likewise, disreputable behavior can bring failure not only to you, but to future generations of your family.

You are one of the unfortunates, chosen by fate or the gods to suffer from ill luck wherever you go. Perhaps you earned this curse through dishonor, oathbreaking, or cowardice. You might bear misfortune as a virtual heirloom, passed down to you by a disreputable ancestor. Other Cursemarked are victims of witch’s curses or similar supernatural effects. Whatever the reason, you can’t seem to catch a break.

Strangely, the presence of a Cursemarked can often bring good fortune to those nearby. As long as your friends are willing to put up with your occasional mishaps, the long-term benefit might well be worthwhile. Meanwhile, however, the Cursemarked searches for some way to atone for their misdeeds, real or imagined, knowing such a road will be long and will certainly grow worse before the end. Yet when the hand of fortune is turned against you, what else can you do?

***Goals.*** Work with your GM to create a goal specific to your campaign or use one from the **Cursemarked Goals** table. The specific nature of each milestone is dependent on the goal selected and should be agreed upon by both player and GM. Use the suggested milestone benefits described in this section or create new ones based on the rules for creating a thread.

**Cursemarked Goals**

| d6 | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | I will atone for my dishonor by returning a sacred relic to a distant tomb. |
| 2 | I can only lift this curse by clearing the name of an ancestor falsely accused of murder. |
| 3 | I must prove my courage to the gods by defeating a tribe of marauding giants. |
| 4 | I will invoke Freyr’s blessing by forging peace between two warring clans. |
| 5 | To end this curse, I shall endure trials of my honor, my valor, and my selflessness. |
| 6 | I must convince [[Wotan]] himself to intervene and convince the [[The Norns\|Norns]] to reshape my fate. |

### Cursemarked Milestone Rules

Cursemarked milestones work a bit differently than other character threads. The milestone features described below trigger when you roll for specific mechanics (saves, ability checks or attack rolls, depending on the feature), and get a result within the range of numbers indicated in the bracket for each milestone.

Milestone features never overlap, so if you trigger a feature, you can’t trigger another until the start of your next turn after the duration of the previous one expires.

### Least Milestone Feature

When you achieve the first milestone of this thread, you gain these benefits:

***Tides of Fate [1–3, Saves].*** Your speed is reduced by 10 feet. When this occurs, the speed of another creature of your choice within 30 feet of you increases by 10 feet. These effects last until the end of your next turn.

***Greater Sacrifice.*** When your Tides of Fate is triggered, you can choose to reduce your speed to 0 until the start of your next turn. If you do so, in addition to the normal benefit, the chosen creature does not provoke opportunity attacks and ignores difficult terrain until the end of your next turn. Once you use your Greater Sacrifice feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Lesser Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you lose all prior milestone feature benefits from this thread and gain these benefits:

***Burden’s Shield [1–5, Saves, Ability Checks].*** Your AC is reduced by 2. When this occurs, the AC of another creature of your choice within 30 feet of you increases by 2. These effects last until the end of your next turn.

***Greater Sacrifice.*** When your Burden’s Shield is triggered, you can choose to reduce your AC by 4 instead of 2. If you do so, the AC of the chosen creature increases by 4 (instead of 2), or you can choose for it to receive the benefit of the Tide of Fate’s Greater Sacrifice. Once you use your Greater Sacrifice feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Greater Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you lose all prior milestone feature benefits from this thread and gain these benefits:

***Threads Entwined [1–7, Saves, Ability Checks, Attack Rolls].*** You can’t take bonus actions or reactions until the end of your next turn. When this occurs, another creature of your choice within 30 feet of you can immediately use its reaction to take the Dash, Hide, or Aid action, or make a single weapon attack against a target in range.

***Greater Sacrifice.*** When your Threads Entwined is triggered, you can instead choose to do nothing this turn besides move. If you’ve already taken your action this turn, you instead forgo your action on your next turn. If you do so, the chosen creature can take one additional action on its next turn, or it can receive the benefit of one of the previous milestone feature’s Greater Sacrifices. Once you use your Greater Sacrifice feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Superior Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you lose all prior milestone feature benefits from this thread and gain these benefits:

***Two-Edged Gift [1–9, Saves, Ability Checks, Attack Rolls].*** Your weapon attacks and spells deal half their normal damage until the end of your next turn. During this time, the first time a creature of your choice within 30 feet of you deals damage with a weapon or spell, that creature deals maximum damage.

***Greater Sacrifice.*** When your Two-Edged Gift is triggered, you can instead choose to deal no damage with weapons and spells until the end of your next turn. If you do so, the next weapon or spell attack the chosen creature makes before the end of its next turn is automatically a critical hit, or it can receive the benefit of one of the previous milestone feature’s Greater Sacrifice. Once you use your Greater Sacrifice feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

## Explorer

*Glory lies just over the horizon. Time to find it.*

Explorers occupy an unusual place in [[Northlands]] society. Though revered for their courage, they rarely find a comfortable place in a community. Driven by wanderlust, curiosity, boredom, or a simple inability to relate to civilized folks, the Explorer spends most of their time in the wilderness. Some climb mountains, others set sail for lost lands, and still others delve through dark tunnels beneath the earth. For an Explorer, the greatest treasure awaits beyond the next hill.

Whether by choice or by fate, you follow the path less traveled. Perhaps you’re a rugged seafarer or mountaineer seeking discoveries in the wild. Other Explorers are treasure hunters, royal mapmakers, or seekers of truth. You might be an exile, outlaw, or iconoclast who simply can’t get along with others. Or you might be driven by vision or prophecy to forsake hearth and home for the unknown.

***Goals.*** Work with your GM to create a goal specific to your campaign or use one from the **Explorer Goals** table. The specific nature of each milestone is dependent on the goal selected and should be agreed upon by both player and GM. Use the suggested milestone benefits described in this section or create new ones based on the rules for creating a thread.

> [!note] GM Note
> This thread brings flavorful Northlands themes into a campaign and can be taken up by characters of any class. However, it can tread on the toes of any rangers in the party. Consult with your ranger players before allowing a character to take on this thread to make sure no feelings will be hurt.

**Explorer Goals**

| d6 | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | I shall find a route through the Bloodfjord Mountains never before traveled by mortals. |
| 2 | To avert Ragnarok, I must carry a prophecy to a far-flung land. |
| 3 | My mother died searching for the headwaters of the Tearstain River. I shall complete her quest. |
| 4 | I seek a sapling of the [[Yggdrasil\|World Tree]], rumored to be growing on a tiny island in the Nieder Straits. |
| 5 | Once I decipher the ancient map I hold, I’ll delve into the depths of Narvegr Maw. |
| 6 | The Frozen Palace of the last Hyperborean lies somewhere in [[The Bleak Expanse]]. I’ll find it and return with his icy crown. |

### Least Milestone Feature

When you achieve the first milestone of this thread, you gain your choice of one of these benefits:

***Celebrity Explorer.*** You also receive a 30% discount on lodging, supplies, and food from innkeepers, merchants, and proprietors, and it’s common for them to offer you things for free.

***Explorer’s Aptitude.*** Your travels instill you with new knowledge. You gain proficiency in your choice of one of the following skills: Athletics, History, or Perception.

### Lesser Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain your choice of one of these benefits:

***Traversal Expert.*** You have a knack for finding easy paths through the environment. You may use your bonus action to move your speed. During this movement, you ignore difficult terrain and have advantage on STR (Athletics) checks to climb, jump, or swim. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

***Scout’s Awareness.*** You have a preternatural sense that allows you to avoid dangers in your surroundings. For 1 hour, you and each ally within 30 feet who can see you have advantage on saves to resist the effects of nonmagical traps and hazards. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Greater Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain one of these benefits:

***Ennobled.*** A monarch has recognized your achievements as an explorer in their or other territories by granting you a noble title, which comes with a moderately sized estate. You have advantage on CHA checks against anyone who would be impressed or intimidated by your title. You also can avoid punishment for minor infractions, though if you abuse this, your title might be revoked.

***Wayfarer’s Steps.*** You can never become lost by nonmagical means and can always retrace your steps. Additionally, as a bonus action, you can take the Dash action and command up to 4 willing creatures who can hear you to use their reaction to immediately take a Dash action. Neither you nor your allies trigger opportunity attacks while moving in this way. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Superior Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain these benefits:

***Far Traveler.*** While traveling, you and your companions may take a forced march without any penalties. You always find good campsites and have advantage on any d20 rolls related to camping or foraging in the wilderness. You also have advantage on WIS (Animal Handling) checks to calm wild animals.

***One With the Land.*** You may study the land from a good vantage point (such as a high position or advantageous overlook) for 1 minute and ask a question concerning the natural world or locations within 5 miles. This functions like the *[[Rituals 4th–6th Circle#Commune with Nature|commune with nature]]* spell. Once you use this feature, you must wait 24 hours before using it again.

## Fatebound

*Your fate is written. Make it glorious.*

Many warriors loudly boast of their desire for a noble death or an honorable end in battle, to thus earn a seat in the great hall of Valhalla. These boasts are but hopeful wishes since most individuals never see the path of their future. You, however, are not most individuals. You have been blessed, or perhaps cursed, with the knowledge of your end, though this knowledge doesn’t tell you if you succeed or fail in the circumstance surrounding your death. You might know you will die facing a dark warlord, but you don’t know if you will defeat her. Despite this, the Norns have given you a rare opportunity. The knowledge you possess will help you ensure your death is worthy of the sagas.

Some of those who are Fatebound are marked from birth. Others learn of their fate from seers, shamans, or divine visions. Some heroes earn this thread after engaging in activities that move the gods or the Norns to reward them with such a potent end.

***Fatebound and Death.*** Keep in mind that having this thread may mean you can’t die before your fated time, but it doesn’t mean you will reach that point whole and healthy. While your fate is inevitable, luck alone determines your condition when and how you meet that end. Do you lead a glorious charge across the battlefield? Or are you left guarding the baggage? Has every confrontation leading up to your end prepared you to defeat your ultimate foe? Or has a lifetime of avoidance left you ill-prepared?

If a Fatebound PC is killed before their time, and magical resurrection is not an option, a GM could instead choose for them to be injured in a significant way—such as the loss of a limb or eye. These injuries should not be able to be healed without high-level magic. Alternatively, perhaps they lose one of their powerful magical items instead of their lives, or they might suffer a level of Exhaustion until they reach their next milestone. The GM and player should agree on this complication.

***Goals.*** Work with your GM to create a goal specific to your campaign or use one from the **Fatebound Goals** table. The specific nature of each milestone is dependent on the goal selected and should be agreed upon by both player and GM. Use the suggested milestone benefits described in this section or create new ones based on the rules for creating a thread.

> [!note] GM Note
> The Fatebound is an unusual thread in that its final milestone specifically describes the manner in which a character might meet with a permanent death, likely as part of the final accomplishment of their goal, and then empowers the PCs allies as a reward. Most importantly, this thread should be something the player consciously selects.

**Fatebound Goals**

| d6 | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | I will die only after finding and facing the mightiest dragon in the land. |
| 2 | I will be slain by [[Sengajordensblod\|Utgard-Loki]] while attempting to thwart his plan to slay the gods. |
| 3 | To defend my people from invasion, I pledge to pay any price, including my own life. |
| 4 | I shall search for and find a fabled lost treasure, but I will die in the process. |
| 5 | I will rise to become the North’s greatest reaver, then perish battling the World Serpent. |
| 6 | I seek the truth behind the fall of [[Legendary Places of the North#Nordheim\|Nordheim]], though it will cost my life to learn it. |

### Least Milestone Feature

When you achieve the first milestone of this thread, you gain your choice of one the following benefits.

***Dream Gift.*** A god, Norn, or other powerful being has given you a gift in a dream. When you awoke, you had a common magic item of the GM’s choice that may aid you.

***Fate’s Blessing.*** Once per turn, while you are at half your hit point maximum or less, you can use your reaction to have advantage on a save.

### Lesser Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain your choice of one of the following benefits.

***Strength of Wyrd.*** While you are at half your hit point maximum or less, once per turn you may add your PB to a damage roll. You may use this damage bonus a number of times equal to your PB, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

***Enduring Wyrd.*** When you take damage, you may use your reaction to gain temporary hit points equal to your PBs. You cannot use this feature again until you take a short or long rest.

### Greater Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Doom Delayed.*** When you would be killed, you may instead declare yourself as stable with 0 HP. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

***Knight of Fate.*** You have become a legend in a territory, and the monarch there has knighted you, granting you holdings that will revert back to the monarchy upon your death. You have advantage on CHA-based checks with the people of this land and are frequently showered with minor gifts and favors there.

### Superior Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefits.

***Last Act of Fate.*** At a moment you have predetermined to be the time when you are to die, when you are reduced 0 hit points or killed outright, you may (no action required) instead set your hit points to 1. You lose any detrimental conditions, and any harmful effects on you are ended immediately. Until the end of your next turn, you gain advantage on all attack rolls and ability checks, you are immune to all damage, and you add your character level to all damage rolls.

At the end of your next turn, you die and can never be restored to life.

***Glorious End.*** If you achieve your destiny in a way that earns you a seat in Valhalla (or some other appropriate afterlife), the allies who witness your death have advantage on all ability checks, attack rolls, and saves for 24 hours as they are inspired by your heroism.

## Herald

*Your stories don’t just recount events . . . they shape history.*

Storytellers are revered in the Northlands. Whether a skald sharing tales around a fire, a lore keeper gathering sagas to pass down the generations, or a grizzled reaver frightening young warriors by recalling harrowing battles, the tradition of collecting stories is respected across nearly all cultures in the region. As a Herald you are a proud, if temporary, part of this tradition, driven by a need to spread stories during your travels to accomplish a specific end.

Perhaps your stories encourage downtrodden karls to rise up against their cruel jarl, warn a region about an approaching invasion, or convince warriors to join your cause. Part skald, part herald, part envoy, you will use your tales and stories to sway the minds of your audience and help shape the world.

***Goals.*** Work with your GM to create a goal specific to your campaign or use one from the **Herald Goals** table. The specific nature of each milestone is dependent on the goal selected and should be agreed upon by both player and GM. Use the suggested milestone benefits described in this section or create new ones based on the rules for creating a thread.

> [!note] GM Note
> This thread brings flavorful Northlands themes into a campaign and can be taken up by characters any class. However, it can tread on the toes of any bards in the party. Consult with your bard players before allowing a character to take on this thread to make sure no feelings will be hurt.

**Herald Goals**

| d6 | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | The feared invasion from [[Jotunheim]] approaches, and the country must be warned. |
| 2 | By telling the sagas, I will gather support for the greatest raid the North has ever launched. |
| 3 | My tales flatter [[Thor]] and [[Wotan]] in the hope they will support our struggle against the forces of Chernobog and [[Angrboda]]. |
| 4 | I spread the word of the great crusade to eradicate the darkness beneath [[Tanserhall]]. Heed my call! |

### Least Milestone Feature

When you achieve the first milestone of this thread, you gain the following benefits.

***Storyteller’s Knack.*** You learn three languages of your choice.

***Performer’s Reward.*** If you have at least 3 days of downtime between adventures, you can earn gp equal to 10 times your character level by performing your stories in taverns, on the street, or for powerful patrons. Additional downtime events don’t increase this reward.

### Lesser Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Enthralling Speaker.*** You gain proficiency in the Performance skill. If you are already proficient, you instead gain expertise. Additionally, while performing before an audience, you can cast either *[[1st-Circle Spells#Charm|charm]]* or *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Suggestion|suggestion]]* as part of your performance without requiring somatic or material components. Use CHA as your spellcasting ability score. After casting one of these spells using this feature, you can’t use the feature again until you finish a long rest.

### Greater Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Persuasive Words.*** If you make a CHA check to change a creature’s attitude or convince them of your cause, you may treat a roll of 9 or lower on the d20 as a 10. You can do this a number of times equal to your CHA modifier per long rest.

### Superior Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain one of the following benefits.

***Heroic Saga.*** Your tales grow so powerful that they can briefly tamper with luck. After you spend at least 10 minutes telling an inspiring story or legend, choose either one creature who can hear you, or up to three creatures. You can’t select yourself as a target. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until for 7 days.

Depending on the number of targets, creatures can receive one of the following.

- **Luck Boon (Three Targets).** Once before the next long rest, a target can expend their boon to gain advantage on a save, attack roll or ability check.
- **Wyrd Boon (One Target).** Once before their next long rest, if the target fails a save or attack roll, they can choose to succeed instead. An attack roll succeeding in this way results in the minimum needed to succeed (not a critical hit, for example).

***Song of Matrimony.*** A member of the royal family in a particular land has fallen in love with you and your performances, and the feeling is mutual. You are engaged to marry into the royal family.

## Legend Hunter

*Neither storm nor night nor bloody fang shall slow your relentless pursuit.*

Hunting is as old as the Northlands itself. From volcanic peaks to frigid fens and aurora-bathed glaciers, the tales of hunters are sung alongside those of heroes and gods. While some may hunt for survival or vengeance, you are different. You are a hunter to be feared, as you hunt for the thrill of the chase and the glory of victory.

As a Legend Hunter, you may have been chosen by the Norns, blessed by a god, or forged in trials of blood and grift. No matter how you come to it, your extraordinary search for greater and greater prey sets you apart from those who hunt merely for survival. You do not merely forge trails; you craft sagas. You do not merely slay beasts; you end legends to become a legend.

***Goals.*** Work with your GM to create a goal specific to your campaign or use one from the **Legend Hunter Goals** table. The specific nature of each milestone is dependent on the goal selected and should be agreed upon by both player and GM. Use the suggested milestone benefits described in this section or create new ones based on the rules for creating a thread.

**Legend Hunter Goals**

| d6 | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | I will prove my valor by tracking down and slaying a legendary monster that has been destroying villages. |
| 2 | I hunt for a mythical two-headed jotun somewhere in [[The Bleak Expanse]]. |
| 3 | Besting a great dragon in its lair shall be my ultimate victory. |
| 4 | I will go beneath the waves to bring low the mother of all krakens. |
| 5 | I will bring back the head of [[Fenris]]. |
| 6 | My legend will be assured when I go among the roots of [[Yggdrasil]] to slay [[Nidhogg]]. |

### Least Milestone Feature

When you achieve the first milestone of this thread, you gain the following benefit.

***Predator’s Mark.*** You have advantage on WIS (Survival) checks made to track creatures, and on a failed check it only takes you half the time to reacquire the tracks (5 minutes in a confined area, or half an hour outdoors).

### Lesser Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain your choice of one of the following benefits.

***Unclouded Sight.*** Your honed senses let you ignore obscurement caused by weather, such as rain, snow, or fog. This includes magical clouds and weather effects, such as *[[1st-Circle Spells#Fog Cloud|fog cloud]]* and *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Sleet Storm|sleet storm]]*, but not *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Darkness|darkness]]*.

***Reliable Senses.*** After failing an INT (Investigation), WIS (Perception), or WIS (Survival) check, you can reroll the check and take the higher result. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Greater Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Finish the Fight.*** When you reduce a creature to half its hit point maximum or less, your next attack that hits it becomes a critical hit. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again for 24 hours.

### Superior Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Legend Breaker.*** When you hit a creature that has legendary resistance or legendary actions, you can choose to forgo dealing damage to force that creature to make a CON save (DC = 8 + your STR or WIS modifier + your PB). On a failed save, the target becomes unable use its legendary resistance or its legendary actions. The target can repeat the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

***The Ultimate Favor.*** You performed a service for a powerful healer, who was caught in an animal trap set by another hunter. In return, they have offered to cast a single spell of healing of 6th to 9th circle on you or a creature of your choice, at a time of your choosing, even if this puts them at great risk. This should be a spell that heals, regenerates, resurrects, or that removes conditions or curses.

## Sworn Huskarl

*You carry out the will of your king, no matter the risk.*

You have pledged your loyalty to an important personage, promising to guard them or travel across the Northlands in their service, risking danger as you seek to accomplish a great task. In the past, it was expected that a huskarl would swear to serve a lord for life or until they were dismissed. While jarls still take huskarls in that way, they also accept the service of huskarls on a temporary basis, to perform specific deeds or until certain threats are dealt with. These are the types of huskarls described in this thread.

The Sworn Huskarl enjoys a position of respect within their lord’s retinue. More than a mere bodyguard, you are a trusted agent acting on your ruler’s behalf. You carry some of their authority with you, but that also comes with the responsibility to act in ways they would approve. Sworn Huskarls who bring shame to their jarl don’t remain in service for long.

A jarl takes on a new huskarl with a simple ceremony in which the huskarl swears an oath in the longhouse before a gathering of other members of the felag. During the oath-swearing, the goals or timeframe for the service are mentioned (unless the jarl keeps your mission a secret). Traditionally, your reasons for serving are respect for the lord, payment, or power. However, in some cases your oath may hold a secret. Perhaps you made it under threat of violence or under false pretenses. Or maybe your lord doesn’t deserve such loyalty. You might serve under protest or with your own goal that’s unknown to your ruler.

***Goals.*** Work with your GM to create a goal specific to your campaign or use one from the **Sworn Huskarl Goals** table. The specific nature of each milestone is dependent on the goal selected and should be agreed upon by both player and GM. Use the suggested milestone benefits described in this section or create new ones based on the rules for creating a thread.

**Sworn Huskarl Goals**

| d6 | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1 | I will find my king’s long-lost child so that my kingdom will have a true heir. |
| 2 | My queen depends on me to find and slay the pretender who claims a blood right to her throne. |
| 3 | I must root out a hidden sect of the [[Cult of Ragnarok]] that grows within my jarl’s land. |
| 4 | Giant raiding parties threaten the land. My jarl calls upon me to destroy them and make the land safe once more. |
| 5 | I can only earn my freedom by finding the stolen crown of my sadistic monarch. |
| 6 | I serve only until I can discover a way to overthrow this corrupt king, who took the throne through dark sorcery. |

### Least Milestone Feature

When you achieve the first milestone of this thread, you gain the following benefit.

***Jarl’s Authority.*** The authority granted you by your loyal service can sway the opinions of others. Before rolling an Intimidation or Persuasion check to influence someone who knows and respects (or fears) your ruler, you can choose to treat the d20 roll as if you’d rolled a natural 15. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a long rest.

### Lesser Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain your choice of one of the following benefits.

***Extreme Loyalty.*** Your dedication to your ruler protects you from the control of others. If you end your turn while charmed, you may choose to take psychic damage equal to your character level, then end that condition.

***Jarl’s Gift.*** To help you carry out their will, your ruler gives you your choice of one of the following uncommon magic items (or another you and your GM agree upon): *[[Wondrous Items A–L#Bracers of Archery|bracers of archery]]*, *[[Potions#Defender Honey|defender’s honey]]*, *sentinel shield*, *[[Rods, Staffs & Wands#Wand of the War Mage, +1, +2, or +3|+1 wand of the war mage]]*, or *[[Magic Weapons#Weapon, +1, +2, or +3|+1 weapon]]*.

### Greater Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Undying Loyalty.*** The strength of your oath steels you when the tides of battle turn against you. When you are reduced to 0 HP, you can use this feature to instead set your hit points to be equal to your character level. This doesn’t help you against an effect that would kill you instantly without dealing damage.

Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again for 7 days.

### Superior Milestone Feature

When you achieve this level of milestone, you gain the following benefit.

***Grand Reward.*** The completion of this milestone earns you a powerful reward. Work with your GM to determine an appropriate very rare magic item, such as one of the following: *[[Magic Weapons#Weapon, +1, +2, or +3|+3 weapon]]*, *[[Magic Weapons#Dancing Sword|dancing sword]]*, *[[Magic Weapons#Oathbow|oathbow]]*, *[[Rods, Staffs & Wands#Staff of Thunder and Lightning|staff of thunder and lightning]]*, or *[[Wondrous Items A–L#Horn of the Hrimthursar|horn of the Hrimthursalr]]*.
