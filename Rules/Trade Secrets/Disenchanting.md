*Optional, from Trade Secrets.*

When magic items are created, the crafter infuses a portion of its own power into the item. With this optional rule, crafters can reverse this process, extracting that power from an item and refining it into an iridescent magical dust. This dust is known among magical crafters as *prima materia* (sometimes shortened to just “materia”). It’s a magical essence that exists, to some degree, in all things. When traded, prima materia is packaged in 1 ounce vials, at a cost of 50 gp per ounce.

## Using Prima Materia

Prima materia is prized for its use in creating permanent magic items, as a substitute for the crafter’s own power. However, it can also restore charges in certain magic items.

### Crafting Magic Items with Prima Materia

The most efficient use of prima materia is to supplement the use of a PC’s own magical power when crafting magic items. PCs can spend it on a 2-for-1 basis to increase the result of the required weekly INT checks. That is, two ounces of prima materia can be spent to add +1 to the check result.

Note that this stacks with the 1-for-1 increase afforded by spending Luck. Because prima materia is a more abundant resource than Luck, however, spending prima materia in this way can’t improve any one check result by more than one step of success.

For example, a wizard is attempting to create a *[[Magic Weapons#Dancing Sword|dancing sword]]*—a Very Rare magic item, requiring 25 weeks of crafting. She has access to 30 ounces of materia.

At the end of the first week, the wizard makes an INT (Arcana) check with a result of 13. Ordinarily, this would mean a week of progress toward crafting the *[[Magic Weapons#Dancing Sword|dancing sword]]*. Instead, she decides to spend 6 ounces of prima materia to change the 13 to a 16, giving her two weeks of progress instead.

Having improved the result by one step, she can’t improve her result any more for now. She saves the rest of her materia for future crafting checks.

### Regaining Charges

Numerous magic items have charges that deplete and then return over time. The recharge rate is variable among magic items, though. Some items regain all charges automatically at a set time, and some never regain them.

Materia gives you a chance to speed up the recharge rate. If the magic item rolls a die to determine recharges, you can use 2 ounces of prima materia to roll the recharge die twice. You must use the higher result.

For example, at dawn, a sorcerer’s *[[Rods, Staffs & Wands#Wand of Magic Missiles|wand of magic missiles]]* ordinarily regains 1d6 + 1 charges. He used six charges yesterday, and he’s pretty sure he’ll need it today too, so he’ll use prima materia to help the wand regain charge. The player rolls 1d6 + 1 twice, resulting in a 2 and a 4. He must take the higher result, so the wand regains 4 charges for a total of 5. Maybe that will be enough.

#### Overcharging with Prima Materia

Ordinarily, a magic item with charges can’t hold more charges than its maximum. That is, a 7-charge wand with only 1 charge spent can’t regain more than 1 charge. However, prima materia allows you to overcharge an item’s charge limit at some risk.

If, as a result of materia use, the die roll results in an overcharge, note how many charges it has above its maximum, then roll a d20 for each overcharge. On a 1, the item cracks from the strain, destroying it completely. For example, if an item has 4 overcharges, the player rolls 4d20 to see if the excess power destroys it. If a magic item survives until it reaches its maximum again, the item stabilizes.

A GM might wish to allow a PC to make an INT (Arcana) check (or other relevant ability check as you see fit) to reroll a result of 1 and prevent the destruction of the magic item by safely draining away excess charges. The DC of this check is based on the item’s rarity, as delineated in the **Overcharge Stabilization DC** table. One check is required for each overcharge a user has to drain away.

**Overcharge Stabilization DC**

| Item Rarity | INT (Arcana) DC |
|---|---|
| Common | 12 |
| Uncommon | 15 |
| Rare | 18 |
| Very Rare | 21 |
| Legendary | 24 |

> [!note] Types of Prima Materia
> While prima materia is by far the most encountered magical essence, others exist, especially on other planes. Their applications are somewhat obscure but they are known as **aura materia**, **terra materia**, **pyro materia**, and **aqua materia .** These four elemental essences are abundant on their respective planes and easy to harvest. Elemental creatures dissipate when destroyed; their dust forms these materia. **Inferna materia** is the essence of lost souls, and is found on fiendish planes, where it is treated as currency. Unlike other magical essences, inferna materia is a gel or viscous liquid suspension: glimmering particles floating in thick, dark ooze. **Celestia materia** is the essence of creatures from the celestial planes or, occasionally, particularly holy figures on the Material Plane. It takes the form of a physical relic, such as bones, hands, feathers, and so on. While celestia materia functions the same way that prima materia does, it is considered far too valuable to be put to such base uses. **Astra materia** and **prima etherea** are both quite common on the Astral and Ethereal Planes, respectively, where creatures seek out and gather its deposits. **Mechamateria** is found almost exclusively in the workings of mechadrons and takes the form of electrical energy (with rare cases of magnetic energy). It is notoriously difficult to extract from mechadrons without specially crafted containers. Handling **Necromateria** frequently proves fatal to the crafter. It is nevertheless highly prized by necromancers, who go to disturbing lengths to refine it from the souls of the recently dead.

## Disenchanting Magic Items

The main method of obtaining prima materia is to deconstruct magic items, reversing the crafting process and destroying the item, leaving only the item’s core form—a weapon, a piece of armor, a tool—and a small amount of prima materia. PCs can spend downtime disenchanting crafted magic items and extracting their magical essence. Disenchanting a magic item requires access to the same tools needed to craft it, along with time to focus on extracting the magical essence. It also requires proficiency in the Arcana skill (though the GM might wish to allow certain items to be disenchanted using the Nature or Religion skills).

Although creating a magic item is restricted by the PC’s level, a PC can disenchant magic items of any rarity, including legendary magic items, fabled magic items, and even sentient magic items. However, the more powerful the item, the longer it takes to disenchant.

***Time.*** Progress toward disenchanting magic items is resolved over days of downtime, with each degree of rarity taking longer to deconstruct, as shown on the **Days by Rarity** table. The GM can alter the number of days required to disenchant a specific item, adding or subtracting time as makes sense for the pace and story of a campaign.

Just as with crafting items, the intricate process of disenchanting magic items means that multiple characters can’t work together to expedite the procedure.

**Days by Rarity**

| Item Rarity | Days Required |
|---|---|
| Common | 1 |
| Uncommon | 2 |
| Rare | 10 |
| Very Rare | 25 |
| Legendary | 50 |

***Cost.*** As opposed to crafting magic items, disenchanting them does not require any money, beyond the cost of tools, and some sort of container for the magical dust (usually a potion vial).

***Resolution.*** At the end of each day spent disenchanting a magic item, a PC makes an INT (Arcana) check (or another ability check the GM allows). The result of this check determines how much usable prima materia is extracted from the magic item, as shown on the **Magic Item Extraction Resolution** table. When the PC resolves the check, the magic item is reduced to a nonmagical item of its type, along with a small pile of iridescent dust.

For example, a wizard disenchants a *+1 dagger* (1,000 gp value) and the check results in a 19. That extracts prima materia equal to 20 percent of the dagger’s value (200 gp) and takes two days, because the item is Uncommon. At 50 gp per ounce, the wizard has extracted 4 ounces of prima materia. He can continue to disenchant the dagger with two more days of downtime.

**Magic Item Extraction Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | No prima materia extracted; item is destroyed. |
| 11–15 | Extract prima materia equal to 10% of item’s gp value. |
| 16–20 | Extract prima materia equal to 20% of item’s gp value. |
| 21–24 | Extract prima materia equal to 25% of item’s gp value. |
| 25+ | Extract prima materia equal to 30% of item’s gp value. |

**Maximum Prima Materia by Rarity**

| Item Rarity | Maximum Prima Materia |
|---|---|
| Common | 10 |
| Uncommon | 20 |
| Rare | 100 |
| Very Rare | 250 |
| Legendary | 500 |

### Disenchanting Sentient Items

Although magical essence can be extracted from most magic items, the sentient ones rarely go quietly. It might plead, make demands, or otherwise attempt to bargain or reason with the PC that is effectively killing it. If all else fails, it might fight back.

When a PC attempts to disenchant a sentient magic item, and the item is aware of the attempt, the PC and the item are immediately in conflict, as described in the *Game Master’s Guide*. Because the item’s existence is at stake, the item attempts to take control of the PC, at least long enough to thwart the disenchantment attempt. It tries to make sure the item can be safely handed off to someone who isn’t attempting to murder it.

While the sentient magic item attempts to control the PC (forcing a CHA save with a DC equal to 12 + the item’s CHA modifier), it might take other measures. If the item has offensive capabilities, it uses those against the PC, and anyone else who might wish to harm it.

Note that a sentient magic item can attempt to take control of its wielder (in this case, the PC attempting to disenchant it) once per day. This means that the rarer the item is and the longer it takes to extract its prima materia, the more opportunities it has to protect itself in this way.

> [!note] Which Items Can Be Disenchanted
> In general, any permanent or enchanted or consumable magic item can be disenchanted to extract its prima materia. If a magic item is paired (such as with boots and gloves), both items in a pair must be disenchanted together to extract the prima materia. Consumable magic items, such as potions and scrolls crumble to dust when their prima materia is extracted.

## Extracting Prima Materia from Creatures

At the GM’s discretion, PCs can also extract materia from the remains of certain creatures. Note that this can veer into creepy territory for some players, so do a quick check to make sure everyone is feeling OK about the topic.

This process is like that for disenchanting magic items, with two major differences: First, the creature must be dead. Second, it takes less time to extract magical essence from a creature than from magic items.

When the PCs have a dead creature available, and the GM rules that its remains contain magical essence, a PC can choose a portion of the remains, remove that part, and grind, file, blend, and otherwise reduce the part to a suspension. The PC can then separate out the magical essence from the physical material.

Extracting prima materia from a creature’s corpse requires access to the same magical tools needed to craft or disenchant items, along with the necessary time. It also requires proficiency in the Arcana skill, though the GM might wish to allow certain creatures to fall under the purview of the Nature or Religion skills.

***Time.*** Extracting magical essence from dead creatures takes a relatively short amount of downtime—from a few minutes to several hours, as shown on the **Time by Challenge Rating** table. The GM can increase or decrease the time required to extract prima materia from dead creatures, in whatever way makes sense for the pace and story of the campaign.

Extracting magical essence from creatures precludes multiple characters working together to reduce the overall time.

**Time by Challenge Rating**

| Creature CR | Time Required |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | 30 minutes |
| 5–10 | 1 hour |
| 11–15 | 4 hours |
| 16–24 | 1 day |
| 25+ | 1 week |

***Cost.*** Extracting magical essence from creatures doesn’t require any money beyond the cost of tools and a container for the magical dust (usually a potion vial).

***Resolution.*** At the end of the indicated time, a PC rolls an INT (Arcana) check. The result of this check determines how much usable prima materia is extracted, as indicated on the **Creature Extraction Resolution** table.

**Creature Extraction Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | No prima materia extracted; remains are destroyed. |
| 11–15 | Extract ounces of materia equal to the creature’s CR. |
| 16–20 | Extract ounces of materia equal to the creature’s CR × 2. |
| 21–24 | Extract ounces of materia equal to the creature’s CR × 5. |
| 25+ | Extract ounces of materia equal to the creature’s CR × 10. |

> [!note] Constructs and Undead
> Refining prima materia from creatures requires that the creature be dead before the extraction process begins. However, Undead are by definition not dead, and Constructs are usually animate, rather than strictly alive. Extracting prima materia from these sorts of creatures requires destroying or somehow rendering them incapacitated. (It also helps if they are unable to move.) This means that Constructs or Undead that are immune to the incapacitated condition must be reduced to 0 HP prior to extracting their magical essence.

> [!note] Which Creatures Contain Prima Materia?
> [!note] > Exactly what creature might contain prima materia, in all of the many worlds of fantasy, is ultimately a call for the GM. As a guideline, consider whether the description of a creature’s type mentions that it is native to another plane and whether the creature is described magical in nature. Here’s a non-exhaustive list of creatures and creature types that might well contain prima materia:
>
> - Celestials
> - Constructs (not including robots)
> - Dragons
> - Elementals
> - Fey
> - Fiends
> - Skeletons and zombies
> - Sorcerers
