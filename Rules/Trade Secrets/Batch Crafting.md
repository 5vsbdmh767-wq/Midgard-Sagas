Trade skills downtime activities are more detailed versions of the [[Between Adventures#Crafting|crafting downtime activity]] (see *Player’s Guide*). Use these options for a more story-oriented downtime with higher risks and rewards.

These downtime activities assume the PC is pursuing these trades independently or with a small group rather than as part of full scale, organized production with many other workers. While most of the products are trade goods, some nontrade goods are included which could be sold in local markets.

Consider building on a downtime’s resolution description to create a full encounter where the PCs’ choices determine the outcome rather than just making an ability check. Mining could uncover a monster-filled catacomb, farms could be beset by floods, and caravans could be raided by bandits. Granted, this challenges the notion of “downtime” as a background activity, but in a merchant campaign, it can be fun to turn mundane commercial activities into a springboard for adventure.

## Brewing

You spend your downtime brewing small batches of beer, wine, or spirits. Brewing can be an expensive hobby, but it can be profitable if you are skilled enough to make high-quality drinks.

***Time.*** To brew alcohol, a PC must spend at least one week of downtime preparing for fermentation and finishing the drinks. In addition, each beverage has a fermentation and aging period before it can be sold or drunk, as shown on the **Brewing Time** table. Since those processes don’t require constant attention, the PC is free to do other activities during that period.

**Brewing Time**

| Product | Fermentation and Aging Time |
|---|---|
| Beer | 4 weeks |
| Spirits | 1 week |
| Tea (any nonalcoholic) | None |
| Wine | 10 weeks |

***Cost.*** A PC must spend 50 gp on initial brewing equipment. This cost only needs to be spent once for a given type of drink being produced. A PC also needs a secure location for brewing equipment and fermentation containers. Finally, the PC must spend gold for raw ingredients for the week’s brewing based on the product they want to make, as determined by the **Brewing Cost** table.

**Brewing Cost**

| Drink | Cost |
|---|---|
| Beer, Cheap | 20 gp |
| Beer, Common | 40 gp |
| Beer, High Quality | 60 gp |
| Spirits, Cheap | 30 gp |
| Spirits, Common | 60 gp |
| Spirits, High Quality | 200 gp |
| Tea (any nonalcoholic) | 20 gp |
| Wine, Cheap | 50 gp |
| Wine, Common | 100 gp |
| Wine, High Quality | 500 gp |

***Resolution.*** At the end of each week spent brewing, the PC can make a WIS (Provisioner Tools) check to determine success, as shown on the **Brewing Resolution** table.

**Brewing Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Disaster. Anyone drinking the resulting brew must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be poisoned for 1 hour. |
| 6–10 | The brew is bad and can’t be sold. |
| 11–20 | The brew is good and can be sold at a base price equal to cost × 1.5. |
| 21–25 | The brew is excellent and can be sold at a base price equal to cost × 2. |
| 26+ | The brew is amazing and can be sold at a base price equal to cost × 3. |

## Farming

You spend your downtime tending to a small farm, raising plants and animals for food and other agricultural products. Farming isn’t a hobby, because it takes considerable time and effort to see the fruits of your labor. However, small investments can lead to large profits.

***Time.*** To farm, a PC must spend at least twenty-four weeks (two seasons) of downtime tending to their plants and animals.

***Cost.*** A PC must have at least four acres of farmland and a small farmhouse to pursue farming. Costs beyond the farm and farmland are based on what the PC grows. During farming, a PC doesn’t need to pay for food or drink because the farm sustain them. Before farming, choose up to four of the following farming options. Each option can be chosen more than once. Add the cost of all choices together for the starting investment or stake.

**Farming Options**

| Product (Supply) | Category | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Barley (seeds) | Plant | 10 gp |
| Cattle & Milk (calves) | Animal | 30 gp |
| Cotton (seeds) | Plant | 15 gp |
| Eggs (chickens or geese) | Animal | 5 gp |
| Hair (kids) | Animal | 15 gp |
| Flax (seed) | Plant | 12 gp |
| Furs (rabbits) | Animal | 10 gp |
| Hemp (seed) | Plant | 14 gp |
| Horses (foals) | Animal | 40 gp |
| Nettles (seeds) | Plant | 10 gp |
| Oats (seeds) | Plant | 8 gp |
| Pigs (piglets) | Animal | 15 gp |
| Rye (seed) | Plant | 10 gp |
| Sheep & Wool (lambs) | Animal | 20 gp |
| Silk (eggs) | Animal | 50 gp |
| Vegetables (seeds) | Plant | 25 gp |
| Wheat (seeds) | Plant | 12 gp |

***Resolution.*** At the end of the 24-week farming period, first roll on the farming conditions table for each category being farmed. Next, for each type of farming option chosen, make an INT (Survival) or WIS (Nature), applying the modifiers from the **Farming Conditions** table for that option’s category. Determine success by consulting the **Farming Resolution** table.

**Farming Conditions**

| d4 | Resolution Modifier |
|---|---|
| 1 | Disadvantage on resolution |
| 2–3 | No modifier |
| 4 | Advantage on resolution |

**Farming Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | The crops/animals are afflicted by disease, predation, or weather. If you farm another season, you can farm the same options by repaying the full cost. |
| 6–10 | Hardships result in poor harvest. You produce goods with a base price equal to your costs. If you farm another season, you can farm the same options paying half the listed cost. |
| 11–20 | An average harvest. You produce goods with a base price equal to your costs × 12. If you farm another season, you can farm the same options without paying the listed costs. |
| 21–25 | An excellent yield. You produce goods with a base price equal to your costs × 20. If you farm another season, you can farm the same options without paying the listed costs. |
| 26+ | A bumper crop! You produce goods with a base price equal to your costs × 30. If you farm another season, you can farm the same options without paying the listed costs. |

## Foraging

You spend your downtime searching for rare ingredients used in medicine, magic, or alchemy. These are typically found in wild and remote places that can be dangerous for the unwary. Foragers usually travel alone or in a group of two. A wise forager never overharvests a site, so they can return the next year.

***Time.*** To forage, a PC must spend at least one week of downtime away from the comforts of civilization, searching wild places.

***Cost.*** A PC must provision themselves with food and water for one week as well as survival equipment appropriate for the environment. Spellcasters can substitute these requirements with magic that provide food, water, and shelter.

***Resolution.*** Before foraging, the PC must state what category of botanical ingredients they are searching for: medicine, arcane ink, spell components, or alchemical reagents. At the end of each week spent foraging, the PC makes an INT (Nature) or WIS (Herbalist Tools) check.

**Foraging Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | You run into trouble in the wilderness, finding nothing of value and spending an additional week getting out of trouble. |
| 11–15 | You find only the value of the inherent beauty of nature. |
| 16–20 | You find 100 gp worth of ingredients for common arcane inks, medicine, spell components, or alchemical reagents |
| 21–25 | You find 150 gp worth of ingredients for fine arcane inks, uncommon medicine, uncommon spell components, or alchemical reagents. |
| 26+ | You find 250 gp worth of ingredients for high quality arcane ink, medicine, spell components, or alchemical reagents. |

## Hunting and Trapping

You spend your downtime hunting and trapping wild animals and weaker monsters. Unlike adventuring, hunters are looking to defeat prey without risking injury.

***Time.*** To hunt or trap, a PC must spend at least one week of downtime.

***Cost.*** A PC must bring or find food and water for a week as well as equipment appropriate for the environment. In addition, the PC must bring twenty arrows (or suitable ammunition) that is expended during the hunt.

***Resolution.*** At the end of each week spent hunting, the PC leading the hunt can make a WIS (Survival) or WIS (Trapper Tools) check to determine success, as shown on the **Hunting and Tracking Resolution** table.

**Hunting and Tracking Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | You are waylaid by powerful monsters or catastrophic weather conditions. You find nothing and waste an additional week fighting for survival. |
| 6–10 | You enjoy a week in the wilds but fail to catch any valuable prey. |
| 11–20 | You find common animals and collect 70 gp in meat and hides. |
| 21–25 | You have a fantastic hunt finding top-quality prey. You collect 150 gp in meat, hides, and common monster parts. |
| 26+ | You hunt down truly exotic and valuable prey, collecting 250 gp in uncommon monster parts. |

## Mining

You spend your downtime mining ore or minerals from a known deposit. It’s hard work, but it keeps you in good shape between adventures and it’s almost guaranteed to make money. The trick is knowing where to mine.

***Time.*** To mine, a PC must spend at least one week of downtime.

***Cost.*** A PC must have mining equipment such as a pickaxe, a lantern, and a donkey cart. The PC must have access to a known deposit of the type of ore or minerals they want to mine. If they don’t know of one, they must first spend downtime prospecting to find one (see **Prospecting**).

***Resolution.*** At the end of each week spent mining, the PC can make a STR (Athletics) check to determine success, as shown on the **Mining Resolution** table. This resolution doesn’t include time needed to move the ore from the mining site to a location where it can be sold or processed, which can be its own undertaking. The base price for each ton of a given ore or mineral is found in the **List of Trade Goods** section.

**Mining Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | You injure yourself and spend the week nursing sprained muscles instead of collecting minerals. |
| 6–10 | You collect 1 ton of ore or minerals. |
| 11–20 | You work hard and collect 10 tons of ore or minerals. |
| 21–25 | You work a rich vein and collect 20 tons of ore or minerals. |
| 26+ | It’s the motherlode! You collect 30 tons of ore or minerals as well as valuable gemstones worth 200 gp. |

## Pottery

You spend your downtime creating pottery.

***Time.*** To make pottery, a PC must spend at least one week of downtime molding and firing their creations.

***Cost.*** A PC must spend 5 sp for standard clay to make normal pottery or 5 gp for fine clay to make porcelain.

***Resolution.*** At the end of each week making pottery, the PC can make a DEX (Artist Tools) or CHA (Artist Tools) check to determine success, as shown on the **Pottery Resolution** table.

**Pottery Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Your work cracks in the kilns and is useless. |
| 6–10 | You make functional pottery, but it won’t win any beauty contests. You can sell your work for 1 gp. |
| 11–20 | You make functional, well-formed pottery with a base price equal to your costs × 10. |
| 21–25 | You produce artful pottery. It has a base price equal to your costs × 20. |
| 26+ | Your pottery inspires odes. Provided you can find wealthy buyers who appreciate it, it has a base price equal to your costs × 100. |

## Processing

You spend your downtime processing organic raw materials to make viable trade products. It is mostly tedious work, but it’s a reliable way to make income.

***Time.*** To process organic trade goods, a PC must spend at least one day of downtime.

***Cost.*** A PC must acquire raw materials needed for processing the goods they intend to sell. Note that some of these materials are exotic in and of themselves and may not be readily available, but these can serve as a stand in for similar local products that require manual processing to be salable.

***Resolution.*** At the end of each day spent processing, the PC produces goods as listed on the **Processing Cost and Resolution** table with the listed base price. These are base cost and price, and you can modify them with market modifier tables in the **Market Price** section to add variability.

**Processing Cost and Resolution**

| Material | Base Cost | Good Produced | Base Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cinnamon Tree Bark | 5 gp | Cinnamon | 10 gp |
| Clove Buds | 15 gp | Clove | 30 gp |
| Fruits and Sweetener | 5 sp | Preserves | 5 gp |
| Ginger Root | 1 gp | Dried ginger | 6 gp |
| Meat and Salt | 10 gp | Jerky | 20 gp |
| Peppercorns | 12 gp | Pepper | 25 gp |
| Vegetables and Vinegar | 1 sp | Pickles | 3 gp |

## Prospecting

You spend your downtime prospecting for mineral deposits. Before prospecting you need to determine which type of terrain is available and which sort of mineral you are looking for. The GM needs to determine which terrains are available to prospect in and the player needs to choose one of those terrains to search.

**Mineral Terrain**

| Terrain Type | Minerals Found There |
|---|---|
| Mountain Ranges, Rock Formations | Copper, Gold, Granite, Iron, Lead, Obsidian, Tin, Silver |
| Riverbeds, Flood Basins | Clay, Tin |
| Arid Regions, Gorges, Canyons | Limestone, Sandstone, Salt |
| Coastal Areas | Clay, Salt |

***Time.*** To prospect for minerals, a PC must spend at least two weeks of downtime in a wilderness area searching for signs of ore.

***Cost.*** A PC must provision themselves with food and water for two weeks as well as survival equipment appropriate for the environment. Spellcasters can substitute magic that provides food, water, and shelter. In addition, a pickaxe is needed to take samples.

***Resolution.*** At the end of each two-week period, the PC can make an INT (Nature) or INT (Alchemist Tools) check to determine success, as shown on the **Prospecting Resolution** table. The PC can discover a source of any listed mineral at or below the result they get, provided it can be found in the place they are prospecting. Prospecting only gives the PC a location where they can later mine the mineral.

**Prospecting Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | You find nothing worth mining or collecting beyond nice views and fresh air. |
| 6–14 | You find a source of granite, iron ore, limestone, salt, or sandstone. |
| 15–20 | You find a source of copper or lead ore. |
| 21–25 | You find a source of tin or silver ore. |
| 26+ | You find a source of adamantine, gold, or mithral ore. |

***Time.*** To sew finished goods, a PC must spend at least one week of downtime.

***Cost.*** A PC must buy cloth and thread for sewing. The type of cloth determines the cost.

**Sewing Cost**

|---|---|
| Burlap | 5 gp |
| Linen | 10 gp |
| Silk | 50 gp |

## Smelting

You spend your downtime smelting ore into metal ingots. Smelting is difficult to do on a small scale, but with the right skills and determination it is possible. Smaller smelters can rarely compete with larger, cheaper, and more sophisticated production.

***Time.*** To smelt ore, a PC must spend at least four weeks of downtime.

***Cost.*** A PC must have access to a high temperature kiln and bellows which cost 50 gp and a set of smithing tools, but these can be used repeatedly. In addition, smelting requires charcoal fuel and other supplies that costs 10 gp per smelting attempt. Finally, the PC must pay for a ton of ore to use in smelting at the base cost listed for that type of ore listed in the **List of Trade Goods** section.

***Resolution.*** At the end of four-week period, the PC can make a DEX (Smithing Tools) or CON (Smithing Tools) check to determine success, as shown on the **Smelting Resolution** table.

**Smelting Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | The smelting effort fails. The metal you produced is unsuitable for any functional purpose. If you smelt again, you can use this material instead of paying for ore. |
| 6–10 | You smelt your ore successfully, but it lacks purity and is good for only limited use. You can sell the ingots at a base price equal to your cost plus 100 gp. |
| 11–20 | You smelt good quality metals from your ore and can sell the ingots at a base price equal to your cost × 4. |
| 21–25 | You smelt superior quality metals from your ore and can sell the ingots at a base price equal to your cost × 5. |
| 26+ | You smelt pristine quality metals from your ore and can sell the ingots at a base price equal to your cost × 6. |

## Smithing

You spend your downtime making metal armor, tools, and weapons. Some adventurers take pride in smithing their own gear, and you can earn a tidy sum if you have sufficient skill.

***Time.*** A PC must spend at least a day of downtime, though many items take longer. The PC must decide what to make. Divide the base price of the item by ten to get the number of days smithing required to complete the item. If an item costs less than 10 gp, you can make additional items so long as the total is 10 gp or less.

***Cost.*** A PC must have access to a forge and a set of smithing tools. The PC must also pay half the base cost of the item for materials and forge supplies. If the item is to be made from a special material, other than masterwork, the PC must also pay the full material cost.

***Resolution.*** Once the time needed to make a given item is completed, the PC can make a DEX (Smithing Tools) or CON (Smithing Tools) check to determine success, as shown on the **Smithing Resolution** table.

**Smithing Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | The equipment you make would barely feed a rust monster. If you want to try again, you can melt it down to pay your materials cost. |
| 6–10 | The equipment you make is of poor quality. You can sell it at a base price equal to the cost you paid or use it yourself. |
| 11–20 | The equipment you make is of good quality. You can sell it at a base price equal to the item’s normal base cost plus twice the materials price. |
| 21–25 | The equipment you make is of superior quality. You can sell it at a base price equal to the item’s normal base cost × 2, plus twice the materials price. |
| 26+ | The equipment is a true masterpiece. You can sell it at a base price equal to the item’s normal base cost × 3, plus twice the materials price. If it is a weapon, it gains the masterwork material type. |

***Time.*** To tan hides, a PC must spend at least two weeks of downtime.

***Cost.*** A PC must buy hides to tan. The cost depends on the type of hides being worked.

**Hide Cost**

| Goatskin | 15 gp |
| Sheepskin | 20 gp |

**Tanning Resolution**

## Woodcutting

You spend your downtime cutting down trees and cutting timber into lumber. It’s hard work, but wood grows on trees, so it’s basically free if you have access to a forest or grove. You can cut down trees for timber or cut timber to make lumber products. This task doesn’t include the effort needed to move the wood from the cutting site.

***Time.*** To cut wood, a PC must spend at least a week at the task.

***Cost.*** A PC must have appropriate axes or saws for the task and access to the wood they want to cut.

***Resolution.*** At the end of each week spent woodcutting, the PC can make a STR (Athletics) or CON (Athletics) check to determine success, as shown on the **Woodcutting Resolution** table.

**Woodcutting Resolution**

| Check Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | You break your tool, ending your woodcutting for the day. You must buy a new tool. |
| 6–15 | You cut 1 ton of wood. |
| 16–20 | You work hard and cut 2 tons of wood. |
| 21–25 | You cut wood like a woodchuck and have 4 tons of wood to show for it. |
| 26+ | Forests fear your mighty axe! You cut 6 tons of wood. |
