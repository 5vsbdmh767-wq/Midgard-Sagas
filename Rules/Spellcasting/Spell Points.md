If your GM opts to allow its use, PCs with the Spellcasting class feature can adopt this variant method of casting to forgo the use of spell slots and instead use a pool of points to cast their spells. This variant system is more complex than standard spellcasting but allows for greater flexibility.

## Spell Point Pool

When using this variant, replace the spell slots you would normally gain with a number of spell points to determine your total available spell point pool. The maximum number of spell points in your pool is determined by your spellcasting class and your level in that class. You receive a different number of points at each level, based on whether you are a full spellcasting class or half caster class or whether you receive the Spellcasting feature through a subclass.

Your class type and level also determine the highest circle spell you can cast with these points. You can’t cast spells of higher circles, even if you have enough points in your pool to do so. See the **Using Spell Points** section for additional rules and restrictions regarding the use of spell points.

### Full Spellcasting Class

A full spellcasting class receives the Spellcasting class feature at 1st level (see class progression tables in the *Player’s Guide* or **Chapter 1** of this book) and eventually learns to cast up to 9th-circle spells. Full spellcasting classes include the bard, cleric, druid, sorcerer, theurge, witch, and wizard. If you are playing a full spellcasting class, use the **Spell Points by Full Spellcasting Class Level** table to determine your maximum spell points at each level and the highest circle spell you can cast with points at each level.

Half caster classes include the paladin, ranger, and warlock. ### Subclass Caster

A subclass caster gains the ability to cast spells through its subclass chosen at 3rd level instead of the Spellcasting feature from its base class. Subclass casters eventually learn to cast spells up to 4th circle. Examples of subclass casters include the Spell Blade fighter subclass in the *Player’s Guide* *or the Con Arcanist rogue subclass in* **Chapter 1** of this book*.* If you are playing a subclass caster, use the **Spell Points by Subclass Caster Class Level** table to determine your maximum spell points at each level and the highest circle spell you can cast with points at each level.

## Using Spell Points

With this ruleset, every spell in the game is assigned a point cost based on the circle of the spell, as shown in the **Spell Points by Circle** table. You expend points from your pool to mimic the process of expending a spell slot of the circle required by the spell. You can’t reduce the total points in your pool below zero, and you regain all spent points when you finish a long rest.

***Cantrips and Rituals.*** Neither cantrips nor ritual spells expend spell slots in the base rules, so they don’t require points to cast under this variant rule.

***Casting At Higher Circles.*** Casting spells at higher circles with spell points works idntically to casting spells with higher spell slots. To cast a spell you know at a higher circle, spend points equivalent to the value of the higher spell slot. The values of each spell slot are the same as the value of it spell, as shown in the **Spell Points by Circle** table. Remember, you must still abide by restrictions around the highest circle spell you can cast as described in the **Spell Point Pool** section.

***High Circle Restrictions.*** Due to the sheer energy necessary to channel spells of the highest circles, casters using this variant are still restricted in the total number of high-circle spells they can cast of certain levels between long rests. Regardless of the total number of points available in a spellcaster’s pool, a caster must abide by the following***:***

- **6th Circle.** Once you expend points to cast two 6th circle spells, you must complete a long rest before you can cast a 6th circle spell again.

- **7th Circle.** Once you expend points to cast two 7th circle spells, you must complete a long rest before you can cast a 7th circle spell again.

- **8th Circle.** Once you expend points to cast an 8th-circle spell, you must complete a long rest before you can cast an 8th circle spell again.

- **9th Circle.** Once you expend points to cast a 9th-circle spell, you must complete a long rest before you can cast a 9th circle spell again.

***Recovering Spell Points.*** Recovering spell points works identically to recovering spell slots. You regain all spell points when you finish a long rest. If your class has a feature that allows you to recover spell slots in some other way, like the wizard’s Arcane Recovery feature, calculate how many spell points are equivalent to the slots recovered. For example, a 4th-level wizard using Arcane Recovery to recover a single 2nd-circle slot would translate to recovering 3 spell points using this variant. If that wizard chose instead to recover two 1st-circle slots, that would translate to recovering 4 spell points.
