%% source: Trade Secrets %%

When the PCs are on a tight schedule, track how much time they spend pursuing this particular downtime. Depending on the local markets and the PC’s goals, they might be able to pick up what they want at the best prices, find unexpected treasures at under standard value, or just check items off of their list. Eventually, shops do close for the day.

## Shopping

*New downtime activity.* See [[Between Adventures]] for the general downtime rules.

When you set out to shop as a downtime activity, select one of the following options, or describe your shopping objective and ask your GM which of these is the most appropriate method of achieving your goals.

***Shopping List.*** Go to exactly the right shop and buy exactly what you need. This is the default method for PCs to replenish adventuring supplies. Use this option when you just want to hit the shops, buy everything on their list, back to the action, and maybe manage a little discount in the process.

***Bargains.*** Sometimes, adventurers want to get the best deal, or maybe they’re looking for a bulk discount on consumable items, like potions, ammunition, or scrolls. Use this option when you know exactly what you want but are willing to try a few more shops to get the best value.

***Browsing.*** Some merchants have out-of-place treasures in their stock that are worth more than they know: an ornate ceremonial sword that’s a *[[Magic Weapons#Holy Avenger|holy avenger]]*; an *[[Wondrous Items A–L#Amulet of Health|amulet of health]]* in a pile of costume jewelry; *[[Wondrous Items M–Z#Slippers of Spider Climbing|slippers of spider climbing]]* that are tucked in the pockets of a second-hand robe. A savvy PC, perhaps with *[[1st-Circle Spells#Detect Magic|detect magic]]* at their disposal, could walk into a shop with a couple hundred pieces of gold, and stroll out with a *[[Wondrous Items A–L#Bag of Holding|bag of holding]]*. Use this option when you don’t have anything specific in mind; they just want to browse for a deal that’s just too good to pass up.

The GM can choose an item to find from within the ranges established by the **Settlement Economy Limits** table, or randomly determine something, such as by rolling on the **Random Magic Item** tables in the *Game Master’s Guide*.

Note, however, that because powerful items can easily unbalance a campaign, choose the out-of-place item carefully or even put limits on its capabilities (such as the *[[Wondrous Items A–L#Bag of Beans|bag of beans]]* having only 1d4 beans left in it).

***Time.*** To shop, you must spend at least 1 hour of downtime visiting local markets and businesses in a settlement.

***Cost.*** Shopping doesn’t require you to spend money, unless you find something you want to purchase. However, note that the maximum amount of money you can spend in any given settlement is based on its size, as shown on the **Settlement Economy Limits** table.

### Settlement Economy Limits

This table lists the reasonable maximum value of goods in any given size of settlement. Settlements only have so much wealth in terms of goods, items, and coin. Therefore, you can only buy so much before the settlement has nothing left for an adventurer.

**Settlement Economy Limits**

| Settlement Size | Maximum Gold |
|---|---|
| Hamlet | 500 gp |
| Village | 5,000 gp |
| Town | 20,000 gp |
| City | 50,000 gp |
| Metropolis | 200,000 gp |

***Resolution.*** After each hour spent shopping, make an INT (Investigation) check. Then consult the appropriate column on the **Shopping Resolution** table, based on shopping goals, to determine if you complete their shopping list, find an item at bargain prices, or discover an overlooked treasure.

**Shopping Resolution**

| Check Result | Shopping List Resolution | Bargain Resolution | Browsing Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | List incomplete. | Pay 110% of list price. | Nothing interesting found. |
| 6–10 | List complete, but add 1 hour. | Pay 100% of list price. | Nothing interesting found. |
| 11–15 | List complete. | Pay 90% of list price. | Find an item in the current settlement’s gp range. |
| 16–20 | List complete. | Pay 75% of list price. | Find an item in the current settlement’s gp range, at 75% of list price. |
| 21+ | List complete. | Pay 50% of list price. | Find an item from the next higher settlement size’s gp range, or an item in the current settlement’s gp range at 50% of list price.* |

\* Maximum gp limit: 200,000 gp.

> [!note] Why Not CHA?
> While shopping and haggling are related, shopping as a downtime activity is more about finding the merchandise than convincing its owner to knock a little off the price. Therefore, this system requires an INT (Investigation) check.
>
> Negotiating for a better price, however, requires a CHA (Persuasion) check, even if the results of a bargain attempt indicate that the current owner is only asking for half the usual price.

## Where to Shop

Although you might imagine downtime shopping in individual buildings and storefronts, PCs are more likely to buy gear and sell loot in a market. Each and every merchant’s stall, wagon, cart, or blanket on the ground is laden with valuable goods, from produce to magic weapons. Finding what you’re after requires asking around.

Markets carry challenges for both merchants and customers. Open-air venues are subject to weather conditions, and the temporary nature of a market square’s business means that a given merchant can be there one day, and gone the next. A particular seller might not return for a week while they follow a circuit from town to town.

### Markets

Markets tend to open around the same time each morning, and close around the same time each evening. While pretty much everything is available every day, a certain cadence develops as various local farmers bring their goods to market on the same day every week, or a traveling trader returns once every month or so. The locals build their weekly schedules around the merchants’, buying grain and flour one day, for example, then returning the next morning for fresh milk and eggs, and the next for fish and meat.

The kinds of goods adventurers purchase tend to be available all week long. But the rarer an item, the more time is likely to pass between merchants passing through who carry it. ### Auctions

Auctions usually take place in a public location large enough for the crowds that gather to take advantage of a bargain. The town square is a common choice. Private auctions are unusual in cities, rare in towns, and almost unheard of in villages. In larger areas, they might occur behind closed doors in private residences or merchant guildhalls, with secure doors and plenty of armed guards to make sure everyone behaves.

Public auctions are announced well in advance of the event, giving buyers time to make financial arrangements. Even though a buyer might outbid competitors during the auction, every successful bidder must pay for every item when the auction concludes. If they can’t pay immediately, the item is offered to the next-highest bidder; there is no “holding” an item while the bidder goes out to secure funds.

Private auctions follow the same rules, but they require a formal invitation. Bidders who cause any sort of trouble are removed from the premises and not invited again. The less legal the goods on offer are, the more likely the auction staff is to take extreme measures to deal with problematic bidders.

### The Black Market

The criminal underworld is an informal collection of those who have forbidden items for sale as well as those who don’t question the provenance of goods before them. They don’t identify themselves as black marketeers, though, and have no specific gathering place.

To make an illicit purchase (or to sell ill-gotten goods), a PC must make a successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) check to find black market merchants, as well as to learn how to approach them. (Characters with a criminal background have advantage on this check.) Success indicates that the PC has found a trader with flexible ethics and morality.

Note that black market doesn’t pay well for loot: about 25% of any given item’s value, rather than the usual 50%, if the merchant has reason to believe that trading for the item could get them arrested. Black market merchants also charge more for the illegal goods that they sell, about 125% of the item’s value.

### Fairs

Communities often hold fairs to attract large numbers of merchants and traders to do business in the area. Usually held in an empty field or meadow bordering the town, these events are announced well in advance (if they’re not a regular occurrence), and visiting merchants outnumber the local shopkeepers by at least three to one. The roster of traveling merchants changes from town to town, though there’s usually a core contingent for whom the road is their home.

Fairs are not just mercantile events, however. Many types of entertainers follow traveling fairs to where the crowds gather. Jugglers, acrobats, magicians, and acting troupes are all common sights.

***Games.*** Various booths and stalls are set up for crowd-pleaser contests for small prizes like a medallion or a purse of copper coins. Each contest usually costs 1 cp to enter, and involves a contested ability check, as indicated on the **Fair Games** table.

### Festivals

More tied to religious holidays than trade fairs, festivals nonetheless include many of the same delights: traveling merchants, entertainers, and criminals. Often held in empty fields next to a temple or shrine associated with the occasion, most festivals are smaller than trade fairs and advertised by word of mouth, from the religious leaders to the congregation to their friends.
