%% source: Night Hunters %%

Curses, diseases, and hazards are excellent tools to increase encounter difficulty and variety and to introduce plot twists. All of these share a format to make them easy to run, and the similarities and differences of each are explained. The sample curses, diseases, and hazards in this section include preset DCs, damage, and mechanical elements ready to use without modification. However, you can adjust any of these encounter elements to be appropriate for PCs of various levels and skill sets. For more information on using curses, diseases, and hazards, and for more samples of them, see the *Game Master’s Guide*.

## Elements of a Curse, Disease, or Hazard

Each curse, disease, and hazard description includes the following information:

- **Trigger:** This describes the conditions that must be met in order to activate the effects of the curse, disease, or hazard. For example, a curse’s trigger might specify how a target becomes exposed to it.

- **Effects:** This describes what happens after the trigger condition is met. It includes ability checks, saves, and other details required to help the GM determine what happens to targets. For example, a disease’s effects might detail the ongoing penalties to a target suffering from it.

- **Resolution:** This describes how a trigger can be avoided (if possible) and ways to stop any ongoing effects. For example, a hazard’s resolution might include details on how to mitigate or end its ongoing effects.

## Effect Severity

When inserting a curse, disease, or hazard, you can alter the degree of danger it poses—setback, dangerous, or deadly—by varying the attack bonus of an effect, the save DC to resist its effects, and the damage it deals, depending on its severity. Consult the **Effects Save DCs and Attack Bonuses** and **Damage Severity by Level** tables for suggestions based on the three levels of severity:

- A setback is unlikely to kill or seriously harm PCs of the indicated levels.

- A dangerous effect is likely to seriously injure (and potentially kill) PCs of the indicated levels.

- A deadly effect is likely to kill PCs of the indicated levels.

**Effects Save DCs and Attack Bonuses**

| Effect Danger | Save DC | Attack Bonuses |
|---|---|---|
| Setback | 10–11 | +3 to +5 |
| Dangerous | 12–15 | +6 to +8 |
| Deadly | 16–20 | +9 to +12 |

**Damage Severity by Level**

| PC Level | Setback | Dangerous | Deadly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 (1–4) | 5 (1d10) | 11 (2d10) | 22 (4d10) |
| Tier 2 (5–10) | 11 (2d10) | 22 (4d10) | 55 (10d10) |
| Tier 3 (11–16) | 22 (4d10) | 55 (10d10) | 99 (18d10) |
| Tier 4 (17–20) | 55 (10d10) | 99 (18d10) | 132 (24d10) |

## Curses

Curses are most commonly encountered when a PC attunes to a cursed magic item. They can also be passed on by a few monsters as part of an attack, such as the werewolf; and they can be tied to locations, individual creatures, or objects. This section details a collection of ready-to-use curses.

> [!note] Targeted Affliction
> Despite the dark nature of horror campaigns, inherited curses might not appeal to your gaming group. Though the house of malignance curse is intended to reflect the petty vengeance of fallen evils, having the deeds of family members used against their PC might not sit well with some players. If you wish to remove the hereditary nature of the house of malignance curse for your game, use the following alternative trigger to make it a lingering curse passed through bloodshed instead of bloodline:
>
> **Trigger:** After slaying a cursed creature, a creature must succeed on a DC 14 CHA save or be cursed and targeted by its effects. The curse has no effect on Constructs or Undead.

### Beastly Transformation

*Curse*

This curse might be bestowed upon a creature by a druid, witch, or faerie folk who was likely angered by the victim or is simply especially mischievous or wicked. The target is transformed almost completely into a beast—with little to no remnant of their former personality, memories, or abilities remaining. Often, these victims wander the wilderness where their transformation took place or linger near their allies or the creature that cursed them without understanding why.

**Trigger:** After angering a certain spellcaster (GM’s discretion), a creature must succeed on a DC 16 WIS save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The target immediately transforms into a Beast of the spellcaster’s choice—cats, frogs, lizards, rats, and ravens are favorites in these situations—and loses all their abilities, features, and traits. Their statistics are replaced with the statistics of the new form. The target has no memory of anything prior to the transformation, though they might linger in important or familiar areas, follow allies or enemies, or show interest in items that were previously important to them.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic partially suppresses the effects until the next dawn: the target remains transformed but regains their memories. The spellcaster that bestowed the curse might be convinced to remove it, though this usually demands payment or service in return.

### Bound to Nature

*Curse*

Inflicted by the divine upon those who seek to abuse the natural world, this curse preys on those who upset the order of the wilds. It is sometimes cultivated and spread by guardians of nature, such as dryads and treants.

**Trigger:** After destroying or otherwise despoiling the natural world—such as by killing the animals/plants, poisoning the soil, or polluting the air—of a region protected by a nature deity or certain Fey (GM’s discretion), a creature must succeed on a DC 15 CHA save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The target must make a DC 15 CHA save at the end of each hour spent in the affected region, typically underground or wilderness. On a failure, they are compelled to take on the nature of a CR 0 Beast endemic to the area (GM’s discretion). They retain their form, memories, and statistics, but their desires and personality now revolve around behaving like the Beast: perhaps howling at the stars, running through the forest, or splashing in puddles.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic—or else the target planting a seed in the soil of an urban area—suppresses the effects until the next dawn. If its effects are suppressed for 12 consecutive days, or if the target successfully befriends a wild animal without the use of magic, the curse is removed.

### Briar Rose

*Curse*

A favorite tool of hags and vengeful druids, this curse afflicts those who handle plants imbued with it. (Such plants are considered magical plants.)

**Trigger:** After taking at least 1 piercing damage from a certain plant (GM’s discretion)—such as taking damage from the briar growth hazard (see *Game Master’s Guide*)—a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects. The curse has no effect on Constructs, Undead, or creatures without blood.

**Effects:** Painful, thorny roots slowly sprout from the target’s body, hampering movement. Each time the target finishes a long rest or deals any amount of damage to a plant or Plant creature, their speed is reduced by 5 feet. This reduction can’t be offset while the target remains cursed. If the target’s speed is reduced to 0, their body is completely encased in vines, and they suffer all the following until the curse is ended: they gain the incapacitated condition, they can’t move or speak, they become unaware of their surroundings, they automatically fail STR and DEX saves, and attacks against them have advantage. If they remain in this state for 24 consecutive hours, they die—their body destroyed.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects for 24 hours, and a *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic removes the curse.

Alternatively, if the target plants a rose bush (or other thorny flowering plant) and waters it with at least 1 pint of their blood, the curse is removed. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 15 INT (Arcana or Nature) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Creeping Death

*Curse*

When a powerful spellcaster or otherworldly entity is cut down, they can use their dying breath to bestow a curse on the creature that dealt the final blow.

**Trigger:** After reducing a certain CR 10 or higher spellcaster or extraplanar entity to 0 HP (GM’s discretion), a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The curse remains dormant for 3d10 + 10 days. Once activated, each time the target finishes a long rest, they must succeed on a DC 15 WIS save or have their hit point maximum reduced by 3 (1d6). If the curse reduces the target’s hit point maximum to 0, they die.

**Resolution:** A *[[9th-Circle Spells#Wish|wish]]* spell or similar magic removes the curse.

### Cruelest Luck

*Curse*

A creature cursed with cruel luck rarely succeeds at anything they attempt, and when things go wrong, they do so catastrophically. Even an activity as simple as boiling water might lead to the target accidentally burning down their house—and their neighbors’ houses. The curse is typically only bestowed by deities or extremely powerful otherworldly creatures.

**Trigger:** After doing something that makes a deity or a certain Celestial, Fey, or Fiend feel wronged (GM’s discretion), a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** All ability checks, attack rolls, and saves are failed automatically unless a 20 is rolled on the d20. If the roll is a 1 on the d20 for an ability check or save, the attempt goes as poorly as possible: tools break, knots come untied, attempts to praise are interpreted as insults, and so on. If the roll is a 1 on the d20 for an attack, the target must roll on the **Catastrophic Failure** table, rerolling any nonapplicable results.

**Resolution:** A *[[9th-Circle Spells#Wish|wish]]* spell or similar magic removes the curse.

Alternatively, the deity or powerful creature that bestowed the curse might offer a quest for the target to redeem themselves enough to remove the curse (GM’s discretion).

**Catastrophic Failure**

| 1d6 | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | You accidentally hit a nearby ally with the attack instead of your intended target. |
| 2 | You drop your weapon or spell focus and lose any additional attacks you could take this turn. |
| 3 | You lose any of your unused actions, bonus actions, and reactions until the beginning of your next turn. |
| 4 | You slip, causing all attacks against you to have advantage until the start of your next turn. |
| 5 | You trip and fall prone. |
| 6 | Your attack rebounds on you, dealing the damage to you instead of your target. |

### Fitful Haunting

*Curse*

Ghosts are the spirits of the unquiet dead, tied to their current plane of existence due to a violent death, unfinished business, a curse, or many other possible reasons. They typically haunt the locales where they died, but occasionally, one will instead haunt a person. Perhaps this individual is responsible for the ghost’s death, or perhaps the ghost is trying to pass on a message or attempting to compel the target to solve the mystery of their death. Whatever the reason, the target of the haunting finds little rest, especially at night.

**Trigger:** A Humanoid has a 20 percent chance of being the target of this curse when entering a haunted area or when otherwise drawing the attention of a spirit.

**Effects:** The target is sporadically pursued by a **ghost**. Each time the target takes a short or long rest, they must roll 1d6. If the roll is a 1, the ghost manifests in some fashion: perhaps it attacks the target and their allies, or perhaps it simply causes aural and/or visual phenomena that disturb the target’s rest. In the latter case, the target must succeed on a DC 15 WIS save or gain no benefit from the rest.

**Resolution:** A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Dispel Evil and Good|dispel evil and good]]* spell or similar magic, cast either on the target or the ghost, removes the curse.

Alternatively, to remove the curse, the target must determine the reason for the ghost’s haunting and resolve it. For instance, if the ghost wants the target to solve its murder or find its body, doing so will allow the spirit to pass on and end the haunting. Destroying the ghost does not end the haunting, though it suppresses the effects of the curse for 24 hours. The ghost then reforms, and the haunting continues.

### Forced Servility

*Curse*

The moldering corpse of a long-dead noble rests amid rotting fineries while the remnants of their soul, maddened in death, now maligns the place, demanding obedience from those who dare approach it.

**Trigger:** After desecrating a certain noble’s remains, a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects, and the noble’s remains crumble to dust. The curse has no effect on creatures with backgrounds in nobility.

Creatures either within the area of a *[[Rituals 1st–3rd Circle#Magic Circle|magic circle]]* spell that is warded against Undead or under the effects of a *[[1st-Circle Spells#Protection from Evil and Good|protection from evil and good]]* spell are immune to the curse.

**Effects:** The target must succeed on a DC 13 WIS save or fall to their knees in supplication, restrained until the beginning of their next turn. During that time, any Humanoid within 60 feet of the target, and that can also see them, must succeed on the same save or also fall to their knees and become cursed.

Afterward, a target is unable to disobey orders given to them by a member of royalty (GM’s discretion) until the curse is removed.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic removes the curse.

### Ghostly Forgotten

*Curse*

This curse is often placed on gold and other treasure found within tombs and similar burial sites. When a creature touches the cursed treasure, they take on aspects of a ghost: they cannot be heard, seen, or felt by other intelligent creatures. Most who suffer from it waste away from loneliness and grief before they ever figure out how to undo their terrible fate.

**Trigger:** After touching cursed treasure, a creature must succeed on a DC 12 WIS save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The target is pulled partially into the Ethereal Plane. They still perceive their current surroundings as the plane where they were cursed but are unable to interact with any of it. Other creatures not on the Ethereal Plane are generally unable to perceive or interact with the target (unless a particular special ability or magic has given them the ability to do so). However, Beasts (and creatures with the Animal tag) can still perceive the target.

**Resolution:** A *[[4th-Circle Spells#Banishment|banishment]]* or *[[7th-Circle Spells#Plane Shift|plane shift]]* spell or similar magic cast on the target from the Ethereal Plane shunts them back to the plane where they became cursed, removing the curse. They appear in the nearest unoccupied space, in relation to their current location.

### Heart of Stone

*Curse*

This curse is often bestowed upon those who have shown an egregious lack of empathy—or at least sympathy—for the ill, suffering, or weak. Those affected usually die from a slow petrification.

**Trigger:** After ignoring or harming certain individuals (GM’s discretion), a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** There are no immediate ill effects. Then, with each following dawn, the target must make a DC 15 WIS save to avoid progressive states of petrification (effects are cumulative):

- On a success, the curse’s effects do not progress.

- After the first failure, the target notices a stiffness in their joints. They have disadvantage on DEX checks.

- After the second failure, the target’s stiffness progresses. They also have disadvantage on DEX saves.

- After the third failure, the target’s skin becomes thick and gray in patches, very much like stone. They also have disadvantage on CHA checks, and their speed is halved.

- After the fourth failure, the target’s limbs become so stiff that they cannot move. They are restrained, cannot perform the somatic gestures necessary for spellcasting, and can speak only falteringly.

- After the fifth failure, the stoniness reaches the target’s heart. They are petrified.

**Resolution:** A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic removes the petrification, but this only returns the curse to its initial onset, so the progressive states begin anew with the next dawn. The curse can only be permanently removed when the target has made appropriate amends to the creature that cursed them—or to the creature that was being avenged.

### House of Malignance

*Curse*

This curse is passed down through generations, sowing discord and chaos on sons and daughters. Often directly bestowed only upon those who slay powerful creatures from the outer planes, it lingers within a family and colors the legend—for good or for bad—of the first in the family to carry it.

**Trigger:** After slaying a certain Celestial or Fiend (GM’s discretion), a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects. When that target dies, the curse is bestowed upon their nearest blood relative (if any). Their family is now effectively cursed, perpetually affecting a new target that shares the bloodline. The curse has no effect on Constructs or Undead.

Alternatively, after a cursed close relative dies, a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** When the target takes 5 or more damage from a single source, they must make a DC 12 CHA save. On a failure, they gain 20 temporary hit points, and while these remain, the target cannot tell friend from foe: all creatures are considered hostile to them for the purposes of class features, spells, and traits. For instance, a paladin’s auras are typically only able to target friendly creatures, and spells like *[[4th-Circle Spells#Conjure Woodland Beings|conjure woodland beings]]* normally summon friendly creatures that, in this case, would become hostile to the target. However, the target is not compelled to attack or react in any specific way.

The target cannot gain temporary hit points again until they finish a long rest.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects until the next dawn.

Alternatively, the curse is removed either if the target chooses to fail their save against a specter’s Retributive Shriek reaction or if the target spends 48 hours within the Unnatural Heat regional effect that surrounds a red dragon’s lair. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 12 INT (Arcana or Religion) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Infelicitous Fate

*Curse*

Sometimes known as the Black Streak, only the gods of fate and fortune can inflict such a powerful curse on those they deem to be deserving of such divine punishment. Desecrating sacred crypts, destroying divine avatars, or challenging the power of fate could draw such ire, promising nothing but ruin.

**Trigger:** After disrupting the oaths or other rituals of a divine being (with a portfolio including fate, fortune, or luck), a creature must succeed on a DC 18 CHA save or be cursed and targeted by its effects. The cost in Luck to influence this check is doubled.

**Effects:** Unless the target spends 1 Luck to counteract the effect when making an attack, their hits instead become misses, and their critical hits become normal hits.

In addition, whenever the target spends any Luck, one ally that can see them (chosen randomly) must succeed on a DC 15 CHA save or also become cursed.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic cast by a Divine caster, such as a cleric or paladin, removes the curse.

Alternatively, it can be removed if an adequate offering of blood and gold (GM’s discretion) is made to the offended deity by the target.

### Interminable War

*Curse*

Either left by powerful beings on magical weapons or naturally permeated into such items through extreme violence and malevolent intention, this curse prevents even the dead from resting until the battle is done.

**Trigger:** After touching a cursed magic weapon, a creature must succeed on a DC 14 WIS save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** When reduced to 0 HP, the target transforms into an enraged **shadow** that attacks the nearest creature to the best of its ability. When the shadow dies, the target reappears with 1 HP in the space the shadow last occupied or else the nearest unoccupied space.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects until the next dawn.

Alternatively, if the target performs funeral rites on the weapon, such as a burial in hallowed ground for a named or unnamed soldier that died in battle, the curse is removed. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 14 INT (Arcana or History) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Lure of the Unholy

*Curse*

This curse is typically placed on tombs, temples, and other locations sacred to something evil. Intruders might then become marked with an invisible brand that pulses like a beacon, attracting all the dead in the region.

**Trigger:** After the sacred site is sufficiently disturbed—usually by killing a particular creature within it, damaging a relic, or looting its treasures (GM’s discretion)—a creature must succeed on a DC 15 CHA save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The target becomes a beacon to the dead. All Undead within 1 mile of the target can sense their direction and exact location and are drawn toward them:

- Undead with an INT of 8 (−1) or lower attempt to kill the target on sight without regard to their own safety.

- More intelligent Undead are also compelled but take up to 24 hours to plan their attack, though not to the exclusion of other plans already in progress.

- PCs with lineages linked to the Undead, such as [[Darakhul Lineage|darakhul]] and [[Dhampir Lineage|dhampir]], can sense the effect but are able to resist acting upon it.

The target cannot benefit from any type of invisibility against the Undead. Any creature with truesight can see the brand on the target’s skin, burning with malevolence.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects for 24 hours. Cleansing the unholy site—typically by removing the evil within (GM’s discretion) and then consecrating the site with holy power, such as with the *[[Rituals 4th–6th Circle#Hallow|hallow]]* ritual—is the only way to remove the curse for good.

### Manifested Unrest

*Curse*

Created and spread by guardians of isolated faerie wilds, this curse pulls the spirit from intruders once they fall asleep and promises only frustration and violence. The curse has become more widespread over the years.

**Trigger:** After taking a short or long rest in a cursed area, a creature must succeed on a DC 15 CHA save or be cursed and targeted by its effects. The curse has no effect on Constructs, Undead, and any creature that doesn’t require sleep, including elves.

**Effects:** When the target takes a short or long rest, they must succeed on a CON save equal to 8 + the number of hours since they last slept. On a failure, they enter a deep sleep and cannot be roused for 4 hours. During this slumber, their spirit rises as a horrified **ghost** that believes they were killed and blames any nearby creatures, including allies. If another creature tries to awaken the target, the ghost attacks them. If the ghost is killed, the target awakens with only 1 HP and gains no benefit from the rest. The target doesn’t remember their experiences as a ghost.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic, if cast on both the slumbering target and the manifested ghost within 1 minute of each other, removes the curse.

Alternatively, sprinkling dust from a lich’s lair or mummy’s tomb on the sleeping target removes the curse, though they will continue to be plagued with intense and vivid nightmares for a number of nights equal to 10 × the number of nights they were cursed. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 15 INT (Arcana or Nature) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Rapacity’s Cage

*Curse*

Powerful undead and dragons—especially undead dragons—curse their hoards of treasure to punish would-be thieves who have run out of good sense. The curse might remain dormant within an isolated mausoleum or strike at the speed of a handshake at a banking house, after tainted treasure changes hands.

**Trigger:** After touching treasure (worth at least 10 gp) from a cursed hoard, a creature must succeed on a DC 16 WIS save or be cursed and targeted by its effects. The curse has no effect on Constructs, Dragons, or Undead.

**Effects:** The target becomes sickened in the presence of a cursed hoard. When starting their turn within 10 feet of such items, they must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be poisoned for 24 hours. While poisoned in this way, they must succeed on a DC 30 WIS save or drop all coins or treasure they carry: any equipment in their possession prior to being cursed is considered safe. They also can’t move toward the cursed hoard for 24 hours, and in some cases, this might prevent them from leaving the treasure room. At each following dawn, the DC for this WIS save is reduced by 1 until it reaches DC 10.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects until the next dawn. Succeeding three consecutive WIS saves removes the curse.

Alternatively, a drop of dragon blood placed on each of the eyes and the tongue of the target traps the curse within a piece of treasure they are holding (target’s choice), removing the curse from the target once the item is relinquished or destroyed. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 16 INT (Arcana or Religion) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Scent of Death

*Curse*

When a mortal deals too often with especially wicked creatures, some of that wickedness can taint the mortal, marking them as a friend to things unholy. Many creatures can innately sense this, whether they understand it or not: other mortals might be less trusting, and animals might steer clear, even attacking when too close.

**Trigger:** After making a deal with a certain Aberration, Fey, Fiend, or Undead, or after a certain number of such dealings (GM’s discretion), a creature must succeed on a DC 15 WIS save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The target has disadvantage on CHA checks with creatures other than Aberrations, Fey, Fiends, or Undead.

In addition, Beasts (and creatures with the Animal tag) within 60 feet of the target treat them as a threat: prey creatures likely run and hide, and predators likely attack.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects for 24 hours. After abstaining from further such dealings for at least 1 month, the curse is removed.

### Seething Wounds

*Curse*

The spilled blood of a dying angel has left a mark of divine judgement upon the killing weapon. This cursed weapon feels warm and inviting to mortals and might shed a gleaming white light or possess a colorful shimmer.

**Trigger:** After first dealing damage with the cursed weapon, a creature is cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** For the next 24 hours, the target automatically takes half the damage they deal to any creature, even if not using the cursed weapon for the attacks. Whenever the target takes damage in this way, painful wounds appear on their body, reducing their hit point maximum by 1. If the curse reduces their hit point maximum to 0, they die. If the target kills one or more creatures before the 24 hours are up, the duration renews for another 24 hours after the current duration ends.

If the target hasn’t killed a creature for 24 hours, the curse falls dormant: it’s still present, but the damaging effects are no longer active. If they then kill a creature, the effects flare up for a new 24-hour cycle.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects until the next dawn, and a *[[6th-Circle Spells#Heal|heal]]* spell restores their hit point maximum but does not remove the curse. If the weapon is used to strike the killing blow on a Fiend of CR 5 or greater, the curse is removed from both the target and the weapon.

### Severed Magic

*Curse*

This curse is a brutal punishment inflicted by slighted sorcerers, warlocks, and witches. It severs a spellcaster completely from their sources of magic. Originally used to curtail dangerous spellcasters, those who use it for personal revenge are reviled among their peers.

**Trigger:** After using a spell on a certain spellcaster (GM’s discretion), a creature must succeed on a DC 17 CHA save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The target loses their connection to all sources of magic. For as long as they remain cursed, they do not regain expended spell slots when they finish a short or long rest.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic removes the curse. However, unless the target remains protected by an *[[8th-Circle Spells#Antimagic Field|antimagic field]]* spell, the originator of the curse can simply choose to reestablish the curse on the target each day.

### Torrential Weeping

*Curse*

Many supernatural beings are both skilled and very willing to maliciously punish mortals with warped versions of their own deepest desires, and this is one such curse. Commonly wielded by powerful druids, genies, and many faerie folk, targets are frequently those hoping to save their lands or livelihood from drought.

**Trigger:** After entering an area of rain caused by this curse, a creature must succeed on a DC 12 CHA save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** While the target is aboveground, a canopy of sad gray clouds (1-mile radius) follows them overhead. They have disadvantage on saves against being charmed.

In addition, each time the target cries, the clouds empty with a steady downpour of warm, salty rain. Each time the target begins a long rest, they must succeed on a DC 15 WIS save or become inconsolably sad and begin crying, triggering more rain to fall for the duration of the rest.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic, if cast upon either the target or their shed tears, removes the curse.

Alternatively, if the target either becomes permanently blinded or spends 6 consecutive days and nights on a different plane of existence from where they were cursed, the curse is removed. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 12 INT (Arcana or Nature) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Watery End

*Curse*

A creature who ventures into sacred or forbidden places beneath the water might find themselves the victim of this curse, drowning on dry land hours or days after coming to the surface.

**Trigger:** After entering a cursed submerged temple, shrine, or similar area, a creature must succeed on a DC 15 WIS save or be cursed and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** After the target surfaces and an additional 6d12 hours have passed, their lungs fill with water, and they immediately begin suffocating.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Water Breathing|water breathing]]* spell or similar magic suppresses the effects for the duration, and a *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Remove Curse|remove curse]]* spell or similar magic removes the curse.

## Diseases

A disease is usually contracted by exposure to a creature that carries it, and some monsters, such as ghouls, can also inflict them as part of their attacks. Some spells, such as *[[5th-Circle Spells#Contagion|contagion]]*, as well as exposure to certain hazards or poisons, can also be a source of disease. This section details a collection of ready-to-use diseases.

Moreover, though a *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* or *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell normally cures diseases, the maladies in this section tend to be more resistant, so those spells do not automatically cure them, as specified in the Resolution description for each. However, a *[[6th-Circle Spells#Heal|heal]]* spell will still cure them, in addition to any other methods described. Also, diseases generally have little to no effect on nonliving creatures, such as Constructs and Undead, and many might also be less effective on creatures with unusual physiology, such as Elementals or Plants.

### Arcanopathy

*Disease*

Within an hour of being infected, a victim’s eyes, tongue, and fingertips grow gray and desiccated. Magic just seems to feed it, making it spread throughout their body until they are ultimately nothing more than a husk.

**Trigger:** When an infected creature either casts a spell or is the target of a spell, each creature within 10 feet of it must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. The disease has no direct effect on targets that cannot cast spells, but they can still be carriers for it.

**Effects:** After a short or long rest, roll a d6. The target must then expend a spell slot of a circle equal to the result—and potentially infect creatures nearby. If the result is greater than the target’s highest available spell slot, then the target must expend a slot of their highest available circle and take necrotic damage. For the damage amount, roll a number of d6s equal to the difference between the circle of the required spell slot (based on the previous roll) and the circle of the actual expended spell slot.

**Resolution:** If the target either casts or is targeted by a spell of 8th circle or higher, the disease is cured.

Alternatively, if the target spends 1 hour or more within the area of an *[[8th-Circle Spells#Antimagic Field|antimagic field]]* spell, the disease is cured, but such a remedy might then afflict the target with the nullifying blight disease (GM’s discretion, see *Game Master’s Guide*). This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 13 INT (Arcana) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Bloodcurdle

*Disease*

From the blood pits of the lower planes, this disease is one of the most painful and deadly of plagues an adventurer might encounter. Victims suffer progressively from sores and painful swelling, slowly advancing to the point that blood starts seeping through their skin.

**Trigger:** When within 10 feet of an infected creature (or its blood or remains), a creature must succeed on a DC 16 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. The disease can also be spread via skin contact with insects or other carrion eaters that have feasted on tainted remains.

**Effects:** The disease has four stages, and the target must succeed on a DC 16 CON save after each long rest or immediately progress to the next stage (symptoms are cumulative):

- In the first stage (roughly 1 hour after infection), the target has visible bruises and sores, and their hit point maximum is halved.

- In the second stage, the soft tissue of the target’s sensory organs and throat become swollen and painful, and they become deafened and can speak only haltingly.

- In the third stage, the target’s skin and blood vessels become fragile and easily burst. They are vulnerable to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage and vulnerable to exhaustion.

- The fourth and final stage is mercifully swift but painful. At the start of each of the target’s turns, their hit point maximum is reduced by 1d4. They die if this effect reduces their hit point maximum to 0.

If kept cold (between 0 and 40 degrees Fahrenheit), the target has advantage on their saves to avoid the progression.

**Resolution:** A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic reduces the DC of the CON save by 2. If the DC is reduced to 0, the disease is cured. An infected creature is unlikely to overcome the disease without intense medical and/or magical assistance.

Alternatively, if the target regains any hit points from the Healing Touch action of certain Celestials, such as angels and unicorns, the DC for the disease progression is also reduced by the number of hit points regained. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 14 INT (Arcana or Religion) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Chalkbone Plague

*Disease*

Victims of this rare disease report heat and pain throughout their bones, followed by bouts of clumsiness. Eventually, even a stumble brings the risk of breaking bones. It is also commonly referred to as Gravekeeper’s Sick and is considered an occupational risk among adventurers, grave diggers, and priests.

**Trigger:** When taking a short or long rest within a diseased tomb or similar site infested with Undead, a creature must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. The disease has no effect on creatures that don’t need to breathe.

Alternatively, after spending 1 hour in a confined space with an infected Humanoid, such as a room or tent, the target must succeed on same save or become infected.

**Effects:** Within 8 hours, fever, swollen joints, and bone pain begin, and the target is vulnerable to bludgeoning damage and poison.

After 24 hours, the target suffers from shaky balance and racing thoughts, so they have disadvantage on STR (Athletics) and DEX (Acrobatics) checks made to balance, jump, or land safely and on checks made to maintain concentration. Each time they are knocked prone, they take 2 (1d4) additional bludgeoning damage.

Upon death, the target’s remains deflate into a pile of boneless flesh and release a chalk-like dust, which carries the disease in a 30-foot radius centered on the corpse, which remains infectious for 8 hours.

**Resolution:** A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease.

Alternatively, after each long rest, the target can make a DC 10 CON save. After three failed saves, their unarmored AC is permanently reduced by 1. After three successful saves, the disease is cured.

### Chattering Death

*Disease*

Transmitted through the saliva of infected animals, this disease takes its name from the uncontrolled tremors that cause victims’ teeth to chatter. It progresses quickly, cycling through symptoms until death finally takes its toll.

**Trigger:** After being bitten by an infected creature, a creature must succeed on a DC 16 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. On a successful save, the target is immune for 24 hours.

**Effects:** The disease has five stages, and the target must make a DC 16 CON save after each long rest. On a failure, the disease immediately progresses to the next stage (symptoms are cumulative). On a success, the disease reverts to the previous stage:

- In the first stage (1d4 hours after infection), the target develops a fever and chills, manifesting as violent shivers.

- In the second stage, the target has disadvantage on DEX checks.

- In the third stage, the target has disadvantage on DEX saves.

- In the fourth stage, the target’s speed is halved.

- In the fifth stage, the target falls unconscious and can’t be awakened. If the target fails their next disease progression save while in this stage, they are reduced to 0 HP and die.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic allows the target to make their next disease progression save with advantage. If the target succeeds on their disease progression save while in stage one, the disease is cured.

### Corpse Rot

*Disease*

This disease is most common in those who must handle corpses regularly, but any contact with infected remains puts one at risk. Infections begin with discoloration around the lips, nose, and eyes and quickly progresses to painful lesions as victims’ flesh begins to rot and fall away, leading to a grisly death if not dealt with.

**Trigger:** After coming into contact with a creature’s rotting remains, particularly one either in a watery environment (see also the [[#Corpse Sludge|corpse sludge]] hazard) or that died of this disease, a creature must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** This disease has four stages (symptoms are cumulative):

- In the first stage (2d6 minutes after infection), the target develops a dark-green discoloration around their eyes, nose, and mouth. If untreated for 5 days, the disease progresses to the next stage.

- In the second stage, the target develops dark, painful lesions on their limbs, and they can no longer regain hit points through magical means. To regain hit points through natural means, such as by a short or long rest, the target must succeed on a DC 12 CON save. If untreated for 3 days, the disease progresses to the next stage.

- In the third stage, as their flesh rots, small chunks of the target’s flesh begin to slough from their body. They can no longer regain hit points through any means. Also, after each long rest, they take 2d6 necrotic damage, and their hit point maximum is reduced by the same amount. They die if this effect reduces their hit point maximum to 0. If untreated for 2 days, the disease progresses to the next stage.

- In the fourth stage, the target becomes weak and disoriented as the infection spreads to their brain and other vital organs. Their speed is halved, and they have disadvantage on all ability checks, attack rolls, and saves. This is the last stage, and if untreated, the target eventually dies from their symptoms.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease if still in its first stage, and a *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease, no matter at what stage. A successful DC 15 WIS (Medicine) check will delay the onset of the next stage by 1 day and can be attempted once per day.

### Cruor Virus

*Disease*

A derangement of the blood, this disease induces spasmodic, jerky movements and fits of violence. Those with advanced symptoms are often put out of their misery for public safety.

**Trigger:** After coming into contact with infected blood or saliva, a creature must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. It can also be spread via the attacks of certain potential carriers, such as Beasts and Undead (requiring the same save). The disease has no effect on dhampirs, vampires (including spawn and thralls), or creatures either without blood or with the Shapechanger tag.

**Effects:** Within 1 hour of infection, the target starts to develop flu-like symptoms, and all of the features from the target’s lineage no longer function normally. The features are generally unusable, though passive features might instead become reversed, such as an increase to speed becoming a decrease or a resistance to something becoming a vulnerability (GM’s discretion).

After the initial 24 hours, the target begins to display violent outbursts and a desire to bite and scratch others, further risking the spread of the disease. If the disease goes untreated for 6 days, the target has disadvantage on all INT, WIS, and CHA checks and saves, and they become hostile to all creatures they encounter.

**Resolution:** A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease if the infection is less than 6 days old.

Alternatively, after the 6-day window has passed, the target can be cured of the disease by becoming a lycanthrope (GM’s discretion, see curse of lycanthropy in *Game Master’s Guide* and lycanthropes in *Monster Vault*) or a vampire spawn or thrall (GM’s discretion, see *Monster Vault*). This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 15 INT (History) or WIS (Medicine) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Deep Mold

*Disease*

This necromantic fungus feeds on whatever organic matter it finds, causing nearly constant tremors throughout the body, interspersed with more intense muscle spasms. The first time one witnesses a victim’s flesh falling from their bones, only for the bones to then walk away, is not quickly forgotten.

**Trigger:** After coming into contact with infected creatures or their remains, a creature must succeed on a DC 14 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. The disease only affects creatures composed of organic matter, at least in part, and has no effect on creatures without it.

**Effects:** Within the first 24 hours of infection, a network of fungal mycelia begins to establish itself in the target, threading visibly just under the skin. After this initial period, the target is vulnerable to all damage but regains 5 HP at the start of each of their turns. After 10 days or after being reduced to 0 HP, the target’s flesh rapidly rots from the bone, leaving behind an animated skeleton.

The skeleton retains the intelligence, memories, and personality of the target (otherwise a **skeleton**) but is considered charmed by all necromancers and Undead creatures with an INT of 5 (−3) or higher, treating them as trusted allies, and must obey any spoken commands they issue.

**Resolution:** A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease if the infection is less than 10 days old. Also, enduring a total of 40 radiant damage within 10 minutes will burn away the fungus and cure the disease. After 10 days, only a *[[9th-Circle Spells#Wish|wish]]* spell or similar magic can reverse the effects.

Alternatively, a tonic can be made that provides immunity to infection for 24 hours and cures the disease if applied within 10 days of infection: DC 14 INT (Alchemist Tools or Herbalist Tools) check. It requires a gelatinous sample from either a gray ooze or ochre jelly and a feather from either a Celestial (such as a couatl or pegasus) or Fiend (such as an erinyes or imp). This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 14 INT (Arcana, Nature, or Religion) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Grinning Pox

*Disease*

Those fighting this disease are a terrifying sight, possessing a rictus grin that seems like it’ll split their face wide open. Some healers hypothesize that it’s related to cackle fever, but no direct link has yet been found.

**Trigger:** After coming into contact with infected bodily fluids, a creature must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. It can also be spread via the attacks of creatures that drink blood (requiring the same save), such as mosquitoes, stirges, and ticks, which are all potential carriers. The disease only affects Humanoids.

**Effects:** Within the first 1d8 hours of infection, itchy blisters appear on the target’s abdomen. After this initial period, the target’s facial muscles tighten and spasm, creating a rictus grin. The target has disadvantage on all CHA checks and gains one level of exhaustion, which can’t be removed until the disease is cured.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic allows the target to immediately repeat their save, curing the disease on a success. Also, after 7 days with the infection, the target can repeat their save once each day after a long rest, and on a success, the disease is cured.

### Sanguine Hunger

*Disease*

You don’t need to be a vampire to crave blood, but the number of ways they can negatively impact the living just seems to grow each day. Those harboring refugees from vampire-ravaged regions might be in for a surprise.

**Trigger:** After taking slashing or piercing damage from an infected creature, a creature must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. It is only spread via the attacks of vampires, vampire spawn, and similar blood-drinking Undead—the only known carriers. The disease only affects living Humanoids that possess blood.

**Effects:** The target is overwhelmed by the need to consume the blood of the living. When a creature within 10 feet of the target bleeds for any reason, the target must succeed on a WIS save or succumb to blood ravening for 1 minute: the DC of the save and the effects of failure are determined by the amount of time since the target last consumed fresh blood (see **Blood Ravening** table), which ends early once the target consumes fresh blood.

**Blood Ravening**

| Last Blood Consumption | Save DC | On a Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 24 hours ago | 8 | The target has a −2 penalty to all ability checks, attack rolls, and saves while within 15 feet of a bleeding creature—distracted by the blood. |
| 24–48 hours ago | 12 | The target has disadvantage on all ability checks, attack rolls, and saves while within 15 feet of a bleeding creature—agitated by the blood. |
| More than 48 hours ago | 17 | On their turn, the target must attempt to bite a bleeding creature or fresh corpse and drink its blood. This bite (see disease description) deals an additional 2d6 necrotic damage, and if damage is dealt, this bout of blood ravening ends. A living creature bitten by the target must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or also become infected. |

While infected, the target also gains a bite attack (a simple melee weapon with which they are proficient), and they can add either their STR or DEX modifier to its attack rolls. On a hit, they deal 1d4 piercing damage (unless they already possess a bite attack with a greater base damage).

If the target is killed while infected, there’s a 20 percent chance they rise as a **vampire spawn** at the next sunset.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease. Also, drinking a flask of holy water—and taking 15 (4d6) radiant damage as a result—will burn away the infection and cure the disease.

### Sunpox

*Disease*

Those confined places that see a lot of activity from the undead, like moldering tombs and sprawling necropolises, can be dangerous to simply spend much time in. As the dust in these areas accumulates, incorporating greater amounts of flaked undead skin and flesh, mortals risk coming one step closer to undeath simply by breathing too deeply.

**Trigger:** After spending 1 hour or more at a time in spaces where Undead frequent, a creature must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. On a successful save, the target is immune for 24 hours. The disease only affects living creatures that need to breathe.

**Effects:** While in sunlight, the target has disadvantage on attack rolls and on WIS (Perception) checks that rely on sight. They also take 1d4 radiant damage for each minute spent in direct sunlight. This damage manifests as blisters and burns on exposed skin, which blacken and crack after long-term exposure.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease.

Alternatively, a balm can be made for the skin, which cures the disease if applied daily for 7 days, with a successful DC 10 INT (Herbalist Tools) check. It requires blessed oil, mugwort, and lavender. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 10 INT (Nature or Religion) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Walking Death

*Disease*

The disease sweeps through dense communities, such as in populous cities, where proximity, confined space, and shared water sources can make it more challenging to avoid contamination. Though its name suggests otherwise, this infection doesn’t typically kill victims directly, but it does often lead to indirect premature death by reducing the means of recovering from other illness or injury—and the experience is torturous.

**Trigger:** After coming into contact with infected bodily fluids, a creature must succeed on a DC 12 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. The disease only affects Beasts and Humanoids composed of organic matter, at least in part, and has no effect on creatures without it.

**Effects:** The target becomes contagious and starts developing symptoms 12 hours after infection: itchy sores and a high fever. After that, the target regains only half the hit points either from expending hit dice or from magic items, spells, or other healing sources (below a 6th-circle equivalent), and they regain neither hit points nor hit dice from finishing a long rest.

In addition, toxins are exuded via the target’s breath and skin. Any living creature within 15 feet of the target, and not already infected, must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be poisoned for 1 hour. This has no effect on creatures immune to poison. (This level of exposure is insufficient to contract the disease.)

**Resolution:** After 2 weeks, the disease runs its course and is cured, leaving permanent pockmarks on the skin. The target’s remains are also no longer contagious 1 hour after death.

### Watery Doom

*Disease*

Transmitted solely via the bite of an infected creature, primarily wild animals, those infected develop a strong aversion to water and other liquids, making bathing and even drinking a challenge. Though typically not directly fatal, victims can die from dehydration.

**Trigger:** After being bitten by an infected creature, a creature must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** The target develops a fear of water, and if they try to drink anything, they must first succeed on a DC 17 WIS save or be unable to.

In addition, their swim speed (if any) is halved, and if they attempt to enter a body of water or similar liquid, they must first succeed on a DC 17 WIS save or become frightened of the water for 1 minute. If forced into contact with water while frightened of it, they take 1d6 psychic damage each round they remain in contact with it.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease.

Alternatively, a tonic can be made, which cures the disease, with a successful DC 10 INT (Alchemist Tools or Herbalist Tools) check, though getting the target to drink it can prove challenging. It requires a solution containing an extract of milk thistle and charcoal. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 10 INT (Nature or Religion) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Wayfarer Mold

*Disease*

Unintentionally generated by mycolid colonies in isolated forests, especially underground, this fungal disease spreads so rapidly among plant and animal populations that it can suffuse an entire region in mere weeks. Victims are slowly colonized by the fungus and transformed.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn within 10 feet of an infected creature, a creature must succeed on a DC 16 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects. The disease has no effect on Humanoids, and it only infects Beasts and Plants, including creatures with the Animal tag and mundane plants.

**Effects:** The target becomes contagious 2 hours after infection, and symptoms begin to appear in 2d4 hours: open sores that seep a sticky pus or sap that hardens over time. After each long rest, the target must make a DC 16 CON save. On a failure, their speed is reduced by 5 feet (cumulative). Once their speed is reduced to 0 in this fashion, they are fully transformed into a fungus, retaining the form of the target, though now capable of extruding pseudopods to help spread the disease.

Mundane plants are helpless against it and become an inedible fungus within a day of infection.

**Resolution:** A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease if the target’s speed is 5 feet or more, but only a *[[9th-Circle Spells#Wish|wish]]* spell or similar magic can reverse the effects if the target’s speed is 0.

Alternatively, a balm can be made, which provides immunity to becoming infected for 24 hours and cures the disease if applied within 8 hours of known contact, with a successful DC 10 INT (Alchemist Tools or Herbalist Tools) check. It requires the combination of either bee or wasp venom with snail slime. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 16 INT (Nature) or WIS (Medicine) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Withering Sickness

*Disease*

Weakness and fatigue are the only obvious symptoms of this disease, meaning victims often don’t realize anything is wrong until it’s too late. Family and friends often notice any changes first, such as reduced muscle tone and sometimes an ashen quality to their skin.

**Trigger:** After touching an infected creature, a creature must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or be infected and targeted by its effects.

**Effects:** This disease has two stages (symptoms are cumulative):

- In the first stage (after the next long rest), the target might notice a slow-building weakness and fatigue. After each long rest (including the triggering one), the target must succeed on a DC 15 CON save or have their STR score reduced by 1. If this effect reduces their STR to 0, they fall unconscious, and the disease progresses to the next stage.

- In the second stage, the target must succeed on a DC 15 CON save daily at dawn. On a failure, they die. On a success, they do not die, and if their STR has been restored to 1 or higher, they regain consciousness.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic cures the disease if the target has not yet suffered a loss of STR, and a *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic fully restores the target’s STR and allows an immediate DC 15 CON save, curing the disease on a success. If the target survives the second stage with any amount of STR restored, the disease is cured, though any remaining STR loss must still be contended with.

## Hazards

Hazards are commonly encountered when PCs explore dungeon or wilderness environments. They include extreme weather conditions (like blistering heat and subzero temperatures), natural environmental features (like lava pits or quicksand), and residual magical effects (like magical mists or overgrown briars). They are also flexible in scale, taking up just a small portion of a larger area or expanding to fit the entire area, such as affecting an isolated spot within a massive forest versus taking up the entire forest. This section details a collection of ready-to-use hazards.

### Angry Earth

*Hazard*

The resting places of the forgotten dead can become infused with the anger and restlessness of their spirits, causing the earth itself to lash out at the living. It lies dormant until a living creature walks upon its soil, and then it erupts, attempting to pull intruders down into itself.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard only affects living creatures.

The area registers vaguely as Undead for spells like *[[1st-Circle Spells#Detect Evil and Good|detect evil and good]]* and a paladin’s Divine Sense feature. Otherwise, a successful DC 14 WIS (Insight) check reveals that something feels wrong about the area but nothing more specific.

**Effects:** A target must succeed on a DC 15 STR save or take 9 (2d8) bludgeoning damage and become grappled (escape DC 15) by the earth underneath, and until the grapple ends, they are also restrained. At the start of a grappled target’s turn, they take another 9 (2d8) bludgeoning damage.

In addition, when a target takes this damage, an Undead creature (GM’s choice) within 60 feet gains a number of temporary hit points equal to the damage.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area, but if restrained, they must first end the grapple. Another creature can use an action to free a grappled target by succeeding on a DC 15 STR check. If a cleric uses the Turn the Profane effect of their Channel Divinity feature on the hazard, its effects are suppressed for the duration, immediately releasing all grappled targets.

### Boneburrow Flies

*Hazard*

No larger than a human fingernail, these noxious flies nest in the rotting remains of the dead. When disturbed, they swarm out to find new homes in the wounds of the living.

**Trigger:** When entering an infested area or interacting with an infested corpse or Undead, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard only affects living creatures. A given area is typically only active for 1d4 hours at a time.

A successful DC 10 INT (Nature) check identifies the hazard.

**Effects:** Tens of thousands of flies crawl from their breeding grounds and attack. On initiative count 20 of each round, a target must make a DC 14 STR or DEX save (target’s choice), and if not at full hit points, they have disadvantage on the save. On a failure, they take 3 (1d6) acid damage, and flies burrow into their flesh, reducing their hit point maximum to their current hit points until the flies are removed. On a success, they take 3 (1d6) acid damage and no additional effects.

**Resolution:** A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Restoration|restoration]]* spell or similar magic destroys any flies within a target’s flesh. Similarly, drinking poison or applying it to the wound—taking at least 20 poison damage within 10 minutes—destroys the flies within a target.

Alternatively, a *[[1st-Circle Spells#Speak with Animals|speak with animals]]* spell or similar magic followed by a successful DC 16 WIS (Animal Handling) or CHA (Persuasion) check convinces the flies to abandon a target. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 14 INT (Nature) or WIS (Medicine) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Conflagrating Threshold

*Hazard*

An otherwise unassuming doorway lies ahead, but this one erupts in a blaze of bone-white flames. Almost seems as if it only targets the living.

**Trigger:** When crossing the threshold (whether a doorway, gateway, or similar portal), a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. Once triggered, the hazard becomes inactive for 24 hours. The hazard only affects living creatures.

A successful DC 16 WIS (Perception) check notes a faint shimmer at the threshold.

**Effects:** Unnatural white flames erupt at the threshold, engulfing and clinging to the target, who is now on fire. At the start of each of their subsequent turns, they must make a DC 16 CON save, taking 10 (3d6) fire damage on a failure or half as much damage on a success. The flames instantly spread to any living creature that touches, or that either moves or starts their turn within 5 feet of, a burning target.

**Resolution:** The *[[Cantrips#Prestidigitation|prestidigitation]]* cantrip or similar magic can snuff out the flames either on a target or at the threshold. The flames cannot be extinguished with water, even if submerged, but if a target succeeds on three consecutive CON saves against the fire damage, or if 1 minute has passed, the fire damage ends as the flames are extinguished.

### Corpse Sludge

*Hazard*

A corpse rotting in a body of water can create a mire of death and disease. This typically happens in the wake of a mass tragedy, such as catastrophic flooding or war, but can also occur from a single drowning. If the contamination goes untreated, it might leech into the groundwater and affect nearby communities.

**Trigger:** When in contact with contaminated water, a creature must succeed on a DC 17 CON save every minute or be targeted by the hazard’s effects. After drinking (or breathing) contaminated water, a target must succeed on a DC 19 CON save or suffer its effects.

A target is also at risk for the [[#Corpse Rot|corpse rot]] disease.

**Effects:** A target suffers from intense nausea and vomiting. For 24 hours, they are poisoned and gain no benefit from short or long rests.

**Resolution:** Each hour, a target can repeat the save, ending the effect on a success. Otherwise, they recover after 24 hours.

### Devouring Entropy

*Hazard*

Tiny motes of darkness float through the air like sizable bits of ash or dust, seemingly guided by some strange, indeterminate eddies, never succumbing to gravity. They also absorb the ambient light, so even in areas with little illumination, they are easy to spot in contrast—but it’s less clear to the unwary just how dangerous they are.

**Trigger:** When either a spell is cast or a charge is expended from a magic item within the area, creatures in the area are targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard can only be triggered once per round, and once triggered three times, it remains active for 1 additional round and then becomes dormant for 24 hours.

**Effects:** On initiative count 20 of each round, the hazard pulses, and targets suffer its effects. The hazard becomes progressively more powerful each time it’s triggered (effects are not cumulative):

- On its first trigger, a target must succeed on a DC 18 CON save or gain one level of exhaustion.

- On its second trigger, a target must make a DC 16 CON save, taking 55 (10d10) necrotic damage on a failure or half as much damage on a success.

- On its third trigger, a target must make a DC 12 CON save. On a failure, they die, and only a *[[9th-Circle Spells#Wish|wish]]* spell or similar magic can restore them to life. On a success, their hit point maximum is reduced to 1. This reduction lasts until they finish a short or long rest.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area.

### Dreamveil

*Hazard*

This miasma drifts through environments, especially underground where confined areas help keep it from dispersing. It smells very faintly of poppy flowers and lulls creatures into a dreamless sleep. It is presumed but unproven to be the pollen, spores, or other byproduct of some plant.

**Trigger:** After taking a short or long rest in a contaminated area, a creature must succeed on a DC 14 CON save or be targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard only affects Beasts and Humanoids, except those that don’t require sleep, such as elves.

A successful DC 18 INT (Arcana or Nature) check identifies the hazard.

**Effects:** A target doesn’t wake up normally and instead continues sleeping for an additional 1d4 hours. They can be forcefully awakened with a successful DC 14 WIS (Medicine) check or by taking damage from an attack.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area, and for the next week, they remain difficult to awaken from sleep, requiring 1 hour longer than normal for long rests. Curiously, a *[[1st-Circle Spells#Sleep|sleep]]* spell cast on a target immediately ends the effects.

### Flash Flood

*Hazard*

During violent storms, hurricanes, and similar weather conditions, a region’s bodies of water can swell suddenly, breaching their banks and washing away trees, homes, and any creatures unlucky enough to be there. Underground communities can also be affected by adverse aboveground weather and might also have their own unique localized flooding issues. A flash flood’s course is often not obvious beforehand.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. Creatures either with a swim speed or that can breathe underwater have advantage on all ability checks and saves associated with the hazard.

A successful DC 17 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check notices the signs and understands the danger: the hazard triggers 1d4 rounds later.

**Effects:** A target must make a DC 19 STR save. On a failure, they take 27 (6d8) bludgeoning damage and are swept 60 feet downstream. On a success, they take half as much damage and are swept only 10 feet downstream.

To attempt swimming, a target must succeed on a DC 20 STR (Athletics) check or be swept 60 feet downstream and gain one level of exhaustion.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area.

### Forest Fire

*Hazard*

Forest fires occur naturally, such as from lightning strikes during dry conditions, but are more frequently started by careless explorers, reckless magic users, errant fire elementals, and similar mishaps. Depending on the weather conditions, the fire might sweep across the landscape, displacing animals and people alike and destroying woodlands and crops, homes, and anything else in its path.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects.

**Effects:** A target automatically takes 27 (5d10) fire damage and must succeed on a DC 17 CON save or take 11 (2d10) poison damage from smoke inhalation. If a target holds their breath (or doesn’t need to breathe), they automatically succeed on the save, and if they fail the save three times, they must then hold their breath or start suffocating. (See [[Life and Death#Suffocating|Suffocating]] for details on holding your breath and suffocating.)

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area. If suffocating, they can’t breathe again until they leave the area.

### Forget-Me-Nots

*Hazard*

These tiny, star-like purple flowers are rumored to have originated in the faerie realms. When disturbed, they release a potent pollen that hangs in the air and affects the memories of those who breathe it in.

**Trigger:** When entering the area, a creature must succeed on a DC 12 WIS save or be targeted by the hazard’s effects. On a successful save, the target is immune for 24 hours. The hazard has no effect on creatures immune to the charmed condition or that don’t need to breathe. If a target holds their breath, they automatically succeed on the save.

A successful DC 14 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check identifies the hazard.

**Effects:** A target forgets everything that happened in the last hour. For every minute spent in the area, they must repeat the save, and for each failure, they forget the prior consecutive hour of memories, progressing backward.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area. A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic restores 1 hour of a target’s memories, starting with the oldest memories and working forward.

### Hungering Bones

*Hazard*

Mass graves, battlefields, and similar areas laden with the unburied bones of the dead can carry a weight beyond the horrors of the conflict they represent. Occasionally, the spiritual effluvium of such regions takes a life of its own.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard has no effect on Constructs or Undead and only affects living creatures.

**Effects:** A target must make a DC 14 WIS save. On a failure, they take 11 (2d10) necrotic damage and are restrained. On a success, they take half as much damage and aren’t restrained. As an action, a restrained target can free themself by repeating and succeeding on the WIS save.

**Resolution:** A *[[Rituals 4th–6th Circle#Hallow|hallow]]* ritual or similar magic destroys the hazard. Also, sprinkling a vial of holy water suppresses the effects within a 10-foot square for 10 minutes.

### Leech-Infested Water

*Hazard*

Bogs, swamps, and similar bodies of slow-moving or stagnant water can become infested with leeches. Crossing these waters can be surprisingly dangerous.

**Trigger:** When starting their turn in the area, a creature must succeed on a DC 17 DEX save or be targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard has no effect on creatures without blood.

**Effects:** Roll a d6 to determine how many leeches attach to a target this turn. At the end of a target’s turn, they take 1 necrotic damage for each leech still attached, and their hit point maximum is reduced by the same amount. They die if this effect reduces their hit point maximum to 0. As an action, a target can remove up to four leeches.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area. A *[[5th-Circle Spells#Greater Restoration|greater restoration]]* spell or similar magic fully restores a target’s hit point maximum—as does a long rest.

### Mudslide

*Hazard*

Heavy rains, rapid snowmelt, earthquakes, and similar events can cause mudslides. This phenomenon typically sweeps down steep slopes, taking creatures, vegetation, and structures with it.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. Creatures with a burrow speed have advantage on all ability checks and saves associated with the hazard.

A successful DC 17 INT (Nature) or WIS (Survival) check notices the signs and understands the danger: the hazard triggers 1d4 rounds later.

**Effects:** A target must make a DC 19 STR save, and if they have three-quarters or total cover, they have advantage on it. On a failure, they take 35 (10d6) bludgeoning damage, are swept 30 feet downhill, and are buried under at least 1 foot of mud. On a success, they take half as much damage and are neither swept away nor buried.

If buried, a target is restrained and begins suffocating, and at the start of their turn, they take 18 (5d6) bludgeoning damage and are swept another 30 feet downhill. As an action, a buried target (or another creature) can free themself with a successful DC 20 STR check.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area.

### Nameless Idol

*Hazard*

These statues depict deities, though often those quite obscure or long-dead. There’s something off about it, but it’s hard to figure out just what—perhaps the features are too unnatural or the angles too impossible—and those who view it just can’t seem to get that visage out of their thoughts.

**Trigger:** When within 60 feet and able to see the idol, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard has no effect on creatures immune to the charmed condition or that are either blind or worship the depicted deity.

**Effects:** On initiative count 20 of each round, a target must make a DC 17 CHA save. On a failure, they are charmed by the idol until the end of their next turn and must move toward it by the shortest, most direct path, regardless of obviously dangerous ground, such as fire or a pit. On a success, they are deafened and have disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks made to perceive anything other than the statue for 1 minute, and they are immune to the effects of the idol for 24 hours.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area, or the idol (AC 16, 125 HP, immunity to poison and psychic damage) must be destroyed. Also, if the idol becomes heavily obscured, its effects are suppressed for the duration.

### Necrotizing Fog

*Hazard*

It’s common for necrotic energies to accumulate in catacombs and similar repositories of large numbers of the dead, but they typically dissipate quickly when sites are well maintained or have significant foot traffic. However, in those that have been abandoned or are in serious disrepair, especially in areas of high humidity or that have some level of flooding, these energies can suffuse the air and linger.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard has no effect on Undead.

A successful DC 18 INT (Investigation) or WIS (Perception) check realizes it’s not normal fog and notices a faint odor of decay.

**Effects:** A target must make a DC 16 CON save, taking 14 (4d6) necrotic damage on a failure or half as much damage on a success. If a target dies, there is a 20 percent chance of them rising as a **zombie** after 1 hour.

**Resolution:** Either sprinkling holy water or casting a *[[Rituals 4th–6th Circle#Hallow|hallow]]* ritual or similar magic on the area destroys the hazard. A *[[2nd-Circle Spells#Gust of Wind|gust of wind]]* spell or similar magic disperses the fog for 1 minute, suppressing its effects for the duration.

### Otherworld Oil

*Hazard*

A shimmering, stinking oil seeps from the carved stones and forms small, discrete pools along the floor. Just getting close to one makes a creature start to question their situation, and touching the oil brings them only terrible, painful visions.

**Trigger:** When entering the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard has no effect on Aberrations—or on creatures loyal to the cult that created the hazard (GM’s discretion).

Though such hazards can span areas of any size, for a single such hazard, the discrete pools are never more than 15 feet apart.

**Effects:** While within 15 feet of a pool of oil, a target’s speed is halved. If a target ends their turn while in direct contact with the oil, they must succeed on a DC 15 INT save or suffer nightmarish visions, taking 10 (4d4) psychic damage and being blinded until the start of their next turn.

Any creature that dies within 15 feet of a pool of the oil creates a new 5-foot-square pool where they lie, and any creature that dies while in contact with a pool instead expands the existing pool by 5 feet in every direction.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area. Alternatively, as an action when within 5 feet of a pool of the oil but not in contact with it, a target can attempt to psychically overpower the oil: they must succeed on a DC 17 INT (Arcana) check. On a success, the entire hazard’s effects (from all pools) are suppressed until initiative count 20 of the following round. After three such successful checks within 10 minutes, the oil seeps back into the stone, and the hazard is destroyed. This alternative can be discovered through research with a successful DC 15 INT (Arcana) check—utilizing the [[Between Adventures#Researching|researching]] downtime activity.

### Sigil of Suffering

*Hazard*

Pain and anguish—like haunting visions, physical distress, and mental turmoil—can all be collected by those especially skilled and then used as a deterrent. When mixed with gemstone dust, platinum flakes, and a bit of the remains of an angel, a sigil can be created specifically to channel that suffering.

**Trigger:** When within 100 feet, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard only affects living creatures and has no effect on Celestials, Constructs, Fiends, and Undead.

A successful DC 12 WIS (Perception) check identifies the sigil as the source of the hazard, and a successful DC 14 INT (Arcana) check reveals the nature of the sigil.

**Effects:** At the end of their turn, if they have moved closer to the sigil since their last turn, a target is wracked with pain and fear, automatically taking psychic damage and then making a WIS save: the amount of damage and the save DC are determined by their distance from the sigil (see **Sigil Damage** table). On a failure, they are frightened of the sigil—or of its general location if they have not found it.

**Resolution:** A *[[3rd-Circle Spells#Dispel Magic|dispel magic]]* spell (DC 15) or similar magic destroys the hazard. Also, physically destroying the sigil—either by doing at least 20 damage to it (based on the surface it’s inscribed on, such as a wooden door or stone wall) or by succeeding on a DC 10 INT (Construction Tools) check—destroys the hazard.

**Sigil Damage**

| Distance from Sigil | Psychic Damage | Save DC |
|---|---|---|
| 10 feet or less | 13 (2d12) | 19 |
| 11–40 feet | 11 (2d10) | 16 |
| 41–70 feet | 9 (2d8) | 13 |
| 71–100 feet | 7 (2d6) | 10 |

### Spirit Crossing

*Hazard*

The veil between the realm of the living and the realm of the dead can be very thin in places, particularly where terrible bloodshed or other tragedy has occurred. Witnesses have reported feeling disorientation, hearing disembodied voices, and in some cases facing off against an actual manifestation.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects.

**Effects:** A target must succeed on a DC 14 WIS save or become frightened of the area, hearing voices of deceased loved ones and seeing faces and figures in shadows and reflective surfaces. If three or more targets fail the save in the same round, there is a 20 percent chance a **ghost** manifests. It’s not hostile unless attacked and vanishes after 1d6 rounds.

**Resolution:** A *[[Rituals 4th–6th Circle#Hallow|hallow]]* ritual or similar magic destroys the hazard. In some cases, the hazard fades on its own in hours or days.

### Tainted Haze

*Hazard*

When a celestial falls, their former grace can detach and attain a sort of life all its own. Appearing like a gossamer fog, the strong, metallic odor of ozone follows wherever it drifts.

**Trigger:** When entering or starting their turn in the area, a creature is targeted by the hazard’s effects. The hazard has no effect on Celestials or Fiends.

**Effects:** The area is lightly obscured (as if by fog). When a target regains hit points (by any means), each target takes 4 (1d8) necrotic damage. Also, a target concentrating on a spell takes necrotic damage equal to the circle of that spell and has disadvantage on the CON saves made to maintain their concentration.

**Resolution:** A target must leave the area. Though similar to fog in many ways, it is not affected by strong winds or other magical effects that might normally influence or manipulate fog. The hazard can move but does so seemingly of its own accord, not in response to outside factors.
