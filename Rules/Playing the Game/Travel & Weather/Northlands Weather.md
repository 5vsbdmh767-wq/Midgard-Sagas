%% source: Northlands Worldbook %%

The [[Northlands]] are cold, and most of the North suffers extreme cold in winter (see [[Northlands#Boreal Dark and Midnight Sun|Boreal Dark and Midnight Sun]]). These optional rules cover the environmental dangers of the North. For general weather, see [[Weather]].

***Extreme Cold.*** When the temperature is 0 degrees Fahrenheit or lower, a creature exposed to the cold must succeed on a DC 10 CON save at the end of each hour or gain 1 exhaustion level, and may suffer hypothermia (see below). Creatures with resistance or immunity to cold damage automatically succeed.

***Foraging for Food.*** In spring and summer, a creature must spend 1d4 hours and succeed on a DC 15 WIS (Survival) check to find food, gaining a number of pounds of food and water equal to its WIS modifier. In fall and winter, the DC rises to 18 in most areas, and to 20 in the mountains, tundra, and glaciers. These are suggested averages for ease of play.

Creatures in the cold burn more energy and need more food and water. As a rule, creatures must eat and drink twice as much as normal during the Northern winter, and three times as much in arctic conditions such as high mountains or glaciers.

***Frigid Water.*** A creature can be immersed in frigid water for a number of minutes equal to its CON score before suffering ill effects. Each minute after that, it must succeed on a DC 10 CON save or gain 1 exhaustion level. Creatures with resistance or immunity to cold damage automatically succeed, as do creatures naturally adapted to ice-cold water.

***Heavy Precipitation.*** Everything in an area of heavy rain or heavy snowfall is lightly obscured, and creatures there have disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks. Heavy rain also puts out open flames.

***High Altitude (10,000 to 25,000 Feet).*** Traveling at 10,000 to 25,000 feet above sea level is taxing because of the thin air. Each hour a creature spends traveling at high altitude counts as 2 hours for the purpose of how long it can travel. Creatures can acclimate by spending 30 days or more at this elevation, but can't acclimate above 20,000 feet unless they are native to such places.

***High Altitude (Above 25,000 Feet).*** Many of [[Jotunheim]]'s peaks rise so high there isn't enough air to sustain normal life. [[Northlanders]] call these places *daudavangr*, the "Death-Fields." Above 25,000 feet, any creature not naturally acclimated and without immunity to cold damage must succeed on a CON save each hour (DC 15, +1 per previous save) or take 2d6 cold damage and gain 1 exhaustion level.

Creatures with exhaustion in a daudavangr suffer blinding headaches, disorientation, and confusion, and have disadvantage on all d20 rolls based on INT, WIS, or CHA.

***Magic in the Death-Fields.*** Fire burns very poorly in a daudavangr. Spells and effects that deal fire damage deal only half as much as normal.

***Hypothermia and Frostbite.*** A creature with a level of exhaustion from the cold must make a CON save (DC 8 + its exhaustion level) each hour it is exposed to cold. Each failed save adds one level of hypothermia, in order. Finishing a long rest somewhere warm removes one level. These effects are in addition to the normal effects of exhaustion.

| Level | Hypothermia | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frostnip | Skin aches, and numb yellow and white patches form. The creature has disadvantage on STR and DEX checks. |
| 2 | Shiverstruck | The creature trembles uncontrollably. Casting a spell with verbal or somatic components requires a CON save (DC 10 + the spell's circle); on a failure, the spell isn't cast, and the slot and action are wasted. Tasks involving STR and DEX take twice as long. |
| 3 | Frostbite | Skin is numb and develops black blisters. The creature takes 1d10 cold damage each hour it isn't somewhere warm, and must succeed on a DC 10 DEX check to do physical tasks such as drawing a weapon, loading a bow, retrieving items, picking locks, or disarming traps. |
| 4 | Ice-Addled | The creature is confused and its speech is slurred. It takes 2d10 cold damage each hour it isn't somewhere warm, has disadvantage on all d20 rolls, its speed is halved, and it can't take reactions or cast spells. |
| 5 | Kissed by the Ice Maiden | The creature believes it is overheating. It must succeed on a DC 12 WIS save or begin removing its warm clothing, and it refuses to wear warm clothing or enter warm places. |

***Slippery Ice.*** Slippery ice is difficult terrain. A creature that moves onto slippery ice for the first time on a turn, or starts its turn there, must succeed on a DC 10 DEX save or have the prone condition.

***Snow Blindness.*** Snow blindness is a burn on the eye from light reflected off snow and ice, usually at high altitudes on white, reflective snowfields. After 1 hour of exposure to a bright, snowy area, a creature must succeed on a CON save (DC 8 + the number of hours spent there). On a failure, it has disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks that rely on sight for 24 hours. If it fails by 5 or more, it has the blinded condition for that time instead. Creatures with immunity to cold damage or naturally adapted to arctic environments automatically succeed. (Snow goggles and snow visors on the [[Adventuring Gear|Adventuring Gear]] page protect against it.)

***Strong Wind.*** Strong wind imposes disadvantage on ranged weapon attack rolls, puts out open flames, and disperses fog. A flying creature in strong wind must land at the end of its turn or fall. In a snowy area, strong wind can whip up a local snowstorm that imposes disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks.

***Thin Ice.*** Thin ice can bear 3d10 × 10 pounds per 10-foot square. When the total weight on an area exceeds that, the ice breaks and everything on it falls through, often into frigid water (see above).

***Travel Pace in the North.*** Snow, ice, and mountains make Northern travel harder. In snow or arctic conditions, creatures can keep a fast pace only with suitable equipment, such as snowshoes or skis.
