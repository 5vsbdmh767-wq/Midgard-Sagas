A person's reputation means everything in the [[Northlands]]. As canny [[Wotan]] said, "*Cattle die, kin die, even the self dies, but glory never dies for the one able to achieve it.*" To be a hero in the North, and so earn a place in the afterlife, is to do deeds with an eye to how they shape your name. Renown isn't fixed, though. A hero might win great honor for killing a mighty dragon, then watch it rot away after acting dishonorably.

Reputation is an optional rule that tracks your character's prestige and lets you spend that renown on cinematic, epic actions by briefly taking narrative control of the game.

## Reputation Score

Your reputation is measured by a score that works much like an ability score modifier. A novice warrior might have +1, while the greatest champion of the realm might have +10 or higher. Unlike an ability score, it starts at 0 and isn't adjusted by skills or character level. It rises when you gain renown for deeds and daring, and falls when you're caught acting dishonorably. Several characters can gain reputation by doing the same deed together. The **Reputation Thresholds** table shows its levels and benefits.

This system tracks only what people know about you. If no one sees you do something dishonorable, your reputation in the mortal world doesn't suffer, and if you slay a great wyrm but no one believes you, it doesn't improve. The gods and the [[The Norns|Norns]] may know what you did, and it may affect your chances of reaching Valhalla, but unless other people witness or learn of it, your reputation modifier doesn't change.

## Deeds and Dishonorable Acts

Deeds are acts of heroism by a character or party: saving a village from raiders, slaying a dragon, finding a fabled lost treasure. They needn't be victories in battle. A merchant who brings home a longship laden with goods can win renown, and so can an artist who makes a masterpiece. Keep track of how many deeds you've done, since they decide your reputation threshold.

***Rule of Five.*** If you accomplish five deeds and others learn of them, you advance to the next threshold.

Not every amazing thing an adventurer does is a deed. A deed is worthy of saga and song: as a rule of thumb, something that dramatically changes the story of the game. Fighting monsters isn't necessarily a deed, since nearly everyone in the North does that often, but driving off the legendary ice worm might be. Doing the same deed twice usually doesn't count the second time. The GM and the player decide together what counts.

***Dishonorable Acts.*** The opposite of deeds. Violence isn't necessarily dishonorable, and neither is killing. In the North, the great dishonorable acts are murdering an innocent, breaking an oath, breaking the rules of hospitality, violating the peace of the althing, and cowardice in the face of danger. The GM decides whether your actions are dishonorable and whether enough people know about them to damage your reputation.

***Rule of Three.*** If you commit three dishonorable acts that others learn about, you lose all your accumulated deeds and drop one threshold.

| Threshold | Level Prerequisite | Reputation Modifier | Challenge Types | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Obscure | 1+ | +2 | Low | Local fame. Your reputation extends to your local hearth or village. |
| Known | 5+ | +4 | Low and Medium | Regional fame. Your name has begun to be spoken of by skalds, merchants, and travelers. |
| Celebrated | 11+ | +6 | Low, Medium, and High | National fame. Stories of your adventures are sung beyond the borders of your homeland. |
| Exalted | 15+ | +8 | Low, Medium, High, and Epic | Global fame. Your reputation spreads far and wide, across the entire Northlands and even to other lands. |
| Legend | 18+ | +10 | Low, Medium, High, and Epic | Immortal fame. You are among the greatest heroes ever to walk the worlds and planes. Tomes of songs detail your deeds, and your name will live on for centuries. |

## Thresholds

Thresholds are reputation levels that measure your renown. Each has a character level prerequisite and gives small benefits in places where you are known.

At each threshold you must accomplish five deeds to advance, but you can't move up until you reach the next threshold's level minimum. For example, a 3rd-level character with five deeds can't gain more reputation or move from Obscure to Known until 5th level. Each time you reach a new threshold, your deed count resets to 0.

Your threshold also decides which reputation challenges you can attempt. You must be somewhere your reputation is known (as the GM decides) to get the regional benefits.

**Threshold Regional Benefits**

| Threshold | Minor Benefit |
|---|---|
| Obscure | You can maintain a poor lifestyle without paying the 2 sp per day cost. |
| Known | You can maintain a modest lifestyle without paying the 1 gp per day cost. |
| Celebrated | You can maintain a comfortable lifestyle without paying the 2 gp per day cost. |
| Exalted | You can maintain a wealthy lifestyle without paying the 4 gp per day cost. |
| Legend | You can maintain an aristocratic lifestyle without paying the 10 gp per day cost. |

## Reputation Challenges

Once per character level, you can leverage your fame to attempt a reputation challenge. If you succeed, you take narrative control of the game for a short time, in effect becoming the GM to resolve the dramatic moment. If you fail, you lose reputation as word of your failure spreads. This puts a great deal of power in your hands, so the GM can always step in if things get out of hand.

### Making a Challenge

You can announce a reputation challenge as a reaction at any time, and with the GM's agreement even if you've already used your reaction this turn.

Tell the GM what your character plans to do, and be descriptive and dramatic: this should be a heroic moment. The GM might suggest changes but generally works with you to agree on what happens to your character, the other characters, and the world. This is called the narrative.

***Challenge Types.*** As part of the narrative, you choose the challenge type: Low, Medium, High, or Epic. You must meet the threshold prerequisite for the type. If the GM rules your narrative sounds like a higher type than you qualify for, you may have to scale it back.

- ***Low Challenge (DC 10).*** A heroic action or dramatic moment that affects the immediate situation or a small group of people. It might turn the tide of a small battle, impress a local lord, or rally a frightened village.
- ***Medium Challenge (DC 12).*** A major feat that changes what a whole town, settlement, or faction does next. It can reshape relationships, affect regional politics, or change the course of a local conflict.
- ***High Challenge (DC 14).*** A feat that reshapes the destiny of a region, kingdom, or major power, permanently shifting politics, military balance, or magical forces.
- ***Epic Challenge (DC 16).*** Mythic events that echo across nations, planes, and generations, worthy of legend and saga, and that may change the course of the world.

***Challenge Roll.*** Once you and the GM agree on the narrative and type, roll a d20 and add your reputation modifier.

***Success.*** Your narrative happens as you described it.

***Failure.*** You lose all your accumulated deeds and drop one threshold.

### Challenge Guidelines

Taking control of the game is meant to let your character overcome obstacles and be the dynamic hero you imagined. It is not a way to one-shot a villain or suddenly get rich. Your character's story is part of the world and of the rest of the party's story, so be polite, be respectful, include others, and have fun. Some guidelines for your narrative:

- It can happen in or out of combat.
- It lasts as long as it takes to complete the challenge you agreed with the GM. Low challenges might take a couple of rounds; an Epic challenge might take an hour, a day, or more.
- What you describe should be logical, even if unlikely.
- You generally don't roll for narrative actions, though the GM may add obstacles that call for a roll.
- It takes the same time as your normal turn, or longer if the GM allows.
- It can include several actions, but unless the GM allows it, you can't cast more than one spell or make more than your normal number of attacks, unless something like a fighter's Action Surge would let you.
- When you attack with weapons or spells, you can roll normally, or with your GM's approval automatically hit and kill a number of foes equal to half your PB, as long as each foe's CR is no more than half your character level.
- You can add or change elements of the world to fit your narrative, if the changes are logical and the GM approves.
- Don't interfere with other players' control of their characters, and check with other players if their characters have a part in your narrative.

## Holmganga (Dueling)

Questioning someone's honor, accusing them of a crime or cowardice, or falsely claiming credit for heroic deeds is asking for a fight. Sometimes it happens by accident, especially at drunken feasts, and [[Northlanders]] laugh it off with a [[Flyting and Kennings|flyting]] or over rounds of mead once a sincere apology is given. Other times only bloodshed can heal wounded pride.

If the offender doesn't publicly apologize within three days, a duel called a holmganga ("going to the island") follows. These duels usually happen on an island, at a crossroads, in a sacred glade, or on a cloth staked to the ground. Each warrior may bring personal arms, armor, and three holmganga shields to the dueling ground.

***Matching Equipment.*** It is honorable to match your opponent's arms and armor, so a character with powerful magic gear facing a warrior with nonmagical gear is expected to use nonmagical arms and armor too. This is custom rather than rule, and onlookers may not know one side has an unfair advantage. A combatant who doesn't follow it might commit a dishonorable act, as the GM decides.

***Holmganga Shields.*** These are thinner than battle shields and break when their wielder is hit by an attack dealing 15 or more damage. A critical hit always breaks one. Breaking a shield or throwing it down ends a "round" of fighting and allows a brief rest (no more than a minute) to catch one's breath. The fight ends only when one combatant flees or can't continue.

***The Duel.*** By tradition a holmganga ends when one fighter is knocked unconscious. In game terms, when you reduce your foe to 0 hit points with a melee attack, you instead reduce them to 1 hit point and give them the unconscious condition. Deaths do happen in duels over serious matters, such as deciding a jarldom or ending a long feud. Killing an opponent in a holmganga isn't dishonorable unless the reason for the duel was minor.

Honorable folk of every profession are expected to duel when necessary. If a duel is badly mismatched, a champion can fight in someone's place, or the defender can petition the local althing to declare it unfair. Anyone who fails to show up is branded a *nithingr* (coward) and an outlaw for a year or two.

***Reward.*** Winning a holmganga upholds your side of the dispute that caused it, and it can count as a deed, as your GM decides.
