> [!note] Optional Content
> Everything from *Player's Guide 2* is optional. Ask your GM before taking any of it for your character. Throughout the rules pages, material from this book is marked: *Optional, from Player's Guide 2.*

Around each corner of the [[The Labyrinth|Labyrinth]], further mysteries await! Ever more varieties of martial and mystical heroes reveal their power in pursuit of worthy quests, alluring treasures, and potent secrets that could change the world.

*Player’s Guide 2* is a major expansion of the options available to characters and campaigns using the **Tales of the Valiant®** tabletop roleplaying game. Written for both PCs and GMs alike, this book offers an expansive set of options to enhance adventures in any game world. PCs will find new classes, dozens of additional subclasses, hundreds of new spells, and a bounty of other features for valiant characters. GMs will find a trove of optional rules to delight players and to customize gameplay as well as a suite of magic items and new rewards to entice adventurers.

## Using this Book

To use this book, you will need the *Player’s Guide* (PG) for the core rules of playing the game and the *Monster Vault* (MV) for the core rules about monsters and for monster statistics. Many spells, diseases, and magic items are referenced from the *Player’s Guide*, and the many monster names that appear in bold can be found in the *Monster* *Vault*—and even a few from *Monster Vault 2* (MV2). Direct references to core rules are listed throughout, and when relevant, this book calls out expansions and optional content found in the *Game Master’s Guide* (GMG).

### What’s in the Book?

This book has six primary chapters and an appendix of useful tables:

- **Chapter 1: Player Options** presents additional features and subclasses for use with the character classes detailed in the *Player’s Guide*. It also contains three new base classes to choose from: theurge, vanguard, and witch.

- **Chapter 2: Lineages & Heritages** describes new lineage and heritage options available to PCs.

- **Chapter 3: Backgrounds & Talents** describes new background and talent options available to PCs.

- **Chapter 4: Equipment & Magic Items** lists new equipment and magic items for PCs to purchase or find during their adventures.

- **Chapter 5: Spellcasting** provides optional rules to enrich spellcasting plus new spells available for each magic source.

- **Chapter 6: Expanded Rules** details additional rule systems to further enrich gameplay, including a base building system, additional downtime activities, rules for gameplay beyond 20th level, and an expanded suite of player rewards.

> [!note] Rule Reminders
> Here is a collection of clarifications and reminders to help navigate some of the trickier rules covered in the original *Player’s Guide*. Additionally, if you see an odd typo or are confused about the wording of a specific rule, you can always check out online errata at koboldpress.com/errata to see if a correction or clarification has been issued for it:
>
> - **Calculating Armor Class (AC).** If you have armor from another source, such as the Sturdy option of the Natural Adaptation trait from the beastkin lineage or the Unarmored Defense feature from the monk class, the armor values aren’t additive. Each of these features gives you a new way to calculate your AC, just like putting on a suit of scale mail armor changes your calculation from the base 10 + DEX modifier to 14 + DEX modifier (max 2). This makes the different calculations incompatible with each other, either requiring you to choose one or to make the highest calculation override the others.
>
> - **Defining Check.** Whenever a feature uses the word *check*, that means the feature affects all three kinds of checks: ability checks, attack rolls, and saves, if not further qualified.
>

- **Appendix: Tables** contains tables for randomly generating names of people, locations, and creatures. It also contains reference tables that condense all of the available beast forms, familiars, and steeds—all in one convenient place.

## Optional Content

While the content in this book builds upon the core rules of the **Tales of the Valiant** roleplaying game, all of it should be considered optional. It is up to each individual gaming group to decide if any or all of the options in this book are suitable for use in any given campaign. As a PC, you must get GM approval before you choose any of these options as part of character creation or in the leveling up of your character. As a GM, you must ensure everyone has equal access to any additional options that you choose to incorporate into the game.

In addition to general agreement over expanded content, there are several instances where variant rule options appear in this book. The GM and PCs must collectively agree to allow the use of a variant rule before it is introduced into gameplay. Session zero (see *Player’s* *Guide*) is an excellent time to discuss any variant rules your table might wish to use, but such discussions can happen anytime between sessions or during a gameplay break.

> [!note]
> - **Defining Pinpoint.** In the context of the rules, the word *pinpoint* means that you know exactly where a creature (or other specified target) is located, and you don’t suffer the typical penalties related to not being able to otherwise see or detect that creature.
>
> - **Luck Works on Death Saves.** As with other saves, Luck can be applied to death saves.
>
> - **One Reaction per Trigger.** If a creature is able to use more than one reaction in a turn, it can still use only one reaction per trigger. For example, if a fighter moves away from a hydra with five heads, provoking an opportunity attack, the hydra gets only one opportunity attack against that fighter—not five.
>
> - **Sunlight Is Always Bright Light.** An area of sunlight is always also an area of bright light. However, not all areas of bright light are considered sunlight.
>
