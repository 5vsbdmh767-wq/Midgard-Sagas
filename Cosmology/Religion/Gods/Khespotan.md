*Lord of Stone, Decider of Destiny, Scrivener of Fate, Keeper of the Vault of Souls, the Earthen Emperor, Divine Minister of Gold and Jewels, Dragon Lord of Certainty*

**Domains:** Knowledge, Mountain, Prophecy
**Alignment:** Lawful
**Favored Weapons:** Hammers, picks, shortswords

His priests tell a strange tale. The first Khespotan was a primordial god who recorded the fate of the world on clay tablets. When a divine dragon swallowed him like a pebble, the earth god fought back from inside its belly, absorbed the dragon from within, and emerged as the dragon god of the earth. Gods are unfathomable.

Khespotan lives deep in the earth in a vast vault, where he keeps the souls of the worthy, millions of them, each resting peacefully inside a flawless gem of great size and fire. He is shown holding a gemstone, and his priests say the great stony wyrm carves the destiny of every being on such gems.

==**Worshipers**==
His faithful seem subdued beside those of [[Baal-Hotep|Baal]] or [[Azuran]], but he is influential in [[The Mharoti Empire]] and shapes its spiritual foundation. His priests and mourners bury most Mharoti, especially the poor jambuka and kobolds. Builders ask him to make their works last the ages, miners seek his blessing for safe passage under the earth, and his clergy trains the architects, scribes, and scholars who design public works and record royal edicts. Most of the empire's duty-bound army worships him. The Silent Sentinels and the Gray Janissaries see their fate as sealed and face every battle fearless and resolute.

==**Symbols and Books**==
His symbols are letters of the Mharoti alphabet carved in stone or clay: sometimes the first letter, standing for all writing, sometimes "khes" in the middle, but often the last letter, for the final destiny of all things. Wealthier worshipers wear gems or metal inscribed with a rune. His holy text, the *Tablets of Fate*, holds his whole creed. Some claim great destinies can be read in it. His clergy say the only prophecy it holds is that all will one day return to the earth. Splinter groups see deeper knowledge there.

==**Shrines and Priests**==
His shrines stand in badlands, on peaks, under limestone arches, and at other striking stone features, and his larger temples include cemeteries or catacombs. The Seminary of the Stone trains architects, sages, and scribes. His richest temple is the Golden Cavern, frequented by mining magnates and gem traders. The faithful make offerings at the Rift of Zasamra in the Dragoncoil Mountains, where the earth swallowed a whole city that, his priests say, committed a great offense against patient Khespotan. Because of their sense of duty and their literacy, his priests make up most of the empire's bureaucracy and run its daily affairs, as royal archivists, scribes, messengers, and army quartermasters, and the merchant class favors them.

==**Masks**==
Khespotan makes no effort to hide; he is what he is. Sects that call him a mask of foreign gods win little attention, given his deep ties to the empire. The only exception might be [[Anu-Akma]] in the south, for some distant similarities.

==**Other Faiths**==
He leaves the other dragon gods to their squabbling, quietly bearing their weight as he bears the weight of all. [[Seggotan]] laps endlessly at stone to wear it away, but Khespotan knows stone becomes sand, and sand forms the bottom of the sea and holds it. The fickle voice of Azuran can be ignored. His one great quarrel is with Baal over the treatment of the dead: Baal favors cremation and Khespotan burial, and their priests fight street battles over the right to bury great officials.

==**What Khespotan Demands**==
Every fate is already set in stone. Accept this. Trace your path by the commandments of the *Tablets of Fate*, and though your fate is hidden, true faith will keep you on the path. Do your duty, endure hardship, keep going. Never complain. When your destiny comes, you will know it. It will end in the earthen arms of Khespotan.
