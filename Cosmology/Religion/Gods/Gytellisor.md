*God of Poetry and Wisdom, Child of the Stars, Lord of Rhetoric,*
*Keeper of Secrets, Patron of Artists, Scribes, and Dreamers,*
*Lord of Lost Horizons, Herald of the Far Shadow*

**Favored Weapons:** Rapier, staff

Gytellisor is a godling of the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]], little known outside it. He looks like a sable elf with fine clothes, long hair, and delicate features, and he is almost always shown reading a book or in an orator's pose, declaiming to his faithful or reciting elven poetry. He often wears a scholar's robes and a deep-purple crown of stars and lights.

## Worshipers

[[Shadow Fey|Shadow fey]] and sable elves alike adore Gytellisor, in small doses. He is always toasted at revels and coronations, at the turn of the seasons, and at the birth of children. But in dark times of war or famine his silver laughter is forgotten, or at least seems frivolous, and then his priests point to his hidden lore as a source of strength against any threat. Poets and scribes find him congenial, since his worship always involves poetry, song, and written invocations.

## Symbols and Books

His symbol is an open book, sometimes marked with a golden apple or embellished with a chain. His books are many and confusing, all written in Elvish, the sacred language of all his services. The most famous is the *Book of Prophecies*, nearly incomprehensible with its antique phrasing and its obscure analogies, parables, and beautiful but empty language. The more accessible *Voice of the God's Grave* is a set of divine pronouncements and aphorisms often echoed in toasts, speeches, and threats. Its chill warning tone sets it apart from other elven gods, for it makes plain that death and loss are part of the elven tradition, and it is widely thought both clear-eyed and somewhat depressing.

## Shrines and Priests

His shrines are usually tucked into the various courts: small nooks holding a dozen scrolls or a chained book for visitors to read. Some are enchanted so that the god's statue speaks a phrase or a whole poem when someone lights a candle or offers flowers. His only large shrine is the House of Dream Birds in [[Corremel]], kept by four priests who run a scriptorium there, copying sacred volumes and, for pay, anything a supplicant brings them. Its first servant is Ainoress Gabboneth, a round-faced, silver-haired sable elf matron who teaches reading and arithmetic to the poor. Flocks of white birds bring her fruit and sometimes coins; she says they are made from the god's dreams to feed the faithful.

## Masks

He is widely assumed to be a minor mask of [[Thoth-Hermes|Thoth]] or [[Baccholon]]. More suspicious minds think instead of the [[Black Goat|Goat of the Woods]] or [[Santerr Illosi]]. Others believe he was an ancient elven poet-saint who wandered into the Shadow Realm, drank deep from the Well of Mimir, and became a demigod.

## Other Faiths

Followers of Baccholon get along famously with his, and bawdy tales say the two gods were lovers in the [[Silendora|Summer Lands]] or in [[Valera]] long ago; the sable elves often invoke them in the same breath. The priests of [[Ninkash]] denounce elven indulgence and the void cult villainy they see hidden behind pretty words, and the priests of [[Kupkoresh]] fear the power of poetry and the forbidden prophecies his priests dangle to draw a crowd.

## What Gytellisor Demands

Speak the truth but tell it slant. Let loose wild whimsy and outrageous love, revenge, and despair. Feel your breath and life in every waking moment, and know that a path once trodden is never the same path twice. Drink deep of kisses, poetry, and love, and damn the consequences. Celebrate your victories with song, verse, and joy, and mourn your losses just as deeply. A shallow life is a life wasted.
