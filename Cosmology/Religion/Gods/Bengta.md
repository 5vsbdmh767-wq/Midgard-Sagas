*The Bear Maiden, Keeper of Community, the Righteous Wrath of Dawn,*
*the Golden Radiance, the Beekeeper, Goddess of Dawn and Honey,*
*Mother of Hives, Maiden of Morning, Saint of Sweet Lavender,*
*Flower Bringer, Strength of the Mountain, Patron of the [[Bearfolk]]*

**Favored Weapons:** Battleaxe, greataxe

The Bear Maiden is beloved across the [[Northlands]]. She is the foremost goddess of the bearfolk and the patron of [[Björnrike]], and legend says she made the first bearfolk, reshaping the great bears of the North into people. Her worship as a protector and champion reaches across the whole North. She has two faces at once: the peaceful giver of nature's bounty, and the fierce guardian, like a mother bear defending her cubs.

The Beekeeper teaches that the community comes first and must be protected, whatever the cost. She leads by example, showing that everyone must pitch in for the good of the whole, much like a colony of bees. She stands for law and order and urges troublemakers to rethink their ways, but she does not hesitate to destroy them if they can't live within the bounds of their society. She is a warrior, and her tales are full of battles against the monsters and giants that threaten settlements. Her dealings with the fey run hot and cold: she often bargains well with the animal lords, and just as often fights the mischievous pucks who tear a community's peace apart. Her weapon is said to be spun from the honey of the Queen of Bees herself, though none of her enemies has lived to confirm it.

She is widely held to be the fierce aspect of [[Lada]], the goddess of the dawn, taking the form of a radiant bearfolk, and she stands apart from Lada's gentler masks in her swift and righteous wrath.

## Worshipers

Bengta is worshiped most by the bearfolk, but she has nearly as many human and dwarf followers. Healers and berserkers pray to her for skill and guidance in battle and its aftermath, and community leaders turn to her when their settlements face hard times. As travel and trade grow across the North, more and more merchants pray to the Golden Radiance for safety from bandits, monsters, and the other dangers of road and trail. Her plain devotion to making good things for good people appeals to many dwarves, and her role as keeper makes her popular with anyone who travels in large groups, as well as with human berserkers, bearfolk warriors, hunters, and devoted parents.

## Symbols and Books

Her symbol is a golden bee gathering pollen from honeysuckle, set before a radiant violet eye, and many of her priests braid honeysuckle into their hair or wear wreaths of blossoming vines. Other common signs of her faith are a single bee or a trio of bees, and a radiant eye of gold and amber.

Her holy books are the *Promise of Light*, a book of prophecies, parables, and stories of kindness, forgiveness, and healing, and the *Summer Book*, a book of prayers, hymns, and blessings. Both are often made in lavish hand-lettered editions illuminated in red, green, blue, and orange, and some are warded against undead and shadow creatures. Another much-loved book is named for one of her titles, *Keeper of Community*: a collection of parables on the virtues of community, battle and marching songs, and recipes. Most copies are crammed with notes and loose pages of new songs and favorite recipes.

## Shrines and Priests

Björnrike is the heart of her worship, and there one can hardly take a few steps without passing a small shrine or statue, or a bench or beehive marked with a favorite saying or line of scripture. The trade roads in and out are lined with shrines and offerings, and her shrines can be found throughout the North, on the wide plains of the south, and even on distant planes. Her temples stand in meadows of clover and lavender, with honeysuckle climbing their walls and sometimes hiding them entirely under vines and blossoms. Every temple is also an apiary, giving honey and wax to the community and keeping enough to please the goddess, and some meaderies give part of their profits to her shrines for good fortune.

Her priests are gregarious and friendly and rarely pass up a chance to share a meal and a story with neighbor or stranger, but for all their warmth they stand on the front line against any threat to their community. They often hold key roles as warriors, leaders, healers, and farmers. Worshiping her means actively making one's community better, so her priests and shrines rarely follow the cloistered model of other faiths.

## Masks

Bengta is widely thought of as a mask of [[Lada]], the human goddess of dawn and healing. The bearfolk, of course, know that Lada is simply a furless human mask of Bengta. Others believe Bengta appears to the dwarves as [[Ninkash]].

## Other Faiths

Her followers get along well with those of Lada, Ninkash, [[Kupkoresh]], and even [[Baccholon]]. They are enemies of the devotees of [[Santerr Illosi]] and of all the dark gods, [[Alquam]] and the [[Black Goat|Goat of the Woods]] above all.

## What Bengta Demands

Put those you care for above everything else. Act selflessly and stay vigilant against the evils that threaten close-knit groups. Create joy and prosperity for everyone within reach, and when that prosperity is threatened, act swiftly to destroy the cause. Followers who spend their time industriously earn her full favor.

## In the Shadow Realm

Bengta is one of the five great gods of the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]], known by name even where she isn't worshiped. She is the patron of [[Merrymead]], where Queen Hollibee is her apostle and her festival is among the first held when the bearfolk wake each spring, and she is honored throughout the [[Moonlit Glades]] alongside [[Umbeserno]], who watches over the bearfolk by night as she does by day. In shadow, some see Lada, Bengta, and Kupkoresh as faces of a single godhead, and the shadow goblins claim she is a child of their own [[Santerr Marossa]]. The priests of [[Vardesain]] in the [[Twilight Empire]] denounce her faith.
