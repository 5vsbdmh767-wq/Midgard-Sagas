*Our Lady of Roses, Goddess of Steel and Lies, Lady of Light,*
*Bringer of Mirth, Holy Mother of Healing, Santerr of Blossoms,*
*the Divine Rose, Patron of Shadow [[Goblins]]*

**Favored Weapon:** Staff

Santerr Marossa is the capering, joyous, spontaneous side of the shadow goblins: a mother goddess of trickery and diligent study, strict with the wayward and quick with a helpful lie when a shadow goblin strays into the dangers of dealing with elves, humans, and bearfolk. She is a goddess of healing, but she isn't above knocking heads together when her priests and celebrants miss the point. Only among the shadow goblins does a trickster goddess of healing seem to make sense. *Santerr* is an Umbral title, something like "saint," and Marossa is a common shadow goblin name, so the title is always used.

She is usually shown as a shadow goblin matron in a horned crescent headdress of silver or ivory and parti-colored robes. The most common images show her calling to the moons, sitting cross-legged throwing fortunetelling bones, or holding a child, and she is sometimes shown laughing and slapping her knee, a pose called the Joyous Mother.

## Worshipers

Her worship is mostly confined to the shadow goblins and is especially common in [[Fandeval]], where she is patron, in goblin villages across the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]], and in the goblin halls and tunnels beneath the great fey courts. A few humans worship her in Fandeval and other settlements.

## Symbols and Books

Her symbol is a red, pink, or white rose, often stuffed into the eyeholes of a goblin skull or set into a glass moon-window. Her relics include undying roses, holy rosewater perfumes, and stone roses carved from jasper, red agate, or garnet. As a goddess of literacy she has a great many holy books, many of them quite specific and strange: *A Treatise for the Wounded: How to Save Your Scabs* is not an unusual title, nor are *The Two Hundred Ways to Raise the Spirits of a Dour Griever* and *How to Feed the Starving: Give Them Food*. Alongside these, her human and elven worshipers take comfort from *The Rosy Path*, a guide to healing; the *Marossa Herbarium*, a naturalist's compendium; and the *Guide to Starlight Weaving*, a mystical tome of cryptic prophecy open to endless interpretation.

## Shrines and Priests

Her shrines are usually hidden in goblin burrows under the great fey courts, out of reach of anyone larger than a goblin or unwelcome in goblin halls, which is nearly everyone but the lower castes of shadow fey, some erina, and now and then a bearfolk druid. They are said to be kept covered in fragrant rose petals except on holidays, when they are cleansed with fire and smeared with blood in rose patterns. Her greatest priestess is said to be the goddess's own daughter, the shadow goblin maiden Rose-Rekinash the Wise, whose almond eyes and milky hair hide a wicked sense of humor and a keen eye for a bargain. Like many shadow goblins she rarely says much to nobles, tall folk, and the very learned, but her memory is flawless and her spy network among the fey is enormous; every important word whispered in the courts is said to reach her ears.

## Masks

She is sometimes believed to be an especially twisted mask of [[Bengta]] or [[Kupkoresh]], and at other times an especially kindly mask of [[Loki|Laughing Loki]] or a more perfumed face of [[Ninkash]]. The goblins say she is the one goddess who made most of the others, and that Laughing Loki, [[Hecate]], [[Lada]], and Bengta are her children among humans, shadow fey, sable elves, and bearfolk.

## Other Faiths

Her followers get along well with those of [[Baccholon]] and Hecate, and especially well with the priests of [[Bastet|Ailuros]], who come to them for rosewater and moonflowers of every kind. They get along poorly with most human gods, especially dour [[Anu-Akma]], foolish Kupkoresh, and gloomy [[Charun]].

## What Santerr Marossa Demands

Live loud and laugh often. Fear no shame, and seize every bite of honey and every sweet blossom. Outlive your enemies, repay their insults with kindness, and then steal their boots. Treat the wounded, the weak, the elderly, and the poor with healing and compassion: someday everyone grows old, and someday everyone suffers. Tell jokes, and leave a rose or rosewater with your prayers in the garden or the temple.
