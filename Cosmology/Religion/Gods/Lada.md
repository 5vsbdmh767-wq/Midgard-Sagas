*The Golden Goddess of Dawn, Love,*
*and Mercy, the Bear Maiden, Lady of the*
*Healing Hand, Daughter of Aten, Mistress*
*of the Petal Palm, Patron of Mothers and*
*Children, Wife of [[Volund]]*

**Domains:** Life, Light
**Alignment:** Good
**Favored Weapons:** Staff and scimitar

Everyone loves Lada, though few truly follow her tenets of mercy and forgiveness. A maidenly goddess of the dawn and the day, she is the tireless enemy of [[Marena]], the Red Goddess.

Most idols and images depict Lada as meek and mild, but she has a powerfully wrathful side, sometimes called the Bear Maiden ([[Bengta]]), that defends children, the elderly, and the weak.

Her mercy can seem harsh, as in the destruction of the undead or a merciful death granted to a plague victim beyond help. She appears as a young woman with braided black hair and bright green or blue eyes, wearing flowers in every season but winter, and fragrant rose petals cover her altars.

==**Worshipers**==
Lada has many worshipers, for who does not want mercy, healing, and light? Young mothers, children, and the sick are all devoted to her, as are many paladins (especially women), some rangers and dervishes, and southern travelers. Her shrines and charms are common among the [[Centaurs|centaurs]], the elves of the [[Rothenian Plain]], and humans from north to south. Each people sees her in its own form, a centaur maiden, an elven matron, or a human woman, but they agree the outward seeming is only a bridge to worship. All say similar prayers and hold their rites at daybreak.

==**Symbols and Books**==
Her symbol is the radiant eye, and her followers are called "bright-eyed." Some of her orders use roses or other flowers as her signs, more often in the North and the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]] than in the South, where her faith began. Her books are both scripture and medicine. The *Golden Book of Ruby Laughter* describes the worship of the sun, and the *Codex of Herbals, Prayers, and Simples* is the touchstone of her healing lore.

==**Shrines and Priests**==
Lada's largest temple is in [[Zobeck]]. The Temple of Golden Lada is a tall, elaborate building of pink stone on a small hill in the Temple District, and its tallest tower rises above the city walls to catch the first light of dawn. Her other temple in the city, the Celestial Dawn Temple in the [[Arcane Collegium|Collegium]] District, is more popular. Petitioners line up before dawn at both to receive the priests' healing touch.

No one is denied healing during daylight hours, though anyone receiving powerful curative magic must serve the temple for a time or perform a great task to repay the debt. The high priestess is Lucca Angeli, a small woman with dark hair and a powerful personality.Born and raised in Zobeck, she spent her youth adventuring and made her name in one of the city's many sieges, when her steadfastness helped the Griffon Knights drive off a dark army of fey.

Her holiest places are high ground, where dawn breaks first, so her temples stand on hills and mountaintops or, where there is no high ground, are built with a tall dawn steeple, and their main doors always face east. Her largest temple is the Temple of the Rosy Dawn in Cronepisht, capital of the [[Magdar Kingdom]], though the Temple of Loving Mercy, on a holy river island near Laksor in [[Nuria Natal]], is held to be her birthplace and her holiest shrine. Many of her temples serve as hospitals and plague wards, and in the South they are often paired with temples of [[Anu-Akma]], since even Lada's grace cannot save everyone from death forever. Her shrines are also common in fields and at crossroads, where prayers are said at first light and sometimes a farewell prayer at sunset.

==**Masks**==
In the Crossroads Lada is the source of all good things, close to health and hearth. In parts of the South, especially Nuria Natal and [[Ishadia]], she is called Hathor, Isis, or Astarte, the calm face of reason, healing, and rebirth and the voice of hope. In the North she is Baldur, god of excellence and virtue. Among the bearfolk she is the much-loved [[Bengta]] the Bear Maiden, and on the Rothenian Plain she is Kamrusepa and wears an elven face. In a few places in the West she is linked with [[Charun]], the boatman and guardian of the dead. Some think [[Holda]] an ancient form of her.

==**Other Faiths**==
Lada is said to be [[Aten]]'s daughter, though their priesthoods rarely get along. She is closer to her divine brother, [[Khors]]. She is the unflinching enemy of the three evil sisters, as her priests call [[Marena]] the Red, [[Sarastra]] of the Night, and the [[White Goddess]]. Her greatest enemy, though, is Mavros-Perun, god of war and thunder, whose destruction and bloodshed her priests find abhorrent, and her priestesses rarely serve in an army devoted to [[Mavros]]. She despises all who prey on the weak or the sick.

==**What Lada Demands**==
Cure all the sick who ask. Defend lovers from every danger and trial. Show mercy to those who ask for it. Her clergy must make a pilgrimage to the healing school of Laksor once every twelve years. All her faithful must defend and protect mothers and children, and none may turn away from poverty and want without a kind word and an offer of help, whether wisdom, food, clothing, a song, or shelter for the night.



==**In the North**==
In the [[Northlands]], Lada is worshiped as Baldur, the Bringer of Light, the Fair, Frigg's Favored Son, the Harbinger of Ragnarok, the Light of the Aesir, God of Joy and Beauty, and the Bleeding God, whose favored weapon is the longsword. See [[Gods of the North]].

Baldur, son of [[Wotan]] and Frigg, is the god of radiance, purity, forgiveness, and peace. He appears as a tall Northlander with black hair plaited with bluebells, a trim black beard, and eyes the blue of the sky on a frozen lake, dressed in gleaming white with polished leather boots, smiling and often singing to himself. He is among the kindest of the Aesir, and tales tell of lost travelers finding food and their bearings in a sun-dappled clearing, of desperate warriors saved by a sudden ray of sunlight in their foes' eyes, and of lances of light that destroy undead outright. He is prophesied to be slain by his blind brother Hodr, which will herald Fimbulwinter and Ragnarok. After the world's destruction he will rise again to rule the gods of the new world.

Valorous warriors, poets, lovers, and travelers follow him, and more Northern paladins swear oaths to him than to any other god, war gods included. Even those who have never prayed at his shrines know some of his songs and sayings. He is no battle god, but his followers know that failing to fight giants and monsters invites a world without light, and against such foes they are implacable. Against human foes they are quicker to give quarter and look for the root of the conflict. His offerings are burned.

His symbol is a golden sunburst shining off the blade of a silver sword. His holy text, *The Mirror of Pure Joy*, is copied by hand on sun-bleached birch bark, and its copies vary widely. Popular children's tales of his deeds include *The Healer of Song*, *Word of the Fairest-Spoken*, and *Rebirth of the Sun*. Every sizable Northern settlement has a temple to him, a multilevel longhouse with a steel spire that must stand taller than the buildings around it, so that sunrise and sunset light it like an aurora. His wayside shrines line the roads and even some mountain peaks, each with something reflective set in it, a silver coin or a shard of steel. His priests, sometimes called lightbringers, wear gold-and-white robes and copper or bronze helmets, some with golden wings or sunbursts, and they are welcome wherever good people gather.
==**Beyond Midgard**==
On the worlds of the [[The Labyrinth|Labyrinth]], Lada is called the kindly younger sister of [[Solana]], the sun goddess. Her altars are covered in rose petals, and her temple doors close only in times of war and plunder.
