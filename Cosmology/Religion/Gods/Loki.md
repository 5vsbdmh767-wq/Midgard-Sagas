*Shapeshifting God of Cunning, Mischief and*
*Malice, Lord of Deception, Patron of Thieves,*
*Father of Fenris, Hel, and Jörmungandr*

**Domains:** Knowledge, Trickery
**Alignment:** Chaotic
**Favored Weapon:** Net

Nobody tells Loki's story the same way twice, and that is how he likes it. He is the trickster of the Northern gods, the one who stirs up trouble and then helps the gods get out of it. In a land that prizes the strong and straightforward, he stands for everything [[Northlanders]] overlook: wit, stealth, and guile. People compare him to fire, bright and useful until it suddenly isn't. He changes shape more easily than any other god and has fathered monsters, among them the wolf [[Fenris]], his half-dead daughter Hel, and, some say, the World Serpent. The prophecies say jealousy will drive him to kill [[Lada|Baldur]] and that his children will bring the twilight of the gods. Whether he was always wicked, or will only become so because no one ever trusted him, nobody knows.

==**Worshipers**==
Clever warriors, cooks, mischief-makers, and fisherfolk, who credit him with inventing the net. Most of his worshippers are scoundrels or monsters, and few people call on him at all, since his help usually causes more trouble than it fixes.

==**Symbols and Books**==
Twin serpents, or an open net. His priests keep no books.

==**Shrines and Priests**==
He has almost no temples, though in a sense every fire is his altar. His greatest priest, Magnate Avgustos Sigismor, a noble of [[Krakovar|Krakova]], is widely believed to have been killed by the darakhul princess Hristina, though a few claim he became a darakhul or otherwise cheated death. His priests hide among dwarves, gnomes, and others, and two of his strongest advocates today are dwarven brothers in [[Wolfheim]].


==**In the North**==
In the [[Northlands]] his favored weapons are the dagger and the net. See [[Gods of the North]].

Loki is the son of a jotun and an Aesir goddess who swore a blood-oath with [[Wotan]] to join the Aesir, though the other gods have never wholly trusted him, and for good reason. His affair with [[Angrboda]] produced [[Fenris]], Jormungandr, and Hel. Wotan and the others believe he is fated to bring about the end of all things and plan to lock him away before he can begin Ragnarok, while Loki schemes to escape any destiny he didn't choose. His greatest trick may yet be turning the Age of the Void into a time of rebirth. For all his reputation, he helps mortals far more often than he gets credit for. He appears as a vain, slightly androgynous man with unkempt red hair, a sly smile, and fine rune-embroidered clothes, and is shown holding daggers, a golden net, or a serpent.

His followers are thieves, cultists, gamblers, reavers, and others who chafe at "normal" society, and many giants give him offerings. They embrace change and seize any chance to tweak the noses of the powerful with a good trick; a popular rite is committing small thefts on a full-moon winter night. Rumor speaks of the Fading Legion, a secret cult of Loki in [[Stannasgard]] that takes on theft, arson, kidnapping, and worse for the right price. His symbols include the blade, the intertwined serpents, and the golden net, and he is linked with the *Perto* rune. If he has a holy book, it is the *Lokasenna*, a comic poem of his greatest pranks, quips, and deeds for and against the gods. "Loki's luck be upon you!" is a common Northern curse, which Loki finds quite unfair.

He has no formal temples in the North, though many pray to him for a little luck, and a few local cults keep shrines hung with preserved serpents in golden nets. The Mirthmasks, a well-known "secret" order of the [[Skaldholm]] bardic schools, playfully hold rites to him, especially on the night of the Askrhlattr festival. Perhaps his best-known priest is Brynja Firegrin, a fire giantkin who uses pranks and laughter to heal and spread hope among the downtrodden.
==**Masks**==
Hermes in the [[Seven Cities]], Kwanzi in the [[Southlands]], and Kvasir to some in the North. Other stories call him a mask of [[Sarastra]] or [[Chernobog]], or even [[Baal-Hotep|Baal]] or [[Veles]].

==**Other Faiths**==
He is [[Wotan]]'s blood-brother and [[Thor]]'s companion, family to the Aesir who never quite trust him. His friends are mostly disreputable: [[Boreas (North Wind)|Boreas]], the Hunter, Chernobog, and Sarastra. [[Sif]], Baldur, Freyr, and Freyja can't stand him.

==**What Loki Demands**==
"Do what you think would amuse me." His followers learn to look respectable while making themselves, like their god, sometimes unwelcome but always indispensable.

==**Beyond Midgard**==
In the [[The Labyrinth|Labyrinth]], Void cultists worship him as the herald of the end times, but others hope his greatest trick will be turning the coming of the Void into a rebirth.

==**In the Shadow Realm**==
In the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] he is called Laughing Loki, one of its five great dark gods, and he has more temples there than anywhere else. Most of his followers in shadow are shadow goblins, gnomes, darklings, and human scoundrels, and his priests are honored among darklings, gnomes, and goblins, less so among bearfolk, humans, and shadow fey. His greatest priest there is Vares the Silent, high priest in the [[Court of Pale Roses]], though some would name Grinning Florbert Redhand of his temple in [[Fandeval]], or a wandering bearfolk preacher and trickster called Furball who entertains and enlightens the bearfolk and sometimes the shadow fey. His largest shadow shrine is the Golden Hall in [[Langmire's Court]], where he is patron, and he has shrines in [[Corremel]], Fandeval, and elsewhere; King Reynard of the [[Court of Mice and Ravens]] is his High Prelate. In shadow he sometimes takes on the features of [[Sarastra]], [[Alquam]], [[The Hunter|the Hunter]], or [[Umbeserno]], though priests who know him see through it. His friends there are the Hunter, [[Gytellisor]], [[Santerr Marossa]], and [[Hecate]], and surprisingly Umbeserno, while [[Charun]], [[Bengta]], and [[Kupkoresh]] are hostile to him.
