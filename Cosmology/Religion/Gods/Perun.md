*Lord of Lightning, God of War and Thunder,*
*Lord of Strife and Rebirth, Patron of*
*[[Valera]] and the [[Seven Cities]], Lord of the*
*Storm Court*

**Domains:** Beer, Tempest, War
**Alignment:** Neutral
**Favored Weapons:** Longsword and spear

Superficially, Perun is perhaps the simplest of gods: a creature of destruction, strength, and war. He has a deeper, stranger side, however, of healing and rebirth. His mysteries involve not just death and glory but also resurrection and a cleansing of the soul. Most soldiers, guards, watchmen, bandits, and scouts make offerings to Perun.

The cities of [[Triolo]] and Valera call him [[Mavros]]. Worshipers in the north call him Thor or Donar, but in [[Zobeck]], he is Perun. As the war god, he is also quite popular among [[Centaurs|centaurs]]. The windrunner elves of the [[Rothenian Plain]] know him as Tilla the Bull-God.

Perun is the Lord of the Storm Court in the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]] and on the Rothenian Plain. His chorus is spear on shield, and his ceremony is the tramp of boots marching to the muster. He seeks to widen the conflicts between nations and to strengthen his worshipers against their enemies. He is the bright blade of youths at their first Mustering and the notched edge of graybeard veterans. All turn to him in anger, and his service is an honorable estate as long as they do not break the Edicts of Just War. In his cavern-temples his priests bring the greatest warriors back to fight again, a power tied to his mastery of the spear-maidens, angelic figures said to have once come to Midgard in the flesh to rule it with perfect justice. He is also a god of chaos and rebirth. The North respects the chaos of war, while southern priests see it as an unwelcome but undeniable part of war's storms, to be mastered.

==**Worshipers**==
The worship of Perun is most visible in spring when the campaign season begins. Many of his worshipers use a lightning bolt, a red bull, or a red circle in their heraldry. His priests are often indistinguishable from mercenary captains, and some serve as captains in the Free Companies.

[[Humans]] are his most devoted followers, especially in the [[Seven Cities]], the [[Magdar Kingdom]], the Rothenian Plain, and the [[Grand Duchy of Dornig|Grand Duchy]] along the border of the Goblin Wastes, and soldiers, guards, farmers, and shepherds alike ask him for strength and guidance. All weapons of war are his, but his favorites are the spear and sword, and minotaurs link the axe with the Bloody God of War. His holy writings are the *25 Martial Books*, in the Northern Tongue, and the mystical *Soldier's Journey*, in the Southern Tongue, both translated for his priests elsewhere.

Perun wants action. Worshipers must seek out battle and keep their martial skills well honed. The perfect death is on the battlefield, and death of old age is abhorred. Followers must attend the mysteries at his temple before any great battle or long journey. They must never leave a comrade's body on the field. Cowardice is shameful. Every worshiper must make the pilgrimage to the Seat of Mavros at least once, and no fight, duel, or challenge may be refused. Retreat is acceptable, but victory is the finest goal.

==**Shrines and Priests**==
The Church of Perun in Zobeck's Temple District is a large stone building with virtually no outer embellishments, and many mistake it for a warriors' guild. Most of the building houses an armory and rooms for martial training. The clerics here train ordinary citizens in basic combat, and the church serves as the unofficial recruiter and officer corps for the Zobeck levies. The high priest is Medlin Gorzax, a gruff veteran in his sixties.

His most famous holy sites are the House of Swords and the Fist of Mavros in [[Valera]] (the Fist welcomes those who call him by other names), the Temple of Twin Thunderbolts in Donnermark, and the [[Western Wastes#The Seat of Mavros|Seat of Mavros]] in the [[Wasted West]], a yearly pilgrimage for soldiers and a bastion of the faith in hostile land. He is popular in [[Rumela]], [[Krakovar]], [[Perunalia]], and on the Rothenian Plain, where the centaurs admire him. His priests often serve as officers in southern armies, where they are thought both the best officers and, given his fanatics, among the worst. His priesthood is more than eighty percent male, though shield maidens and amazons are well represented. His best-known orders are the Numinous Spear, the Keepers of the Seat, and the Defenders of the Lightning Fist. The Order of the Storm in Perunalia is thought somewhat heretical, since it stresses the storm god over the war god, though the same order in the Magdar Kingdom is more orthodox, and few want to face the god's daughters in battle.

==**Masks**==
Perun is the voice of thunder and war, perhaps one of the least subtle masks in any pantheon. Surely he is the same god called [[Thor]] in the North, Mavros in the Seven Cities and the Wastes, and Mavrash in distant [[Ishadia]]. Yet the three faiths differ more like brawling brothers than twins, and which of them is the true faith and which the masks is a bitter argument.

==**Other Faiths**==
Perun is the husband of [[Marena]] the Red Goddess in the North and the Crossroads, and the son of [[Wotan]] in the North. Otherwise he has few ties to other faiths, for his priests say soldiers are his children and victory his only love. His greatest enemies are [[Lada]], whose priests scorn the honorable wounds of battle and preach peace, [[Hecate]], a deceptive goddess of schemes and dishonor, and [[The Hunter]], whose bloodshed is wasteful and ignoble.

==**In the North**==
In the [[Northlands]] he is [[Thor]], the Thunderer, God of Lightning, War, and Strength, Slayer of Monsters and Protector of Mortals, and Patron of the North, [[Dwarves]], and Reavers. His favored weapon there is the warhammer. See [[Gods of the North]].

The red-maned Thor is the boldest of [[Wotan]]'s sons and the mightiest warrior in the heavens. He rides a chariot pulled by celestial goats and wields the rune-etched hammer *Mjolnir*, and he is the eternal foe of giants, trolls, and monsters. Thunder is his war cry and lightning the swing of his arm. He is not known for wisdom, but his courage, good humor, and roaring love of battle are unmatched. Between fights he lives in his hall, Bilskirnir, with his wife [[Sif]]. He appears as a burly man in sleeveless armor with wild red hair and a beard like a blazing storm cloud, the magic belt *Megingjord* around his waist and Mjolnir at his side.

Warriors, reavers, raiders, guards, and anyone who lives by strength of arm follow him, and everyone from raiders to farmers makes him offerings; peaceful folk hang his hammer on their doors to keep trolls away. Dwarves are both wary and reverent of Thor. They honor him as creator and protector, but some believe he smote their empire during the Reaving for favoring [[Volund]] over him. He teaches that strength is both virtue and weapon and that every challenge should be met head-on. Death in battle is the highest honor, and he despises a "straw death" of age or illness. A popular battle cry is "*Thor's thunder in your ears!*"

His most sacred symbol is the hammer, worn as a charm or painted on shields, and he is linked with the *Thurisaz* rune. Lightning is said to strike places of great valor or danger, and thunder during a birth or a duel is a blessing. His followers keep no holy books, preferring to tell his stories around the fire, such as the *Lay of Hymir*, in which Thor hooks [[Gods of the North#Other Deities|Jormungandr]] on a fishing line, and *Haustlong*, in which he battles a mighty jotun.

Most of his shrines are open to the sky, on windswept cliffs or storm-wracked coasts, where offerings of bear's blood, oxen, and shattered weapons are left on the altar, ideally during a thunderstorm. His greatest temple is the Shrine of Golden Hammers in [[Thrumagard]], led by Marshall Wulf Thorsen, a famed dwarven priest of the Order of the Thunderer. His clergy wear armor at all times, never refuse a challenge, and preach strength, honesty, and defiance in the face of evil. Sparring between Thor's priests and Sif's is common, testing whether brute strength or refined technique wins the gods' favor.
