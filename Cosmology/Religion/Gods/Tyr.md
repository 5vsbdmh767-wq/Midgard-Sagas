*The God of Justice, the Odd Handed, Battle Brother, One-Handed,*
*the Einherjar, Wolf-Handed*

**Favored Weapon:** Longsword

The God of Justice is widely worshiped by the humans and dwarves of the [[Northlands]], and his priests are found among all the so-called civilized peoples. Giants and their servants fear and loathe him and his clergy, but even they respect his right to do his work on the battlefield. Wherever oaths are kept or blood is spilled in conflict, Tyr is said to walk.

Unlike other war gods, Tyr does not embrace chaos. His name is invoked when oaths are sworn and contracts signed, and the duality of war and justice runs through his worship. Battle, he teaches, is a means to protect communities from giants and the other enemies of an orderly society. In peacetime, leaders look to his priests to help them deliver justice and do the most good for the most people.

The sagas say he lost his right hand helping the Aesir bind [[Fenris]]. The truth is a little different. To avoid being bound, Fenris played at being tame enough to live in peace with the gods, and to prove it he urged Tyr to put his hand in the wolf's mouth. True to his nature, Fenris bit it off and swallowed it in one gulp. The other gods sometimes snicker about Tyr's gullibility, though his glare silences them quickly.

## Worshipers

Warriors, magistrates, guards, judges, and community leaders honor the Odd Handed. Those who feel wronged call on him for justice and aid, as do duelists and anyone seeking to redress wrongs done to them in battle.

Tyr calls on his followers to obey the laws and commands of their jarls, since order is what separates the hero from the cur. Bring battle to the forces that oppose righteous order, and treat others with hospitality and honor.

## Symbols and Books

His symbol is a longsword held upright. *The Law of Sword and Axe* holds the tenets of his faith and serves as a guide to a good and just life. It is written plainly to need little interpretation, though scholars argue over it anyway. Oaths in the North are often sworn with the left hand on it.

## Shrines and Priests

His temples are sturdy and workmanlike, more like fortified keeps and armories than works of art. Where most Northern buildings are wood, mud, and moss, Tyr's are stone if at all possible. In a siege they serve as command centers and, if the siege goes badly, as the last bastion. In peacetime they serve as courthouses and sometimes as feast halls.

His priests neither shy from battle nor lust for it, seeing war as one way among others to settle disputes and secure peace. In larger cities, some serve as judges and magistrates, making sure contracts are honored and laws followed to the letter.

## Masks

In the North, the southern god Horus is considered a mask of [[Wotan]], or sometimes a distinct god called Tyr.
