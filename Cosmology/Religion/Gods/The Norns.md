*Weavers of Fates, Ladies of Destiny, Wyrd Women, Choosers of the Slain,*
*the Judges, the Cradle and Grave*

**Favored Weapon:** Dagger

The Norns are three goddesses who live at the foot of [[Yggdrasil]], tending the tree and weaving the thread of fate for every mortal. Their names are Uror, Verdandi, and Skuld. They are widely thought to be sisters, though neither they nor the Aesir have ever said so. The Aesir claim them, but they may have been Vanir, or older than both.

Because they are part of everyday life in the North, and their presence is a sign that all is as it should be, few tales cast the Norns as heroes, though they appear as truth-tellers and fate-spinners in many of the sagas.

## Worshipers

Nearly everyone in the [[Northlands]] pays the Wyrd Women deference. Expectant mothers make offerings for a safe birth, parents pray for their children's health, farmers for good crops, community leaders for safety from raiders, warriors to survive battle or to earn a place in Valhalla, and sages for wisdom.

## Symbols and Books

Their symbol is a bloody pair of black shears beneath a pigeon with entrails spilling from its belly. Their holy book, *Of Warp and Woof*, holds the prophecies that foretold the creation of the Aesir, the war with the Vanir, the many wars with the giants of [[Jotunheim]], and the death and rebirth of the universe at Ragnarok. It is written in a baffling poetic style that scholars still find new ways to read.

## Shrines and Priests

The Norns don't encourage temples, and the few ever built quickly fell to misfortune and ruin. Shrines, though, are everywhere. Few Northern homes lack an effigy of the Norns, where the household makes a daily offering of meat and mead, usually before the first meal of the day, though some traditions hold it must be after sunset.

Their priests are called seers. Many are women, though male seers are not unheard of. As healers and prophets they are honored wherever they settle, and the doorway of a seer's home is rarely without offerings of meat, honey, or fine craftwork from the local folk. Worship of the Norns reaches every people of the North, even clans of goblins and giants. The trolls of [[Trollheim]] have embraced the Wyrd Women in particular, and produce talented haruspices who read the future in the entrails of birds, beasts, and occasionally themselves.

## Masks

In the North, the clockwork goddess [[Rava]] is associated with the Norns.
