The primary location binding the setting together is the “space between the worlds” known as the Labyrinth. It encompasses entire cities and pocket dimensions, though most of the Labyrinth consists of tunnels, bridges, and stranger pathways between the worlds. Regardless of whether the true nature of the Labyrinth is a dream or a mushroom root or the World Tree or a cracked egg of a world, the Labyrinth is a livable place. It is suffused by ether, which connects it to the elements. It is lightly connected to the Astral but more tightly to every other world, from the Hells to the fey realms to the new worlds and empty world husks drained by the Void. Precisely because it connects them all, this great maze makes it possible for the inhabitants of the Labyrinth and the worlds it touches to oppose the Void and its demons and cultists.

### Seven Secrets of the Labyrinth
These seven secrets of the Labyrinth setting give it a distinct character. Natives of the Labyrinth often take 
them for granted, but most visitors to the maze find them surprising or non-obvious.

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Worlds Can be Created and Destroyed | The people of the Labyrinth know that entire worlds can be born and die in days or can last timeless ages beyond mortal understanding. But worlds are not eternal: they can be grown, built, or dreamed into existence, or they can arise spontaneously out of the Astral Sea. |

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| The Maze Is Not a World, Only a Road | The Labyrinth itself is not a world but a set of passages, tunnels, rivers, roads, and other connections, along with a few small demiplanes. It may have been dreamed into existence by the minotaurs or (more likely) it predates them. Regardless, they awakened it somehow in the construction of their Great Maze. This maze connects all mazes on all worlds, it was the first world-linking path, and it gave the Labyrinth its name. |

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Five Connections | Five connectors are the foundation of the Labyrinth: the greatest of these is its network of passageways, caverns, and tunnels—more commonly called pathways—which can be walked or traveled between worlds. In addition, four natural connectors exist: the River Styx, the branches of the World Tree, the Astral Sea, and the most dangerous path, which connects things through a deadly empty space, always close to chaos, called the Void. There’s some dispute as to whether the void-powered tears in reality truly can be called a connection (or indeed whether they are truly natural). However, they function well enough as connectors for void cultists and others willing to use them. |

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| All Roads Go Through the Maze | While there are shortcuts into the Labyrinth and back out again (primarily portals and rifts), all travel between one world and another goes through the Labyrinth at some point. |

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Mortal Worlds Stand Alone | The worlds connected by the Labyrinth are all unique and all mostly inhabited in some fashion; they are much larger, deeper, and more populated than the Labyrinth itself. Their inhabitants are mostly ignorant of the existence of the Labyrinth; only Fiends, Celestials, Voidlings, and the walkers of the Labyrinth know how and where to leave a mortal world for the pathways. |

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Walkers Create Paths, Paths Create Roads | While no one would say that the Labyrinth itself is sentient, it does seem to grow by absorbing power from all four sources of magic: Arcane, Divine, Primordial, and Wyrd. The character of the Labyrinth can change if more travelers and walkers wielding a particular sort of power spend time on its pathways or settled in its caverns and strongholds. |

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Void’s Hunger Cannot be Satisfied | While the void always seeks to devour and destroy worlds, its hunger never ends. There is no world so rich and no bargain so fine that the Void will cease to corrupt and eat worlds. |

### What It Looks Like
Most of the Labyrinth is tunnel, cavern, and underground river, along with hedge mazes, endless halls and stairways, starlit paths over glittering darkness, and foggy riverbanks that loop back on themselves. Much of it is dank and worn. The exceptions are the places people have made livable:

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Bottled cities | Pocket worlds with buildings, a steady day and night, and enough farmland or orchard to feed a thousand people or more. The [[Keepers of the Keys]] build them with Arcane magic, and [[The Smithy]] is the greatest of them. |
| Demiplanes | Bigger, small worlds still growing: a patch of forest full of alseid, or a chunk of elemental earth crawling with midnight dwarves. Many of today's worlds are thought to have started this way. |
| Ruins of the Ancients | The wheel-shaped cities of the vanished giants who first built the Labyrinth, each with four or eight roads leading off to other worlds, most of them dead ones now. |

Travelers sort the worlds they visit into five kinds:

| World Type | What It Means |
| --- | --- |
| Sunlit | Safe, growing, and untouched by the Void. |
| Warded | Safe, but guarded and watchful against evil visitors. |
| Shadowed | The Void has a thin hold, and evil has strongholds on the margins. Midgard is one. |
| Darkened | Undead, fiends, deadly mists, and plague are common. |
| Lost | Burned, frozen, airless, or crumbled. Nothing that lives can thrive there. |

### Who Lives in the Maze
Anyone from any world might settle in the Labyrinth after finding their way in. Its peoples are usually described as settled (living in bottled cities and demiplanes), nomadic (wandering from world to world), or a mix of the two. The great factions are covered in [[Factions of the Labyrinth]]. Others worth knowing about:

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| The far traders | Peddlers and tinkers who go farther than anyone else and are rarely trusted but rarely turned away. |
| The Boatwrights | They ferry travelers up and down the River Styx. |
| The celestials | Their bright worlds keep to themselves. When a celestial host rides out, which happens once in centuries, the Labyrinth itself shifts around them. |
| People who stumble in by accident | Called Wanderers. Those who die lost in the maze sometimes rise as the Lost, undead who never stop looking for the way out. |

### Keeping Time
With no seasons of its own, the Labyrinth counts time in torches (an hour, about how long a torch burns), days of 24 torches, seasons of up to 91 days, and years of four seasons. Every faction uses the Hub calendar, which counts from the year the minotaur Arvannos first wandered into the Great Maze, about 5,100 years ago. The current year is 5124, in the Age of Dreams, which began when [[Cartokk]] the Dreaming God woke. The holidays nearly everyone keeps are the World Tree Festival in spring (masks, dancing, and Tree Knot cakes, announced by the ratatosk who always see the first buds), the solemn Feast of Arvannos in summer for those lost to the maze and the Void, the rowdy First Pathway Feast in autumn for young explorers, and New Year's Day, a day outside the calendar when the clocks stop.

### Getting Around
Every trip between worlds passes through the Labyrinth somewhere. The ways through it:

| Name | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Walking the maze | Tunnels, roads, and passageways, on foot or horseback. The safest way, and the way expeditions find new worlds. |
| The oak roads | The branches of [[Yggdrasil]], the World Tree, used by the Old Ring. Wherever a forest grows, the Elders seem to have a way in nearby. |
| The river road | The River Styx, which only goes where it goes. The [[Minor Wayfaring Groups\|Boatwrights]] know it best. |
| The star roads | Routes across the Astral Sea, comfortable for big groups but plagued by derro pirates and worse. |
| Portals and gates | Built by the Keepers of the Keys (clockwork and precise) and the Concord of Stars (blazing bright, and short-lived). |
| Dreams | Servants of the Dreaming God sometimes fall asleep dreaming of a place and wake up there. |
| Void rifts | Tears opened by cultists and the creatures called rippers. Unstable, and they leave ruined land behind when they collapse. |

Shortcuts can misfire. A portal, dream, or rift sometimes drops travelers somewhere else entirely for half a day or more before sending them on to where they meant to go. Walking never does this.
