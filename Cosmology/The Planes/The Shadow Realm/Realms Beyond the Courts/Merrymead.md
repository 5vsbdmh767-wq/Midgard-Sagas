A city of joy, light, and strength would be unusual in any kingdom; in the dark lands of the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]], Merrymead seems like a mirage. It is built of great oak timbers daubed with clay and painted in bright, warding patterns of flowering vines, with beehives on every rooftop and droves of pigs grown fat on acorns wandering its streets. It is a lively spot in a shadowy forest, busy with trade because it sits at the crossroads between [[Fandeval]], the [[Moonlit Glades]], and the forests of the shadow fey. It is a very seasonal city, fast and even frantic in high summer and fall, sleepy in winter, and hard-pressed in spring.

## A Road to Midgard

Merrymead's role as a crossroads reaches beyond the Shadow Realm. The forest paths northeast of the city hold a shadow road to the [[Wasted West|Western Wastes]] of Midgard: the many layers of fern, bracken, and undergrowth lead straight into the temple of [[Bastet|Ailuros]] in [[Bourgund]], where worshippers are sometimes surprised by the sudden appearance of bearfolk.

## The Honey Fair

Each year at high summer, the king and queen of Merrymead open a trade fair, one of the few in the Shadow Realm. Anyone may come who can keep its rules: fair speech, fair bargains, and no brawling outside the Royal Ring. Many deals are struck for great vats of honey and mead, for lengths of bearfolk timber and goblin moonsteel, and even for strange treasures dug out of [[Soriglass]] or brought down from the stars. Sometimes the fair carries on into the mortal world: as it ends, the bearfolk king of [[Björnrike]] opens the Honey Road for a week and invites the bearfolk of the Shadow Realm to come to his sunny lands, renewing the bonds between them and the lands they came from long ago.

## The Slumbering Winter

Merrymead still has people in the winter months, but its bearfolk do little but sleep, dream, and wake now and then to see the snow. During the slumbering months the regency goes to a trusted human, such as the high priestess of [[Charun]], the boatwoman Linne Mallemele, or to the high priestess of Umbeserno, Snuffling Tilda, Keeper of the Snoring Temple, who, like all priestesses of the Lord of Sleep, goes to sleep late and wakes early. Whichever of them it is watches over the bears and keeps the city's business moving, slowly, until spring. Their main goal is to lay in enough for the spring hunger.

## The Spring Hunger

With the spring thaw Merrymead stirs and wakes, very thin and very hungry. The darakhul seem to understand the bearfolk hunger in a way gnomes and humans don't quite, and their merchants always push through the late snows to bring fish, spring lamb, potatoes, and other provisions. Enormous amounts of food are eaten in a few weeks as the bearfolk wake family by family, and spring provender costs double the rest of the year. Soon the festivals of Bengta and Charun are held, the shrine of Umbeserno performs the rite of Waking the Last Sleeper, and the dream season ends as the bearfolk go back to their hives, fishponds, groves, and fields.
