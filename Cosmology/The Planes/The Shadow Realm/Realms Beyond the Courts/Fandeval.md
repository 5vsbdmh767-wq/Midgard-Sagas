Shadow goblins and umbral humans are many in the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]], but they usually live under shadow fey or darakhul rule. Not in Fandeval, the City of Goblins, a free city where the Lethe meets the [[The Ever River|Styx]], with strange customs and traditions going back to the founding of its goblin dynasty: its moon towers, its gangsters, and its scavengers on the river.

The city was granted to the Barkwater soothsayers, a clan of shadow goblins, for services rendered to the Crow Prince, a fey lord who has since abandoned his own court. The goblins still remember his gift fondly, and in gratitude they still carry his messages and tokens to Queen Valda in the [[Court of Golden Oaks]].

## The Crow Prince and the Goblins

While he ruled, the Crow Prince shared treasures, fiefdoms, arcane lore, and more with the ravenfolk, shadow goblins, and umbral humans who supported his work in illusion magic, ancient shadow roads, lunar prophecy, and moonlight weaving. The shadow goblins were especially valuable in that last art, and he rewarded their generosity with his own. The quest he pursues now may involve the goblins in some way, and now and then he visits Fandeval to consult its soothsayers and scholars.

## The City

Small towers throughout the city call out the hours of moonrise, midday, and moonset and watch for fire, since the city is built mostly of wood. The shadow goblins cut their timber in the nearby forest, and though most people think of Fandeval as a river or swamp town, it turns out just as many barrels, ships, and carts for the shadow roads.

## The Waterfront

Fandeval makes what it can of the black waters of the Styx: boatbuilding, a very busy fish market, and costermongers' stalls selling freshwater oysters. Shadow goblin fortunetellers often read fish guts or oyster shells, so their stands clutter the waterfront too, between reeking pubs and well-watched warehouses. Shadow goblins have a wider reputation as lazy thieves, but among themselves they look after their friends and neighbors.

The largest house on the waterfront is Grandfather's House, because the waterfront belongs to Grandfather Clay Nethersmoke, a particularly rotund shadow goblin. His network of corrupt lieutenants, dock bosses, watchmen, and even judges, mooncatchers, and guildmasters skims a little silver from every barge. He is a loyal supporter of the king, and he takes a toll on the sale of almost everything brought through the gates or made in the city, from fish and beer to memory philters, nets, and other enchanted goods.

## The Cat Legion

Among those who patrol the city is a company of notably quick and lithe catfolk, descendants of visitors from a distant desert land, who serve King Flibbarn as an elite guard and fighting corps. The Cat Legion guards its privileges as the finest warriors and sharpest-eyed bodyguards in the king's service, and its members also act as his messengers and agents against the shadow fey, the ghouls, and others. Grandfather Clay seems to have some arrangement with the legion: his shops and warehouses are rarely bothered, while his rivals' are sometimes shut down and their illegal goods seized.

## The Wading Giants

Three river giants, also called Styx giants, live on the eastern shore and carry travelers into the Sodgrass Marshes and across the river from near the Styx Tower to the Goblin Plains. The giants, nicknamed Memory, Sorrow, and Loss, use glistening mooncatcher's nets, made only by Fandeval's mooncatcher goblins and priests of Hecate, to pull souls and memories out of the water. Bottled, these are sold to the mooncatchers and wizards, who turn them into memory philters, healing draughts, and sometimes moonsteel weapons.

## Moon Towers and Moonlight Masses

Fandeval is famous for its moon towers, each topped with a globe-shaped dome that lights up at night. Built as shrines to [[Hecate]], and as acts of showy devotion by the wealthy, they also house the Lady of Darkness's priests. The brightest is joined to the Cathedral of the Dark Moons, which holds private services for initiates of the goddess's inner mysteries at each new moon and full moon (both moons wax and wane together). Her Luminance Salarra Mortre leads them, and her rites include the Transformation of Moonlight, which turns lesser fey into shadow goblins. Other ceremonies are said to turn silver into memory philters or to weave nets for the Styx giants.

## Shores of the Great and Endless Sea

Beyond the marshes the sea is dark, rough, and dangerous, and the shadow fey, goblins, and umbral humans all know the ebon tides run strong and destructive there, raising reefs, shoals, and towering waves. Most fisherfolk keep to the marshes, the river, and the tidal flats. The small Saltworker Tribe of shadow goblins boils seawater in kettles over timber from the Salt Wood, making a fine, dusty, flavorful salt sold in Fandeval and across the realm. No trade goes far on the [[The Ebon Tides|Shade Sea]], though. Rumors of islands beyond the horizon, made by archmages or raised from the depths by great priests of [[Charun]], have never proven true. The sea is the edge of every land the umbral peoples know, and their ships always stay in sight of the coast.
