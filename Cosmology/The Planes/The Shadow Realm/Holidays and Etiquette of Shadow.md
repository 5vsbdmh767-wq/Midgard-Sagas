## The Five High Holidays

The peoples of the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] rarely celebrate anything the same way everywhere, but five holidays are widely kept, or at least politely observed.

### Twin Moon Festival

Everyone in the realm depends on its two moons, Celene and Lindar, for light, seasons, and harvests, and when both are full at once it is a celebration of life, hope, and radiant feasting. The shadow fey, goblins, and bearfolk try to outdo one another with bonfires and dances. [[Humans]] and darakhul are less keen but keep the day: usually a torchlit procession through the settlement to the fields or a shrine of [[Hecate]], a short blessing, and a feast with sweet cakes.

### River Soul Harvest

When the [[The Ever River|River Styx]] rises above its usual height, the priests of [[Charun]] often declare a River Soul Harvest Day. People fish things out of the water with moonlight nets and silver hooks, and some daring followers of the Boatman ask to be sprinkled with river water, all for good fortune in the months ahead. Fish, oysters, clams, and seaweed are the feast foods and offerings. The shadow goblins of [[Fandeval]] keep it most of all, but others take part, if only to win a bit of witchlight or enchanted flotsam to brighten the house.

### Marsh Mustering (Witchlight Day)

A favorite of children near any marsh or water, the Marsh Mustering is said to have begun as a rite of the King of Toads, an old fey lord otherwise mostly forgotten. It falls in spring, when the marshes are busy with water birds, and the druids encourage the young to go after the marsh creatures. A good catch of lively frogs, dragonflies, witchlights, and the like means a good harvest to come. Cakes and candies shaped like stars, frogs, and ducks are part of it, along with sugared eggs and sweets made from marsh roots.

### Feast of All Souls

Kept mostly by humans, goblins, and bearfolk, the Feast of All Souls remembers the dead, with grave goods left in graveyards and burial grounds and candles burning in great numbers for the departed. Some shadow fey use it to show off their wealth with candles decorated in gold leaf or silver pearls. The darakhul keep a very different version, celebrating rebirth: the shedding of mortal days for a second life.

### Double Dark Blessing

When both moons are new and darkness covers the plane, the dark gods and their cults celebrate, along with many evil spirits, haunts, and shades. In [[Soriglass]] it's kept with cold wines; in the temples of Charun, [[Alquam]], and [[Anu-Akma]], with fiery offerings. Elsewhere it is kept in much darker ways, with blood and bone and animal sacrifice.

## Etiquette of the Courts

The Shadow Realm is home to many peoples, but it is best known as the home of the [[Shadow Fey|shadow fey]]. They look much like humans or elves, but their culture is alien to most mortals, full of shifting and confusing rituals, edicts, and rules. Some of their customs change over time, but a few things hold.

### The courts are the foundation of the realm

Many lords and ladies of the shadow fey, sable elves, gnomes, and others claim to rule the Shadow Realm, but most such claims cover only a fragment of it, or only a particular time or season. [[Sarastra]], the Queen of Night and Magic, rules the Winter Court; the [[Court of the Moonlit King|Summer Court]] belongs to her estranged husband, the Moonlit King, who has not attended it in a very long time. Those who behave as the fey rulers prescribe find their time at court easier and safer.

### Wheels within wheels

The fey don't necessarily mean what they say. They are immortal creatures of whim and caprice, and they enjoy watching mortals struggle to work out what they really intend.

### Standing matters

To anyone without the standing and importance to deal with them, the courts are impenetrable mazes or empty shells. A visitor's reputation at court rises and falls with what they do: threatening a fey noble, being fooled by a shadow fey illusion, or brawling in court will cost them, while besting a worthy fey in a duel of arms or wits, receiving a courtier's gift or invitation, or saving a courtier's life or dignity will raise them.

### The fey envy mortals

Few would admit it, but the shadow fey and gnomes are jealous of mortals. They see mortal experience as truer and more primal than the lives of longer-lived, more sophisticated peoples.

### Harming a servant is dishonorable

The shadow fey may or may not like or trust their servants, but they rely on them to keep their lives comfortable, and harming or killing one is abhorrent to them.

Beyond those, shadow fey manners change with the week, the month, or whichever court holds power. At any given time, a visitor might find that:

- Civilized folk wear powdered wigs.
- Sackcloth is all the rage, and anyone not wearing a sackcloth dress or breeches is a fool.
- True gentlefolk duel with insults, not steel.
- Hunting is done only on the sixth day of the week.
- Only pretentious twits play cards.
- All the ladies of the court must use illusions to appear human.
- Boasting of one's own deeds is never acceptable on the second day of the week.
- Anyone above a certain station must have exactly thirteen servants.
- Root vegetables are disgusting and offensive to every civilized creature, and must not be eaten.
- No shadow fey may be harmed outside an honorable duel.
- No one runs, since it makes you sweat like a common laborer.
- No one enters a room without being announced in song.
