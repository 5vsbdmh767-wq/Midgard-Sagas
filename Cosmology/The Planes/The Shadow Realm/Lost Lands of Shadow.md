A place as changeable as the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] inevitably loses some of its places. They come loose from the [[Shadow Roads|shadow roads]], drift on the currents, and are seen only now and then. The driftwood villages are one example and [[Oshragora]] another, though its movements are fairly steady. Others were once well known but are now very hard to find: the Radiant Isle, the Land of Apple Blossoms, the Keep of the Shadow Drakes, and Paletree Rise out on the Whispering Plain. All are well-known legends among the umbral humans, the shadow fey, and others, and none stay put on a map.

## Keep of the Shadow Drakes

This cluster of black towers looks more like a tangled grove of trees than any fey building. Nonderil, an elder shadow drake, raised it as a gift for his mate, Violanda, and it became a seat of draconic power and a school for mages of shadow and illumination. It has been seen on the Whispering Plains, as an island in the [[The Ebon Tides|Shade Sea]], and even as a dark cloud castle floating over the hills. Wherever it appears, dozens of drakes, dragonettes, and dragons nest there, since they are the only ones who can open its gates and doors.

Rumors run wild: that the keep is full of the bones of a thousand fey, or the grimoires of a hundred mages, or the wealth of generations of gnome and human traders; that it taps a radiant well for arcane power the way you'd tap a barrel of wine; that the whole building is inscribed in Draconic with the history of the Ancients, who in this telling were shape-shifting dragons. There are bones and books there. But mostly the keep is a nesting ground. The dragons and drakes of the realm gather there until their wyrmlings hatch, then leave as soon as the young can fly, and the place passes to the next flock looking for a perfect nest. Eggshells litter the courtyards.

## Land of Apple Blossoms

Humming with bees and fragrant with apple blossom in spring, the Land of Apple Blossoms is described sometimes as an island in the sea and sometimes as five hills and seven villages, home to fair, kind-hearted humans and bearfolk who settled there long ago and wisely chose never to connect their land to the fey. It is the subject of songs and sagas, many about its treasures: golden bees and the honey of immortality, apples that reverse aging and cure the sick, berry bushes hung with jewels the size of a thumb, and springs of sweet water, pure as snow, full of bright red salmon every autumn. Some of this is surely poets at work, but tales keep returning to a circle of apple druids and to the guidance of Halla Berlesfand, a great druid of wisdom and kindness. The people there are said to grow tall and fair, untroubled by hunger or plague.

Once every few years, the great druid opens a moon portal to [[Fandeval]], and a few of the young people take apples, pears, and other fruit to market, trading them for Fandeval's salt, spices, and other rare goods.

## Paletree Rise

This drifting village grew up around a ring of oaks and a single stone that some think was one of the failed stones [[Hecate]] set in the waters before the founding of the realm. The legends say it floated well and gathered earth and plants, but never took root. The village is seen in a dozen places a year and drifts faster than most. Some of Hecate's faithful think it an especially holy place, worth seeking out. Others take the "pale tree" of its name as a sign that it is dangerous and unstable and best left alone. Chaos cultists and the satarre say it might be just the place to unbind and unravel magic, for the same reason.

## The Radiant Isle

A secluded island somewhere in a black lake in the south of the realm draws dozens or even hundreds of witchlights to its shores, and its pale glow can be seen from the far side of the lake. Those brave enough to cross find a building made of solid light on an otherwise bare, rocky outcrop: low and sprawling, with a blue central dome and white marble wings radiating from it. Witchlights flit across the lake and glide straight through its glowing walls. For those who can't, an ordinary entrance opens into a library, where instead of books and scrolls, sheets of many-colored light are stacked in neat piles, each one holding recorded knowledge. The witchlights move through the archive a few at a time, adding their own light to what's there, and brass-and-silver automatons move among the stacks, watching the shelves and the visitors.

Visitors who win the automatons' help can find strange lore gathered from across the realm, and even the multiverse; the library is said to connect to other storehouses of knowledge on other planes through doors edged in strange alphabets. The liosalfar, elementals of pure colored light, visit often. They love patterns and hate anything that disrupts the patterns of "color" they see in the world. They can be invaluable in tracking down a particular piece of lore, but anyone who threatens the archive earns their wrath.

## Natural Anchors

The shadow fey, bearfolk, and others raise buildings, castles, and whole cities on foundation stones that hold firm through the tides, but some parts of the land hold firm on their own, with no magic at all. These shadow landmarks, or natural anchors, are striking features: white cliffs, house-sized boulders out on the plains, a beach of pure white sand, a range of purple dunes, a particularly stunning peak or outcrop. They endure because the memory of the realm and its people keeps them. Most are tied to names, legends, or figures from history, and the older and more visited a site is, the better it resists black water, harsh radiance, and the like. Natives call them natural anchors: places too distinctive to wash away or to sink silently under new land.
