The [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] has existed as long as the mortal world has. It is often described as a distorted shadow of the mortal world, and there are resemblances, dark reflections, and mirrored echoes enough. But much of it belongs only to itself, above all the lands the shadow fey, goblins, bearfolk, humans, and darakhul have worked into realms and kingdoms. Great mages and priests bend the land and everything built on it into new forms, and the realm often changes its own shape in ways no one can predict.

## The Making of the Shadow Plane

In the Shadow Realm, the world is what you make it, and the first great power to learn this was not one of the fey but [[Hecate]], goddess of the moon. When she found the plane it was an ocean of light and darkness: a morass of radiant beams, trickling shadow mires, and rolling, chaotic hills and waves. She conjured a place to stand by making a stone marked with radiant runes, and so invented foundation stones. Then she brought the tides to still the waters, so that they flowed and echoed rather than crashed and stormed. Every one of her temples in the Shadow Realm still sings of her as She Who Steadied the Tides.

To make room for her followers, she raised white birch groves, tall red pines on the hills, and moon stones, and turned the waters into gentle ponds and deep lakes that reflected her glory. When it pleased her, she shared the place with her most devoted followers among the goblins, the shadow fey, some exiles from the elf lands, and later the bearfolk and a few night-loving humans. Over time more and more of the land grew solid. All around its shores the shadow still has no end, a roiling ocean of raw chaos and deepest night, touched with moonlight.

## Early Visitors

Hecate is widely held to have invited the first visitors, though some claim an older people lived in the realm before her. She brought the shadow fey, and then the sable elves came, and both brought servitor peoples with them. The shadow fey brought shadow goblins, humans, and gnomes. The sable elves brought courtfolk halflings, erina, and now and then alseid, quicksteps, and various sprites and pixies. Fey courts and kingdoms soon covered the land, in a distant age both sides call the First Flowering of Shadow.

### The Stones of the Ancients

Some ancient sagas, and fragments of early script found on strange foundation stones, speak of the Ylgaar, a race of river giants who held the plane before the shadow fey but failed to tame its tides. A few of their driftstone islands remain. Some wash up on the Saltwood strand, and now and then a goblin explorer or fisherman finds their ruins at sea. From time to time, mad prophets and divinations known only to the shadow fey reveal a whole city or region of empty roads, crumbled keeps, and echoing hills, home to a few gaunt giants who seem to know nothing of any earlier glory. The greatest of these lost lands was Olderain, said to be anchored around a grove of golden apples and the bright white tower-tomb of the last of the giants' wizards. Many goblins will happily sell you a map to it, and a boat to get there.

## The Peoples Who Came

### The Shadow Fey and Their Servitors

The Scáthesidhe, the [[Shadow Fey|shadow fey]], are always quick to say they were the first to arrive. They usually leave out why: they broke with the elves of the [[Silendora|Summer Lands]] in pursuit of power and found it in the land Hecate showed them. Their mastery of the realm reaches back past recorded history, though there have never been many of them. They brought the elven custom of using servitor peoples as their eyes, ears, and hands. The wyrd gnomes, the most distinguished of arcanists, still hold influence among them. The goblins who came with them grew attuned to moonlight magic and fey trickery. And human servants are fairly numerous, especially in the cities.

### Humans

Many humans came to the Shadow Realm as servants or dupes of the shadow fey, and some fell into it by misadventure, straying from a shadow road. Most, though, descend from those who sought the place out to gain power, study magic, or escape their own troubles, and they founded the cities and baronies humans rule today. Umbral humans, as they are called, are often thought ambitious, greedy, or a little treacherous compared to steadier servants like goblins and gnomes.

### The Darakhul

With their hatred of daylight, the darakhul found the Shadow Realm almost pleasant, and they built a new empire there. The [[Twilight Empire]] began as a renegade legion that fled the mortal [[The Darakhul Imperium|Empire of Ghouls]] after a failed coup, and its emperor in exile has since built it into a realm of its own, with ties to [[Evermaw]]. Undead dragons, their unhatched wyrmlings, and lich-kings belong to it. The darakhul keep wary, peaceful dealings with the shadow fey courts, which control most of the roads they need. Their lands also hold far more shades than anywhere else.

### The Bearfolk

The [[Bearfolk|bearfolk]] came to shadow not because they were drawn to its power, but because others were. The "shadow chewers" are said to have first chased the shadow fey into the darkness and found their planar bolt-hole. They soon realized they could never win the war against the undead and the shadow fey without understanding the Shadow Realm. In time, the first bearfolk druids, rangers, and other visitors built settlements full of light, raised warding stones, and grew whole circles of moonlit oaks, to keep evil from ruling the shadows unopposed. The moon wells, the groves, and the bearfolk themselves have given the realm its places of refuge.

### Other Peoples

[[Trollkin]], [[Ravenfolk|ravenfolk]], [[Alseid|alseid]], [[Erina|erina]], quicksteps, and others came by many paths and in much smaller numbers. [[Shades]] are fairly well known though few, and the verminous satarre are often found trying to unravel the plane through sabotage. [[Ratatosk]] seem to enjoy the company of elves, bearfolk, and gnomes and sometimes visit their settlements. Hobgoblins often arrived as mercenaries or as leaders among the shadow goblins. More rarely there are gnolls, usually servants of death gods such as [[Anu-Akma]], and subek, tosculi, orcs, dragonborn, and even catfolk, often on pilgrimage to Hecate or [[Bastet|Ailuros]]. [[Sydereans|Aasimar]] and tieflings visit as planar travelers, but they are always strangers, never natives.

## Midgard and the Shadow Realm

The Shadow Realm has many ties to Midgard. People of the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]], of [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau]], and of [[Nuria Natal|Nuria]] have all found their way into it, as did a surprising number from the Magocracies after the Mage Wars, the bearfolk as a whole, and a splinter faction of the Ghoul Empire. The trollkin share an ancient bond of blood with creatures who dwell in shadow, and the elves of the [[Arbonesse]] walk secret paths beyond the material world. Even so, most people of Midgard know little of the Shadow Realm beyond "a place of elves and hostile fey," and have enough everyday dangers at home not to go looking for more.

## Ways Between the Worlds

The Shadow Realm connects to the mortal world mainly through the [[Shadow Roads|shadow roads]], known to geomancers, elves, and the shadow fey. Most travelers on them only want to get somewhere quickly, but some get lost, or are tempted off the path by a vision, a treasure, or a refuge just beyond it. Once off the road they are in the Shadow Realm itself, and those without skill or magic find it hard to get back. Many stay.

Other connections are rarer, and fall into three kinds.

### Rituals and summoning

Rituals can call creatures out of the Shadow Realm, and powerful casters there can pull mortals in. A few casters deliberately twist a *shadow walk* spell, or speak a glamour to travelers on a shadow road, to draw them deeper into shadow. Most who arrive this way come unwillingly, and want nothing more than to get home.

### The shadow roots of Yggdrasil

The World Trees that climb to the celestial realms sometimes have hollow sections of trunk leading down toward [[Yggdrasil]]'s roots, and these pass through the Shadow Realm. This is not the same as taking a shadow road. The roots lead out of the mortal world into a tangle of passages that end in hells, planes of the undead, the ghostly afterlives of brooding northern pantheons, and other dark realms, and a traveler who doesn't know the planes can end up somewhere with no connection to shadow at all. Trollkin, bearfolk, ravenfolk, and gnomes, among others, know how to step aside into shadow this way. [[Humans]], goblins, darakhul, and the shadow fey avoid it.

### Cities fallen into shadow

Several cities have fallen into the Shadow Realm over the ages, beginning with [[Corremel]] in the days of the Gods War, and later [[Oshragora]] and parts of the Twilight Empire. Cities tied to the human god-kings and the lich-kings of the undead princes seem especially prone to it. Most fall because a mortal caster draws so much power from shadow that it consumes them and everything around them, and shadow corruption drags the whole place out of the mortal world. Corremel's fall left lasting links between Corremel-in-Light, as the folk of shadow call its rebuilt mortal twin, and Corremel-in-Shadow, ruled by the shadow fey on a dark river.
