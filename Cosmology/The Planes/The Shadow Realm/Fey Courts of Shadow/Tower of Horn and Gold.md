High in the Mistcall Mountains west of the shadow fey courts, where gray snow falls like ash from a pyre, the Tower of Horn and Gold broods, so tall its top scrapes the clouds. Its black stone was hewn from the mountains, and its name comes from its cladding: rows of great curving horns jutting from its walls, giving it a wicked, serrated outline, and bands of shadow-mined gold, a wavering blend of gleaming yellow and pitch black that catches and swallows any stray light. It is one of the oldest remnants of the shadow fey's first desperate venture into darkness.

The tower is no mere watchtower. It guards a cleft in the mountains that leads to tunnels that have never seen light, where shadow drakes have long lived and died. The drakes keep a loose alliance with the tower's fey: they defend it and serve the high knights as messengers and mounts, and in return they may hunt the lands around it. Above all, the shadow fey mine the tunnels for the elusive veins of shadow gold, and the drakes take a generous share. Most of the tower's goblins mine and smelt without rest or holiday.

Shadow gold is much like mortal gold, but it holds the essence of shadow. Raw, it looks mottled gold and black; cast or forged and polished, it can't be told from ordinary gold, until it is melted, nicked, bent, or scratched, and the shadow shows through. The shadow fey mint coins from it and trade them freely to mortals in payment and in bribes, and it is said that every shadow gold coin you accept from the fey costs you a little more of your will and soul.
