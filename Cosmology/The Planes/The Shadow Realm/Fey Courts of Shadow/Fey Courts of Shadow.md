The [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] has always belonged most of all to the fey, who used it as a source of magic and a way to travel long before they learned to make foundation stones and settle it. Their courts are much like those of the elves of [[Silendora]], the Summer Lands, in size and organization, but darker, and usually with far more gnomes, goblins, and humans. The [[Shadow Fey|shadow fey]] have never been many, but their promises of power and their gift for enchanting and controlling mortal rulers have let them pull whole cities into shadow.

## What Is a Fey Court?

A fey court is much like a human fiefdom: a land ruled by a noble or monarch who embodies its people and draws like-minded fey to their side. A small court might call itself the Baron's Court of Millrun and rule a handful of villages along a stream. A great one, like the Court of Night and Magic, might have lesser courts sworn to it, be ruled by a queen, and command whole cities, forests, and small armies.

Any fey can found a court, by claiming lands and castles already there or by mastering the ebon tides to make their own from nothing, though only the most powerful or charismatic manage to grow a backwater called Frog Manor into a wildly popular Court of Emeralds. Shadow fey, shadow goblins, lunar elves, sable elves, wyrd gnomes, quicksteps, living stars, and even trollkin (and sometimes half-elves) have all ruled courts. [[Bearfolk]], darakhul, halflings, alseid, ravenfolk, satarre, shades, and humans can be members of a court, but can't found or rule one.

The rules for titles and court names are arcane even by fey standards. A new court takes a properly modest name, such as Lilypad Manor, the Court of Cherries, or the Court of Three Bridges, ruled by a lord, lady, or keeper. In time it might grow into the Court of Ruby Blossoms or the Court of the Silent River, ruled by a princess, marquess, or count. A ruler can propose a grander name, but if they rate their own advancement too generously and the other fey refuse to use it, it becomes a mark against an "upstart court," and its popularity may wither.

### Changelings at Court

Many courts keep a tradition of hosting changelings, which they see as an honorable custom of fostering the children of other peoples. (The parents of the stolen children see it rather differently.) Human children are taken as infants, taught Elvish, Umbral, and other refined tongues, and raised until their manners and natures match their shadow fey, gnomish, or sable elf parents. The fey don't take dragonborn, dwarves, kobolds, tieflings, or trollkin as changelings, and they consider the animal folk, such as alseid, catfolk, ravenfolk, and subek, half-fey already. Stolen halflings generally become court servants. See [[Peoples of Shadow]] for the changelings of the umbral humans, and the [[Heritages#Changeling Heritage|Changeling Heritage]] for playing a fey-raised changeling.

The greatest shadow fey city, [[Corremel]], the City of Lanterns, has its own page.

## The Courts

- [[Court of Golden Oaks]]: the sable elf court of Queen Valda, joined to [[Yggdrasil]]
- [[Court of Mice and Ravens]]: the court of King Reynard, the Fox Lord
- [[Court of Midnight Teeth]]: the gnome court of Countess Phylomara and her mirrors
- [[Court of One Million Stars]]: the floating court above the clouds, ruled by Prince Valendan
- [[Court of Pale Roses]]: the court of King Frulio Cornutus and its illusion gardens
- [[Court of the Moonlit King]]: the Summer Court of Sarastra's absent husband
- [[Court of Winter's Love]]: the coldest and most desolate court, ruled by the ghost queen Kalaslurr
- [[Court of Sparrows]]: the court of the Undefeated King Marrow, sworn to King Reynard
- [[Dalliance]]: a river town of pleasure houses where the Nightbrook meets the Lethe
- [[Langmire's Court]]: the court of Oshlan the Chef, Lord of Misrule
- [[Nightbrook]]: the minor court of the three Ladies of Nightbrook
- [[Tower of Horn and Gold]]: the shadow fey fortress over the shadow drakes' gold mines
- [[Witch Queen's Court]]: the court of Nicnevin, Queen of Witches, on the Dolorous Mountain

## Fallen Courts

Over the ages of fey rule, many rulers have faded into oblivion, living on only in song and legend. Some built halls so fine that new dynasties rebuilt and remade them. A few were abandoned, and their halls remain as places where dark magic is worked, curses linger, or plotters gather. These fallen courts are well remembered, and still visited from time to time.

### Blackwolf's Court

A notorious court of warmongers, tournaments, duels, and deeds of prowess, Blackwolf's Court was one of the few ruled by a quickstep, Prince Blackwolf, who gathered powerful shadow fey, a horde of shadow goblins, and a gifted human wizard called Onri Farromir, the Mage Prince. These masters of night fighting and dueling overthrew Nightbrook and ruled it independently of the Court of Night and Magic for more than twenty-five years, teaching swordplay and banditry to anyone who swore to them. Blackwolf was finally cut down by Reynard, the Fox Lord, who kept Nightbrook for a while, until the tides drifted it too far away, and then gave it to Sarastra as a gift.

### Botethar's Court

A shadow goblin court under King Botethar, remembered for its astonishing lunar towers and astrological hexes, and for employing dwarven masons, rarely seen in the Shadow Realm, probably brought to build Botethar's tallest and most ambitious temple spires to [[Hecate]].

### Court of Drizzling Fire

A court of dancers who wove enchantment through movement, conjuring cold fire and impossibly potent enchantments through a school of patterned, paired ritual magic. All its secrets were lost when the darakhul overran it long ago, and its remains became the foundation stones of Gloomhaven. Its last rulers were the shadow fey Countess Tommika Telanni and her consort, Ondros Paraselli, and some believe they became darakhul themselves and rule again in the [[Twilight Empire]].

### Court of Knives

One of the strangest fallen courts, founded by an esoteric sect of dwarven ringmages, it became a place of smithing and wizardry where quickstep duelists paid fortunes for enchanted moon steel, shadow fey enchanters sought finely made rings and bracers, and ravenfolk warriors bought axes that could cleave stone or steel. Its king and master smith was a twisted, thin-bearded dwarf named Olbert the Lame, said by some to be a son of [[Volund]]. The court fell when Olbert refused to sell a vorpal shortsword to a shadow fey prince, even for a king's ransom. Assassins killed him soon after, and his followers fled a plague of wraiths and darakhul.

### Court of Silver Words

Once a court of sable elves devoted to spoken magic, poetry, and history, the Court of Silver Words was famous for its scriptorium, which made both magical and ordinary books and scrolls, many of which still exist. The *Book of Ebon Tides* itself is said to have been written here by the court's last ruler, the young Princess Tephanora, who was murdered when shadow goblin rogues stole the book, supposedly taking it to [[Fandeval]], though it has traveled far since. The court's carved marble and limestone buildings still stand, and visitors leave honey, flowers, candles, flags, stones, and little written notes at a memorial statue of the princess.

### Drifting Courts

The Court of Wine and Roses and the Lost Court of the Triple Moon drift with the tides. See [[The Ebon Tides]].
