The shadow roads were the first tool the natives of the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] used to keep their settlements from drifting apart on the [[The Ebon Tides|ebon tides]]. Without them the courts could never have found one another reliably, and every new court would have stayed alone. That is why the shadow fey, shadow goblins, bearfolk, humans, and even the darakhul all work so hard to patrol and keep up the roads. Every spring they go out to "walk the roads," setting fallen milestones back up, checking bridges, and renewing weak enchantments. Road building and road warding are both honored trades.

Tricksters and villains work against the roads, either to carve a road of their own or to deny one to an enemy. If a shadow road is cut, whether by terrain-shifting magic or by simply digging a ditch and pulling up stones, the two places it joined come loose. The cut ends may bend and curl and find each other again by chance or with magic. If no one mends the road, it fades back into the muck of unformed shadow or sinks into the earth without a trace, and the places it joined drift apart.

## Building a Road

The first shadow roads are credited to Hargen the Builder, a bearded gnomish engineer and surveyor who learned to fix shadow matter with rune-marked milestones, enchanted paving stones, and celestially inspired geometry. The work has long since become routine, and it falls into three stages.

### The survey

A surveyor, often a wizard or cleric, takes silver chains, a theodolite, and a notebook to the foundation stone where the road will begin. Usually this is an old path: a forest trail, a track through tall grass, or a muddy stretch of river or hills that has been useful long enough that the locals will pay to make it permanent. The surveyor lays silver markers, measures every curve, turn, and crossroads, and leaves a long thread, or for grander roads a thin silver chain, along the route.

### Laying the stones

Once the course is plotted, time matters. Grass, trees, and swampy patches are dealt with by gravel fill, flagstones over a roadbed, a jolting way of wooden slats, or just cleared brush and a line of markers, waystones, and guide poles. Shadow goblin or hobgoblin gangs usually do this work for fey roads; young bearfolk, erina, and even molefolk do it for roads into bearfolk lands.

### Binding the points

The last step is arcane. A ritual binds the foundation stones, silver filings, a crumble of prayers, and chunks of opal or moonstone into the road, making it proof against the slow warping of the tides. A bound road can still stretch, bend, and turn as the places it names drift. Shadow fey clerics of [[Hecate]] usually perform the binding with shadow goblin mooncatchers and moon priests, though bearfolk druids have lately become masters of it too, and the darakhul do it with a little more bone and blood and a little less moon and silver.

A new road is expensive. In easy country, each mile costs at least 100 gp and up to a month's work to build, and nearly as much to keep up; in marsh, hills, or deep forest, five or ten times that.

Once bound, a road lasts until it is destroyed or unbound. Some are little more than forest trails linking a village or a ring of standing stones to a larger town. Others are paved the whole way and stay that way even as the land around them changes. A road is a living thing, and it stretches to fit the shifting terrain.

## Old Roads and Their Gods

Some shadow roads are holy, tied to ancient gods older than the fey. These "pilgrim roads" or "old paths" are among the oldest, best-warded, and most revered roads in the realm, including the road from [[Fandeval]] to the Court of Night and Shadow and the short stretch from the Hawthorne Grove to [[Moonlit Glades|Solvheim]], a city built on the ruins of an ancient fey court. Their roadside shrines often honor a small pantheon, with the [[The Old Roads|Old Roads]] beside [[Hecate]], [[Charun]], and sometimes [[Bengta]] or [[Bastet|Ailuros]]. A shrine might be a little stone- or tile-roofed cabinet, a small cave, something like a shepherd's hut, or just a convenient boulder or cliff. Travelers leave worn-out boots and sandals, candles, and the like.

The roads have minor gods of their own, road saints akin to river spirits or to the fey lords who rule small domains. They are often found in pairs, and they are called the gods of the Old Roads. They have no priesthood except the Doorlord, but travelers honor them in small ways. Their voices can be heard at their shrines, small roofed statues that stand most often where a shadow road splits into a shadow path and a side path, sometimes called a shadow door or portal path. A traveler who leaves a small offering and stays close to the shrine can rest there in relative safety, because something of the old consecration still holds. Some of the gods of the Old Roads have ancient names carved in runes on their shrines: Clauveg, Eiber, [[Hune]], Mantellon, Raggel, Viacorra, and Vanta. Their names are most often invoked when opening a shadow portal.

## Famous Roads and Portals

The most famous shadow roads are the original Black Road to [[Zobeck]], the Lantern Road between the two halves of [[Corremel]], and the Cat's Road near Fandeval. There are many others, and portals to [[Free Cities of the Desert|Siwal]], [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau]], [[Triolo]], and the Scarlet Citadel. The best known of the rest are these:

| Road | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Mage's Road | The [[Court of Midnight Teeth]] | The mage city of [[Magocracy of Allain\|Bemmea]] |
| Ailuros Road | Ailuros's temple in [[Merrymead]] | [[Bourgund]] |
| Flower Road | The [[Court of Pale Roses]] | [[Gardens of Carnessa\|Carnessa]] |
| Moon Road | Fandeval | [[Kammae Straboli\|Kammae]] |

Others are far more obscure. The Trickster's Road to [[Skaldholm]] is known mostly to the bardic College of Shadows and some elderly bearfolk. The Maze Road from the Echoing Shore to [[Capleon]] is known among minotaurs and some shadow goblins. The River Road in the [[Arbonesse]] continues on to brighter realms. And the road to the gnomish city of Hexen in [[Niemheim]] is best known for ending in a mirror in the Court of Midnight Teeth.

Not every road leads to Midgard. Roads run to [[Silendora]] and the Summer Lands of the elves, to [[Yggdrasil]] through the [[Court of Golden Oaks|Court of the Golden Oak]] and various bearfolk roads, and, alarmingly directly, to [[Evermaw]], Niflheim, and the Luminous Hell of Fire and Sulfur by way of roads in the [[Twilight Empire]].
