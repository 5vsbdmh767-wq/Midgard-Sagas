A bright light on a dark river, Corremel shines like a beacon from a thousand black-iron lanterns. It is the sparkling stronghold of the shadow fey and the source of much of their wealth and power, ruled by the Black Prince, Hander Svenk. Its foundations are enormous, in its palaces and its charcoal-cobbled streets alike, and its watch is vigilant and suspicious of troublemakers. Mortals call it Corremel-in-Shadow, though the city's own people rarely do.

Corremel fell into shadow in the days of the Gods War, when magic rose to such heights along its arcane river that all its beauty was swept away into the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]]. Its rebuilt mortal twin is known in shadow as Corremel-in-Light. A small circle of mages in the city who call themselves the Bright Sparks dare to believe that Corremel could fall back out of shadow and become a mortal land again, if the right old gods could be made to pay attention.

## Trade and Roads

Corremel stands on the banks of the River Lethe near two [[Shadow Roads|shadow roads]] to the mortal lands, which makes it a great trade hub where the people of shadow mix with mortals of every kind. One road runs along the riverbank inside the city to the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]] near [[Zobeck]]; shadow goblin guides sometimes offer to help visitors open its portal, though they usually just open it and leave the rest of the journey to the traveler. The other runs to Corremel-in-Nuria, a city of ale on a crocodile-haunted river in [[Nuria Natal]]. Through these roads, mortal food, textiles, and trade goods reach the shadow fey. The Lantern Road between the two halves of Corremel is one of the most famous shadow roads in the realm.

All that mixing shows in the city's buildings, an amalgam of styles, and in its dialect of Umbral, which is full of human loanwords. Its bridge is the main crossing between the forests and courts of the Queen's Wood and the plains, the Saltwood, the [[The Ebon Tides|Shade Sea]], and the markets of [[Fandeval]]. Visitors come from all over the realm for trade goods, for the services of arcanists, spies, loremasters, and assassins, and for the pleasures the fey offer, at the right price.

## The Law in Corremel

Visitors should know some of the city's stranger laws.

- Speaking ill of the Queen of Night and Magic or of the Black Prince is treason.
- Vandalizing an image of any member of the court means permanent expulsion from the city.
- Damaging someone's reputation is punished as harshly as harming their body, or more so. (Gossip is nonetheless a local pastime.)
- The accused has no right to representation at trial, and guilt or innocence can be decided in their absence.
- Ignorance is no excuse for acting ignorant.
- The Black Prince has the final word and is exempt from all laws.

## Places in the City

### Horn Gate Inn

Many mortal visitors find this lively tavern, loud with music and talk, their best first stop. It stands on the northern edge of town, a big horn-colored mansion with a slate-gray roof and many gables, turrets, and balconies, all hand-carved trim, hardwood floors, and great stained-glass windows inside. Every bedroom has a stone fireplace. The music changes nightly but is always merry, and playing here is an honor given only to the best musicians; locals come to dance jigs with friends and visitors, and outsiders may find fey forwardness a bit much. The inn deals only in mortal coin, not any currency minted in shadow, though the owners will take goods for lodging, and its food is ordinary mortal fare, free of the realm's corruption. Harried Harald, a disheveled, hardworking human in his late fifties, tends bar and shares news. Tamikula, a sharp-eyed half-elf with little patience for rude customers, waits tables and goes out of her way for polite ones. The inn is run by Rodrig Goldseam, a portly, middle-aged dwarf. The staff call the inn's most opulent room the Whispering Room, and say sleeping there is a bad idea.

### The Pluming Lantern

Locals call this pleasure salon the Stick and Dome: a big square building of turquoise marble with few windows, a crystal dome that scatters broken rainbows inside, and a single narrow tower at one corner. Perfume and incense hang thick in the air and cling to every cloth, and faded tapestries cover the walls. A public bath costs 1 sp, a private bath for up to eight costs 1 gp per person, and a secret bath, enchanted against every kind of eavesdropping and popular for making deals, costs 100 gp per person. Nearly any consensual pleasure can be had here for a price, from 10 gp for half an hour with a skilled courtesan to thousands for far stranger delights, but only for mortal coin; the house takes no trade in goods or services. It is run by Masika Gamall, a priestess of [[Bastet]] in exile from Per-Bastet.

### The Sheltered Market

Most of the goods that come into Corremel are bought and sold in this huge, ancient obsidian building ringed with fluted columns, its walls and ceiling covered in friezes of the shadow fey in diplomacy. Unlike the city's other markets, it is almost staid, with stalls in uniform lots and the richer merchants taking several side by side. Its owner, Mistress Twylla Yoop, runs it with greedy efficiency and fines anyone who breaks the rules posted all around it. In disputes she rules for whoever pays the biggest bribe, and often fines the loser for wasting her time. Those who can't pay their fines are sent to Scourge Circle, roughly a day for every 50 gp owed. She goes easier on musicians and much harder on kobolds and goblins.

In theory almost anything short of an artifact can be found here.

- Any ordinary, non-consumable item takes 1d3 hours to find, or 1 hour with a successful DC 10 CHA (Deception, Intimidation, or Persuasion) check.
- An exotic item, or a common or uncommon magic item, takes 1d6 hours and a successful DC 20 INT (Investigation) or CHA (Deception, Intimidation, or Persuasion) check.
- A rare magic item takes 4d6 hours and a successful DC 25 INT (Investigation) or CHA (Deception, Intimidation, or Persuasion) check, and its sellers rarely take coin, preferring barter.
- Very rare or unique items may not be available at all, at the GM's discretion, and usually cost a dangerous task.

### The Black Prince's Palace

The palace is a cluster of six amethyst towers, each crowned with a statue of a former ruler of the city, joined to a larger central tower with a conical roof and flying buttresses. The lower floors of the central tower are open to the public, and visitors tour its ancient collections of art, trophies, murals, and hangings. The upper floors belong to the Black Prince and his household; his hundreds of shadow goblin servants live in two basement levels below. The six outer towers are prisons for the few who have truly earned the prince's anger, and three are occupied: one by his first lover, who spurned him; one by a lunar elf assassin who nearly killed him in public; and one by a shadow fey critic who published a satire showing the Black Prince as a kind-hearted fairy, and whose tower now rings with mocking laughter at all hours. Now and then the prince's pale younger sister, Rosali Svenk, the Pale Witch, wanders the central tower, unnerving staff and visitors alike, and wherever she is, everyone hears the clink of the tiny animal skulls on her gnarled staff as if she were right behind them.

### Firefade Park

Maples and oaks fill this park on the northern bank of the Lethe, separated by narrow paths of gray cobbles, and the whole park glows a dim orange-yellow. Fire does only half damage inside it, and anyone caught trying to burn its trees is held for trial at the Black Prince's leisure. Its central clearing is never empty, because everyone comes to see the great fountain: a bear's head with a human head in its jaws, a frog in the human's mouth, and a carp held by the tail in the frog's, all carved in the same white marble as the River Bridge. From the carp's mouth pours a constant stream of pure, cold, clear water straight from the [[Elemental Plane of Water]]. Six well-armed shadow fey guard it, and anyone who wants to drink must first pay the prince's clerk, Tamrana Lanzt, in silver or something stranger. Drinking without paying means arrest. A creature that drinks removes one level of exhaustion and gains temporary hit points equal to its level, and can't benefit again until it finishes a long rest.

### Shady Lane

Most of the city is brightly lit, but less savory folk always find the dark corners. Shady Lane, off Guild Avenue, is a narrow alley where the lanterns burn dimmer and even magical light is muted, to the lamplighters' frustration; it is never brighter than dim light. Now and then, people who wander into Shady Lane come out, confused, onto another street that doesn't connect to it at all.

### Lamplighter's Guildhall

In the best-lit city in the Shadow Realm, the lamplighters do a brisk business. Their guildhall is an ostentatious blue-and-gold mansion with many gables and turrets four stories tall. The guild is run by Head Lamp Marti Dwerik, a ruthless careerist who removes rivals before they can organize and makes her dozens of underlings report to her throughout the day. Most of the city's lights are magical, and the guild has many talented casters; the dozens of members with little or no magic, called "skips" behind their backs, tend the ordinary lights and report any broken or counterspelled magical ones to the Illuminator's Committee.

### The River Bridge and the Lethe

The city's most famous sight is the ancient white marble bridge across the black River Lethe, which appears on Corremel's coat of arms. It is older than the city itself; lunar elves and [[Hecate]]'s faithful say the goddess set its foundation stones herself, and little candlelit shrines to her spring up on both banks. Every support of the bridge bears a carving of a soul recently lost to the river, faces horrified and haunting, and the carvings keep changing as the Lethe claims the unwary. The Lanternkeepers patrol it constantly, because it is one of the few safe ways across. The Lethe is a torrent of inky shadow, freezing cold and haunted by those who died in or near it, and many who tried to wade or swim it never came out.

### Joyful Park

On the southern bank of the Lethe, Joyful Park is a favorite stop for visitors, close to the bridge and full of street entertainers performing in its small amphitheater.

### Market Stalls

Carts, makeshift stalls, mats piled with goods, hawkers, and wandering peddlers fill this riot of color, smell, and noise, where most of the city's food is bought and sold. Vendors from the mortal world, the Shadow Realm, and many other planes compete here, and quickly learn not to get attached to any one spot. The stalls are overseen by Ander Wyth, a charmingly wry shadow fey who treats every vendor alike and settles disputes in the most ironic way possible, so that neither side is ever quite satisfied.

- Common food, including dishes from every nation of the mortal realm, takes less than an hour to find.
- Exotic food and drink take 1d4 hours of searching and a successful DC 15 INT (Investigation) or CHA (Deception, Intimidation, or Persuasion) check.
- A rare or illicit substance takes 2d4 hours and a successful DC 20 check of the same kind. Some things may not be found at all, at the GM's discretion.

### The Tumbles

Most of the buildings in the Tumbles are crumbling or rotten, some missing walls, stairs, or whole sections of roof, and pieces of houses have been known to fall on passersby. Its people are often desperate for food, water, or medicine, and most of Corremel avoids it. The fabric of the Shadow Realm is thin here, and invisible portals to other planes open often. They don't last; each eventually collapses, tearing away a little of the neighborhood with it. The adventurous and the desperate wander the Tumbles looking for a way out to somewhere else, and a few locals hire themselves out as guides. There is said to be a small shrine to Laughing Loki here, which can only be found by those who aren't looking for it.

### Scourge Circle

This small marble-tiled square is full of cages, platforms, stocks, pillories, and other devices of punishment, and at any time twenty or more creatures are being punished there. Painful punishments and executions happen in Corremel, but the usual punishment is humiliation, and the First Tormentor, Smiling Aymag, is famously inventive; lately she favors having prisoners stripped, shaved, coated in honey, rolled in breadcrumbs, and posed for the public. Prisoners are kept in sparse cellhouses just north of the square.

### Shiallee's Carriage House

This big, beautifully kept lot has room for many carriages, and its staff are skilled and meticulous at repairs. An immense white-brick mansion on the lot welcomes every visitor to Corremel, for 5 gp, which mostly limits its clientele to natives of the realm and the more successful mortal merchants. It has twenty guest rooms, a small bathhouse, two dining rooms, a small ballroom, a large study, and a games room with billiards, darts, chess, cards, and dice. The study holds books from many planes on everything from angelic plumage to the horns of demons. Guests dance every evening in the ballroom under floating glass lanterns, to the playing of a shadow fey quintet nicknamed the Handsome Five. The elusive, rarely seen owner, Shiallee, seldom takes coin for anything but carriage work; she prefers favors, services, and information, and sells them in turn. If it happened in the Shadow Realm, Shiallee has probably heard of it, or knows someone who has.

### Lanternkeepers' Barracks

The city watch lives in this large gray stone hall with a peaked black roof. They are called Lanternkeepers, or just Keepers, for the oval silver lanterns of magical light each carries as a badge of office. It is a crime punishable by death for anyone else to own one, and if one is ever lost or stolen, the whole barracks turns out to get it back. First Blade Aten Aerlich and Second Blade Tristelia Caernefille run the barracks strictly. Several hundred Keepers serve, with at least twelve five-person patrols on the streets at any time, usually a captain and four guards, sometimes joined by a mage or a priest of [[Sarastra]]. A gatehouse by the barracks is supposed to help the public; in practice, the Keepers posted there despise the job and pointedly ignore visitors.

### House of Dream Birds

This square house surrounds a closed courtyard full of lemon trees, flowering vines, and small white birds. It is a temple to [[Gytellisor]], the dreaming god, tended by the sable elf priestess Ainoress Gabboneth. The faithful come to feed the birds, write gloomy poetry or joyful love notes, and drink wine or smoke requiem in the evenings, so it feels more like a fashionable public house than a temple. Every day, though, the high priestess and her acolytes perform the rites and sacrifice a bird on the god's small basalt altar. The temple's scriptorium copies the god's sacred verse and, for a fee, anything else the faithful, merchants, lovers, or spies want written.
