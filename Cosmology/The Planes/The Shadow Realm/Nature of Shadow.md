*Optional, from Book of Ebon Tides.*

To natives, a traveler's questions about how anyone survives in the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] are foolish. The laws of nature may differ from the mortal world's, but the realm is hospitable to anyone who takes the time to learn its ways. This page collects those ways, and the rules that go with them.

## Moons, Stars, and Sky

The Shadow Realm has no day and night. A perpetual twilight covers it, deepening to full darkness in the valleys and canyons that cross the land. This light isn't sunlight, so creatures that suffer in the sun, vampires included, endure it without trouble.

The main sources of light are the realm's two moons, Celene and Lindar, which most inhabitants know as the feminine and masculine faces of [[Hecate]], or sometimes as Hecate and [[Kupkoresh]], or [[Lada]]. Their phases are linked: when Celene is full, so is Lindar, and when Lindar is new, so is Celene. They give just enough light for the realm's pale, thin crops and trees to grow.

The stars are a handful of jewels, often hidden by cloud, but when they show they are just like the mortal world's: chips of brilliance, red, blue, and diamond white. The constellations the umbral peoples know are the Dragon, the North Arrow, the Leaping Salmon, the Swan, the Hunter, and the Greater and Lesser Fox. The sky is usually cloudy or misty, but when it opens it is richer than most, black and gray with overtones of mauve, violet, and deep blue, and sometimes vivid orange and burgundy under the full moons. Its clouds are usually gray or white, less often silver, blue, or even luminous.

## Beasts of Shadow

Most plants and animals live just as you'd expect from the land around them, and most are entirely ordinary. Some are dark reflections of their mortal kin, such as black-furred squirrels and twilight-purple deer with black antlers. Some are plainly warped: the owl-like, human-mouthed stryx glides silently through the dark forests, shadow fey nobles keep flocks of steel-winged eala, and the corrupted, unicorn-like shadhavar race across the plains. White wolves, black bears, and crimson-feathered hawks are common, living on pale field mice, black squirrels, and similar shadow-hued prey. Monsters are far more common than in the mortal world.

Ancient beasts still thrive in shadow: mammoths, giant elk, ground sloths, sabertooth tigers, and cave bears. The dreams of the bearfolk seem to keep them near, and they are most common in the [[Moonlit Glades]], though the sable elves and shadow fey know well how to hunt them in the dark forests and open grasslands. Many are called "elders," such as the elder elk or elder mammoth, and they are especially prized prey, sometimes guarded by will-o'-wisps, witchlights, or stranger things. [[Goblins|Goblin]] tribes now and then bring one to a court or to [[Fandeval]] as a curiosity.

### Shadow-Touched Beasts (Variant)

Ordinary Beasts in the Shadow Realm have darkvision to 60 feet and resistance to cold damage. At the GM's discretion, these traits fade a week after the beast leaves the plane.

## Food and Water

Fresh water is hard to find, because darkness flows through many waterways instead of water. The natives of the realm drink this inky stuff happily, but visitors from the mortal world find it bitter and unpleasant. It does quench thirst, but it carries the taint of shadow corruption. A few springs give pure water, and sometimes a stream runs clear for a while before darkness fills it again; those who haven't given themselves over to the realm guard such places fiercely. Fruit grows, glossy and black, and animals can be hunted, but food from the Shadow Realm tastes bland to visitors and often hints at rot or mold.

#### Foraging

Visitors have disadvantage on checks to forage for food and water in the Shadow Realm, and they find only half the normal amount (natives have no penalty). A visitor can choose to forage normally, without disadvantage and for a full yield, but anyone who eats or drinks what they gather this way is exposed to shadow corruption.

#### Spotting bad water

A native of the Shadow Realm can tell whether a body of water is hazardous with a successful DC 13 WIS (Survival) check; visitors make the check with disadvantage. On a failure, the water seems normal.

### Dark Water

The black shadow that passes for water in the Shadow Realm runs swift and cold. Every stream, river, and lake counts as frigid water, whatever the surrounding climate. Worse, the spirits of things that died in or near the water are a hazard of their own. A creature that starts its turn in dark water must succeed on a DC 12 STR save or be grappled and pulled 5 feet deeper by the hungry spirits; the grapple lasts until the start of the creature's next turn. Boats give only uncertain protection. A creature that drowns in dark water can't be returned to life by *revivify* or *raise dead*; it takes more powerful magic, such as *resurrection*, to free its soul. Shadow goblins, shadow fey, river giants, and most fiends are immune to dark water.

## Entering the Shadow Realm

Natives need no help getting in, but the realm draws visitors from the elven lands, the mortal worlds, and the hells and heavens, some looking for souls lost in the Styx, some for places to recharge powerful magic weapons. There are five main ways in.

#### Nodes and portals

Gates, portals, and nodes look like doors, arches, standing stones, or arcane circles on the far side. Each must be activated by a magic item, a seasonal sacrifice, ritual magic, or similar power, and once open it stays open briefly, long enough for several creatures to pass.

#### The River Styx

Travelers float in from the hells, the roots of [[Yggdrasil]], and anywhere else the [[The Ever River|Styx]] flows. Charun's boatmen, yugoloths, and even angelic guides have been known to point out safe places to land.

#### Radiant wells

These glowing beacons connect to the celestial planes, but only angels, celestials, and good-hearted creatures can generally use them to enter. The fey, the undead, and others treat them as hazards.

#### Spells

*Plane shift* and *gate* can bring a party to the Shadow Realm, and powerful fey and ghoulish casters make shadow portal scrolls to bring entertaining new visitors in.

#### Shadow roads

The most common way in is by the roads that join the worlds. They are hidden and hard to open for humans and other non-fey, but much easier for shadow fey, elves, gnomes, and their like. A trip between the Shadow Realm and the mortal world takes several days. See [[Shadow Roads]] and Traveling the Shadow Roads below.

## Where the Veil Wears Thin

The Shadow Realm is a reflection of the world's fears and hopelessness as much as of its shape, and it draws heavily on symbol and resonance. Its power waxes and wanes with the conditions that match or oppose its nature.

### Convergence

At certain times of year the barrier between the worlds grows thin. Usually that is not enough to open a passage, but it can draw creatures that help wear the barrier down. The depth of winter, and above all the solstice, resonates with the cold dark of shadow. Festivals at that time celebrate the sun's return, partly in defiance of the very real danger of that night: lanterns and bonfires drive back the dark on the longest night, and joyous revelry weakens shadow's power. Solar eclipses, and, the elves say, the rising of the fabled planet Melgros, show shadow's strength. The deepest caverns and tunnels beneath Midgard have never seen sunlight, and some of them bore not only through rock and earth but through the veil itself. Explorers fleeing a darakhul patrol might cross into another world without knowing it.

### Erosion

The two worlds affect each other constantly. Events and people in one can change the other, and some acts can forge a connection between them. Traumatic events that breed sorrow and drag down hope become chisels chipping at the boundary. Murders and executions fill a place with fear, and a place often used for such bloody work takes on an air of unease. An orphanage where children languish until all hope of love dies grows darker every year, and so do sanitariums and mortuaries. In such places, doorways can open into the Shadow Realm.

### Dark Folk

Humanoids corrupted by shadow pass its taint to their children, and over generations these children change. Called dark folk, they shun the light, seek out gloom, and often develop innate shadow magic as they grow. They form or join shadow cults and never feel at home except where the Shadow Realm's grasp has crept in, or better yet, within the realm itself.

## Hazards of the Shadow Realm

Most of the realm is ordinary earth and forest, but some dangers are unique to it, or far more common there than anywhere else.

### Doomsand

Doomsand works like quicksand for sinking and escaping, but this ashen-gray slurry is soaked in shadow and drains the will of anyone it holds. A creature that starts its turn in doomsand must succeed on a DC 12 WIS save or become despondent. When it finishes a long rest, it can repeat the save, ending the effect on a success. Doomsand is usually found in marshes and swamps, or as fine dust in deserts.

### Ebon Tides

Ebon tides are great waves of shadow mire and crackling shadow matter, a temporary pulse of arcane power that unmakes the land as it passes. See [[The Ebon Tides]] for their signs.

### Hungry Gloom

Some places in the Shadow Realm, and places in the mortal world where the barrier to it is thin, develop hungry gloom. These areas look especially dark and grim and swallow light. Light that overlaps hungry gloom is reduced by one step (bright light becomes dim light, dim light becomes darkness), and darkvision can't improve the light there. Shadow-touched creatures see in hungry gloom as if it were bright light.

### Leaden Clouds

These dark, heavy clouds weigh on the spirit. A creature beneath a leaden cloud of sorrow must succeed on a DC 12 WIS or CHA save to make an attack, cast a spell, or move faster than a normal walk, though it can still defend itself. Some items, such as emerald goblets, and spells such as *aid*, *calm emotions*, *enthrall*, and *lesser restoration*, as well as a barbarian's Rage, negate the effect.

### Radiant Wells

A radiant well looks like a beacon of light at the surface and swallows anything thrown into it. It is a link to one of the celestial planes, and it sheds searing bright daylight in a 60-foot radius and up into the clouds, which makes wells excellent landmarks, though ebon tides can move them. A creature that enters a radiant well falls for 1 round, then is flung upward out of another well within 2d20 miles (1d100 miles for an evil creature). A Small or smaller creature is thrown about 30 feet up before falling beside the well, possibly taking falling damage; a Medium or Large creature about 15 feet; a Huge or larger creature simply lands at the well's edge. Especially large or active wells sometimes deal radiant damage to evil creatures, and fey wizards and clerics sometimes use them as portals to the celestial planes.

### Shadow Mire

Shadow mire gathers along the shores of ponds, lakes, and streams: pure shadow matter that has eaten away the soil, roots, and plants and replaced them with a thin layer of viscous purple-black gloop. It looks like dark water, and at night can't be told from water at all; leaves and reeds float on it and it reflects the moonlight. When a creature steps into shadow mire, a great wave rolls out from it. Each creature within 30 feet that is smaller than the one that stepped in must succeed on a DC 13 STR (Athletics) or DEX (Acrobatics) check or be knocked prone. The wave then rolls back over the creature that stepped in, which must succeed on a DC 13 CON save or be knocked prone and unconscious. Some wizards can distill or conjure pits of shadow mire to reshape a riverbed or undermine a bridge.

### Witchlights and Will-o'-Wisps

Lesser lights wander the Shadow Realm on their own inscrutable business. Witchlights are tiny constructs of crystal, believed to have been made as friends, servants, and companions for the Ancients of shadow. Only powerful wizards know how to make them, but many roam free, guarding dangerous places, helping travelers, seeking old runestones, or gathering at certain seasons to dance and blink in unison. Some follow strangers for hours out of curiosity. Some are friendly, some lead travelers astray, and a few serve as familiars to shadow fey and shadow goblin casters. Their larger cousins, will-o'-wisps, are generally malign and far more dangerous; they lure travelers into doomsand or shadow mire and attack boat lines and rope bridges.

## Shadow Corruption

The Shadow Realm threads its tendrils into anyone it can reach, tainting those who bargain with dark powers, linger too long in places soaked in shadow, or simply eat the wrong food. Visitors are most at risk; natives know the dangers, though they too sometimes fall. Creatures corrupted by shadow grow distracted and withdrawn and shun light in every form, and at the worst they give themselves wholly to the Shadow Realm.

Constructs, fiends, shadow fey, shadow goblins, and Undead aren't susceptible to shadow corruption. Constructs (other than gearforged and similar beings) have no souls; fiends and Undead are already claimed by a darkness of another kind; and the shadow fey and shadow goblins have lived in the realm so long that it has already changed them.

Shadow corruption has six levels. An effect that causes it says how many levels it gives. A creature suffers the effect of its current level and every level below it.

| Level | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | Disadvantage on WIS and CHA checks made against creatures that aren't shadow creatures. |
| 2 | You gain darkvision to 30 feet, or your existing darkvision increases by 30 feet. |
| 3 | Disadvantage on WIS (Perception) checks and attack rolls made in bright light. |
| 4 | Disadvantage on saves made in bright light. |
| 5 | You take 2d6 radiant damage when you start your turn in sunlight. |
| 6 | You become a shadow thrall (see below). |

#### Gaining corruption

For each week spent in the Shadow Realm, a susceptible creature must make a DC 10 CHA save. On a failure, it gains one level of shadow corruption. On a success it resists for now, but the DC of later saves for this reason increases by 1. The DC returns to 10 when the creature gains one or more levels of shadow corruption, or when it spends at least a week outside the Shadow Realm.

#### Shadow magic

Shadow magic is dangerous for mortals to wield. When any humanoid other than an umbral human, bearfolk, darakhul, shadow fey, or shadow goblin casts a shadow magic spell, the next time it finishes a long rest it must make a CHA save with a DC of 10 + the highest circle of shadow magic spell it cast. On a failure, it gains one level of shadow corruption. [[Gnomes]], elfmarked, and elves make this save with advantage.

#### Removing corruption

Shadow corruption can be removed in three ways.

- A corrupted creature that spends 1 week outside the Shadow Realm per current level of corruption reduces its level by one. If it casts or is affected by a shadow magic spell during that time, the recovery starts over.
- A *dispel evil and good* spell cast on the creature reduces its level by one.
- An emerald goblet can remove one level each time it is used.

#### Shadow-touched

A creature that is native to the Shadow Realm, or that has at least one level of shadow corruption, is shadow-touched.

### Despondent

A despondent creature finds it hard to see how anything could turn out well, no matter how hard it tries. While despondent:

- It has disadvantage on ability checks.
- It has advantage on saves against being charmed, and disadvantage on all other saves.

*Calm emotions* suppresses the despondent condition for its duration. *Lesser restoration* or more powerful magic ends it.

### Shadow Thrall

A shadow thrall counts as a denizen of the Shadow Realm and won't willingly leave it.

- If forced to leave the Shadow Realm, it becomes despondent until it returns.
- Its body becomes mottled with shifting patches of inky black. It automatically fails all WIS (Insight) checks and all CHA checks (except Intimidation) against creatures that aren't shadow creatures.
- It is charmed by any fey or shadow fey native to the Shadow Realm with an INT or CHA score higher than 14. This charm can't be removed while it remains a shadow thrall, but *remove curse* or *lesser restoration* suppresses it for 24 hours.

## Traveling the Land

### Reading the Tides

A successful DC 14 WIS (Survival) check by a party member or guide reveals how far and in what direction the land is shifting. On a failure, the party is lost for at least a full day of travel; a new check can be made the next day as new landmarks come into view. The check has advantage if the party is traveling on a shadow road.

### Drift and Tides

When the ebon tides are strong, a guide makes a WIS (Survival) check for wilderness travel and consults the table below. Ignore the table when the plane is quiet.

| d20 | On a Shadow Road | Overland |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Travel time +100% | Travel time +200% |
| 2–5 | Travel time +50% | Travel time +100% |
| 6–9 | Travel time +10% | Travel time +50% |
| 10–14 | Normal travel time | Travel time +10% |
| 15–18 | Travel time −10% | Normal travel time |
| 19 | Travel time −20% | Travel time −10% |
| 20 | Travel time −30% | Travel time −20% |
| 21+ | Travel time −50% | Travel time −30% |

### Signs of a Tide

| d10 | Manifestation |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strange chatter in the wind and voices just out of hearing. |
| 2 | Currents under stone and the sound of water far from any river. |
| 3 | Echoes of the past: ghostly figures, forests, even cities appear and disappear. |
| 4 | Black wind, like a very thick fog, reduces vision to 30 feet. |
| 5 | Rolling earth, like a mild earthquake; the terrain spins or tilts. |
| 6 | Shadow trembling: all light and shadow quiver as if someone were shaking the moon. |
| 7 | A sound through the sky like half a crashing wave and half a massive gong. |
| 8 | Shadows thicken across the region and make the terrain difficult for an hour. |
| 9 | A new radiant well opens nearby, throwing a beam of light into the sky. |
| 10 | A blaze of light passes through the party like a wave, blinding everyone for 1 round. |

### Foundation Stones and Shaped Land

Breaking a small foundation stone takes a *shatter* spell or 20 bludgeoning damage; a medium one, *dimension door* or 50 bludgeoning damage; a large one, *disintegrate* or 150 bludgeoning damage. When a foundation stone breaks, creatures inside the structure feel it shudder for a moment, like a small earthquake.

In the Shadow Realm, *hallucinatory terrain* lasts 3 days instead of 24 hours, and *mirage arcane* lasts a month instead of 10 days. [[Bearfolk]] druids and priests of Hecate can make the results permanent.

## Traveling the Shadow Roads

The network of [[Shadow Roads|shadow roads]] grows, withers, and changes over the years, but traveling them feels much the same whoever made them. Built by magic and guarded by the fey, the bearfolk, and other road wardens, they are swifter and surer than the open land, where every grassland and forest can shift and warp. They are still dangerous.

### Road Wardens

The roads are usually quick and easy, unless the lords of the nearby lands cast charms or hexes to slow visitors or enemies. When bandits, enchantments, or natural or supernatural obstacles block wagons, pilgrims, merchants, or noble processions, road wardens are sent from the nearest court or city to clear the way. The great chapterhouses of the road wardens stand in the [[Court of Mice and Ravens]] and the Court of Roses, and in [[Corremel]], [[Dalliance]], Fandeval, and [[Merrymead]]. The shades and gnomes keep no road wardens near [[Soriglass]] or along the far reaches of the western woods, but the darakhul legions do the same work in the rifts in the emperor's name.

Road wardens are often veterans of court life who took the post once they tired of the daily rituals, gatherings, and petty slights of fey status-seeking. They are deadly warriors, enchanters, and rogues, feared by bandits, satarre, vampires, and void cultists for their skill at sniffing out road-cutters and other plotters against the [[The Old Roads|Old Roads]]. They are also famous tale-spinners, and delightful company for their deep knowledge of the roads, the tides, and arcane matters. A few of the best known:

| Road Warden | Description |
|---|---|
| Ambrosus Fairhand | A sable elf general who tired of the [[Court of Golden Oaks]] and now leads sable elves and erina along the northern banks of the Styx. He cuts off the hands of bandits and road-cutters, always pronouncing, "Sentence performed in the name of Queen Valda." |
| Hillar Vandegast | A shadow fey archmage who rides a nightmare and keeps a small company of young squires and apprentices. He guards the eastern Queen's Wood and turns criminals into toads, sparrows, and sometimes weasels. |
| Gillyan the Sailor | A shadow goblin once of Fandeval who saw something in the Sable Sea and swore never to sail again. He favors radiant magic, and is said to have once won and lost a crown of infinite midnight in a card game. |
| Kei Greenleaf | A gnomish druid who serves the bearfolk and guards the trails of the north, said to work with erina and alseid, though they are rarely seen when Kei questions a traveler. |
| Toress Bonespitter | A darakhul captain posted outside the great ghoul rift, who enjoys human and fey company but despises bearfolk. Her darakhul and human rangers guard the new ghoulish roads around the rim of the rift. |
| Garwen the Pure | A bearfolk paladin and relentless defender of light and justice who lets nothing slide. Smugglers, necromancers, thieves, and even simple gamblers may be made to pay a toll, or carried bodily to the court of justice at Merrymead. |

### Highway Robbery

With so many visitors, highway robbery is almost irresistible. A group of huge bearfolk, a company of darakhul soldiers, or a heavily armed shadow fey stepping out of the shadows will often demand a toll, or that travelers "deliver up a parcel to the road gods." Most visitors pay, but sometimes the robbers get more than they bargained for, and then they scatter into the rough ground beside the road. Most visitors are reluctant to leave the road, and bandits count on it.

### Travel Times

Travel along a shadow road is fast but not instant, and the well-kept, well-warded roads of the shadow fey, bearfolk, and elves are faster than most.

| Journey | Time |
|---|---|
| A major mortal city with fey or gnomish influence to a major elven or fey court | 1d3 days |
| From regions without elves, fey, or gnomes | 1d6 days |
| Through regions with little or no magic, such as wastelands and mage-blasted deserts | 1d12 days |
| Across seas, oceans, or continents | Up to 2d12 days |

### Road Lore

For creatures of the Shadow Realm, the roads are commonplace. A creature from elsewhere can make an INT (Arcana) check, with a −5 penalty for non-elves, to see what it knows about them.

| Check | What the Character Knows |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | No one knows how the shadow roads work. |
| 6–10 | Shadow roads join two points by magical travel. A spell or passphrase is often needed to open one, and elves, shadow fey, and similar peoples seem to have an easier time on them than anyone else. |
| 11–15 | The start and end of a shadow road are set in advance, and the doors to them are never open long. |
| 16–20 | A road's destination can be changed, but only by the fey. Otherwise, new destinations take obscure incantations or arcane tinkering. |
| 21+ | Some shadow roads are guarded, and others lead in only one direction. |

### On the Road

To outsiders, the shadow roads can be an overgrown chaos of hedge mazes, twisting passages, hollows, and delves, and none of them are known for wide views or straight paths. Travelers can leave the path and wander, but once they try to go home it takes them 1d3 days to find their way back, and many never return, because leaving the road gives up the protection of the fey who built it and of the warding stones and waymarks along it. Shadow creatures, fiends, and celestials all use the roads for their own ends and dislike competition, and more powerful travelers draw more powerful attention. Fey and Undead are common, along with night beasts, marauding giants, and other shadow creatures. Some roads are watched by the shadow fey from black stone keeps, whose servants prey on the unwary, or at least collect tolls in memory philters, scrolls, or silver.

## Navigating the Black River

The Lord of the Rivers is [[Charun]] the Boatman, and his followers travel the [[The Ever River|Styx]] most often, and most safely and swiftly, just as the shadow fey pass most quickly through their forests and the darakhul through their dark canyons. The rivers loop and wander across the planes in ways that are hard to predict; sometimes going downriver leads somewhere that is surely upriver by road, and sometimes one river melts into another, especially aboard a shadow goblin barge or with a conjured ferryman. River giants, shadow goblins, and shadow river lords are common along the banks.

For ordinary travelers without a magical ferry, going downstream is easy enough at about 5 miles per hour with the current, but the way is often maddeningly indirect, with strange tributaries, other boats glimpsed in the endless mist, and rapids, whirlpools, and backwater oxbows that lead nowhere. Encounters on the river are common, usually with shadow goblins, river giants, or water fey, more rarely with fiends or incorporeal Undead.

For each day of navigation, a character makes a DC 15 INT (Arcana) check. On a success, the party travels as expected; on a 20, they arrive after a single day on the water. On a failure, they loop or dawdle and make only 10 miles of progress that day. On a roll of 1 or 2, roll on the table below.

| d12 | Result |
|---|---|
| 1 | The boat lands exactly where it set out that morning. |
| 2 | Ghosts plague the boat all day, shouting, raising noxious mists, and obscuring sight. |
| 3 | Some coins or minor possessions (rope, an amulet, favorite socks) go missing. |
| 4 | A member of the party gains a level of shadow corruption. |
| 5 | A familiar or companion animal falls ill and must be carried off the boat. |
| 6 | A member of the party becomes partly transparent, bones showing. |
| 7 | The boat slides into another plane of the GM's choice, such as one of the hells, the celestial planes, or a mortal land. |
| 8 | Everyone aboard is drained by the journey and gains a level of exhaustion. |
| 9 | The river's life-draining power deals 2d12 necrotic damage to each passenger each hour. |
| 10 | The boat enters dark water, and a water elemental attacks. |
| 11 | The boat travels without effort, guided by unseen forces, and all its passengers gain the benefits of a long rest. |
| 12 | One passenger has a vision of the afterlife and may have a premonition of danger, learn a secret from a ghost, or gain 2d12 temporary hit points from understanding the river's power. |
