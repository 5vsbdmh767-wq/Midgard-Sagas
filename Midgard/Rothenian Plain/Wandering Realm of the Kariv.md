The Wandering Realm is wherever the [[Kariv]] wagons roll, across the [[Rothenian Plain]] and beyond, where their sheep and horses graze and every clan matriarch is a queen. Each family lives in its tabor, a wagon that is both home and household, and every tabor must move on at least once a season, because all the Kariv carry the Wandering Curse.

At the great gatherings of the wagons each year, a few leaders stand out and are hailed as the king and queens of all the Kariv. The titles pass as leaders age, die on the road, or step aside, and a bolder or slyer rival can sometimes take one, though beating an elder is never certain. The great families are the Dakat, Galati, Heph, Leanti, Lovari, Kalder, Merceri, and Sergin. The Kalder are said to be sinking ever deeper into darkness.

Things are tense between the Lovari and the Galati. Mama Rye of the Galati, who is also First Diviner of [[Zobeck]], believes a young albino seer named Sanash the White Eye, born to a noble family in Zobeck, is destined to unite all the clans. Sanash now rides the Plain with Zobeck mercenaries and Galati warriors, trying to win the other families over.

Kariv symbols vary by family, often birds, wheels, and barn animals. They trade in horses, mules, fortunetelling, ironwork, tinsmithing, and wool. Don't ask about Kariv princes: every clan leader is the prince, duchess, boyar, or grandee of something. The Kariv love tales of King Jarek, the greybeard who outwits his loud, strutting rival Prince Isak, a fool who is loved anyway.

## The King and the Queens
Iqbal Lovari, the Tall King, is nearly 7 feet tall and reed-thin, close to 70 and still said to have "the strength of three bears," with Northern giant and Southern fey ancestry rumored in his blood. A master smith and warrior, cold in dealings and fiery in battle, he has one green eye and one blue, the mark of the Second Sight. He is a traditionalist ("The Kariv know no home... and acknowledge no masters but themselves") and a bitter enemy of Mama Rye. He is missing, last seen east and north of Olersheim hunting, drinking, and dealing with fur traders. His firebrand grandson Jacob Lovari leads the search from a camp at the feet of the northern mountains. Some Lovari accuse Mama Rye of foreseeing that his path would cross a great white wyrm's lair and nudging the silver traders to send him there. Tensions between the Lovari and Galati run high.

The twin queens Sanchari and Innessa, in their mid-40s, have led together for 13 years. Sober Innessa is Queen of the Road, deciding where the families travel; flamboyant Sanchari is Queen of the Tents whenever they make camp. That the sisters share one body bothers no Kariv: they know two minds and two souls live in it, and that outsiders would call it madness. They help Jacob's search with magic and coin, think Sanash is under the influence of the Cloven Nine, and will tolerate her only as long as she doesn't challenge them.

## Sanash the White Eye
A young albino woman with alarming gifts of divination, born into a noble family in [[Zobeck]], who left home to travel the Wandering Realm. Mama Rye is convinced she will unite the clans and redeem even the Kalder, and the Galati quietly support her claim to succeed King Iqbal. She roams the Plain with Zobeck mercenaries and Galati warriors, eager to impress the other clans. She claims to have seen, in a waking dream, a way to cure the Arrid: if one walks a thousand steps in the shoes of a young Kariv in love, their dark thoughts will lift and they can wander again. Most Kariv doubt it.

## The Wander Curse
By firelight the Kariv are dancers, clever horse traders, and bold lovers. When the embers die they drink, gamble, take chances, and cheat the *Gadscho*, their word for anyone not Kariv. And before the season turns, however sweet the pasture, they move on. Kariv who don't move at least a few days' ride every few months grow infertile, and their line ends. Some say the curse came from the fey and the Green Gods after a Kariv king broke an oath to the gods; others blame a bad bargain with Baba Yaga. No one knows if it can be lifted, or whether the Kariv would stop wandering if it were.

Those who stop are the Arrid, and the Kariv mourn them as dead. First their spirit wanes, then the color drains from their clothes and goods, and finally they become barren. In far corners of the Wandering Realm, little communities of Arrid live in sad huts, and many fall into dark thoughts and the service of fiends who promise to restore what they lost.

## Kariv Taboos

| Taboo | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Black Dogs | Never meet a black dog's eye, for it means death. To wish ill on someone, drape a black dog's pelt near them as they sleep or nail one to their wagon. The wicked are dragged to hell by Old Gnash, a huge black dog who eats souls. |
| Bloodsworn Oaths | Words mean nothing, but an oath sealed with Kariv blood must never be broken. The oathbreaker is cast out forever, and the one who kept the oath must kill them. |
| Clan Horses | Horses are family. Letting one be hurt or killed without trying to save it, or saving yourself at its expense, brings the same death or injury on your family, as the clan decides. |
| Dead Bodies | Corpses are unclean and must never touch bare skin. |
| Rover's Burden | Anyone found on the road in need must be healed, fed, and sheltered, even an enemy, who is tended until well and then challenged honorably. |

## Places of the Wandering Realm

| Place | Description |
|---|---|
| The Cloud-Soaked Cliff | A cliff above a glacial trail between the Wandering Realm and the [[Jotunheim]] Mountains, often wrapped in cloud, where the Lovari keep their sacred forge. Each year they haul ore, tools, coal, and water up, build a temple to Svarog of logs and bright red silk in two days, and hold the wild festival of Svarnesh, then forge for a week. Svarog himself is said to bless some of the work. The finest piece is thrown off the cliff into the ice as an offering. |
| The Wandering Bazaar | A market of wagon stalls that circles Ingot Lake, fed by a tributary of the Tanais, moving every few days, with a big fishing contest each spring. Ama Tidefan, a shrewd, good-natured Kariv woman of 50, oversees it, keeps out stolen or evil goods, and steers deals toward the merchant collective, which pays guards and builds wagons. Haggling is part of the culture. Anyone may open a stall for 500 gold, as long as it brings no trouble. Patient browsers sometimes find magic sold by the ignorant or desperate, but showing too much interest can make the seller change their mind. |
