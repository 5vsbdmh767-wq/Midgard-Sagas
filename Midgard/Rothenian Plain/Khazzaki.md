The Khazzaki are the human nomads of the [[Rothenian Plain]], descended from Northern raiders, old steppe peoples, and [[Kariv]] wanderers. They herd horses and oxen, farm a little, and raid a lot, and they are proud of being free riders rather than serfs. Anyone who swears to the khan is welcome in their tents, and a guest is honored for a night even in the dead of winter. Their ponies are small, fast, and nearly tireless, and are never shod unless they have to leave the Plain. The Khazzaki fight just as well in snow as in summer, so their neighbors never relax at the first snowfall.

A typical camp holds a few hundred people and a family of allied centaurs, the kin ludi or horse friends, since the Khazzaki believe [[Volund|Svarog]] turns his most deserving riders into centaurs. Camps wander constantly, and a ride of a hundred miles is nothing to them. At the end of every summer, each camp sends envoys to swear fealty to the khan at Misto Kolis, a capital of pavilions and wagons that moves with its people and is famous for its wrestlers and jugglers.

Bodhan Zenody has ruled for almost twenty years. He is an archer, a scholar, and a patient bargainer, and word is that he's ready to gamble on one great raid, whether on the [[The Mharoti Empire|Mharoti]], the far east, [[Niemheim]], or the [[Magdar Kingdom]]. If it fails, one of his three sons will take his place. The Khazzaki have tried and failed many times to take the Mharoti city of Kaa'nesh in the south, but when the dragons sent an army onto the grass, the riders chased it all the way home.

##### Red Mounds of Rhos Khurgan
In the north of the Khanate, near the Tanais River, stand 31 red stone burial mounds of a forgotten race. The Khazzaki visit them only after a victory, to drink, sing, and pour wine onto the mounds for their ancestors. Refusing to give the mounds their share is one of the worst insults there is, and people have been buried alive for it. A 32nd mound stands alone by a lake a dozen miles east, and the Khazzaki stay away from it because it's cursed. Priests who rest near the mounds find their prayers answered strangely.

The Khanate's symbol is a black circle on a gold field. Its trade is horses, oxen, leather, saddles, plunder, and tents. The Khazzaki roam from the Plains of Rhos Khurgan, east of the Nieder Straits, south to the [[Despotate of the Ruby Sea|Ruby Sea]] and the foothills of the Dragoncoil Mountains, a land of grass, rolling hills, gentle rivers, and a few small taiga forests, where wild horse clans pour blood and wine over ancient barrows for Svarog the Rider.

## Misto Kolis, the City of Wheels
The capital rolls across the Plain on creaking carts: great pavilions called *orgoo* and collapsible wooden buildings that pack onto wagons when the city moves. It often looks like one giant drunken party of wrestlers, jugglers, and jesters, though a great deal of trade and diplomacy goes on quietly amid the games. The khan's Woolen Palace and the Kariv Leanti family under Clan Mother Lumenita Leanti hold great influence, especially while the khan is raiding. More [[Kariv]] gather here than anywhere else. Its population is about 8,200 (7,500 humans, 600 centaurs, 100 huginn), though it swings wildly.

## Troubles of the Khanate
The black strangles, the withering sickness striking the horses of [[Trombei]] and the centaurs, has reached the Khanate's herds. So far the khan has kept it in check by killing and burning sick animals and burying the ashes deep, but it spreads fast.

The khan has recently allied with the Grassweavers of Perun, a strange order of druids: overly tall, spindly humans in armor of woven grass and masks of sticks and grass, always in groups of exactly seven sharing one low grass hut, who speak Common to outsiders but rustle and click among themselves. Wherever they walk, the natural order slides sideways. They heal the khan's wounded and worship the electric storms that tear across the Plain, and he hopes they can cure the black strangles. They hate the kin ludi centaurs, and the khan is looking for a way to end that hatred. No one knows where they came from or what they want.
