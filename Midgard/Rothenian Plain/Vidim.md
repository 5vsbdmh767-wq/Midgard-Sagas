Vidim, the Kingdom of Ravens, is small, poor, proud, and important. It sits where the [[Rothenian Plain]] meets the Nieder Strait, so the wealth of the steppe passes through it on the way west, and it stands as a wall against the reaver dwarves and the giants coming down from the Riphean Mountains. Its farmers are serfs bound to the manors of the boyars, the nobles who rule in the tsar's name.

The tsar is famous for his piety and his love of anything new, and just as famous for his fits of cruelty. In a single year he has thrown himself into court dancing, memorizing poetry, religious zeal, and woodworking. His daughters and a tight circle of advisers keep the kingdom running.

The tsar rules through two groups who don't like each other: the boyars, and the huginn, the [[Ravenfolk|ravenfolk]] who are Vidim's best soldiers, traders, and spies, and who keep the Raven's Road safe. In the capital, nearly a hundred huginn live in the Perch, a ramshackle tower of poles and flapping canvas beside the temple of Wotan, and boyars and ravenfolk fight duels beneath it. Off the northern coast, five fingers of pure salt rise from the sea and change their gesture every winter solstice, and a pod of cruel merfolk now sinks any ship that comes near them.

The tsar's full titles are Protector of the Faithful, Keeper of the Runes, Master of the Nieder Straits, Guardian of the Shrines, and Father to the People. He is tall, with a thick black beard and piercing pale blue eyes, and his face swings from stern to jovial with startling speed; his silver rings click as he strokes his beard. He wears rich velvet robes and the traditional pointed cap. Vidim trades in cattle, lumber, peat, flax, linen, leather, wheat, barley, and apples, and trades generously with the dwarves.

## Boyars and Huginn
The boyars are the tsar's nobles, knights, and judges, famous for fur hats, enormous beards, and brightly embroidered robes, since a certain noble plumage is expected. They fight gnomes, reaver dwarves, centaur bands, Northern giants, and every other threat. The huginn worship [[Wotan]] and his messengers, are close to the priesthood of the Rune God, and are cunning thieves and scoundrels. Their chieftain, the grey-feathered warrior Ukwak, rides with the boyars, and their most notorious spy, Viryeshka Krasny, has taken a human name and can pass as human, so good is her mimicry. The two groups distrust each other, and the tsar can't rein in either.

### The Perch
The Perch's poles and planks are carved with runes and eyes, the symbols of Wotan; some are wards and others magical sentries that cry out in the huginn's speech at trespassers. Its flapping canvas walls are always in disrepair. Non-huginn never go inside. Because it stands beside the temple and the Scarlet Palace, it has never been attacked outright.

Some huginn of the Perch have begun to molt: their feathers thin at the shoulders and waist and grow back gilded or silvered, and the molted dream of the Storm Court. One claimed to have dreamed the first steps of the road to Valhalla, walked north, and was never seen again. Some call it Wotan's reward, others [[Loki]]'s trickery. The Doomcroakers shun the molted ones, who show up as dark spots in their divinations.

## Vidim, City of Crows
A small city in the eastern style, with earthen ramparts and a moat instead of stone walls, though the tsar is replacing its wooden hoardings with stone as time and money allow. Inside, slate and thatch roofs crowd over leatherworkers, dyers, spinners, and weavers. The main square holds the Scarlet Palace of carved wood and gilded eaves, the Reaver's Hall (once the dwarves', now used by boyars and merchants), and the Giant's Skull cauldron, the skull of a Thursir chieftain slain by the current tsar's father. Visitors think it lucky; the tsar secretly wishes he could do half as well.

## The Salt Fingers
A few miles past Bjornshafen, five pillars of pure salt rise from the Straits. Each winter solstice eve they bend and reform into a new, startlingly lifelike gesture, never repeating; their link to the stars is confirmed but undeciphered, and some say they point to where the stars will stand on the day the world ends. Above water they are smooth and elegant; below, twisted and cracked and crusted with black mussels and stinging anemones, and no one who dived to see their roots has come back. For years scholars, mystics, and priests made winter pilgrimages to a tent city on the beach, until the deep-stitchers came: a cruel merfolk pod in masks of rotting octopus who sink any ship within miles and mark their waters with kelp woven into the bodies of their victims.
