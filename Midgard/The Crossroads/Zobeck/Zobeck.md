The Zobeck Revolt is 92 years past. Only a few dwarves and [[gearforged]] remember it firsthand. The brash, rich city found its footing as an independent mercantile state. The sons and daughters of the revolt’s leaders became the city’s consuls and lord mayor and formed the Free City Council, a ruling body elected by the human, dwarven, and kobold citizens of Zobeck. The kobold slaves of ages past were freed from their shackles, and after centuries of prejudice, kobolds have finally begun to rise to positions of power...progress remains slow however.

 The citizens of Zobeck have largely cast aside the gods and traditions of the [[House Stross|Stross Dynasty]] and now honor [[Rava]] the Gear Goddess as well as some older deities. All faiths are welcome in Zobeck, though most of its people have no love for [[Sarastra]], the Goddess of Night and Magic. Only a handful of Sarastrans live in the city, most of them [[shadow fey]], whom the other citizens of Zobeck have yet to accept. Even the appearance of Winter’s Kiss—the shadow fey embassy—within the city has done little to ease the tension between Zobeck’s older citizens and their new neighbors.

### Life in Zobeck
Despite commerce’s importance, not everyone in Zobeck is a merchant or business owner. The average citizen makes their wage through common labor as a farmer, apprentice, launderer, miner, sailor, servant, stevedore, watchman, wait staff, or any of a myriad of other professions. Some individuals, of course, prefer illegal endeavors, such as smuggling, thieving, and narcotics dealing. For the most part, however, Zobeck is a city of hardworking people who highly value their freedom. 

### People of Zobeck
As a thriving trade center, Zobeck draws people from across Midgard. The Free City calls to enterprising individuals desiring to make their own way in the world. It offers even the lowliest peasants the chance to forge new beginnings. Opportunities in nearly every arena abound for those who know how to grab them, and many come to the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]] in hopes of bettering their lot. In Rava’s ticking city, people can join the hum of the seamless trade machinery and forge their own destiny. 

Zobeck’s population has grown slowly but steadily over the past 10 years Though the change seems insignificant, Zobeck is beginning to feel growing pains. Housing is growing scarce, and the city’s poorest residents are being forced underground by the cost of rent—into the dark and cavernous Old City deep beneath the stones of Zobeck. Still, the city welcomes all visitors. In a city of commerce, newcomers are all viewed as walking money, and the citizens of the Free City are eager to relieve them of their coin—whether legally or not.

### Culture
Zobeckers value commerce above everything except freedom. They fiercely celebrate their hard-won freedom during the Stross’s Fall celebration, and none of them forget that freedom can be tenuous—especially with neighbors like [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau and Doresh]]. Still, common Zobeckers give thanks daily for the ability to run their own lives and make their own way in the world, and they teach their children the importance of this gift.

Zobeckers have a tremendous love for their patron deity Rava and her inspired gearforged creations. They also hold the Free City’s other four main gods in high regard. Most of them stand ever vigilant against cultists, especially those of [[Marena]], who seek to undermine the accepted faiths in Zobeck.

### Education, Technology, and Magic
The Free City houses the Arcane Collegium, an institute of learning, specializing in the study of magic, though its staff includes experts in language, alchemy, geargrinding, and clockwork craftsmanship. Currently, about 400 students matriculate in the various schools of magic, including clockwork, necromancy, illusion, divination, and the unusual study of star and shadow magic called the illumination school—a field closely related to illusion with a touch of creation, conjuration, and necromancy mixed in. The practice of star and shadow magic, like clockwork magic, is native to Zobeck. Outside the Free City, only a few elves may actually know its associated spells. Zobeck is known far and wide for its clockwork technology. Common clockwork devices include the clockwork scullion, clockwork watchman, and clockwork weaving spider, in addition to autoscribes, bolt-throwers, climbers, fireboxes, market scales, trip-hammers, walking towers, and more. 

### Government
Zobeck’s government is a many-layered nightmare to visitors seeking to bend it to their own purposes, though familiar enough to locals who know how to get things done. For the most part, it serves the master merchants with security and safety for trade, the common people with justice and fair dealings in the markets, and the poor with work as conscripts or ditchdiggers. 

### The Free City Council
The Free City Council replaced the Stross-era Praetorian Council after the revolt. Once composed of hand-picked noble allies of House Stross, the Praetorian Council generally deferred to their lord’s wishes in running the city and spent most of their time scheming and plotting against one another. They pilfered money from the city to fund their own projects and interests, ruinously taxed the city’s bourgeoisie, restricted trade with tariffs on goods entering the city and exit fees on goods leaving the city, and used the watch as their personal enforcers to settle vendettas, illegally detain citizens, and seize private property. This flagrant corruption laid the groundwork for the violence that destroyed the old aristocracy. 

After the Great Revolt, the rebels imprisoned or executed any Praetorian Council members they caught. The revolt’s leaders created the Free City Council to administer the city as the Praetorian Council should have done. Its standing consuls were citizens who helped lead the revolt and who held strong interests in the city: mostly guildmasters, priests, and even kobolds. The council is charged with ensuring the welfare of the city and its citizens, protecting Zobeck from all threats to its freedom, and maintaining the flow of commerce within the Free City. 

### Lord Mayor
The sitting consuls choose the lord mayor from among their peers to serve a 10-year term, though most have held the position for life. The lord mayor oversees the administration of justice by appointing all of Zobeck’s judges, establishing and provisioning the army, appointing all knight-commanders of the Citadel, and commanding the Free City’s militant orders—except the paladins of the Order of the Undying Sun. This chapter of the Undying Sun predates the city’s independence and only serves on the condition that their commander answer to no one “not of noble blood.” In practice, the order acts as an independent military force.

#### Current Lord Mayor Olleck
The newly chosen [[Mayor Constantia Olleck]] represents the growing dwarven influence in the city. She has sworn to serve a single 10-year term. She took over after the old mayor, Karillian Gluck, was asked to step aside by the shadow fey (and who did, fearing for his life). Constantia showed great courage in commissioning several efforts to drive the fey out or at least limit their influence to the Undercity and a few of the outlying districts. She speaks with the shadow fey ambassador, but the two will never be friends.

### Consuls
Generally descendants of the Great Revolt’s leaders, the 12 consuls serve for life or until they receive a “silent office” (a retirement sinecure). Sitting consuls fill any vacancies on the council from among the city’s most prominent civic leaders, typically guildmasters, merchants, or powerful members of the priesthood—but once in a while, the consuls choose an adventurous individual seeking a quieter life.

By tradition, the Free City Council always includes the guildmaster of the Arcane Collegium and the Kobold King of Kings. During the revolt, the leaders gave the city’s watch commander a lifetime council seat but secretly decided that, unlike the deal they struck with the kobolds and the Arcane Collegium, this “seat for life” would only extend to that individual. Upon his death, the council did not give the position to his successor but added a second seat for a cleric of Rava. To this day, this “betrayal” remains a point of contention between the council and the watch. The watch’s current captain, Horvart Edelstein, is bent on regaining his “rightful seat” on the council.

#### Current Consuls

**Halsen Hrovitz**. 
Halsen is the fourth of his name, and the Hrovitz family founder was once known as the “merchant to the noble House Stross” (an honor they’ve not mentioned in generations). Hrovitz deals in finished dwarven weapons and armor as well as unrefined copper, flax, sheep, and mining tools, and he also trades heavily with the kobolds of Lillefor (unusual for a human merchant) for mithral, iron, and precious gems. 

**Kekolina of the Derry Mine.** 
Kekolina is a long-serving kobold. Rather an oddity, she represents the mine gangs that provide silver and wealth to the city. She is honored among the mine gangs as having the ear of St. Piran, the local patron saint of miners. She keeps kobold interests always in view. 

**Lady Wintesla Marack.** 
Lady Marack is beloved as a priestess of Lada for her healing of the poor and the sick. She is also a well-connected merchant, selling timber, wool, and tin from Zobeck to the dwarves, the [[Magdar Kingdom|Magdar]], and especially the [[Perunalia|Perunalians]]. The amazons of the duchy find it more congenial to do business with a woman, so her oxcarts and barges carry much of the Zobeck trade to and from Perunalia. 

**Lector and Consul Radovar Streck.** 
Radovar, the city’s most famous alchemist, has been promoted to lector of the [[Arcane Collegium]], a title usually reserved for those times when the guildmaster is otherwise engaged. And indeed, he has promulgated a number of edicts in [[Orlando]]’s name when the titular guildmaster has been out of the city. In addition, he seems to be investigating the alchemical properties of shadow with help from a handsome young shadow fey apprentice named Frost, and he enjoys occasional visits from a dust goblin bringing needful items from [[Magocracy of Allain|Maillon]] and the [[Western Wastes|Goblin Wastes]]. 

**Melancha Vendemic.** 
Melancha is the golden-voiced consul, capable of moving rhetoric in defense of causes of law and security. Her arguments are often carried out through mocking songs in the taverns rather than confined to discussion with other consuls. She has a great ear for what discomfits or worries Zobeckers. 

**Myzi** I. Myzi, called the Mouse King and Lord of the Undercity, is a consul and (most believe) a corrupt rogue. He has a drooping moustache and a twitchy nose, and he seems to always have the news from the docks, the smugglers, and the riverfolk. 

**Ondli Firedrake.** A dwarven priest of Rava (and Volund), Ondli has served as first consul, or council house chairman, for 30 years. His consul peers selected him to guide the meetings, recognizing him as the most patient and fair-minded among them. 

**Orlando.** Guildmaster of the [[Arcane Collegium]] and consul and member of the Free City Council, Orlando has withdrawn from many of the Collegium’s affairs and is spending many days of summer and fall with Aldona Silberhof, a whip-smart sorceress who serves as a captain in the Runkelstad Wands. While his enemies gossip about his lack of attention in Zobeck’s affairs, his friends seem pleased that Orlando has found an equal in arcane matters. 

**Quetelmak**. Kobold king of kings and consul to Zobeck, Quetelmak seems like a kobold king who might stick around for more than a season. He has weathered two years since his ascension to the position and consulship. This seat’s consul fluctuates with the rapid rise and fall of the kings of kings in the Kobold Ghetto. 

**Selena Harbeck**. Guildmistress Harbeck keeps the Weaver’s Guild disciplined and extremely productive, building clockwork weaving spiders to create cloth and tapestries at a rate no other town can match. Selena opens the guildhall each day with a prayer to Rava, and she is on excellent terms with Consul Hrovitz and Lady Marack, her principal suppliers of raw wool and flax. Guild tapestries are especially popular in Bemmea and the [[Seven Cities]], and Consul Harbeck often visits both sites on extended business tours and as an unofficial envoy for Zobeck’s interests in the south and west. 

**Sir Jorun Haclav.** Field marshal of the Free Army, captain of the Zobeck Hussars, consul, and master of the citadel, Sir Jorun continues to expand the hussars and has sent a company of 200 human light infantry to stand with the [[Magdar]] on the border of the [[The Mharoti Empire|Dragon Empire]].

### The Citadel and the Blue House
In time of war, Field Marshal Sir Jorun Haclav commands most of the city from the Citadel and can give orders even to the Council and most of the guilds. In peacetime the Citadel prepares for the next assault on the city's freedoms and works to push Zobeck's influence into the wild country to the north. The mayor, the Council, and the field marshal all agree that this is a time of urgent preparation. They disagree on whether [[King Lucan]] of the [[Blood Kingdom|Greater Duchy of Morgau]] or the dragon armies of [[The Mharoti Empire|the Mharoti]] are the greater threat. Both have recently seized new territory, and Sir Jorun keeps telling the trade-loving citizens that the city is not ready for a long war against larger, battle-hardened armies.

Outside wartime, the Citadel answers to the scarred and outrageously glamorous Lady Fenyll Marack, Praetor of the Blue House. The Blue House is Zobeck's secret police, along with any malcontents who can be talked into serving the city's greater good. Lady Fenyll often visits talented troublemakers under arrest and offers a shorter sentence in exchange for "a little work outside town." The work is always dangerous: sabotage against the Blood Kingdom, spying on a cantonal merchant suspected of harboring [[Mammon]] cultists, or sneaking aboard a flying city of Sikkim. She comes from a long line of merchants, is enormously wealthy, and drives a hard bargain with everyone. Where her diplomacy, sabotage, and misdirection fail, Sir Jorun's generalship takes over.

### Zobeck's Districts
Zobeck's quarters are each distinct, from the flood-prone slums along the river to the red-tiled roofs of the Citadel, where the wealthiest burghers live.

#### Citadel District
The Citadel rises over the north of the city and guards the river approach from upstream. It is home to the Order of Griffon Riders, a company of scouts, arcanists, and daredevils who fly patrols against centaurs and bandits across [[The Margreve|the Margreve]] and act as the city's eyes in wartime. Their speckled griffons rarely number more than five or six, each tended by its own staff of grooms and trainers.

#### Collegium District
The district is named for the [[Arcane Collegium]], and its other great landmark is [[Lada]]'s Temple of the Celestial Dawn. Scholars, scribes, mages, students, and alchemists gather at the Hedgehog tavern or browse the shelves at the Book Fetish.

The Collegium rarely opens its doors to outsiders. The usual ways in are the Steam Gate, which opens onto Arcane Square across from the Hedgehog, and the Water Gate, a slippery, guarded stair down the embankment to a single pier. Those admitted are mostly hired help, agents of the Collegium, or people offering something valuable in exchange for the Collegium's wisdom. Inside are two small courtyards and a dozen two-story buildings of gray and yellow stucco with red tile roofs, a black tower, the gray hall of the summoners, underground laboratories, and alchemical bunkers near the river. Tarnished silver runes run along every eave, gate, and window, and the grounds are guarded by clockwork traps, enchanted gargoyles, and undead under the masters' control.

About 40 apprentices study there: 19 humans, 14 [[Kobolds|kobolds]], five golden-bearded dwarf siblings from Mischau, and a [[Trollkin|trollkin]] named Herring. A staff of about 40 includes alchemists, scribes, tutors, clockworkers, a priest of [[Rava]], clockwork scullions, and a falconer from [[Free Cities of the Desert|Siwal]] named Kaashif al-Rashid. The Collegium teaches two rare schools of magic: clockwork magic and illumination magic (stars and shadow). It leads the mortal world in shadow magic, though the [[Shadow Fey|shadow fey]] are better at it and rarely share.

#### Dock District
Also called the Gullet, the docks along the River Argent are the heart of the city's trade, slightly busier even than the [[Great Northern Road]]. Merchants, barge polers, and stevedores work the wharves at all hours, and taverns, gambling dens, and bordellos stand beside warehouses and dry docks. Brawls are common, and the City Watch patrols heavily to keep commerce moving.

##### Blue Barbers of Wharf Street
The Blue Barbers are a dozen blue-haired gnomes who claim to come from the [[Court of Midnight Teeth]] in the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]], not from [[Niemheim]]. They met suspicion at first, but their razors, moustache wax, and hair tonics have won them loyal customers among the Griffon Knights, the hussars, and many dwarves. Some mutter that they are spies and assassins for the shadow fey.

##### Bargewright Maesker
Maesker is a young, cheerful, hardworking man who builds and repairs barges quickly and cheaply, with a crew of halflings, humans, and [[Ravenfolk|ravenfolk]]. Captains chat with him constantly, so he is remarkably well informed. He won't hire kobolds; the scars on his arms come from knife fights against them at the Fierce Lynx.

#### Gear District
On the western side near the Dwarven Gate, the Gear District belongs to tin and brass merchants, gear grinders, and [[Gearforged|gearforged]] repair shops. The city's gearforged gather here, and the best dwarven clockwork mages and engineers build their wonders. The district centers on the Steamworker's Guildhall and the Geargrinder's Emporium, both built at huge expense with cast-iron ribs. Each month, mages, geargrinders, kobold clockworkers, and dwarven engineers bring a new clockwork guard to life (some believe the city is slowly building a gearforged army), and most months a privately funded gearforged as well. Private gearforged must pay off the cost of their making, usually by serving a wealthy family, temple, or guild. Dwarven mule trains bring iron, rare alloys, jewels for precision gearing, and offerings for the shrine of [[Volund]], where an ever-burning altar and anvil stand before his statue.

#### Kobold Ghetto
The Kobold Ghetto is a warren of streets no more than six feet wide where the Argent meets the Derry, with roofs meeting overhead to keep out the sun. It has two official entrances, the Ghetto Gate and the Water Gate, both watched from both sides. Several kobold kings rule its tribes, and one King of Kings (or Queen of Queens) holds the rest in check until they unite against the throne. Queen Clarhida ousted Kuromak VII five years ago; King Quetelmak ousted Clarhida two years later.

Ninety-odd years ago the kobolds were slaves of [[House Stross]], and the Ghetto was their pen. They mined silver, built clockworks, and ran the deadly steam boilers, and some believe they invented the everwound spring, the aeolipile generator, and the reciprocating balance wheel that made Zobeck famous. Today they are free and legally equal, but still a people apart, mostly because they work through the night and sleep by day. Their pastimes include rat fights, owl races, pigeon hunts, and amateur alchemy.

Under Quetelmak, the kobolds have built chain bridges of linked iron and barrel floats to other districts, and at night even across the Argent to the Margreve side. Smugglers use them to dodge the gate taxes. Whenever the Watch tries to seize one, the chains are cut loose and recovered later. Mayor Olleck hates them, but she is now negotiating with Quetelmak over a "Bridge Tax," and the king likes the idea of a kobold-controlled share of the treasury.

Beneath the Ghetto run miles of warrens and smugglers' tunnels, and the Pit of the Fierce Lynx, an illegal fighting pit run by the friendly, scheming promoter Yshka Bishka. Weekly fights pit roosters, hounds, and humans, with the occasional knife fight or kobold honor duel. Winners are treated like royalty for a week. The mayor has offered 500 gp for anyone who brings Yshka Bishka in "for questioning or burial."

### Places of Interest
#### Old Stross Public Bathhouse
Just south of Crown Square, facing the Founders' Statues, these spring-fed baths once served only the aristocracy and were opened to everyone after the Revolt. They are a monument to the Revolt and a rare glimpse of the old regime's luxury. The bathhouse is neutral ground and sanctuary: no weapons or armor are allowed inside except the Watch's, so trade factors soak beside gang lords and guildmasters at all hours. The street level holds the entrance; below, under the street, are mineral baths, plunge pools, a massage parlor, lounges, and a gymnasium.

#### Temple of the Celestial Dawn
The Dawn Temple is one of Lada's two temples in the city, built of pink stone that glows at sunrise. Its priests heal anyone who asks, not only those who pay, so each morning a line of the sick and injured stretches down the street waiting for the doors to open at dawn. Gold seems to cover every statue and pillar, though most of the golden statues are illusions over stone or wood. Lada's avatar has appeared here several times in the city's history.

#### The Wheatsheaf
A smugglers' tavern in Lower Zobeck and second home to anyone who needs a thug now and then. It serves strong beer, spicy food, and information, especially from the Redcloaks kobold gang and the Cloven Nine. Its regulars are rogues, fences, cultists, and assassins, always alert and looking for an opening, yet the place is usually peaceful: the city's most powerful gangsters want it kept under a flag of truce. When violence does start there, it is never a brawl. It is murder, and those who break the peace tend to "leave town by the river."

#### The Cloven Nine
The most notable criminal gang in Zobeck. It began as street kids banding together and became a vicious, refined enterprise of diabolist tiefling warlocks, wizards, and sorcerers, each running a crew, led by Izachar, called "Eyebite." Their strength comes from rumored patronage by Lord Greymark, an alliance with two [[Kariv]] clans (the Galati diviners and the Heph diabolists), and their own bargains with devils. They deal in curses, divinations, enchantments, settling matters of honor and revenge, black markets, burglary, extortion, and quiet killings, and they run the Smuggler's Market in the Gullet. They drink at the Wheatsheaf and the Silk Scabbard, but their headquarters is the Broken Seal, the worst-reputed tavern in the city. When an assassin killed their founder, Akad the Elder, they found the culprit and impaled him on the Puffing Bridge. Their mark is a nine-pointed star with one bent tine, and those who have betrayed them panic at the sight of it.

#### Winter's Kiss, the Shadow Fey Embassy
The home of His Excellency Glaninin Thelamandrine, Ambassador-in-Extraordinary of the Winter Court. For decades it was hidden. It now stands openly on Alchemist's Folly, though its exact location drifts a street or two this way or that, and few dare visit. The ambassador has invited most of Zobeck's gentlefolk (but no priests of Lada or Khors) to dine and talk about the succession in [[Grand Duchy of Dornig|the Grand Duchy]] and a possible alliance against the Dragon Empire. The Council has not agreed to any alliance yet, but the shadow fey are patient. The fey recently gave the Griffon Knights a set of black and silver speckled griffons, smaller than the Ironcrag breed but swift and quick to learn.
