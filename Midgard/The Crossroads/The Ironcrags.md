Located in the Ironcrag Mountains and their foothills west of [[Zobeck]], the Ironcrag Cantons are centers of dwarven trade, industry, and culture. Each canton is defined by a settlement that has existed at least 100 years and encompasses a set of halls. More than mines and simple shelter, proper halls must include forges or smelters, a brewery, clan homes, and at least one temple or shrine.

The 13 settled cantons are Bareicks, Bundhausen, Grisal, Gunnacks, Hammerfell, Juralt, Kubourg, Nordmansch, St. Mishau, Templeforge, Tijino, Vursalis, and Wintersheim. Some of the cantons have two or three names: one in Common, one in Dwarvish, and one in the Southern Tongue. The former canton of Citadel (Friundor) filled with prospectors during a gold rush a decade ago. The gold is gone, and derro and devils in the caverns have driven the last prospectors to madness.

The cantons are fractious, and rarely take on risky military ventures. Zobeck has made trade deals with several of them one at a time. The [[Magdar Kingdom]] hires a great many Ironcrag mercenaries when war threatens.

Each canton displays its own crest. Its trade goods are iron, weapons, armor, beer, cheese, leather, wool, adamantine, mithral, and gold.

## Canton Life
[[Dwarves]] think of their canton first and their race or clan second. The canton is home and hearth, family and wealth, and safety from an uncaring world. That does not make cantonal dwarves any happier than anyone else: they are famously dour, fractious, and opinionated about everything, and they unite only against an outside threat, which gives the elders and the captains of the Free Companies an excuse to lecture the young about the importance of the Free Dwarven Cantons.

Dwarves are reluctant to count themselves, or at least to share the count with anyone outside their own canton. The best guesses say the cantons hold as many as 150,000 dwarves and perhaps 25,000 slaves outside Grisal, most of them human.

Other settlements rise and fall without being counted among the 13. The small settlements of Roglett and Mynnasgard have been inhabited for about 50 and 80 years, and are on their way to canton status. Wintersheim in the north and the dark canton of Grisal, across the River Argent near [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau and Doresh]], lie apart from the rest; varying altitudes and passes make some cantons far easier to reach than others.

## The Major Cantons

### Bundhausen
The central canton stands above Lake Soizal, on land that was once part of the elven barony of Liadmura. Half its city is on the surface, a hub of trade and the home of the yearly Dwarfmoot, where the cantons air grievances, make laws, judge disputes between cantons by common vote, and sometimes declare war or make peace with the lowlands (which to a dwarf means everything outside the Ironcrags). Elven towers stand beside piles of mine tailings, the Eagle Emperor's winged emblem and flowered knot can still be found around the canton, and the griffons the elves brought still snatch unguarded sheep and ponies. Bundhausen is the canton most open to outside embassies, and many humans wrongly assume it is the capital or speaks for all the halls. It speaks only for itself.

### Grisal, the Black Canton
Claimed by both the Grand Duchy of [[Dornig]] and the Greater Duchy of Morgau, Grisal pays fealty to neither. It lies on the east bank of the River Argent, above the Zombie Wood of Zwargau and within sight of a temple of the Red Goddess, where the dead are brought to serve the living. Its dwarves are among the most pious of all; fully a tenth are clerics, paladins, or temple servants, honoring [[Volund]] and his son the sun god Khors, [[Sif|Grajava the Shield Maiden]], [[Wotan]], and [[Perun|Thor-Perun]]. Mostly the canton strives to hold the undead at bay, but its young warriors sometimes raid into Doresh by daylight, slaughtering zombies and skeletons by the score and crossing running water back home before nightfall. Those who fail to return are sometimes seen again as black-armored servants of [[Marena]].

Grisal's large human and goblin population dates from before it was a canton, when elfmarked nobles of House Hirsch-Dammung ruled it for Dornig. The dwarves took the land to keep it from Prince Lucan of Morgau (now [[King Lucan|king]]). Dornig's nobles and the Imperatrix have never acknowledged the claim, and it has caused repeated border skirmishes, though the Grand Duchy's internal troubles have put it in abeyance for now.

The Black Fortress of Grisal was built against Dornig archers and [[Krakovar|Krakovan]] hussars. It is the training ground of Grisal's paladins of Khors, and its scouts watch for darakhul riders from Gybick. Its commander, Jaro Whitebeard, is a longtime mercenary who became a general late in life. He is struggling with thousands of Krakovan refugees on his roads, building human villages in Grisal; he finds it hard to turn them away, and several Dwarfmoots have failed to settle the question.

### Gunnacks
One of the few cantons to keep a king until recent times, Gunnacks is home to the enormous Gunnacksen clan, merchants and caravaneers who travel from [[Wolfheim]] to [[Free Cities of the Desert|Siwal]] and the [[Rothenian Plain]]; its young learn to drive mule teams before their beards grow in. The Gunnacks were among the first to come south from the old halls of the North, so they hold the finest locations in the Ironcrags: upper halls overlooking three beautiful valleys, easy passes north and east, and plentiful forests, iron, and stone. The other cantons joke that they have no reason to leave home, but they do. They travel and trade until they are rich, then settle down and raise huge families, since a Gunnacks dwarf's worth is measured by their children. Some speak of splitting the canton in two.

### Hammerfell
One of the great ironworking halls, Hammerfell sometimes seals its doors for a summer or a year, a sign of war with something from the depths, or of a critical stage in some new feat of forgecraft. Outsiders rarely learn which. Its dwarven name, Mazzot, means "hammer," and its smiths make the finest dwarven plate. Its Hammershields bear magic runes or living blazons, heraldic beasts that can be summoned to serve the bearer, and their making is entirely a secret of the canton.

### Kubourg
Called Friunsgorla in Dwarvish, Kubourg is a great castle on a small hill surrounded by green fields. Its folk are the Ironcrags' butchers, cheesemakers, and leatherworkers, breeders of donkeys, mules, and dogmoles, and makers of jewel-toned inks and black powder. They have also cornered the hops supply, which makes them essential to every dwarven stout, and their priestesses of [[Ninkash]] brew the canton's finest holy ales. Arquebuses, heavy crossbows, wealthy friends, and the Dwarfmoot halls just across the lake keep greedier neighbors from raiding them.

### Tijino
The southernmost free canton lies just north of the royalist canton of [[Melana]] and the city of [[Triolo]]. It sells ironwork and weapons to anyone who can pay, along with good wood for spears, axe hafts, and crossbows. Its great hall is a springtime gathering place for Free Companies hiring out to the feuding cities of the south, and in Ironcrags slang, "when we cross Tijino Pass" means "when we go raiding."

### Wintersheim
Wintersheim keeps [[Northlands]] traditions and close ties to [[Stannasgard]], to Melana, and to [[The Wolfmark|the Wolfmark]], and is comfortable with "kingly cantons" ruled by bloodlines. Its dwarves are famous for the ranger society of the Order of the White Wolf, for jewelry and runecarving, and for copying the old sagas for temples and wealthy libraries. They are more famous for their dragon. Hrothvengr, a white dragon of middle years, guards the hall's entrance on an enormous bed of copper coins and odd pebbles that he is convinced is worth a fortune. The other cantons have tried magic, drugs, and bribery to recruit dragons of their own, without success. The Wintersheim dwarves treat him with respect, and visitors who mock him tend to find themselves charmed, sometimes for years, polishing his coins to a warm red glow.

## The Minor Cantons
Smaller cantons are often specialists, highly capable in their fields; their small numbers usually reflect poor mines rather than poor effort.

| Bareicks | The smallest canton, with poor mines and rich herds of sheep and cattle, so every dwarven herder is assumed to come from here. It also produces the most dangerous mercenaries in the Ironcrags, berserkers with nothing to lose who won't go home unless rich or dead. When one dies, his companions break camp and carry him home the next day. |
| Juralt | Warm lowland valleys ringed by peaks and fine pasture, defended by swarms of half-tamed griffons, pike walls, and battle wagons. Among the most warlike cantons, it raids the lowlands every other year, and its neighbors call it reckless and greedy, more interested in plunder than mining. |
| Nordmansch | Two rival halls, Upper Nordmansch under the peak called the Sentinel and Lower Nordmansch in the forested hills, mining silver, iron, and mithral, making enchanted steel and magic rings, and supplying the finest axes and mine timbers from the black pines of the enchanted Ordenn Forest. It keeps more rune magic and lithomancy alive than any other canton, and is the only canton that keeps no slaves. It takes little wergeld for blood feuds, which other dwarves credit to its reverence for [[Lada]]. |
| St. Mishau | A strange canton where all female dwarves are cloistered, and the males, who speak with an odd accent, serve in Free Companies only every five or six years. It mines adamantine, coal, a few enormous gems, and quicksilver from hidden little mines, and trades with Zobeck's kobolds, which other dwarves consider dishonorable. Jealous rumors claim it serves [[Mammon]], St. Charon, or a dark fey goddess. |
| Templeforge | Also called Favgia Baselgia, the holy ground of [[Volund]] and home of the dwarven airships, since the first was built by a priest of Volund who lived here. Volund's Hammer, with which the god made the first gearforged, rests in the everburning forges of the Great Temple of the Sacred Hammer, a smithy, temple, and school famous for its daily Recitation of Steel and evening Hammer Chorus. Pilgrims come from Zobeck, the Magdar Kingdom, and the [[Seven Cities]], and dwarven smiths try to visit for a blessing once a decade. Rarely, the smith-priests forge a weapon or shield as a "gift of Volund" for a famous dwarven warrior; such bearers are called gift-worthy or temple-gifted, and losing the item is devastating. |
| Vursalis | The snowiest canton, crowned by Mount Mergansar, the Kronhorn, and the Peshvai Glaciers, and also a breadbasket whose barley, wheat, and potatoes feed its neighbors. Slaves bought from Juralt and the youngest Vursalis dwarves do the fieldwork, and a law keeps the young in the fields until they pass a test of knowledge and dwarven deportment. |

## Lost Halls
Not all cantons thrive. The Golden Citadel of Friundor, Krongard, Sargau, Villershall, and Volund's Beard have all been abandoned.

| Hall | Fate |
|---|---|
| Friundor (Citadel) | Rich until its gold ran out. New veins sparked a rush of humans and dwarves, but derro and devils set the miners against one another, and the halls are empty again. |
| Krongard | Once held the western approaches, until the mages of [[Allain]] leveled it in an eldritch siege, still a source of anger among the dwarves. |
| Sargau | Destroyed from below by ghouls and derro, though its halls are still celebrated in song. |
| Villershall | Destroyed by fellow dwarves: mercenaries hired by the Gunnacks clan, who ruined their trade rivals with a year-long siege. |
| Volund's Beard | Abandoned. |

## The Silbertal
The upper reaches of the River Argent form the Silbertal, a valley that divides the cantons from Grisal and is claimed by [[Zobeck]]. It is fairly wild, with small goblin raiding bands and the occasional ogre or winter wolf, and Zobeck's merchants have been clamoring for safer roads. Small abandoned mines and a long-neglected dwarfhold dot its hills.

## Liadmura and the Elves
Under the glacier of Mount Locchis lies the Locchistal, the valley of standing stones and ancient groves where the dwarves hold the Dwarfmoot. What they rarely mention is that many of the oldest stones belong to the elves of Liadmura, the Eagle Court, from the days when the elven empire conquered in the name of Valeresh. Rusted elven arrowheads still turn up in the valley, and some white eagles and golden griffons there are thought to descend from elven bloodlines. High atop Mount Locchis, a small Eagle Shrine to Valeresh still stands, too high and cold for the dwarves to tear down. Its stones now bear offerings to [[Wotan]], but its elegant pillars and magical warmth are clearly imperial. Mentioning the elves within a dwarf's hearing is an invitation to a fight.

### The Ruins of Liadmura
The ruins of Liadmura, Nest of the Eagle Emperor, stand high in the southern Ironcrags, where white cliffs loom over small forested valleys and alpine lakes. The walls, towers, griffon perches, and eagle nests have weathered gracefully since the elves left for [[Valera]] and Thorn, but the dwarves give the place a wide berth, and rightly: children of the briar, wood woses, banshees, feral griffons, and even rocs said to descend from the Eagle Emperor's own mounts live among its pink walls and silver-sparkling towers.
