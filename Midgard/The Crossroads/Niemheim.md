Niemheim is the realm of the gnomes, nine cities and towns in the dark Wormwood Forest east of Krakova, beyond the [[Blood Kingdom]] and on the edge of the [[Rothenian Plain]]. Any caravan bound for Niemheim must cross the Oros Bridge north of [[Zobeck]].

For generations [[Baba Yaga]] preyed on the gnomes, and stories still tell of a lost generation of gnomish children taken into Grandmother's stewpots and never seen again. The gnomes survived by turning to dark protectors. The temples of Niemheim are now given over to Chernobog and darker powers, with only [[Lada]] keeping some small attention from the old faith. Outsiders tend to treat the red-capped gnomes of Niemheim with suspicion.

The nine cities are said to be linked to nine swords of the gnomish princes of old. King Redbeard has sought to recover them.

Niemheim's arms are three red crows on a white field with a checkered blue border.

## The Devil's Offer
The gnomes were not always servants of Hell. They lived among the people of [[Krakovar|Krakova]] for many years as friends and allies, teaching fey sorcery, weaving, and gardening, happy to talk pottery, alchemy, tanning, and manuscript illumination. Then, 200 years ago, a gnome prince broke a blood oath of loyalty and service sworn to [[Baba Yaga]]. The prince died swiftly; his people suffered slowly. Night-haunts, strigoi, ala hags, and the psoglav demon-dogs, all Grandmother's servants, hunted them village by village.

Then a devil of the Eleven Hells made the King of the Gnomes a very generous offer. He would hide the gnomes deep in the Krakovan woods in a dozen far-flung villages, ringed by erinyes and barbed and bearded devils as wardens against Baba Yaga, in exchange for a small price in blood and souls. That the gnomes took the bargain says a great deal about what Grandmother had done to them. The alternative was extinction in her stewpots.

## The Infernal Gnomes Today
Once a month the gnomes make a blood sacrifice, and if no strangers come to their tidy towns, they must offer one of their own. So gnome thugs, illusionists, and enchanters work the roads and taverns, amusing travelers, and someone always grows curious about the silent land under the pines when a gnome bard spins a tale.

The towns are well ordered, with pretty gardens, neat squares, and half-timbered houses, half above ground and half below, easily overlooked under fey glamours. The gnomes tan calfskin and hunting leathers, weave clever woolens, burn charcoal for the hottest kilns outside the [[The Ironcrags|Ironcrags]], and make bright glazes and prized little kitchen knives of Niemheim steel. They also produce suspiciously fine vellum in quantities enough to fill the scriptoria of [[Magocracy of Allain|Allain]], and no one asks where it comes from. Darakhul and centaurs who enter without permission vanish, and soon after, a fresh shipment of fine tallow candles, sharp knives, and neatly mended tunics heads out on a dwarven mule train to [[Vidim]] or [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau]].

The influence of the Hells bends magic under the Wormwood's boughs. Illusions cast in Niemheim are extremely potent and hard to detect, and spellcasters are said to have been fooled by their own illusions there. The gnomes themselves see through all but the most powerful.

### Gnome Names
Niemheim gnomes never give their true names. They introduce themselves with a dodge such as "I am a master smith and you may call me Cinderspark," and keep true names within family and close friends; even the king's true name is a secret guarded by his wife and daughters. They believe Baba Yaga can find and kill any gnome whose true name she sniffs out, so an invitation to a naming ceremony means a gnome trusts you with their child's life.

### Glower Stones
The gnomes' first line of defense, set throughout the forest and especially at its edges, are glower stones: a crude, hunched gnomish figure of stone and bone, ringed by sharpened sticks hung with the rotting viscera of Niemheim's enemies. Its face changes expression, neutral and welcoming for those allowed to enter, glowering for trespassers, and it alerts nearby communities that someone has entered the Wormwood. The most dangerous are infused with hellfire, shoot burning rays from their eyes, and shriek in Infernal; they can be recognized by the living, howling heads on their stakes. Ambassador Xingat considers them art.

### Sproutings and the Tree of Sulf
Infernal copses of trees have begun appearing across the [[Rothenian Plain]]. These sproutings grow to full size in less than a day and cover around 5,000 square feet, each home to 20 or so gnomes in crude huts. The other peoples of the Plain avoid them, though the [[Khazzaki]] sometimes trade plunder for herbal poultices.

A few miles south of the Wormwood stands the dreadful Tree of Sulf, a corrupted [[Yggdrasil|World Tree]]: a tall oak with sickly yellow bark, twisting branches, black and crimson leaves, and a sulfur stench that carries for miles, whose roots draw on the Eleven Hells. Its copse bristles with spree demons and glower stones. Devils use it as a shortcut to and from the Hells, and now and then an infernal traveler steps out of it to stay with the gnomes or journey on to Demon Mountain. Sablehorns, a high cleric of Chernobog, works to stabilize its inconstant portals. Its smoldering acorns are handled with thick gloves, used to enchant knives and hide armor, and fermented by some alchemists into a brimstone liquor that sells for a fortune in Holmgard.

## King Redbeard
The king is tall and imposing for a gnome, his long beard curling into five flaming points that glow like cinders and shed motes of ash. His face is deeply lined and his brow rarely unfurrows. He wears the Wormwood Robe, made of living moss and fungi, said to let him cross his forest at tremendous speed, and carries an antler scepter with a different colored flame burning on each point, each granting a powerful spell. He is intensely pragmatic. His people are split between those who live as if the bargain did not exist and those who relish its evil, and he is rarely free of the company of the cruel and sophisticated bone devil Ambassador Xingat.

### The Hidden Palace
Redbeard's palace in Holmgard is a court of justice and a military fortress, warded against shadow magic, divination, and scrying, and guarded by more than 200 veteran wands and blades sworn by blood oath as the Gentleman Irregulars. Gnomes invited to the palace receive their invitation at dawn and wander the streets until they are somehow conjured along a magical path to it. Non-gnomes need a gnome guide and are escorted out blindfolded on owlback. Visitors find the devils annoying: they speak only Infernal and take offense at imagined insults. Roads to the planes are common there.
