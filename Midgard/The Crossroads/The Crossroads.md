The Free City of [[Zobeck]] shares borders with three nations: the dwarven cantons of [[The Ironcrags]] to the west, the human [[Magdar Kingdom]] to the southeast, and [[Perunalia]], ruled by women, downriver at the mouth of the River Argent. Together with [[The Margreve|the Margreve Forest]], the Smolten Hills, and the underground realms of [[The Darakhul Imperium|the Ghoul Imperium]] and the kobold city of Lillefor, they make up the Crossroads of Midgard.

The rest of Midgard turns around the Crossroads like spokes around a wheel. Everything going north and south or east and west passes along the River Argent, the Sultan's Road, the [[Great Northern Road]], and other well-worn routes, each with its own dangers. The Crossroads folk borrow language, customs, tools, and even gods from all their neighbors, but they keep their own code, their own pride, and their own way of doing business. For their gatherings and the dwarven custom of taking thralls, see [[Customs of the Crossroads]].

## Trade by Road and River
The minstrels sing of silks and spices, mithral and magic. They leave out the sweat and the donkey dung. As the Crown Square merchants say, "There's no such thing as easy money." Someone has to haul the iron, wheat, silver, ale, wool, and timber, and someone has to defend it from robbers, ogres, and petty lordlings who close their bridges and demand a toll. Most merchants prize short, dull runs to Cronepisht or Hammerfell. The big profits come from rare goods: the Templeforge airships of the Ironcrags, the spice-scented Flying Cities of Sikkim, and the dark roads of the shadow fey that run from Zobeck to the courts of the Shadow Realm.

### River Traffic
Floating a barge downriver to Sveretska or Perunalia is easier than marching up a mountain, but not by much. The river gods are fickle in spring, river trolls can capsize any barge, lorelei distract pilots, and hill giant bandits sink cargo with a few boulders and loot the wreck. Going back upstream, the guards pull the oars past miles of forest that hide bandits, snags, and sandbars, and some barge hands turn pirate. At Stefanstor, the warden always wants "a word and a kind gift" on top of the Zobeck river toll.

### The Mountain Road
The road into the Ironcrags runs up the Silbertal, past the ruins, over the Gunnacks Pass to the Fork. North leads to Grisal, Hirschberg, northern [[Grand Duchy of Dornig|Dornig]], and the Wolfmark. East leads past the cheesemakers of Kubourg to Salzbach, [[Magocracy of Allain|Tintager]], and Bemmea. The passes close each fall and reopen in spring as torrents of snowmelt, and there are bragging rights for the first mule train through. Dwarfholds and shepherds' huts along the way offer shelter for the night, though stories say (never where dwarves can hear) that some mule trains vanish under the mountains to dig for bearded masters.

### South on the Sultan's Road
Goods to the Magdar Kingdom and beyond go by oxcart, at maybe 12 miles on a good day. Oxen are cheap, and the carts can be circled at night, which matters: the [[White Mountain Marches]] are a nest of robbers, and [[The Mharoti Empire|Mharoti]] raiders are patient and vicious. The unspoken benefit is fast promotion. Half the guards will likely die before reaching Harkesh.

### The Great Northern Road
Zobeck's griffon riders guard the [[Great Northern Road]], which runs over 200 miles through the Margreve to Castle Valach and on toward Bratislor. It is only a wagon wide in most places, a green tunnel under the meeting branches where night falls early even in summer. On foot it takes about a fortnight; riders without wagons take 8 to 10 days. Coaching inns stand one to three days apart. Spring melt and mud are the worst of it, and merchants who set out before Zobeck's Road Opening festival find the inns closed and hungry things emerging from the hollows. It is the only passable route to the Red Queen's Courlandia and the [[Blood Kingdom]], and Zobeck's second source of wealth after the river.

## Strange Customers
Zobeck's place at the heart of the Crossroads comes partly from its river and roads, and partly from its trade with unusual partners, above all the shadow fey and the kobolds of Lillefor.

### Trade with the Shadow Fey
Thirty years ago, a young highwayman named Antonidas Jabber and Tuck Marick, youngest son of the Marick merchant house, decided on a heroic drinking binge that the minstrels' tales of cruel fey were one-sided, and that there was money to be made trading with the Shadow Court. The first emissaries they sent to the Winter Court came home in pieces over six months, but before the year was out the first glass caravans returned from the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]]. Their company, the Chartered Merchants of Scáthesidhe, still brings intricate wares straight from the Winter Court's capital, and it introduced moonsteel to Zobeck.

The fey took gold for trinkets like ghostly silver lutes, goblets of spun ice, and essence of blizzard, but the rarest goods sold only for happy memories, years of the buyer's life, or a night's company. Asked why they valued mortal company so highly, the fey said, "It warms us." Jabber and Tuck are still crude and greedy, but more careful now. Rumor ties them and the fey to the old mayor Karillian Gluck's retirement, and since the shadow fey embassy opened, all moonsteel sales go through the embassy and require an oath never to use the weapons against their makers.

### Lillefor
The kobold city of Lillefor lies near Morgau and Doresh. Two things keep it safe from the Ghoul Imperium: its merchants sell the ghouls meat, paper, and metal goods, and its tunnels are tiny and cut through stone too hard to burrow. It is a haven for small folk. Dark creeper bandits sell their loot there, derro sell ore, and goblins hire out as bat riders and scouts. The kobolds discourage Big Folk and gnomes. King Kekarrac rules from a central square at the heart of the trap-riddled tunnels, and small, diplomatic visitors can bribe their way to a pass of 8, 12, or 24 hours. Larger visitors must squeeze through every passage and are watched constantly. Zobeck's kobolds think the Lillefor kobolds foolish to live beside so many undead, and a little envious that they are independent. King Kekarrac and Zobeck's King of Kings exchange garish gifts every year, each trying to outdo the other.

| House Greymark | Zobeck's largest and richest trading house, dealing in leather, wool, apples, ale, clockwork, amber, and silver. Rumors of slaves, Margreve timber and mandrake, and Shadow Realm goods are common, and those who ask too many questions find their business suffers. |

## Crossroads Mercenary Companies
Recruiters promise plunder and easy money. Mostly it is boot leather, tents, and camps, and few mercenaries live to retire to a manor house.

| Company | Description |
|---|---|

Their ranks include ratfolk, dust goblins, and halflings, favoring small folk for stealth and getting into places others can't. They won't take gnomes, citing only "a bad deal in the past." |
| Black Brotherhood | A huge army of disciplined soldiers from all over Midgard, bound by faith in [[Mavros]], for whom war is a lifelong pilgrimage. Captain Laurius Nuno's pavilion moves each season to wherever conflict is brewing; he most recently served against the Mharoti. Its companies have ended up on opposite sides of the same war. |
| Hogar's Horribles | A small company of half-bloods (elfmarked, hellbred, demonmarked, and dragonkin) led by the tiefling wizard Harmund Hogar, who was cheated of his Dornig father's company and estate by his relatives. They take the most dangerous jobs. |
| Hrothgar's Marauders | Feral but capable gnoll rangers and skirmishers from the [[Rothenian Plain]], superb with bows, led by Hrothgar Torn-Ear, who wears his enemies' teeth and searches between jobs for whoever wiped out his pack. |
| Huginn and Muninn's Company | [[Northlanders]] sworn to [[Wotan]], flying his two ravens on their banner, with three strongholds in the North. Their leader, Almut Shield-Breaker, called Wotan's Son, is a berserker who turns to banditry when peace lasts too long, to keep the ravens fed. |
| The Raven's Sons | About 50 cynical, cruel humans, ravenfolk, and centaurs who have fought for the Despot of Reth-Saal, the Master of Demon Mountain, the [[Vidim\|Tsar of Vidim]], and once for King Lucan. | Trollhaugen Pikes | Pikemen formed to fight the trolls of Trollhaugen, now hired as private armies and housecarls. They ignore taunts, attack aggressively, and fight with two-handed weapons rather than shields. Led by the trollkin Meshko Stubblehide, who hates trolls and giants. |

### The Clanking Legion
The gearforged have always been soldiers. The Nurian dwarves made them for war under [[Ptah]], and the rebels of Zobeck used them to break the aristocracy's heavy troops. The Clanking Legion is a company of more than 250, about half of them human or dwarven, led from the front by Sir Arlandros Brassheart, a gearforged son of a dead noble house of Kitheros whose armor is covered in a thousand dents and repairs and whose instinct for ambush and improvised tactics is uncanny. The legion strikes hard and fast and thrives on the impossible; it recently stormed a mid-river tower in the Argental by throwing up a rope bridge, and nobody is saying how.

| Officer | Role |
|---|---|
| Reshazir Rubat Aswat ("Feathers") | [[Ravenfolk]] priest of Thoth, scribe, quartermaster, and scrounger. The joke is that it takes half the legion to keep the other half running. |
| Wepwaset Sed | Aging dwarf follower of Ptah, enchanter, armorsmith, and keeper of the Ritual of Soulforging that makes most of the legion's gearforged. He complains he could be home in Nuria making jewelry; he retired once and lasted a week. |
| Ulana Resdota | The legion's wizard, who supports with enchantment and invisibility. Camp stories say she knows ley lines and shadow roads, and has some tie to the Master of Demon Mountain. |
| Darrenzo de Triolo | Master of Archers and second in command, a lethal longbowman whose black iron body bears the symbols of [[Hecate]], whose priesthood he has joined. Some comrades find him too eager to spill blood and burn villages for his goddess. |

The legion wins battles but has a mixed record as a business. Patrons, especially in the [[Seven Cities]], sometimes fail to pay, and the clankers have taken their pay in loot or marched over to the other side. Worse, the legion keeps accepting commissions that are almost, but never quite, suicidal. Its veterans suspect a hidden enemy they call the Dragon Spirit is setting them up as a sacrifice, perhaps to [[Baal-Hotep]] or [[Mot]].
