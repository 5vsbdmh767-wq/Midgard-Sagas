All through [[The Margreve]], standing stones mark the places where ley lines cross or bend. Most of Midgard's runestones just sit there. The Margreve's stones sometimes get up and dance. Usually they stay quiet except at the solstices and equinoxes, but a druid communing with them, someone attuned to ley magic, or a *meld into stone* spell can wake them at other times. When they dance, they can strengthen a ley line, weaken it, or bend it to a new course, and they have been known to hold celebrations, imprisonments, and executions too.

Anyone close enough to watch the stones dance tends to start dancing along with them, sometimes for hours, and it is exhausting. Forest folk know to look away.

The great stones follow the two big ley lines that run through the forest, the Black Road and Red Boar's Run (see [[Ley Lines & Shadow Roads]]). The rest are lesser stones, mossy and half forgotten, scattered by the hundreds through the woods.

##### Stones of the Black Road
The Black Road stones are simple menhirs set in a triangle, or two uprights with a lintel. Where there is a lintel, you enter the road by walking through the doorway.

| Place | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Mother's Maw | Two uprights and a lintel that look uncomfortably like a giant's open mouth. It is cold underneath, even in summer. |
| Hungry Bears | Three stones leaning together without quite touching. [[Bearfolk]] like the place and run off anyone who camps there too long. |
| Silver Spinners | Four stones veined with something silvery, with nets of web sometimes strung between them like a canopy. |
| Teller of Tales | Three standing stones and one fallen. The standing ones carry runes that change from visit to visit, and if you ask politely they sometimes talk about the weather, or about danger, depending on how the forest feels about you. |
| Three Soldiers | Three pale gray stones on a small hill said to be an elven general's grave, carved with runes telling a warrior's life. |

##### Stones of Red Boar's Run
These stones are carved with elvish runes and feel less friendly. Offerings to [[The Hunter|the Hunter]] and to [[Marena]] turn up at them, and more rarely to [[Sarastra]], Holda, or [[Yarila & Porevit]].

| Place | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Queen's Daughters | Three stones around a long-looted cairn. They sometimes weep golden tears that smell of pine and dry into amber. |
| The Coven | Reddish-brown stones carved with spirals and stars. Three or four usually stand here, but visitors have counted up to six, and some seem to wander off. Women find it easier to open the road here. |
| The Spires | Seven pale stones, each over thirty feet tall, aligned to the sun and stars. Druids come at the solstices to take measurements, and it's said you can step from the top onto Red Boar's Run and be at the Nieder Strait in a day. |

##### Lesser Stones

| Place | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Antler Hill Stones | A dozen smooth stones carved with antlers or ferns, sacred to the [[Alseid\|alseid]], who say each marks a great druid's grave. The spirits sometimes rise to strike down trespassers. |
| Fish Stones | Five blue-gray stones carved with fish, frogs, and a crab, always standing in water. Every spring they croak deafeningly. |
| Gentlemen of the Gate | Four stones and two lintels making a pair of doorways, said to belong to a trickster god. Gamblers on a losing streak make pilgrimages here, and bandits like it because lawmen can never seem to find it. |
| Three Silent Lads | Three leaning stones that turn up on the forest's western edge, between Obertal and Valach. [[Ninkash]] worshippers hold a drinking festival wherever they appear, and by morning the stones have moved on. |
| Troll Picture Stones | Tan stones carved with birds and deer, once honored by trollkin and darker fey. They are abandoned now, but the place still hums with power. |

Every so often a small dancing stone chooses to follow a spellcaster around as a familiar, rolling along behind them. It understands Common and Sylvan, though it can't speak.
