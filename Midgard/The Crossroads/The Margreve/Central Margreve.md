The Central Margreve is the stretch of [[The Margreve]] between the [[Great Northern Road]] and the Cloudwall Mountains. It is lighter and more open than the west, and you can usually see fifty feet or more through the trees, sometimes over a hundred. Pine, spruce, and cedar fill the north and the valleys between the Cloudwall foothills, and the floor there is a carpet of needles and ferns. The south is broadleaf woodland, thick with fallen leaves, creeping ivy, and flowers that bloom only at night.

The whole region smells of cedar and musk, and it feels far more alive than the west, in ways that are not always comforting. Diseased and malicious beasts are common. The Spider Crones keep their huts here and trade advice and poison for the right gift, feral hunters kill people who wander too deep, and leshy wipe out trails behind travelers. This is also where [[Baba Yaga]] keeps her walking hut, and stray spells of hers and her sisters are said to hide in the hollows and chase each other through the trees. The Central Heart, where the forest's power runs wild and feral, lies in the north of the region.

The rivers here run strange. Grandfather's Tears, which drains the Pine Bogs and the Cloudwall streams through Three Sisters Lake and down to the River Argent, carries the forest's spent magic out of the wood. Its bed glows faintly blue at night, wood rots almost as soon as it touches the water, and the fish and frogs that live in it have grown horny plates instead of scales. Only tin-bottomed boats or living canoes grown in the east can survive a trip upstream.

##### Places Travelers Speak Of

| Place | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Armiger Wood | A stretch of forest beside Grandfather's Tears where rusted armor encircles the tree trunks, as though a regiment had turned into trees where it stood. Forest goblins sometimes drum on the old breastplates before they go raiding. |
| Cradle Grove | Old maples with basin-shaped burls that catch rainwater. The people of Ulchik Village baptize their newborns with it and leave them overnight in the burls, dedicated to the forest. |
| Darkbud Marsh | A marsh by the River Argent where a sacred lily-of-the-valley grows. Picked, it never dies, but its white bells blacken at the touch of sin or deceit. Courtiers in the cities have started wearing them as proof of their honesty, and forest folk worry the flower will be picked out of existence. |
| Dead Druid's Run | Whitewater rapids in the north with five jagged, man-shaped rocks standing in the current. Ulchik villagers say they were druids turned to stone by one of Baba Yaga's children, and that if you listen past the roar you can hear them screaming. Whoever frees them is supposed to get a wish. |
| Five Wagon Oak | An abandoned windmill on the southern edge, all that remains of a farming village built from a single giant oak. The mill creaks at night though its wheel hasn't turned in a century, and people who hear it slowly go mad. |
| The Fogrolls | Hot springs and bottomless mud fields along the southern edge. All winter, a fog bank rolls off them and hangs over the river and the woods for miles. |
| Moondrawn Caves | A cave network in the far north where a spring is said to carry moonlight down into the deep. |
| Palewood | A birch copse around a limestone crag riddled with holes, where the little wicker effigies that forest folk weave to carry away their sins somehow gather. |
| Spiritsign | A clearing ringed by tall trees, sacred to the alseid, who say the dappled light there shows omens to the true of heart. They recently won it back from griffons. |
| Twilight Fork | An ordinary trail fork, except at dusk. The story goes that someone torn over a life-and-death choice can take both paths at once. People who try it come back with half a soul. |
| Ulchik Village | One of the most remote human settlements anywhere. Its people worship Pyotr, a tree aspect of [[Yarila & Porevit\|the Green Gods]], and the adults live as trees do, often in tree shape, while the children tend and water them. |
| Whistlehollow Village | A village south of Armiger Wood that recently threw out its old leaders in favor of "new ways," much to the alarm of its neighbors. |
