Stannasgard is the most open and cosmopolitan of the dwarven reaver kingdoms, a key trading enclave and a gateway to the Nieder Strait. For many merchants it is as far into the North as they care to go. Its wealth comes from rich iron and adamantine mines, and its smiths have made it famous for crafting, above all shields and armor; many count Stannasgard's shields and armor among the finest anywhere, and southern lordlings often ask its paladins and defenders for aid. Its banner is the anvil of [[Volund]] and the longship of [[Njord]].

It lies just across the strait from the ruins of Thorn. It has great temples to [[Wotan]], [[Thor]], [[Volund]], and [[Sif]], and many shrines to Grajava the Shield Maiden. Its huge priesthood earns much of the city's wealth crafting holy relics infused with divine power. Pilgrims come from as far as [[Zobeck]] and the [[Seven Cities]], dragging sanctified anvils, to seek blessings at Volund's Shrine of a Thousand Anvils, a cavernous temple on a craggy peak littered with the tools, weapons, and armor left by supplicants. Stannasgard rarely marches to war, but when it does, its warriors seldom fail; thousands of them went to [[The Wolfmark]] to fight the vampires and darakhul of [[Krakovar]] and [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau]].

## The Dragon's Blow

Stannasgard was a major shipbuilding center until two years ago, when the red dragon Visandred the Horse-Eater, long thought dead, dropped from the sky, burned its docks and slips, and wrecked its trading fleet. Most of the ships he burned had already been bought and paid for by foreign rulers. Jarl Beragorn sent his army to bring the beast down, and it never returned. The lost army, the ruined shipyards, and the collapse of its trade have strained the city's coffers and its reputation, and foreign enemies eye it greedily. Much of the Order of the Thunderer has come from [[Thrumagard]] to help defend it. Its forges still ring day and night.

Over the last year, many giantkin and trollkin settlers have come by longship from the southern shores of [[Jotunheim]] and built longhouses and farmsteads along the coast. Some call it an invasion; others see hope in it, and it has brought both tension and new strength to the beleaguered country.
