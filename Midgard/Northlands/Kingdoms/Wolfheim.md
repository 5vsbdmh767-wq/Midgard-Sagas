Wolfheim clings to a harsh stretch of wild country between [[Jotunheim]] and the sea, west of [[Trollheim]] and [[Huldramose]]. Proud, bold, and deeply traditional, its people hold to the old reaving ways more fiercely than any other nation. Others think settling this rocky land a suicidal mistake; Wolfheimers think it a worthy challenge. Its banner is the anvil of [[Volund]] over a wolf's head.

The kingdom is a mountainous peninsula of high peaks, craggy valleys, and steep hillsides reaching like a claw into the Nieder Strait. Its people divide it in three. The Spine is the center, where the Rygg Mountains run the length of the land and the towns cling to the coast where scattered farms and forests can survive. The Claw, to the south, is a storm-scoured badland of bare stone and twisting shale, said to be haunted by the dead of the Reaving. The Roof, to the north, is forested mountain valleys full of ancient ruins and failed settlements, home to trolls, hill giants, and hidden bands of tribal elves; its timber is valuable but takes armed guards to harvest.

## History

Long ago, dwarven vampire lords secretly took control of the hold of Glymshall and turned its beautiful valley into a nightmare of blood. Then came the Reaving: the land broke, the sea rushed in, and the vampire lords withdrew deep underground. Where other dwarves abandoned their holds for [[Tanserhall]] and other safe places, the survivors of Glymshall refused to leave, and they might have starved for it. The skalds say old King Ragnr sacrificed himself to [[Yarila & Porevit|Freyr and Freyja]], begging them to save his people, and the next night packs of winter wolves and worgs brought freshly killed reindeer to the dwarves. The old enemies swore a sacred alliance so both could survive, and named their new land Wolfheim. Fear and hatred of the undead have stayed in the kingdom's bones ever since.

Since its founding, Wolfheim has been the spiritual home of traditionalist reavers, but it takes in anyone who accepts its ways, which makes it a beacon for young dwarves, humans, trollkin, and others who want to live the reaver life. Its jarldoms raid and feud constantly, though nearby trolls and giants take most of their wrath. With few farms, its settlements keep great reindeer herds and raid other lands, even across the Nieder Strait, for treasure, grain, and hops.

## The Wolfmark and the North

Nine years ago, Wolfheim's reavers carved out [[The Wolfmark]] across the Nieder Strait, seeking farmland the kingdom badly needs. The jarldom's busy trade ships soon brought home a scourge of vampirism, necromancy, and undead. Relations soured until the Wolfmark declared itself independent and crowned its ruler King Skuti the First. Some Wolfheimers think they are well rid of the upstart; others seethe at the insult and ready their longships to raid their former kin. Meanwhile, common folk set guards through the long nights against vampires and worse.

The chaos around Wolfheim also offers opportunity. Some jarls want to lead their karls north to reoccupy and farm the lands south of the ruins of [[Legendary Places of the North#Nordheim|Nordheim]], while steering clear of the ruins themselves, and a small settlement has already gone up in hope of approval. The giants and trolls of Jotunheim have no wish to see the dwarven holds challenge them again, and send their young warriors to test themselves against Wolfheim's reavers.

## Life in the Kingdom

Arable land is scarce. People grow oats and root vegetables on hillside plots and fungus in underground caverns, fishing boats work the Nieder Strait for fish and whales, and every community's herds of reindeer give the meat and milk that are the staple of the diet. Wolves are part of daily life, guarding herds and patrolling the wilds alongside dwarven and human scouts. Harming a wolf, worg, or winter wolf is a crime punishable by death, and every year, when the first snow falls, the old pact with the wolves is renewed in a ceremony called Vetrnaetr.

A rash of vampirism and necromancy now plagues the realm. Beyond the town walls, trolls, ogres, and other monsters roam the mountains, and scouts watch for raids from other Wolfheim towns as well as from the giants of Jotunheim.

## The Wolf King

Thulsamir Wolfbrother is a cheerful dwarf past his third century, with a ruddy face, an impish grin, sharp blue eyes, and fiery red hair graying at the temples. He wears stout wool worked with precious-metal thread, a cloak of elk fur, and a crown hewn from the anchor of his first longship. Longtooth, the winter wolf who leads the local packs and has been his friend for years, is usually at his side.

As a young ranger he helped seal the alliance with the worgs and wolves and earned his name. He took the throne in his 121st year, when his father died in a kraken attack on the city. Where his father and uncle clung to the past, he embraced raiding and helped shape the reaver culture the whole North follows today. He tries to seem stern but can't hide his rakish humor, and he loves tales of valor. He hides a deep fear of the undead, and the vampire plague brings out the worst in him.

His great ambition is to free Wolfheim from depending on raids for grain. His dream of conquest across the strait ended with the Wolfmark's independence, which he still seethes over, so he turned north toward the lands around Nordheim. Those plans stalled a year ago when his nephew and heir, Blovurr, died. Now he struggles with vampires and necromancers, giants pressing in from Jotunheim, and his feuding jarls.

| Eyrsvik | Seat of Jarl Kjar Valdsson, a sizable town by Northern standards, with a deepwater harbor in a curve of the Rygg and some of Wolfheim's best farmland. |

| Rafrborg | Seat of Jarl Sigtove Amberbraid, a busy town and the most open to new ideas and other peoples. Many of the kingdom's best shield-maidens come from here. It trades heavily with Huldramose, and a trollkin embassy from that nation was recently given land inside its walls. |
