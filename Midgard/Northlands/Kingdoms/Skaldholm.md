The small island realm of Skaldholm is known for its peaceful ways (other [[Northlanders]] say "spear-shy"), its healing hot springs, and its skaldic schools, each run by a famous bard who insists their style is the best. What the scholars here don't know isn't worth knowing, or so the scholars say, and they have earned the boast: the island's skalds, bards, and sages have gathered the written and oral histories of the North and beyond into one of the best-known histories of the world. Its graduates serve in the courts of jarls, kings, and distant emperors, and every village is proud to have a Skaldholm skald perform at its hearth. They carry news and rumors along with their songs, and many an adventurer learns that the difference between a saga of a hero and a saga of a villain is the skald who sings it. Its symbol is a golden lyre.

## The Island

Skaldholm lies south of [[Trollheim]] at the end of a volcanic island chain left by the making of the Swive. Its 1,800 square miles are lush, temperate rainforest divided by rocky ridges that radiate from Bragafjall, a dormant volcano. Geysers and hot springs dot the land, with a faint mineral tang instead of sulfur, and visitors come from all over the North to bathe in their curative waters, which several druid communities tend. Ash, elm, spruce, and rowan grow here, along with tonrosp, "music aspen," a golden, resonant hardwood found nowhere else and prized for the finest instruments, wands, and staves. Birds and small animals are most of the island's wildlife, so it is spared most of the North's usual dangers.

Fire giants once held court in obsidian halls on Bragafjall. The first dwarven empire drove them out and found the healing springs, and though the hold of Ishamagard was built on a nearby peak, no settlement went up on Bragafjall itself, where fire still burned underground. A community of dwarven druids tended the springs and groves and kept a sanctuary of peace while the rest of the North turned to reaving. When the Swive flooded the plains and Ishamagard fell into the sea, the druids raised their mountain above the water. Most of them died in the working, but an island remained. Bragi, the son of [[Wotan]], led his followers there and commanded them to study song, story, saga, and history, and their settlement grew into a city of bards, sages, and skalds.

## Society and Rule

Most islanders work in or for the skaldic schools. The schools produce the North's finest musicians, and their rivalries go well beyond rude songs: student feuds sometimes end in injury or death. The academy leaders owe fealty to the Master of Thyles, who rules the whole island. The title is said to have been given to the first ruler by Bragi himself, and each Master gives up their birth name for it. A contest of song, tale-telling, and flyting (a contest of insults) decides the Master every nine years. The Master sits at the center of a network of skalds who all report back, and few Northlanders dare to cross a ruler whose skalds can wreck a reputation with a few songs in feast halls across the North.

Everyone knows, and no one says aloud, that the Master keeps a semi-secret band of agents called the Shadowsingers for espionage, blackmail, and the occasional assassination. Anyone who wants the Master's favor can attempt the Four Magnificent Challenges of Skald Thalwyn Rotjun, a dangerous contest that changes every year and keeps petty requests away.

The streets are never silent. If an islander can't hear a song or tale somewhere, they start one, and whole village squares sing as they work. Fishing fleets work the Swive, farms grow crops in the volcanic soil, and the hot springs let islanders raise rare medicinal herbs, cloudberries, and even ice peppers.

## The Master of Thyles

Eirik Ellefsen won the title seven and a half years ago. He is a thick-browed man in his late sixties with deep-set green eyes and long silver hair in a braid, dressed in practical Northern clothes and one outer robe of wool-lined silk embroidered with musical notes and runes, a gift from the child of a southern emperor from his years in the desert courts. He was a noted harpist, and though his hands are crooked with age, he still plays in quiet moments and moves listeners to tears. He is cunning, shrewd, and comfortable with manipulation, but bound by a fierce sense of honor and purpose. Under him, Skaldholm has stood against the werewolves of [[Vargrike]], lent support against the vampires in [[Wolfheim]], and kept up its predecessors' cold war with the agents of [[Boreas (North Wind)|Boreas]].

Through his connections the Master was among the first to hear that Ragnarok was near. Then something happened that he cannot remember: [[Loki]], it is said, took the knowledge from him as payment for an old bargain, and all he knows now is that he has forgotten something important. The island's skalds call it the Riddle of the Forgotten Thing and puzzle over it without success. The Master has sent Nalgar to seek wisdom over the sea, Randulfr to hunt for the lost memory in the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] and beyond, and Sylvoll to beg the gods for help.

## The Darkening Song

For each of the last six months, a bard has been found nearly lifeless, with no memory of what happened and no trace of their musical skill or training left. Their friends say each victim was unusually inspired in the weeks before, performing far beyond their usual skill. No cause or culprit has been found.

## The City of Skaldholm

The city curves around a natural harbor, built low and wide of the island's smooth pale stone to weather the storms off the Swive.

## Places on the Island

Most islanders live in the city. A few small villages lie on the north side; the south is thought too rocky to settle.
