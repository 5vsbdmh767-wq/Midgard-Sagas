The Kingdom of the Bear claims the rolling hills and coastal plains south of the Reaching Mountains, from the Tunturi Range west to the sea and from the Hrygangur Peaks south to the Vidbrun River. It is some 60,000 square miles of deep forest, hills, and valleys with only a few scattered communities. The [[Bearfolk|bearfolk]] range widely and don't mind trespassers, so long as they respect the King and Queen of Bears and defer to their people when challenged. Its banner is a beehive and a bear paw.

The land is rich, but its people must stand constant guard. Vaettir and malmgoltr lurk in the forests, the mountains spawn ogres and trolls, and nomadic raiders cross the southern border each spring to hunt beasts and bears alike. To the north, skirmishes with the wolves of [[Vargrike]] threaten to become a war. The worst threat lies east, beyond the Tunturi Range, where oni warlords lead armies of ogres, trolls, and tundra gnolls and look on the Kingdom of the Bear with covetous eyes.

## History

Legend says [[Bengta]] the Bear Maiden made the first bearfolk from the great Northern bears. The kingdom's recorded history begins with the fey lord Mesikämmen, called "Old Honey Paws" for his fondness for sweets. About 450 years ago, after the dwarves who followed [[Volund]] abandoned their old holdings and went south, the ogre hordes of the oni lich Dakhad Atlan swept over the eastern mountains. The bearfolk were then scattered, nomadic families, and Mesikämmen united them under his beehive-and-paw banner. The Mountain Wars lasted fifty years, until Old Honey Paws defeated Dakhad Atlan at the Battle of the Broken Peaks. The victorious bearfolk founded Björnrike and made Mesikämmen their first king, and so far their only one. The borders drawn by the Mountain Wars still stand.

About twenty years ago, Yohana Honeyhair, a druid of the Bear Maiden, became ruler of the port city of Bjeornheim, which by an old agreement with the fey lord chooses its own semi-independent monarch from its foremost noble families. She made it the trading hub it is today, and the city's giant guardian bees flourished under her and Bengta's blessing. After decades of wooing, Old Honey Paws married her two years ago. Their son, Prince Alrek, was born the same year, and the guardian hives spread a hundredfold across the whole kingdom. Many took it as proof of a divine bond between the prince and Bengta's sacred bees.

## The Bee-Blight

Recently a wasting illness has struck the guardian bees, and they are slowly sickening and dying. At the same time, the young prince fell ill, and he grows worse each day. The kingdom's druids can cure neither. The queen has stayed in Bjeornheim with her son and sends envoys and hired adventurers across the North and beyond, chasing every rumor of a cure. The king sees an attack on his family and a herald of war. He is arming and training his bearfolk, and on the advice of his counselor Brother Fjal he has begun sending raids into neighboring Vargrike.

To the east, an old enemy has returned. Five years ago a band of adventurers accidentally freed Dakhad Atlan from his prison. The undead warlord is rebuilding his army one tribe at a time, and his scouting parties of ogres and trolls already cross the Hrygangur Peaks to massacre bearfolk communities.

## Life in the Kingdom

Stout walls surround Björnrike's scattered communities, and travelers keep close guard at night: dark beasts, trolls, fey, and stranger things take the unwary. Most communities are a dozen or so families, and generations of intermarriage bind them tightly. The bearfolk work hard and spend their leisure hunting, drinking, and brawling. They are not overly welcoming, but they show newcomers respect and courtesy until the moment they no longer deserve either.

Hives of ferocious bees guard the kingdom, and nearly every settlement has at least one. They swarm anyone who threatens it by day. Locals eat so much honey that they smell of it, and the hives leave them alone.

The rivers run thick with steelbelly trout and spined pike, and in summer the fields drone with grand honeybees. Bearfolk breeders raise fine horses. Cherry orchards supply Bjeornheim's famous sweet cherry wine and cherry liqueur.

## The Bear King and Queen

Mesikämmen is an immortal fey lord. He usually appears as a bearfolk with brown and silver fur, and sometimes as a huge bearded human in worn hunting leathers, and in either form he wears a crown of holly leaves. He is brash, short-tempered, and self-indulgent, gruff and coarse on the surface but warm and fiercely protective beneath, which sometimes leads him to act without thinking. In better times he and his retinue spent their days hunting in the hills, brawling, and reveling. He has never cared much for talk of omens, Fimbulwinter, or Ragnarok. Now his mate and cub are all he thinks about.

Kingship is decided by yearly challenges, open in theory to anyone. The bearfolk who compete treat it as a chance to prove their worth to their king, not a real attempt at the throne. Mesikämmen doesn't hold back, and he has beaten every challenger since the founding.

Queen Yohana is the dignity and calm beside her husband's roar: a mature, golden-furred bearfolk druid in simple robes of fine cloth, with a silver circlet set with carved gemstone bees. She is wise, thoughtful, and a shrewd negotiator. She was slow to accept the king, but she loves his great heart and how he dotes on their child. She keeps Beeulf Hall in Bjeornheim as well as a seat at the capital.

## Places of Note

| Gloaming Crag | The capital, a formidable fortress on a massive outcrop of granite. Within are expansive dining chambers, festive mead halls, and tapestry-lined, saffron-hued corridors, where the king and queen rule a court of bear jarls, witches, and oracles. The crag's interior is a warren of caves and passages built from ancient dwarven tunnels; guardians, traps, and wards protect its prison, treasury, and other secrets. Lately it buzzes with warriors in training, ratatosk messengers, and smiths forging arms. |
| Bjeornheim | The second city and the most cosmopolitan, a safe and profitable port behind high walls, where the guardian bees began and where the largest hives still are. Traders from across the North, the south, and the far east come for honey, fish, and lumber, or to worship in the golden Temple of Hive and Flower, sacred to [[Yarila & Porevit\|Freyr and Freyja]]. Its brewers make the finest mead and ale in the North. |
| Dalrstadr | A fortified farming town in the wide valley between the kingdom's two rivers, ruled by the old werekin druid Jarl Vantir Wolfhide. It grows much of the kingdom's food. A family of bulettes has been plaguing it, and scouts tracked them to ancient tunnels beneath the town that are not dwarven work. |
| Alfrgan Gap | A secluded, circular valley at the foot of the Hrygangur Peaks, choked with oak and bramble, hiding ruins left when the elven courts withdrew. Few treasure hunters return. Those who do speak of plants and beasts that attack at the bidding of spectral elven knights. |
| Hrygangur Peaks | The eastern border, up to 7,000 feet high, home to pockets of bearfolk, werekin, and stubborn fey. Old dwarven forts line the ridges, ruined in the Mountain Wars or abandoned to monsters and bandits. |
| The Icewood | Issvaedis, a forest of pine and oak frozen year-round. A deep stone well at its heart breathes freezing fog called the Whisper of [[Boreas (North Wind)\|Boreas]]. Garmr, frostveils, and thuellai haunt it. The bearfolk and werekin of Galdby, on its edge, go in for pelts and enchanted ice. |
| Moonrise Lodge | A fortified lodge in the north that shelters werewolves cured of their curse. Raiders from Vargrike attack it often. |
| Skass Moors | Barren, windswept hills north of Bjeornheim, an open road for Vargrike's raiders. Hag covens gather in hilltop ruins at auspicious times for rituals and prophecy, or so they claim. |
