Where the shadows of the Reaching Mountains fall across boreal groves and rolling taiga lies Vargrike, the Kingdom of the Wolf, where the lupine reigns supreme. Its hills echo with howls raised to [[Fenris]], the Great Wolf, and its shrouded forests see bloody rites. Outsiders call it a cursed wilderness overrun by werewolves, worgs, winter wolves, and [[Werekin|werekin]], but the truth of Vargrike is as hard to pin down as the aurora. Its banner is a blue wolf's head on golden ermine.

Vargrike covers 65,000 square miles of wild land with few settlements and little farming. The Tyskr River marks its border with [[Trollheim]], and the Ulfsorr River and the mountains its border with [[Björnrike]]. The land teems with elk, reindeer, moose, and deer. Saber-toothed tigers compete for the hunting, polar bears prowl the high peaks, rocs and giant eagles snatch the unwary, and young giants, trolls, and ogres come down from the peaks to test themselves against the pack warriors. Tall oak and spruce, iron and copper in the hills, and gold, labradorite, and gems in the mountains lie mostly untouched. Woe to trespassers, though: the only law here is tooth and fang.

## The Packs

Lupine packs of both four-legged and two-legged folk roam the taiga, hills, and forests, squabbling over territory and prey. Other peoples are tolerated if they are strong and eaten if they are not. Kindly wolves may keep "unfurred" folk as pets; most treat them as cattle. [[Humans]] and other non-lupine folk can be minor but legitimate pack members, alongside lycanthropes, werekin, winter wolves, worgs, and ordinary wolves.

A wolf jarl called an uljar (roughly, "alpha") rules each of the major packs, holding wide lands and guarding the weaker lupine folk in them from monsters and rival packs. Most packs are either all natural-born or all cursed werewolves, and which they are colors their views on spreading the curse. Only the Quickclaw pack takes both.

Leadership here is less about strength than outsiders assume, and more about force of personality, savage determination, and the pack's support. Failure and hardship topple leaders far more often than challenges do. Settlements are organized like wolf packs, by extended family, with everyone working for the group's survival and happily destroying any nearby pack that shows weakness. Raiding parties cross the borders to plunder Trollheim and Björnrike for loot, food, and livestock. Each season a few mammoths cross the Reaching Mountains, usually followed by hill giants or trolls, and the packs celebrate with organized hunts of both.

## The Fey and the Cure

Hidden among twisted trees and secluded dales are elven ruins abandoned in the Great Retreat and even older redoubts from the Vanir War. More and more of them are filling with strange and dangerous fey, who capture two-legged lupine folk and "cure" them, willing or not. The Court of Masks, once the court of the fey lord Reynard, is the center of it: Reynard's trusted agent Count Rauthun leads a mixed company of fey and returned elves from its ruins on a crusade to wipe out the curse of the wolf. The cursed come back cured, but few survive the days after, as old rivals take advantage of their weakness; even those who love them often kill them out of pity or disgust. Natural-born lycanthropes and werekin who are taken never come back.

Word of the cure has spread beyond Vargrike, and newly made lycanthropes now flood into the kingdom, along with people hoping to steal its secret. The furious Varulfur has decided it is time to purge her land of the fey.

## Wargaz the Cruel

Wargaz, Varulfur ("alpha of alphas") of Vargrike, is an ancient werewolf with gray-white fur scarred by countless challenges. She prefers her hybrid form, armored in a bloodstained coat of plate made from mithral and remorhaz hide. In human form she is a wrinkled but tall and proud woman with long gray hair. Her only mark of rank is a thin silver circlet, worn despite the pain it causes her; the puckered scars it leaves are a testament to her strength. Six hundred years ago she brought the scattered packs of wolves and lycanthropes in the northeast under her rule and crowned herself queen of the lycan folk.

She is canny, treacherous, and often violent, and manages the packs' dangerous politics by setting them against each other or against Vargrike's neighbors. She seems savage and barely restrained at first meeting; those who survive it find a shrewd mind always calculating. Her exact age is unknown, and she has outlived a thousand mates and rivals. Some whisper she is a demigod daughter of Fenris, and her guard of monstrous wolves, the moon hounds, twice the size of worgs, are said to be signs of his favor. She does nothing to discourage the rumors. Lately the uljar grumble that her failure to deal with the fey shows she is growing old or afraid, but the common folk still hold her in near-mythical reverence, or at least great fear.

Legend says a fanged cave there opens on a trapped labyrinth guarded by divine servants of [[Wotan]], and at its heart Fenris sleeps in a colossal cavern, bound by the gold links of the chain Gleipnir. Most Vargrikers won't even talk about where it is. |
| Drakla | A small mountainside mining town of iron, silver, and mithril, one of Vargrike's few sources of metal. Most miners are dwarves or werekin unafraid of silver. The Wildsong pack protects it; the Storm Howlers raid it often. |
| Grim's Hollow | The vast stump of a severed [[Yggdrasil\|World Tree]] alone on the tundra. Young worgs and winter wolves crawl through its roots as a rite of passage to a cavern far below, where ancestral spirits snarl prophecies into their ears. |
| Korpn | A riverside village of werekin and trollkin that owes fealty to no pack. It is ruled by Mother Hyndla, a grumpy red hag, who protects it in exchange for monthly gifts of the blood she needs to live. Rumor says she and Wargaz were once lovers. Four months ago, a dying werekin explorer stumbled into Korpn claiming to have found a prophetic pillar of ice high in the Reaching Mountains, the "frozen oracle"; many have gone looking since, and most return empty-handed or not at all. |
| Pallid Tower | One of three fey-held ruins, a thin helical tower of pale stone on a peak at the mountains' edge with no visible entrance. Its fey all fly, and use their speed to raid lupine settlements. |
| Rokvi | A small fortified village where the Ulfsorr opens into the Klosfjord. Its battle commander, Tvor Helskarn, a nightgarm, leads regular raids into Björnrike. |
| Runewell | A fey ruin at the end of a secluded valley: a nine-level stepwell descending to a deep pool of cerulean water that never freezes, with rooms and passages opening off each level. Its fey favor water. |
| Tursa | A walled fishing village on the coast, nominally a neutral port for ships sailing the Swive, with skilled shipwrights, fresh water, and supplies. |
| Yfirgefin, the Forsaken Forest | A dense, primeval wood of oak and ash across southern Vargrike. The cursed Bloodtide and Verdant Fang packs hunt each other in it and raid across the border into Björnrike. Both fear the fey ruin at its heart. |
