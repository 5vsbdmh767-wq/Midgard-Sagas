North of the Nieder Strait, life is hard. Running out of firewood isn't a cold night, it's a choice between freezing and walking into a dark forest full of things that eat people. Everyone from king to thrall works every day to survive, and anyone who won't work is treated as a danger to everyone else. No central authority takes taxes or a cut of anyone's loot, and none reins in monsters or bullying jarls either. When trolls or a jarl's huskarls come, each family and its friends decide whether to fight or run. It is also, everyone agrees, breathtakingly beautiful: frost glitters like diamonds in the morning sun, and waterfalls thunder down cliffs through mist and rainbows.

Most [[Northlanders]] are karls, free farmers, craftsmen, and part-time raiders and traders who answer to no one but their own oaths. Above them are jarls and kings, measured by followers, ships, and land rather than blood. Below them are thralls, though good thralls are treated as family and often freed. [[Humans]], dwarves, giants, trollkin, and the rest share much the same customs of honor, hospitality, feasting, the althing assembly, wergild, duels, and the reaver raid.

Kingdoms in the North are small and many. It seems every jarl with a long hall and a few ships calls himself a king, and few kingdoms last more than a generation or two. The ancient kingdoms of Aurvang, Issedon, and Nordheim are gone, along with the gates to Valhalla and the Storm Court that once stood here. Domovogrod, the Silver Mountain Kingdom east of the Strait, has recently fallen to giants (see [[Rothenian Plain]]), and the giants are marching.

## Life in the North

### The Reaver Mentality

Southerners think the "reaver mentality" is about raiding and pillaging. To Northlanders it is a way of life framed by honor, sharpened by fate, and shaped by hardship. The Reaving and the slow cooling of the climate cost the North much of its farmland, and raiding became less about plunder than about the survival of clan, family, or felag. Raiding is not a crime but part of the fabric of society, and a successful reaver is admired for courage, cleverness, and generosity with the spoils. Defending one's kin is just as honorable. Time has honed this into a handful of sayings:

- Express your honor through bold deeds, loyalty, and matching beliefs with action.
- Face the odds with acceptance, a grin, and a bloody blade.
- Earn your reputation through deeds, not by blood or bought with coin.
- Fight with courage and never break an oath.
- Treat guests as family.
- Ensure you die well and earn a place in Valhalla, for fate is already written.

### Fellowship and Rank

Outside family and clan, the strongest bond is the felag, meaning "fellowship" or "partnership," tied to the power of the Fé rune, which stands for the division of wealth. Felag oath-takers swear loyalty to their lord and fellow warriors in a sacred bond that sets the felag's aims, its right to depose and elect leaders, and the fair division of spoils.

Social rank in the North is fluid and earned. Karls who directly serve a lord are huskarls, "karls of the house," the lord's allies, officers, and bodyguards. A wise lord is free with gifts and chances for glory, or karls look for another patron; out on the cold margins, the blandishments of [[Loki]], [[Boreas (North Wind)|Boreas]], giants, or trollkin sometimes win them over. Kingship is not especially sacred. Some rulers claim descent from a hero or god, now and then with real divine favor behind it, but success is the only true measure of nobility, rivals and pretenders are never lacking, and successions are always disputed.

### Honor and Reputation

Reputation is everything. Warriors tell tales of old comrades, skalds sing of deeds valorous and vile, and everyone from karl to king trades news and gossip. A good name is all that remains after death, so Northlanders don't just want to do the right thing, they want to be seen doing it, and dishonor is hard to shed. Honor isn't goodness: wicked reavers can be honorable, and some good Northlanders are called nithingr, honorless, for abandoning the North's crueler customs. An honorable person is generous with gifts and hospitality, fair in judgment, and fearless in battle, and also clever and witty, able to recite poetry, dodge the tempers of the gods, and be thought cunning by friend and foe. Northlanders take life stoically, calm in the worst of times.

Optional rules for tracking a character's renown are on the [[Reputation]] page.

### Fate and Luck

[[The Norns]] weave destiny for gods and mortals alike, and none escape their doom. Baldur's dreams foretell his death, and [[Wotan]] knows he will die in the jaws of the Fenris Wolf. For mortals, fate is a series of fixed events from birth to death, the great turns of a life, but no one knows what their own fate holds, so Northlanders seldom worry about it: why fret over what is already written? They don't believe in coincidence. When old friends or enemies meet, they nod knowingly at their destined reunion, and when a ship is lost or a companion dies, it was meant to be. Good people struggle on with cheerful resignation and hope tomorrow's thread is kinder.

Luck fills the gaps between fate's fixed points and shapes how they come to pass. A warlord fated to fall in battle in her 44th year will fall; whether as a common warrior, at the head of her hand-picked jarl's guard, cut down while fleeing, or after great deeds that earn her Valhalla, is a matter of luck. Luck isn't random. It is tied to fate and to one's own deeds, growing with honor and reputation and shrinking with cowardice, betrayal, and vile acts. Northlanders ally themselves with lucky people as signposts of fate and keep away from unlucky people and things.

This acceptance is the root of the Northlanders' fierce reputation. Warriors know they cannot escape their death, so they charge into danger and try to make that death worthy of Valhalla. A heroic end is a fine outcome, and many heroes would not want to come back from it. The dishonorable go to Niflheim, Hel's hall, a prison of sorts, though Hel is always open to a bargain. Returning from the dead isn't cheating fate; it just means one wasn't fated to die then.

### Seers

Outside the usual social order are the seers, blessed or cursed with the gift of prophecy. With rituals, runes, and substances eaten or inhaled, they glimpse the Norns' weaving. What they foretell is never clear, only a hint or a guide, and never guaranteed. Prophecy in the North isn't a way to escape fate but a way to find one's way toward it. Seers live apart, in caves, lonely cabins, or ruins, partly by choice and partly because others shun them, for the path brings false visions, hallucinations, and instability, the price the Norns charge for a glimpse. Communities value and fear them in equal measure, and it is accepted that harming a seer risks the Norns' anger; even mindless monsters seem to leave them alone. A reading is a two-hour ritual, and a seer usually asks hacksilver or goods worth your level times your proficiency bonus in gold pieces, more for an important or accurate reading.

Optional rules for seers' prophecies and omens are on the [[Prophecies and Omens]] page.

### Feasting and Hospitality

The communal meal is a cornerstone of Northern life, from a farmer eating with family and hired hands to a reaver captain hosting her shield-maidens and huskarls or a king's great festival. At the feast table warriors learn one another, settle questions of status, and build the bonds a warband needs to survive; feasts also mark harvests and holy days, praise the gods, and bind families and communities. News is shared, gossip spread, and every kind of deal struck, from betrothals to barter to raids.

Hospitality is one of honor's greatest duties. A warm hearth, a hearty meal, and stout walls are the only shelter from the dark and dangerous wild, and guests bring news. How someone behaves as guest or host is a telling mark of character. Guests repay their host not with coin but by behaving, defending the host from attack, and giving gifts or help while under the host's roof. Even deadly enemies and monsters keep the laws of hospitality, or at least their letter: attacking your host is a great dishonor, but it's perfectly fine to take your leave and ambush them outside town.

Ale and mead are served from vast open barrels and carried to drinkers seated by reputation, the favored nearest the lord of the hall on a thronelike chair (two thrones, for married rulers). Guests' weapons are kept nearby but not allowed at the table, where they'd tempt drunken diners to break hospitality and shame everyone. Each people has its quirks: jotun see nothing unfair in challenging guests to giant-sized games of strength, kobold althings are notorious for backstabbing politics, and what's served at a troll feast may not suit everyone, though it's still rude not to eat.

### The Althing

Outlanders are often surprised by the North's democratic ways. Families and neighbors settle local matters themselves, but every region holds a regular althing, part court and part trade fair, where Northlanders settle quarrels, swear peace or war, make alliances, celebrate great deeds, and invoke ancient laws. A council of wise law-speakers and jarls oversees the debates and tries to broker settlements, but all discussion is public and every free person may speak. Local lords and bullies decide much in the North, but big decisions need public approval, though intimidation and backstabbing go on behind the scenes. The law-speakers rule constantly on hunting and grazing rights, unpaid debts, stolen property and cattle, and even kidnapping and murder. When no settlement can be reached, the injured rely on the duel or declare a feud.

Families bring thralls, cloth, fine weapons, salt, wax, hides, raw materials, and handmade goods to trade, and artisans, rune masters, and wizards come looking for customers; in a land with few cities, the althing is the best chance to buy exotic or magic goods. Distant kings and jarls sometimes send warriors and silver-tongued bards to look after their interests, and locals despise them; it isn't unusual for such an emissary's head to be sent home as a message that the community runs its own affairs. That, and duels, are the only violence allowed, though most consider it a poor althing if fewer than three or four people travel the Hel-road. Althings meet at traditional places: a jarl's longhouse, a sacred glade, a blessed rock, a World Tree, or other hallowed ground, often on borders so rival regions can keep apart between debates. Divine afflictions and public condemnation fall on anyone who breaks the althing's truce.

### Wealth and Trade

Gold is rare in the North and has a reputation for provoking men to violence and greed. Silver is common, and most Northern coins are silver, often worth five or ten Southern silver pieces. Dwarves in particular would rather make valuable things than coins and are easily offended by an offer of coin, and all Northerners prefer substantial, beautiful treasures worthy of a song. Hacksilver, simple rings and armbands broken into pieces for smaller sums, is common currency, and most traders judge coins and hacksilver by weight. Almost all trade is face-to-face barter, with small talk, haggling, and ale to loosen tongues and purse strings. Most commoners aren't paid wages but given hospitality and gifts in return for their work, though it is customary to reward adventurers for their deeds.

### Justice, Duels, and Feuds

Few acts count as crimes in the North, mostly theft and insults to someone's good name, and justice means compensation or avoiding a feud, not some abstract right and wrong. Unless the crime was especially heinous, such as treachery, wrongdoers usually aren't treated as criminals, even by their victims. The usual penalty is wergild, a fine paid to the victim or their family, traditionally in silver rings or hacksilver, after which the victim and their kin swear to let the matter drop. An insult to a family's honor must be answered, however many generations it takes, though such grudges are often set aside for a raid or a common cause. A jarl's word and the holmganga take the place of advocates and juries; minor crimes are punished with servitude or imprisonment, major ones with ritual scarring or death.

Questioning someone's honor, accusing them of a crime or of cowardice, or falsely claiming credit for great deeds is asking for a fight. Sometimes it is settled by a [[Flyting and Kennings|flyting]], a contest of insults; serious matters are settled by [[Reputation#Holmganga (Dueling)|holmganga]], a bloody duel. A duel's outcome on the Isle of Swords is never questioned (see [[Legendary Places of the North#Islands of the North|Islands of the North]]).

When debate and holmganga fail, or no one wants them, a feud begins. Family, neighbors, and friends are expected to avenge the victim by inflicting the same harm on the wrongdoer and their kin, sometimes from a sense of justice, more often as an excuse for robbery and murder. Feuds spiral as each side calls in debts, and can last generations, until one side flees, is destroyed, or (rarely) both grow tired of bloodshed. Both sides court adventurers, and a guest's duty to their host or even breaking up a brawl can drag an outsider into a feud, sometimes on the wrong side.

Outlaws, "outside the law," can be killed without wergild or feud. Their families should treat them as dead, and strangers should treat them like a wolf at the door. Outlawry may be for life or for a set time, until a year passes or the ruler who declared it dies, and it applies only in the outlaw's own althing or kingdom; neighbors may ignore or even welcome them. It carries little stigma by itself (it's an occupational hazard for adventurers), though the deed behind it may.

### Boreal Dark and Midnight Sun

Elsewhere, weather is an inconvenience. In the North, the wind cuts like a blade, blizzards blind, snowdrifts cut farms and towns off for months, and the cold turns blood to ice, and since the Reaving the land has grown steadily colder. In winter the sun sinks lower and lower until it barely shows above the horizon, the grim time called the Boreal Dark. The farther north, the longer it lasts: the valleys of Jotunheim go about two months without true daylight, half of that in perpetual night, and the Bleak Expanse longer still. In summer it reverses, and the days of the Midnight Sun, sacred to [[Lada|Baldur]], bring nights no darker than twilight and welcome relief from the horrors of the dark.

## Peoples of the North

The North has always been home to many peoples, and nearly all of them follow Northern ways rather than customs of their own. The true measure of a person here is courage, truthfulness, and honor, along with hard work, perseverance, and self-reliance, and hospitality to travelers and kin is a point of pride.

The major peoples, with long histories here and realms or large populations of their own, are the [[Bearfolk|bearfolk]] of Björnrike, the reaver [[Dwarves|dwarves]] (including the rare Baudsmidr, who seldom leave the hearts of the dwarven holds), [[Giantkin|giantkin]], [[Humans|humans]] (pale [[Northlanders]] and the olive-skinned Plainsfolk, descended from immigrants from the eastern steppes), [[Trollkin|trollkin]], and [[Werekin|werekin]], who are especially common in Vargrike.

Smaller peoples include [[Beastkin|beastkin]] (found mostly in Björnrike, Skaldholm, and, oddly, Thursrike), [[Elves|elves]] and [[Elfmarked|elfmarked]] (the hidden alfar who stayed to guard their holy sites, the wild ice elves or [[Frostfell Elves]], and visitors from the south), [[Gnolls|gnolls]] of the tundra, [[Goblins|goblins]] (many of them servants of worgs and nightgarms), winterfolk [[Halflings|halflings]], [[Kobolds|kobolds]] (once enslaved by the reaver dwarves, many of them followers of Loki), [[Ratatosk|ratatosk]], and [[Ravenfolk|ravenfolk]]. Goliaths, orcs, and tieflings are rare, and the locals usually just call goliaths and orcs "trollkin." Fey are everywhere: ice maidens in the high frozen places, hags in the cold bogs, rum gremlins on the docks and under mead halls. Giants of every kind fill [[Jotunheim]], and ogres and trolls live in great numbers from sea to swamp to ice.

## History of the North

This account follows the Bard's Chronology taught in the schools of [[Skaldholm]].

### The First Age

- Aurgelmir, the first giant, forms from the Void. Gods and giants are born from him, and he makes a world for his children.
- The gods fight the giants for the first world. When Aurgelmir sides with the giants, the gods slay him and break the branch joining the mortal world to [[Yggdrasil]]. The first world is destroyed, and only two of Aurgelmir's firstborn and some lesser giants, such as the jotun, survive.
- The gods make a new world from Aurgelmir's corpse and hang it on Yggdrasil's broken branch.

### The Vanir War

- The Vanir, gods of nature, war against the Aesir, gods of glory, and dwarves and elves are made to fill their armies. [[Wotan]] brokers a truce, and the two become one pantheon in Asgard.
- The fey lord Reynard abandons his Court of Masks, in what is now Vargrike, and flees back to the fey realms.
- The jotun build Sengajordensblod around the Heartstone, thought to be Aurgelmir's heart. Small wars break out between the gods and the jotun lords, and the great redoubts of Drekholl, Jarnskul, Kjothall, and Kupparsheim rise.
- The jotun lords exile most stone giants from the North for a crime they refuse to name. Most flee south; a few clans stay.
- [[Bengta]] the Bear Maiden turns some northern bears into the first bearfolk.

### The Rise of the Dwarves

- Tanserhall and the first dwarven holds are founded, the heart of an empire across what is now the Nieder Strait. The dwarves dominate the elves or push them south, the giant clans flee north, werewolves are penned in the empire's northeast, and kobolds are pressed into the mines.
- The dwarves drive the fire giant jarls from Bragafjall and found Ishamagard, and a clan of dwarven druids begins tending the mountain's slopes.
- The goddess [[Gullveig]] teaches the cloud giants cloud weaving and helps them build Bjarmia.
- Elves plant a branch of Yggdrasil in mortal soil, the first World Tree, and others follow in distant lands.
- The giant goddess Mengloth imprisons herself in a hollow mountain and won't say why.
- The fey lord Mesikämmen arrives by a World Tree and settles among the bearfolk.
- Dwarven vampire lords secretly take control of the hold of Glymshall.
- [[Thor]] destroys the jotun hold of Gatusalr to steal a pair of magic goats.

### The Reaving

- High King Vaknir Grathegn crafts the Dominion Orb, binding his empire to his bloodline, and is assassinated soon after. The backlash from his sudden death shatters the orb and sends shockwaves through the land bound to it.
- The ground shatters and the Uttermost Sea rushes in over most of the empire. Only Tanserhall survives intact; the other holds above water are mostly abandoned.
- The surviving dwarves suffer decades of hunger and isolation that nearly destroy their society.
- Jarl Ragnr gathers the survivors of Glymshall and founds [[Wolfheim]] through an alliance with the winter wolves and worgs.
- Wotan's Promise: Wotan and [[Volund]] come to the Jarl of Tanserhall in dreams, offering Valhalla to dwarves who prove courageous and valorous.

### The War of Stone and Tree

- The surviving dwarven clans blame the elves for the empire's fall and declare war on them. The elves flee south, leaving only a few hidden communities.
- Dwarven rebuilding is slow, and humans settle the fjords and bays of the southern coast.

### 3,000 to 2,001 Years Ago

- History begins to be recorded, first in oral tales and epic poems, later in written runes.
- The Age of Upheaval (3,000 years ago): an ancient island kingdom far to the southwest sinks, and tidal waves ravage the North's coasts.
- Short summers and long winters drive humans to shelter with the dwarves in exchange for work in the mines and farms, and dwarven war bands raid other peoples for workers, earning the name "reaver dwarves," which they embrace.
- Stannasgard, Thrumagard, Aurvang, and Nordheim are founded (2,750 years ago).
- Hyperborea appears in the far north, its circular wall impervious to giants and gods alike.

### 2,000 to 1,501 Years Ago

- Boreas, the primordial spirit of the North Wind, arrives in the North (2,000 years ago), exiled by the other three winds for his hatred of mortals and their upstart gods.
- Elves from the [[Silendora|Summer Lands]] settle in the south, stirring hope among the North's scattered elves and new wars with the dwarven holds.
- The Age of Ice (1,750 years ago): Boreas is reborn as a god, bent on destroying his fellow winds and the gods of mortals and covering the world in ice. The North slowly cools, the glaciers creep south, and giants follow them, with ogres and trolls raiding ahead.

### 1,500 to 1,001 Years Ago

- Birth of the Reavers (1,500 years ago): after 250 years of falling temperatures, three brutal winters in a row bring famine. Dwarven longships go raiding for supplies, and survival and Wotan's Promise together become the Reaver's Way.
- Thrumagard is lost to trolls and other monsters.
- The Vanguard Kingdoms (1,250 years ago): Aurvang and Nordheim ally with Issedon, aided by Hyperborean wizards, and build the Wall across the northern passes.
- Boreas unleashes the living glaciers.

### 1,000 to 501 Years Ago

- Something rises from the depths and destroys Nordheim from within.
- The Fall of the Wall (900 years ago): the Vanguard Kingdoms fall to the ice. Aurvang and Issedon are destroyed, and Issedon's survivors rally at Noatun and found [[Trollheim]].
- The Swive (850 years ago): Boreas tricks the old sea god Aegir into flooding the plains between Björnrike and Huldramose. Njord, Noatun's archmage protector, holds back the waters while humans and dwarves labor 81 days on an enchanted sea wall. Most of the Bragafjall druids die raising their mountain above the flood, creating the island of Skaldholm. Wotan imprisons Aegir, and Njord rises to become the new god of the seas.
- Followers of Bragi settle [[Skaldholm]] (825 years ago).
- The werewolf warlord Wargaz the Cruel unites the packs of the northeast and crowns herself queen of [[Vargrike]] (600 years ago).

### 500 to 101 Years Ago

- The Great Retreat (500 years ago): the elves withdraw to the Summer Lands, abandoning their Northern courts, including the Moon Palace.
- A quarrel between Volund and Thor over the empty elven lands ends with Volund's faithful dwarves leaving for the south; those who remain keep both faiths and the Reaver's Way.
- The Mountain Wars (450 years ago): the oni lich Dakhad Atlan invades the bearfolk lands west of the Tunturi Mountains.
- Mesikämmen defeats Dakhad Atlan and becomes king of the new realm of [[Björnrike]] (400 years ago).
- The dwarven explorer Halva Wayseeker vanishes in the far north, and songs of her adventures become hugely popular.
- Juri Jensson of Trollheim invents square-rigged sails, giving Northern longships great speed.
- Alaric the Mithral-Dwarf and the Band of the Thunder Hammer retake [[Thrumagard]] and open it to humans, halflings, and ravenfolk as equals (150 years ago).

### 100 to 11 Years Ago

- The dwarven warlord Ottar Harjaz seizes parts of Trollheim and Huldramose, and his realm shatters when the Bear King kills him during his failed invasion of Björnrike.
- The vaettir Liv Iron-Eyes rises from the sea to save the giantkin of Eikinstad (about 25 years ago).
- Yohana Honeyhair becomes Queen of Bjeornheim (20 years ago).
- Bloody Revna, a murderous vaettir, begins attacking settlements along the western coast of the Haamu Hills.

### The Last Ten Years

- Elves begin returning from the Summer Lands, and elven scouts are reported in their old Northern outposts and ruined cities (10 years ago).
- Dwarven reavers establish [[The Wolfmark]] and seize the city of Jozht from the [[Blood Kingdom]] (9 years ago).
- Adventurers clear the abandoned fortress of Kupparsheim, which serves for a time as a trading post.
- King Asvaldr of Trollheim leads a great raid south and vanishes in the Nieder Strait (7 years ago).
- More and more fey settle in Vargrike's ancient ruins at the bidding of the fey lord Reynard.
- The thursir and other giants of Thursrike and the western mountains march on the human and dwarven lands, and many trollkin rise to defend them. The jotun Guthmundur Raggnarson seizes Kupparsheim and makes it the capital of [[Thursrike]] (3 years ago).
- The Wolfmark declares itself an independent kingdom.
- King Mesikämmen marries Queen Yohana, and Prince Alrek is born that year. King Uffi elevates Veska Flamehair as Trollheim's first trollkin jarl. A dwarven vampire wizard of ancient Ishamagard wakes from a centuries-long sleep and makes a lair near Blodtindr Pass (2 years ago).
- Blovurr, heir to Wolfheim, dies. Asvaldr returns to Trollheim, saying he escaped captivity in a southern land (1 year ago).

### Today

- The Falling Wall still holds against Boreas, but every year another section topples and lets more of his power into the world.
- Trollheim teeters between the traditionalist jarls led by Asvaldr and King Uffi's progressive supporters.
- A strange illness strikes the guardian bees of Björnrike, and Prince Alrek sickens with them. Queen Yohana sends agents across the North in search of a cure.
