The gods of the [[Northlands]] are mysterious and familiar, slippery and demanding. They have their own goals and needs, and above all they play a game of power and deception among themselves, because gods, once made, can also be unmade. They are the greatest allies of mortals and their greatest foes and betrayers, and they are mostly turned inward on their own rivalries, barely hearing mortal voices. Anyone who walks the path of priest or petitioner should understand this first.

## The First Gods

The story begins in the First Age. Life sprang up in the center of the Void where rime met flame, and that life was Aurgelmir, the first and greatest giant. From him came the titans, the divine jotun, and the eldest of the gods. Aurgelmir made a world for his children, but the gods soon fought the giants for it. When the giants were on the edge of defeat they cried out to their father, and he fought beside them. The gods slew him and cut the branch of [[Yggdrasil]] the world hung from, destroying it. All of Aurgelmir's first offspring died except [[Bergelmir]] and [[Angrboda]], who saved the lesser jotun and the rest of his descendants. The giants have never forgotten the murder, and they still hope to see the gods' dominion ended and their forefather avenged.

The victorious gods made a new world from Aurgelmir's body and hung it on the broken branch: his bones became the earth, his blood the rivers and seas, his teeth the mountains, and his hollow skull the sky. Then they fell to fighting among themselves. The gods of nature and the gods of passion and glory warred for the new realm until [[Wotan]] brokered a truce. The two sides traded hostages, peace returned, and the smaller peoples took their place in the world. As far as the giants are concerned, they are still its true heirs.

## Masks and Mysteries

Gods are not individuals but archetypes, universal forces shaped by faith and place, and their names, forms, and domains change from region to region. The gods of the south are masks of the Northern gods, and the reverse. They are a shifting family of alliances and betrayals, each clinging to divinity in the knowledge that even gods can wither and die. Only a few hold sway in any one place: perhaps five in a city, three in a town, one in a village. Faith here is practical rather than exclusive, and people call on whichever god suits the moment.

## Shades of Gray

Other lands sort their gods into good and dark. The North does not. Wotan seeks knowledge but manipulates friend and foe alike. Thor defends mortals and leaves destruction in his wake. Freyja brings beauty and walks hand in hand with war. Even [[Loki]], destined to begin Ragnarok, has saved the gods as often as he has endangered them. Bound to fate and struggling against it, the gods scheme, betray, love, and wound each other, and none of them thinks twice about using mortals as pawns.

Few [[Northlanders]] devote themselves to a single god. Most hold that all the gods are powerful and best not angered, keep one or two close to their hearts, and pray to local gods while traveling. A human wouldn't think twice about a hasty prayer to Angrboda in the face of some monstrous foe. People revere the gods not for their place in some cosmic balance but because they are the storm, the hunger, the secrets beyond sight, and the power to move the heavens.

## Piety and Sacrifice

To outsiders the Northern gods seem forbidding, bloody, and grim. Northerners take joy in gods who call them to excellence, invite great boasts and greater deeds, and laugh louder than thunder. Most people treat the gods like unruly kings: dangerous if crossed, helpful when bribed or flattered, and thankfully easy to appease with tribute.

Blood is the usual sacrifice. Goats, cattle, or horses usually suffice, but when dire portents demand it, humans, dwarves, or trollkin go beneath the knife. Each god likes offerings made a certain way: Wotan's are hung and pierced by spears, Baldur's are burned, and Thor prefers heads bashed in with a sacred hammer.

Druids, oracles, and witches are all common kinds of priest. Full-time clerics in the southern style are rare and treated more as favored champions than clergy. Most priests have another role too, from jarl or captain to soothsayer, hunter, or smith, and worship happens in feast halls and forges, on battlefields and the decks of longships.

## Minor Gods of the North

A few lesser gods are worshiped in the North, or once were. Shrines to them turn up in some communities, usually kept by particular groups or peoples. The great exception is the Norns: nearly everyone in the North respects the three ladies of fate and prays to them now and then.

### Heimdall

*Eternal Guardian*

Heimdall is one of the major gods of the North, the god of watchfulness and loyalty who guards the Bifrost and the Gates of Asgard. His senses are amazingly keen: he can see a hundred miles in daylight or the darkest night, hear grass growing, and feel the footfall of an ant. He is the patron of the besieged, popular all along the Reaver Coast, and with scouts, lookouts, and those who guard treasuries and grain stores.
