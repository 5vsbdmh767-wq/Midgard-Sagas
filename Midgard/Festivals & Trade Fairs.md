Six festival days fall between the months of the year (see [[Seasonal Calendar]]), and most of Midgard keeps them in some form. Smaller festivals belong to one god, one region, or one bad night of the year. From summer until harvest, the free cities and big market towns also hold trade fairs, and merchants travel for weeks to reach the ones that matter to them.

The roads are never safe. Merchants move goods by mule train, barge, and wagon from spring to late fall, and nobody sensible does it without guards. Wagon masters are picky about who they hire, and a new hand usually starts out as a scout, an archer, or the one who cranks the crossbows.

##### Major Festivals
| Festival | When | What Happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New Year's Dawn | First sign of spring, Springmelt | A procession to the town center before dawn. When the sun clears the horizon, everyone bangs pots and rings bells to scare off bad luck. [[Lada]]'s light (or a wizard's fireworks) drives away evil spirits. Families trade gifts. |
| Mustering Days | Springmelt or Sowing, declared by the local priest of Thor, [[Perun]], or [[Mavros]] | Three days that open the campaign season: one to travel, one to swear oaths and sign contracts, one for blessings before the march. Young warriors fight Mustering Bouts to crown a "Chosen of Mavros." Big in the [[Magdar Kingdom]]. |
| Rites of Spring | Spring equinox, Thunders | Priests of [[Yarila & Porevit]] and village elders bless the fields and flocks. The second day is the Day of Misrule, when a child is crowned king or high priest for the day and the adults have to go along with it. |
| Crown Festival | Goldflower | Each nation honors its ruler. [[Blood Kingdom\|Morgau]] celebrates [[King Lucan]]'s coronation. The [[Magdar]] queen hands out bread and olives at her palace. |
| Summer Festival | Summer solstice, High Summer | A day of trade, oaths, betrothals, and horse dealing at a crossroads or fairground. Priests of [[Volund]] bless anvils and livestock, hold wagers, and draw up marriage contracts. Trade in [[Zobeck]] stops for the day. |
| Harvest Dances | After the harvest, Redleaf | Dancing by moonlight, gambling, foot races, music. Everything but taverns, inns, and shrines closes. |
| Lantern Festival | A month after the winter solstice, Snowfall | Parades of lanterns and candles to call back the sun, with prayers to Lada and Khors. The devout keep vigil until dawn. |

##### Minor Festivals
| Festival | When | What Happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slaughter Festival | Harvest Tide | Also called the Brewer's Festival. Autumn butchering and the first ale of the season. |
| Ghost Night | First new moon of winter | Also called Hag Night. A goblin festival of masks and candle offerings to the dead. |
| Night of Open Roads | First winter moon | A night for speaking with ancestors and the dead, and an easy night to slip into [[The Shadow Realm]]. Necromancers love it. |
| Axe Festival | A red lunar eclipse | Rare. A night for offerings to [[Hecate]], or to [[Marena]] for those with darker tastes. Some witches swear it's the best night to craft magic items. |
| [[Björnrike\|Bear King]]'s Challenge | Yearly, in the north | Contests of strength and wit. The winner is crowned King of Bears, and some say becomes one of the [[Bearfolk\|bearfolk]]. |

##### Trade Fairs
| Place | Month | Known For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Zobeck]], Midsummer Fair | Midsummer | Clockwork, fine weaving, and jewelry. The shadow fey have started selling moonsteel and memory philters here, and the Griffon Knights sometimes auction a spare griffon egg. |
| [[Krakovar]], Farmer's Fair | Midsummer | Amber, fish, horses, honey, and blood coins. Zobeck's old rival fair, now under [[Blood Kingdom\|Morgau]]'s rule. |
| Cronepisht, Horse Trade | High Summer | Horses, oxen, and sheep, plus winter wolves, dogmoles, and stranger beasts. |
| Kubourg Cantonal, Dwarfmoot | Goldflower | Dwarven ironwork, cheese, wool, mercenary hiring, and court rulings (see [[Customs of the Crossroads]]). |
| [[Melana]], Armoring Fair | Mustering | Armor, knives, and weapons. The whole city smells of oil and iron for days. |
| Zigistad, Wine Week | Goldflower | Wine and metal ingots. Rowdy. [[Centaurs]] are not welcome. |
| Salzbach, Salt Fair | Redleaf | Salt and dyed leather. Quiet, with a lot of money changing hands. |
| [[Magocracy of Allain\|Maillon]], Alchemist's Gatherum | Harvest Tide | Leftover potions sold off before winter. Some are remarkable and many are fakes. |
| [[Trombei]], Golden Ceres Fair | Last Leaf | Beer and horses, and many nights of drinking. |
| [[Friula]], Scribe's Fair | Sowing | Books, scrolls, dyes, pearls, and vellum. |
| [[Triolo]], Fleet Fair | Thunders | Sailcloth, rope, glass, and spice. |
| [[Capleon]], Baron's Trade Fair | Goldflower | Fish, tea, salt, and spice. Also known for spies and assassinations. |
| [[Bourgund]], Rose Knight's Emporium | Redleaf | Perfume, silk, lace, fey magic, and duels. Bandits wait outside the borders. |
| [[Magocracy of Allain\|Bemmea]], Gathering of [[Thoth-Hermes]] | Midwinter | Arcana, cantrips, and familiars. A fair for wizards and bookbinders. |
| Birch Queen's Fair | Last Leaf | Held somewhere in the [[Arbonesse]]. Sells cloaks, cantrips, herbs, memories, skills, and luck. The shadow fey often attend. |
