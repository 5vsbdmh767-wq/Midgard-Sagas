The three Free Cities were founded by independent-minded [[Nurians]] out in the deserts beyond [[Nuria Natal]], to spread the River Kingdom's reach and guard its frontiers. They have done well enough on their own that Nuria finds them more useful as friends than as vassals, and they stay on good terms with it.

Siwal is the largest town of the Sarklan Desert, an oasis city of basalt minarets and gardens that climb even its outer walls. Sandships glide in through tall gates to dock at the thorny wharves of the Iron Spine, camel caravans come through the Camel Gate, and the city is famous for its dancers. Its huge necropolis north of town is avoided after dark, for good reason.

Saph-Saph fills a long hollow of springs and date groves on the [[Tamasheq]] Trail. Its fortress-city holds the white marble temple school of Aten, and nearby the Oracle of the Sun has told the fortunes of Nurian lords for a thousand years. Makuria, on the Lotus Trail to the south, lives by hiring out guards and by playing Nuria and the Kushite princes against each other. Its queen is a skilled schemer, and the Order of Horus keeps cavalry and heru warriors there.

Deep in the Crescent Desert lies the hidden green valley of Golden Ulthar, the ruin of a lost civilization, where a lonely sphinx priestess still keeps its last temple. See [[Dominion of the Wind Lords#Golden Ulthar|Golden Ulthar]].

## The Free Cities and Nuria

Saph-Saph lies west of the river in the Crescent Desert, Siwal east of it in the Sarklan Desert, and Makuria to the south. Their merchants run the desert trade routes by sandship and camel and control the flow of goods from distant lands, and that trade enriches Nuria as much as it does them. Even so, there is a small but growing feeling in Nuria Natal that the Free Cities ought to be brought properly back into the fold, and the god-king is said to have his eye on Makuria and Siwal. The cities trade gladly with Nuria and would fight to the end to stay free.

## Siwal, City of Gardens

Siwal is the largest stop on the Mhalmet Road between Per-Bastet and [[Mhalmet]], so rich cargo passes through it every day, and every caravan must bring enough guards to run a gauntlet of raiders and bandits on the way. It is a city of basalt minarets, granite fountains, and marble monuments, built around a natural oasis and kept green by elemental water magic, with gardens on its rooftops, in its parks, and along its walls. Small souks fill its courtyards, and the Grand Souk follows the ebb and flow of each season's trade. For all its opulence, its people keep their treasures behind high walls and heavy gates. Urchins and storytellers fill the streets, temple bells ring all day, and the wailing of mourners drifts in from the necropolis.

Siwal claims its own sultanate because of its sandships. It has only a handful, but they link Saph-Saph, Nuria, Per-Xor, Per-Bastet, and even distant Cindass, Sarkland, and the southern kingdoms, and together with its water priests they keep the city out of the hands of both the [[The Mharoti Empire|Dragon Empire]] and the River Kingdom. Siwal is worth more to everyone as a neutral party than as a tiny vassal.

The Defender of the Faithful, Sultan al-Mamun, rules with his vizier, Akil ibn Khaldun. A hundred troubles beset them, from assassins and sand pirates to the intrigues of djinn and forbidden romance. Commerce comes before everything in the City of Gardens, and as long as the profits keep rolling in, the sultan is inclined to let other trouble right itself. The sultan's latest obsession is his Garden of Delights, filled with rare plants from all over the [[Southlands]] and beyond. The gravebinders and the priests of Anu-Akma, who hold sway over the necropolis, would say it is the Purifier of Souls who truly rules Siwal, and what they ask for rarely fails to happen.

Siwal is famous for dance as other cities are for carpets, books, or steel. The Zahrah al-Zaina are among its finest dancers, the sultan's harem has its own troupe, and less reputable dancers work the caravanserais and the shops near the Iron Spine. The city is also known for its dervishes.

### The Grand Necropolis

Ten thousand heroes lie buried in the Grand Necropolis, a few minutes north of the city, among the graves of ordinary folk of every station, from infants to generals. Visitors pay to view the heroes' tombs, funerals are held daily, and the walls have to be expanded from time to time. Scattered among the graves are the burrows of ghouls, the dank caves of vampires, and the empty places ghosts love.

The gravebinders, four families who pass their secrets down the generations, make the shrouds, embalm and groom the dead, lead the mourning, and consecrate the graves, both to see the dead safely into the afterlife and to keep new undead from rising. Their charms are said to be magical, and they are most effective for those who can pay. Gravebinders are respected and feared. [[Gnolls|Gnoll]] warriors patrol the avenues by day and destroy any undead bold enough to stir. Desert trolls have been the necropolis's gravediggers for centuries, and the troll Golamesh Abu Karim and his nephew Gulgash keep a watch fire burning all night at the Eastern Gate.

The law of the Grand Necropolis is simple. Under the sun, the living reign and may make what arrangements they will. After nightfall the spirits, ghouls, and vampires hold their own court, and the living who dare enter are fair prey. At night the necropolis becomes a true city of the dead, where the lords of the dead fine and punish those who break their laws, and Siwalians stay out after dark by long tradition. The darakhul warrior Wakkil Al-Ghul, who rose from his grave two centuries ago, has long called himself the arbiter of its law. Young people sometimes visit the vampire oracle Tanya Ollevit to trade blood for charms and visions, and a family of gypsosphinxes led by Shafiqa Siham lives high in the tallest crypts.

Lately a self-styled ghul king has risen in the depths of the necropolis and broken its oldest law, letting its servants act at any hour. Ghouls have attacked gravebinders, mourners, and embalmers in daylight, and Wakkil Al-Ghul has been noticeably absent since. Rumor also says Shafiqa Siham has left to find her family a new home.

## Saph-Saph, Shining Jewel of the Sun

Saph-Saph, on the Tamasheq Trail in the Crescent Desert, is often thought of as one city but is really a small state of several settlements spread through a large wadi of long, deep hollows full of springs and date groves. Its people are hardworking and close-knit, and there is little strife between the settlements. Its sultan, Benerib, is an archmage and the Most Generous Patron of the First Temple.

Per-Saph, the greatest of them, is a city-fortress built around the white marble Temple School of Aten, which has trained generations of priests and paladins. The homes of its citizens form the temple's walls, nearly six stories high in places, and in times of danger senior students stand with veteran paladins to defend it. Its people are universally devout. Graduates are encouraged to travel and spread Aten's worship, and the most zealous take it on themselves to hunt down the priests and cults of other gods.

Nearby Saph-Ket is home to the Grand Observatory, where the Oracle of the Sun, a gearforged first servant of Aten, has foretold the destinies of Nurian lords for more than a thousand years. Each night he watches the stars, the moon, and the heavens and dictates to two scribes, one recording his observations and the other his words when he falls into a trance and speaks of events as if he were watching them. His predictions are always true, though usually too small to matter. When one is important, he sends it to the god-king. The original transcripts are kept in a vast, dusty archive beneath the observatory. The best lens grinders and glassblowers from many nations come to Saph-Ket hoping to be chosen to work on its telescopes, and those who are not sell fine glassware and lenses from shops nearby. The grovemaster of Saph-Ket tends a stand of cedars whose wood builds one new sandship each year. The people of Saph-Ket are known as more inquisitive than their devout neighbors in Per-Saph.

Its other notables are Agleed, Most Capable and Wise Shipmaster, a heru; Akhraten, Lord Commander of the First Temple of Aten; Hemada, Keeper of the Fortress Embers; Remitaug, Steward of the Spring of the Sun, a dwarf druid; and Wewanjet, Captain of the Trade Gates.

## Makuria, Citadel of Winged Truth

Makuria, south of Nuria on the Lotus Trail, is the ugliest city in Nuria's orbit. Smoke from its smithies fills the sky, hammers ring endlessly, and soot coats its hard-angled sandstone buildings in gray, so that little grows there. Patrols march its streets in lockstep and punish small crimes as harshly as large ones. Each year from late Harvest Tide to Deep Winter, migrating geese and wading birds blanket its river and banks, and thousands are taken for meat and for the quills that feed Nuria's scribes.

Makuria cares less for trade than its sisters. Its strength is in its soldiers, trained at the Citadel of Winged Truth and counted among the best in the River Kingdom, and in its smiths, the most talented in the land, who make weapons and armor in great quantity. Caravan masters, mercenary captains, and fight-pit owners spend a lot of time in its garrisons and training yards, and every evening many yards become hippodromes for chariot races and showy gladiatorial bouts. The Order of Horus keeps powerful cavalry and heru warriors here, along with scouts, saboteurs, and rabble-rousers who use the will of Horus to keep the trade routes open.

Queen Baktweret is a practiced schemer. For more than a decade she has played the nobles of Nuria against the border princes of Kush, profiting from every skirmish she starts, and she knows Nuria uses her as a pawn against Kushite bandits and gnoll raiders. Would-be rivals for her throne are threatened or persuaded, and when words fail, disposed of in ways that discourage others for a while. She rules from the Palace of Salt and Jade, built of white clay brick and tiled in brilliant green nephrite, where a statue carved from a single piece of jade shows her standing over twelve kneeling gnoll warriors. Her Scimitar Throne overlooks a sunken arena so that she and her favorites can watch blood sports, and gossip says she takes a victorious fighter as her companion each night.

The Steel Fang Circus holds combat three nights a week, pitting the city's best gladiators against one another, against exotic beasts and monsters, and, when Ib Safwat can get one, against dinosaurs. When he books a tyrannosaurus, every seat is full. By contract with the queen, criminals are made to fight in the pit before meeting their punishment.

On the road south of Makuria stands the Rock of Horus, stronghold of the Talons of Horus (see [[Nuria Natal#Orders and Societies|Nuria Natal]]).
