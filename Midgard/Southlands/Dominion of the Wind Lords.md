West of the Crescent Desert lies the Stone Desert, plains of red and black rock running down to the sea, with springs and oases hidden among them. It is ruled by the Wind Lords, ancient beings made from the breath of the first giant. They care little for anything beyond their borders but take a close and personal interest in what happens inside them. Chergui, the East Wind, is the kindest to mortals. Shemral, the West Wind, hates them and wants them gone. Khamsin, the South Wind, swings between the two.

In the Dominion nearly everything in nature has a spirit, awake and with a mind of its own. For eight hundred years those spirits have fought a civil war between the Wind Lords and [[Boreas (North Wind)|Boreas]], the North Wind, and mortals caught in the middle suffer for it. Caravans are struck by angry spirits and whole tribes vanish beneath battling earth elementals.

The Dominion's great days are long behind it. The Moon Kingdom of the minotaurs and the civilization of Golden Ulthar are ruins now, and the desert is home to gnolls, the jinnborn, and the proud nomad [[Tamasheq]] tribes. [[Minotaurs]] from the [[Seven Cities]] have lately begun returning to rebuild their old coastal cities of Cindass and [[Roshgazi]], and four ships of the Lost Fleet of Roshgazi have just come in out of the mist, three hundred years late, with crews that have not aged a day.

## The Lands of Wind and Sand

In the far northwest of the [[Southlands]], the Stone Desert stretches to the sea in sun-baked plains of red and black rock, with none of the crescent dunes found in the other southern deserts. It hides bubbling springs, seasonal waterholes, and scattered oases. To the south the land climbs into the high plateaus at the heart of the Dominion, where more rain lets oak, cedar, and juniper grow on the slopes and in deep canyons, and lions, leopards, bears, and macaques live. Nature spirits dwell in every crag, hilltop, oasis, and stream.

South of Hartani Bay, between the coast and the Chelamite Mountains, lie the Granite Teeth, broken badlands of jagged canyons, spires, and crumbling cliffs. To the southeast the dry, windswept Mukupe Sahel is the border country between the Stone and Crescent deserts. The western Crescent Desert is full of monsters, dune mimics, sand silhouettes, and sandwyrms among them, and some of its oases hold crocodiles or are lures made by miragers and oozasises. East of the Howler's Road ley line lies the hidden Valley of Golden Ulthar, and far to the south, near the Amud and Mbazha mountains, the Crescent Desert is called the Ishmai Desert by its proud sand giants.

The Dominion itself runs more than a thousand miles from the ruins of Jelle-Anda to the borders of Dabu, taking in the Stone Desert and much of the coast. For generations its people and spirits have fiercely guarded a secret world of wanderers, hidden cities, and treasure. That is beginning to change. After a series of visions, the Spider Prophet persuaded the Wind Lords to let foreign traders into the city of Inkabut, and a privileged few are now even allowed into wondrous Kel Azjer.

## The Wind Lords

The Wind Lords are ancient and powerful beings from the earliest ages of the world, made from the breath of the giant Aurgelmir. They have almost no interest in lands beyond their borders but take an active, personal part in the Dominion's affairs.

| Khamsin, the South Wind | The hot wind of the southern wastes, a being of extremes in temperature and temper, whose mood swings between those of the other two. |

Near Kel Azjer, four black towers stand on lofty peaks, one for each Wind Lord. The Tamasheq believe the Wind Lords know everything ever spoken aloud, and that anyone who climbs to the top of a tower will be granted great knowledge. The South, East, and West Winds still dwell in theirs, though they are rarely seen. They receive no visitors, but petitioners may climb as far as the bases to leave gifts. Boreas's broken tower on Kuoma Peak was abandoned at his exile and tempts those hunting obscure lore. To reach its top, one must get past the vicious elementals in the caverns beneath the frost-covered mountain and then defeat the imajaghans of the three Boreas tribes who guard it. It is said that those who can endure the cold at the open top for a night and a day will hear the answers they seek in the howling wind.

## The Spider Prophet

The Spider Prophet holds power in the Dominion second only to the Wind Lords. They are the highest priest of Kwansi, the chaotic desert god, and favored by the nature spirits, who whisper secrets to them from all across the Southlands, so that no one knows more of the Dominion's plots. The prophet is a perplexing figure, described as both young and old, male and female, who always speaks with the same voice from a body covered in swarming spiders. Many worshipers believe the swarm is the true prophet and the body beneath only a vessel. The prophet is old enough to have seen the fall of Roshgazi and Cindass, prepares for the war with the [[The Mharoti Empire|Dragon Empire]] that will almost surely come, and relishes the chaos it will bring. If the Wind Lords go to war, the Spider Prophet will lead the spirits and the Tamasheq into battle.

Thirty-seven years ago the prophet had a vision of Mharoti dragons entering the Dominion disguised as Tamasheq nomads and tearing it apart from within. It has not come true, but Tamasheq and jinnborn suspected of being Mharoti spies have been locked in the dungeons beneath Inkabut ever since, some for decades. Only three have ever been released.

### The Amghid

The Amghid, the Hand of the Spider Prophet, are the closest thing the Dominion has to an army: five thousand foot soldiers shrouded in black turbans and lithams. Their commander, the imajaghan Iskaw ag-Alkher, takes a practical view of her oaths and trains them in stealth and assassination as well as battle. They guard the Dominion's borders, though they spend more time keeping rival tribes and spirits from destroying one another. Their ranks include lightly armed scouts and heavily armored imajaghans, elemental paladins skilled at fighting otherworldly foes, who can call the power of air, earth, fire, or water into their weapons.

## The Nature Spirits

Most things in the Dominion have a spirit bound to them, awakened by the Wind Lords thousands of years ago: spirits of wind, stone, fire, and sea, as aware as any living thing. They answer to the Wind Lords and prefer to deal with mortals through the Tamasheq and jinnborn chiefs and the imajaghans, though they speak and act when they choose. All of them can speak to the Spider Prophet mind to mind, though they only trouble the prophet with matters of great importance. Only fools anger a land where the wind, the rocks, and the oases themselves can turn on them.

Each spirit has its own character, though most follow their nature. Rock spirits are stubborn and patient, wind spirits live for the moment and love new things, water spirits flit from thought to thought, and fire spirits are focused and intense. The best known include Simoon, a poisonous and cruel wind; Aejej, a wise whirlwind of flame; Gregale, a kindly oasis near the western coast; and Harmattan, the rock on which the Dominion's laws are carved.

The spirits loyal to the Wind Lords and those loyal to Boreas have been at war for eight centuries. Being immortal, they fight on a scale mortals can barely see: the buildup to a skirmish takes months or years, and decades pass between great battles. But the war is no less deadly for mortals caught in it when it flares. Warring spirits sometimes lash out at passing caravans, and now and then a whole band of nomads is crushed beneath battling earth elementals.

## The Tamasheq

About half a million [[Tamasheq]] live in the Southlands, most in the Stone Desert but many in the Crescent and Sarklan deserts too. They speak their own tongue, Tamasheq, as well as the Southern Tongue, and often [[Gnolls|Gnoll]], Draconic, and Minotaur. They are organized into *vanhu*, tribes of several families each, and spend their lives moving from oasis to oasis with herds of goats and camels, trading as they go. They take great pleasure in convincing the [[Nurians]] and [[Kushites]] that they are simple nomads. In truth they have a thriving, advanced civilization that they long hid from the world, and only in Kel Azjer do they show their greatness.

Tamasheq show their status by their long white robes and colored turbans, their finely tooled saddles, and the copper and silver hilts of their *takoba* shortswords. Their skill at desert travel brings them wealth, trade routes, and influence. They can see a caravan safely across the desert, and they can destroy even a well-guarded caravan or sandship. Most make small offerings to the spirits before any undertaking, and they reveal as little as they can about the spirits to outsiders. That makes them seem proud and standoffish, but once the Tamasheq take a stranger into their confidence, the friendship is for life.

## The Jinnborn Tribes

Insular tribes of [[Elemental Scions|jinnborn]] are scattered throughout the Dominion and, more thinly, the Crescent Desert. They say they came out of the deepest deserts and were the first mortals to walk the world, when all the world was desert beneath a fiery sun and they ruled all they saw. They call themselves the *sab siraat*, the "people of the path." Descended from the jinn, air jinnborn are proud and aloof, fire jinnborn impulsive and harsh, water jinnborn carefree but changeable, and earth jinnborn stoic and determined, though every tribe has its own customs, laws, and lore. They seek wealth when they can, revel in revenge when they must, and cherish close companionship always. Outsiders find them capricious or even wild.

Each tribe honors a patron jinn as its founder, usually led by the extended family of that jinn's first creations. Jinnborn who follow their path faithfully hope to go after death to the Hidden World, a place of delights that sages think lies somewhere between the Ethereal and Elemental planes. Jinnborn who turn their backs on the desert and stay too long in towns are said to pick up strange quirks and risk madness.

## Religion

Only Boreas among the Wind Lords is a true god with worshipers. The jinnborn mostly honor their patron jinn rather than gods, though the jinn grant no divine spells. Many Tamasheq revere Eshu, god of travelers and nomads, and others follow Kwansi, Horus, or Djyy.

The Spider Prophet receives Kwansi's revelations in the Oracle Shrine beneath Inkabut. Traveling temples, mobile shrines of Horus, often go with Tamasheq caravans in the Crescent Desert, and the Desert Falcon's great temple, Khepri Khnum, stands on a mountaintop in the Chelamite range, home to Horus's army of ten thousand birds and served by beautiful harpy priests with fantastic plumage, some of them owl harpies. Holy sites of Djyy lie along the Tamasheq Climb ley line in the Stone Desert and the Granite Road in the Chelamite Mountains, usually caves with springs or streams that offer shelter to caravans.

## Trade

The heart of the Dominion's trade is Abeu Plaza in Kel Azjer. Tamasheq and jinnborn caravans carry fabulous carpets, precious metals, gemstones, and exotic goods west from the hidden city to Hartani Bay and around the Stone Desert, or east to Inkabut, Cindass, and Roshgazi. Many go on along the Tamasheq Trail through the Crescent Desert to Saph-Saph and distant [[Nuria Natal#Corremel, City of Ale|Corremel]], and on the way home trade slaves and dates for salt at the Khephani Salt Flats or the Ghatazi Salt Pits. In the past five years the Wind Lords have relaxed their old ban on outsiders, and foreign merchants now crowd the souks of Inkabut from as far as [[Capleon]], [[Barsella]], and the Kingdoms of Salt and Steel. Most come overland from Cindass, and a few by airships driven by bound air elementals or djinn.

## Kel Azjer

North of the Chelamite Mountains, on a high mesa, stands Kel Azjer, the hidden capital of the Tamasheq. Far from the herders and camel traders of their reputation, it is a city of golden-spired towers, wide plazas, and busy workshops of leatherworkers, vrilsmiths, martial orders, and elemental scholars. The Tamasheq Climb ley line runs through its center and lets its people open doorways to other planes.

## Inkabut

The Dominion's second city, and the only large settlement most outsiders know, stands just inland from the eastern coast of the Stone Desert, north of the old Moon Kingdom. It is home of the Spider Prophet, who guards the borders and commands the Dominion's armies. Its gates are always busy with caravans from Cindass and beyond, and its narrow, maze-like streets are lively from dawn to dusk. Its population is 36,750.

## The Ruined Cities of the Moon Kingdom

About fifteen hundred years ago, [[Minotaurs]] from the eastern end of the Middle Sea settled the Lands of Wind and Sand and founded Roshgazi and Cindass on the coast and Kadralhu and Derrada inland. These cities were home to minotaurs and to their human allies, the [[Qamari]], cousins of the Tamasheq. Two centuries later they founded a colony and harbor at [[Capleon]] for the sea trade between Roshgazi and [[Kyprion]]. The minotaurs traded with Nuria too, though that relationship sometimes came to skirmishes. They had come from the eastern islands of the White Sea, from Kyprion, and from the coast between Harkesh and Marea.

About 460 years ago the western cities broke from the rule of the Queen of the Minotaurs in Kyprion and declared the independent Moon Kingdom of Tes-Qamar. The "Golden Pair," King Kaprys and Queen Melenni, braced for war with the eastern minotaurs, but it never came. The Queen in the East, as she was later called, tried and failed to win them back with peaceful overtures, and then the rising threat of the dragons took all her attention.

The Moon Kingdom prospered until the Mharoti attacked three hundred years ago, less than fifty years after the founding of the Dragon Empire. The dragons razed Derrada and Kadralhu and left much of Roshgazi and Cindass in ruins. The minotaurs fled across the sea to Capleon, [[Triolo]], and Kyprion, which all still have large minotaur populations, but not all arrived. The famed Lost Fleet of Roshgazi vanished in the mists before reaching Capleon, and whole clans were thought lost. About ninety years later the dragons came again, but the Wind Lords drove them off and they turned their attention to Nuria.

Now things have come full circle. With Kyprion threatened by the Dragon Empire, a steady stream of minotaurs is returning to Cindass, and four ships of the Lost Fleet have made landfall at Roshgazi after three hundred years.

Cindass was the northernmost city of the Moon Kingdom, a beautiful port famous for its deep-water harbor and fine white marble buildings. Much of it was reduced to rubble in the Mharoti attack, but many survivors refused to leave and began rebuilding. Under the wise rule of Emir Palana Tellisha, a minotaur mage, it has grown to about fifteen thousand people, half of them minotaurs, and trade with Kyprion, Triolo, and Capleon is thriving so well that the descendants of many old Cindass families are coming home. New walls have gone up, the harbor has been repaired, and a new labyrinth has been built. Marble statues of the Moon Kingdom's rulers and heroes stand throughout the city, and in Tes-Qamar Plaza a thirty-foot statue of the Golden Pair by the master sculptor Ashtzath Surechisel honors its founders.

Cindass's merchant-captains carry salt, spice, and marble north to the Seven Cities and bring back timber, amber, and pearls to sell in Inkabut, Saph-Saph, and beyond. In the last few years merchants from Kyprion, Triolo, and even Barsella have set up in the city for the overland trade and built secure stone warehouses called the Strongboxes, a tempting target for local cells of the Hand of Nakresh. The city's fleet has been strengthened, and a cabal of wizards under the water jinnborn First Warmage Hamid Mareshi now serves the navy. Returning minotaurs have brought labyrinth magic back with them, and the city's mages are blending it with jinnborn elemental spells. The emir hopes Cindass will one day be strong enough to take its long-overdue revenge on the Mharoti.

### Roshgazi

Roshgazi was the capital of the Moon Kingdom and the center of western minotaur culture. Three hundred years ago the dragons attacked it by night, hoping to destroy the orb of dragonkind the minotaurs were said to possess, which could supposedly dominate dragons and even the scions of [[Mharot]]. No one knows whether it existed. For fifteen days a score of dragons and two legions of dragonborn and kobolds fought for the city and burned the Queen's Navy. When they broke through, they sacked the city, enslaved the survivors, filled in the wells, and destroyed the aqueduct. Where nearly a hundred thousand once lived, about two thousand do now: explorers from the Seven Cities digging for gold in the deepest tombs, some Qamari, and flesh-hungry ravening minotaurs.

There is new hope, though. Senator Evadne, a master of labyrinth magic, has returned with the Lost Fleet after three centuries at sea and believes she knows how to repair the Heart of Roshgazi. If she succeeds, more minotaurs will surely return, as they have to Cindass.

### Derrada and Kadralhu

The dragons left almost nothing of Derrada. Today a small settlement stands around the oasis on the southeastern edge of the Stone Desert where the city was, essentially a fortified caravanserai beside a large shady pool. The pool is home to the powerful nature spirit Loundja, who came after the city fell, found that the dragonkin had poisoned the water and salted the land, and in fury healed both. Water jinnborn later built the caravanserai from the ruins' stones to protect travelers from gnoll raiders. First-time visitors are startled when Loundja rises from the pool and lectures them sternly on how to behave, and they must promise to respect the jinnborn, other travelers, and the animals and plants before they may stay.

The ruins of Kadralhu lie half-buried in the northern Mukupe Sahel. The Tamasheq and jinnborn avoid them, for too many of their people have died there to sandwyrms that lie beneath the sand with only their ribcage-like back bones showing, and to dune mimics. Explorers are drawn by tales of the treasure of the minotaurs who shut themselves into the city's Spiral Labyrinth to escape the dragons. Its entrance is marked by a pair of giant marble bull's horns over a stair leading down into the earth.

## Perilous Sites of the Western Desert

The map shows the desert west of Saph-Saph and the Howler's Road as empty, but ruined cities, wondrous valleys, and faerie castles lie hidden among the dunes for those who know where to look.

### Castle Halcyon

Travelers in the western Crescent Desert sometimes glimpse a castle of white marble shimmering in the distance as they come out of a sandstorm. It is Castle Halcyon, home of Tarkun the Desiccated, a fey lord who attempted a coup against a fey lady, failed, and was stripped of his power and banished to the deep desert to wither and die. He survived by seizing hold of the desert's ley lines, at the cost of his once-beautiful form, and now cannot leave the desert by any means. He welcomes visitors to his castle if they bring splendid gifts and lavish flattery. Those who fail to show him proper respect are said to regret it.

### Kadrana

Deep in the southwestern wastes, when the sands drift right, the top of an ancient tower can be seen above the dunes. It is the Phoenix Tower, the highest point of the fallen flying city of Kadrana, buried on the Howler's Road ley line. Giants of what is now the [[Haunted Lands of the Giants|Haunted Lands]] built Kadrana as a temple-city for their goddess of rebirth, Kaima. When she died, her body was laid in a Vault of Rebirth to await her return in a thousand years, and as her divine essence flowed into the vault, the city rose into the sky about 850 years ago. For generations its giants traded from the sky with [[Valera]], Kel Azjer, Roshgazi, Aerdvall, and other wonders, but they grew arrogant and forgot their goddess, and about five hundred years ago, after their water was poisoned and a maddening plague swept the city, Kadrana fell into the Ishmai Desert. Tales of the exposed tower are told in the jinnborn town of Hallampor.

### Golden Ulthar

In the Crescent Desert northwest of Saph-Saph, the sands give way to a hidden miracle, the Valley of Golden Ulthar. Long ago the whole Crescent Desert was green. As it slowly turned to desert, this one valley stayed fertile and for an age was home to a great civilization, Ulthar, which reached its height about four thousand years ago, trading with Nuria and Glorious Umbuso. Ulthar's influence still lingers in the valley in strange and powerful ways.

At the center of the valley's long green scar stands Serbata, the ruined temple complex that was Ulthar's capital, crumbling walls around a looming step pyramid, its outbuildings long ransacked and overgrown. Inside lives Sepenret, the Desolate Priestess, last priestess of Ulthar, a unique sphinx with a woman's upper body, a lion's body, eagle's wings, and a tail that fans out like a bright lotus. She spends most of her time in a vast columned hall full of stonework that still holds Ulthar's power. Now and then, though she has no living mate, she lays an egg of stone that also carries the kingdom's magic. Scholars and adventurers sometimes come to consult her. She makes them answer a hard riddle or solve a fiendish puzzle before she will hear them, and sometimes asks those who impress her to join the Claws of Sepenret and perform a service. Those who refuse may find themselves under a geas.

Around ten thousand humans loyal to her live in the valley, along with rival tribes of wererats and other [[Werekin|lycanthropes]] of many kinds.

A ruined city in the Mukupe Sahel, near the gnoll land of Dabu, occupied by goblin and gnoll brigands, raiders, and slavers. Its name means "devouring darkness" in [[Goblins|Goblin]]. Some say it was founded in the Ankeshelian expansion: its step pyramids are of a glassy black stone not found nearby and look nothing like the architecture of the region, and the worn glyphs on its oldest pyramids and pillars, which glow faintly at night after thousands of years, only faintly resemble Nurian ones. The other draw is that it is one of the few places where vril technology can be found. Its brown-skinned "rust goblins" know vril weapons well and use them in defense and raiding.

The tyrant Gar'bori Enkidai came over a decade ago, slaughtered and devoured dozens of goblins and gnolls to cow the rest, and with his new allies plundered the great obsidian pyramid at the city's center, now his heavily trapped lair. Many of the other great tombs and pyramids that hold vril hoards are still guarded by the shining metal golems of Mardas Vhula-gai and remain untouched. Scholars and wizards from Nuria Natal, Kush, Lignas, and even [[Magocracy of Allain|Allain]] have taken an interest, but the growing goblin and gnoll population frustrates most archaeologists and mercenaries. Caravans still come to trade for vril items and batteries, and usually succeed, though there is always a risk the goblins will murder or enslave them instead. Visitors who come by day with a goblin or gnoll guide and look tough enough are mostly left alone. No one should walk its streets at night, when Gar'bori and his bouda companions hunt.

Places in the ruins include the Great Pyramid; the unplundered step pyramids and their golems; a ruined pyramid over the underground temple of Anu-Akma; the grand tent of Yarza Harfi, stolen from a sheikh of Siwal; a shrine of Bastet full of feral cats that attack anyone who enters without a gnoll priestess and a carcass to feed them; the yurt of Yrsa the Inquisitor, who sends treasure home to the rulers of Dabu; a fountain whose water spirit, Zwila, attacks any nonhuman who enters its ring of pillars; the Black Pool, a brackish fen of black grass, broken statues, and giant crocodiles; and a small obsidian temple of Nkishi, the gnoll god of slaughter, whose sect is small but growing.

### The Sand Giants

The sand giants came to Midgard from the Plane of Fire about 750 years ago after a quarrel with the efreet, and under their chieftain Azhad al-Hani carved out a vast stretch of the southern Crescent Desert, which they call the Ishmai Desert. As their land grew, so did Azhad's power, until thirty years later it overwhelmed him and he turned into sand and became one with the desert. Every sand giant proudly traces their line to him. They fight with falchions and javelins of tempered glass.

## History of the Lands of Wind and Sand

At the dawn of creation the giant Aurgelmir breathed out, and his breath split into the four Wind Lords, Boreas, Chergui, Khamsin, and Shemral. Avoiding the Vanir War, they came to Midgard, settled the high deserts and plateaus of the northwestern Southlands, and woke the spirits of wind, stone, fire, and sea in every rock, spring, and peak to serve them.
