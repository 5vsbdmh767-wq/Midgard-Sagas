Nuria Natal is the oldest human kingdom in Midgard, more than five thousand years old, stretched along the magical River Nuria south of the Middle Sea. The titanic ley line called the Angel's Road runs the length of the river, and over the millennia its wizard-kings and sorcerer-queens learned to use it to stretch their lives into a kind of half-divinity, and to be woken from death when their descendants need them. Its gods are close at hand: Aten, Horus, and Bastet have walked the land when called, and have thrown back the dragons of [[The Mharoti Empire]] more than once.

When the gods don't answer, the [[Nurians]] wake their dead kings. Seven have been raised to fight the Mharoti in recent years. Two died in battle, one went back to his tomb, and four still walk the kingdom and have no intention of leaving. The living king, Thutmoses XXIII, is a worried man in the middle of his life with a beard woven with gold and mithral, and his authority has worn thin with ancestors looking over his shoulder. The cities of Per-Xor and Per-Bastet still pay their tribute but complain more loudly every year. Against any foreign invader, though, the whole kingdom stands behind him.

With the dragons busy elsewhere, Thutmoses is spending the peace on trade, sending priests of Thoth-Hermes north to the [[Seven Cities]], the [[Magdar Kingdom]], [[Zobeck]], and [[Grand Duchy of Dornig|Dornig]]. The Southern Tongue, Nurian, is the language of much of the world's ritual magic, and Nuria is also home to the Red Portals, doorways along the ley lines to other places and times, and the catslide alleys of Per-Bastet, which carry anyone who follows a cat to some other cat-loving city.

## The Land

The River Nuria runs the whole length of the kingdom, tumbling down from Black Lotus Mesa in the south and thundering into the Middle Sea in the north. For a dozen miles on either bank stretches the farmland that feeds the River Kingdom: pasture for cattle, golden wheat, and fields of flax and hemp for cloth and trade. Boats ply the river day and night all year round. The river floods from Goldflower through Harvest Tide, when the snow and ice of the Mbhaza Mountains melt.

West of the river the Crescent Desert runs out toward Dabu, the [[Dominion of the Wind Lords]], and the Mbazha Mountains. It is a source of salt and the country of the sandships that run to and from [[Free Cities of the Desert|Saph-Saph]]. East of the river the Sarklan Desert stands between Nuria Natal and its hated enemies in the Mharoti Empire, and caravans cross it on the Mhalmet Road from Per-Bastet to [[Free Cities of the Desert|Siwal]] and on to [[Mhalmet]].

The deserts are dry, cool in winter and fiercely hot in summer. Rain is almost unheard of in summer and modest in winter, and most of it falls along the coast and the northern river. Horem-gatra is the wettest city in the kingdom.

In winter, sandstorms and the worse siltstorms can rage for days and change the land across hundreds of miles. The [[Tamasheq]] wrap their mouths and noses in lithams of layered linen and cotton against them.

## Culture and Customs

Nurians see themselves as the protectors of their enchanted river. They raise stone monuments, monolithic statues, and sphinxes to show their love of it, and build great temple complexes to their gods. Their god-kings and god-queens are worshiped as living deities. When one dies, the body is prepared with magical rites and laid in a great pyramid tomb. Nurian mages built these tombs as resurrection tomb-engines, powered by the ley lines, so that in times of crisis the old rulers can return to defend the kingdom. Usually they go back to their tombs when the danger passes. Only the gods know which ones will wake, or why some stay.

Nurians are proud of their kingdom, and history matters to them. Most can name their ancestors back several generations, and every tavern is full of people telling improbable stories of how a grandmother turned a battle, or saved a god-king, or became the avatar of a god.

That pride has a darker side the Nurians rarely admit. Secret societies breed in the dark alleys and bright salons. Thousands of fiends, many summoned by Nurian mages and priests, are loose in the River Kingdom. Many of its temples and monuments were built by slaves, and nonhumans are routinely bought and sold on Nuria's auction blocks. Because the threat of invasion comes from the northeast, scalykind are hated: dragonborn, kobolds, lizardfolk, and serpentfolk are met with revulsion and distrust when they are not killed or enslaved outright.

## History

All the tales agree that Nuria Natal was founded more than five thousand years ago by seven families who gathered on the river at the end of a long journey. One of them was a wizard, Senewosret, who sensed the arcane power swirling in the water and found that drinking it let him work the ley lines. He took charge, named the river Nuria, meaning "luminous," and had a city built on its banks. His successors, all descended from the seven founding families, reached near-divinity through their experiments with the river's water and its ley lines, and learned the rites that wake them from death when the kingdom is in danger. With the gods of the Nurian pantheon they have watched over the River Kingdom since before Ankeshel sank.

Over the centuries Nuria has lost some of its old lands. The Free Cities of Makuria, Saph-Saph, and Siwal are the plainest examples, though the River Kingdom still talks of bringing Ishadia and Kush back into the fold. The Free Cities are happy to trade with Nuria but would fight to the end to stay free. Since the Mharoti rose almost four hundred years ago, Nuria Natal has been at war with the Dragon Empire. The war is not always hot, but it never stops simmering, as the dragons send war bands and raiders to test the kingdom's defenses.

## Government

The living god-king, Thutmoses XXIII, frets on his throne in Nuria. He is a stately man of middle age with a shaved head, piercing eyes, a regal nose, and a long perfumed beard woven with mithral and gold. Once an all-powerful monarch, he has watched his influence wane since his divine ancestors returned. Four of the seven god-kings and god-queens woken to drive out the Mharoti are still at large, building their own reputations at his expense, and a fifth god-queen has lately risen in Laksor. Grumbling about his vigor reaches him regularly from Per-Bastet and Per-Xor, and word of growing violence around the ruins of Tes-Luria has begun to reach him too.

With the Dragon Empire quiet for now, Thutmoses has sent delegations north to the wealthy lands and city-states there, led by Bebnum the Most Wise of Thoth-Hermes. Trade with Capleon has grown considerably, and the Nurian Galley from Triolo now sails five times a year instead of three. Talks continue with Queen Kitane of Kyprion, who wants Nurian protection since her island was invaded. The king means to spend the profits on an army strong enough that Nuria need not call on its god-kings when the war with the Mharoti resumes, and he is said to dream of reclaiming Ishadia, Kush, Makuria, and Siwal.

For all his troubles, ordinary Nurians love their god-king. As long as Nuria's enemies are held in check and there is bread and beer for everyone, Thutmoses sits secure. It helps that he controls the vast machinery of grain and bread, and the loyalty of Ninkash, the matron goddess of beer.

### The Inheritance of the Crown

Every god-king keeps a register of heirs, at least twenty names long and usually far longer, and sees to their education. Heirs are usually chosen from the god-king's own family, with a few from the other six founding families, though the law allows any person of Nurian blood. When the god-king dies, the first heir drinks Black Urd Lotus, the narcotic essence of the lotus that grows at the river's source on Black Lotus Mesa, in the Well of Ascension in the Palm Palace, and faces the Tests of Inheritance in a waking dream. The tests are harsh and sometimes fatal. Those who die are mummified and laid in the dead god-king's tomb as guardians, and the priests of Anu-Akma, who oversee the tests and refine the lotus, go down the list until one heir survives to take the Petal Throne.

## Magic of the River

Magic is everywhere in Nuria Natal. Geomancers and wielders of hieroglyphic magic draw on the ley lines, wizards travel by the Red Portals, and the catslide alleys carry the unwary from one cat-loving city to another.

### Ley Lines

The titanic Angel's Road runs the length of the River Nuria, and at the Middle Sea it turns east to Efisis in the Dragon Empire before heading southeast into [[Ishadia]]. Many lesser lines branch from it. The legendary Oasis of Figs drifts along them, appearing somewhere new in the desert every sunset. It is ruled by Sultan Hajani the Benevolent from a colorful marble palace, time passes strangely there, and visitors who eat the sultan's iridescent figs often find they cannot leave. West of the river, the Howler's Road runs from the Pyramid of Khensu through the Crescent Desert to Saph-Saph, [[Roshgazi]], and Cindass, and on to [[Magocracy of Allain|Bemmea]]. See also [[Ley Lines of the Southlands]].

### Red Portals

Centuries ago, Nurian wizards learned to open doorways at points along the ley lines, which they called the Red Portals. The Honorable Society of Portal Wizards still studies and works them from Per-Anu and regularly hires bold folk to go through active portals and report back, if they return. The portals lead to [[Shadow Roads|shadow roads]], other planes, other worlds, and other times. Some are anchored to an archway or door, while others are invisible, or only a faint shimmer in the air. To use one, a traveler pictures themselves walking a shadow road just before stepping through. Some run one way only.

### Catslide Alleys

Catslide alleys are portals that link places where cats and other felines are held in high esteem. Per-Bastet has a dozen or so, mostly in the District of the Cat. Anyone who follows a cat into one is likely to come out somewhere else that loves cats: Triolo, the court of the Dragon Emperor in Far Cathay, the home of the winged cats in [[The Shadow Realm]], or stranger feline realms on the Plateau of Leng or among the hellcats of the Eleven Hells. Like cats, the alleys are fickle.

## Trade and Caravans

Most of Nuria Natal's trade moves by caravan. Camel trains gather in Nuria and travel overland to Corremel or Per-Bastet before setting out into the Crescent or Sarklan deserts, and sandships skim both deserts with staples and luxuries. Nurian linen, grain, jewelry, perfume, and spell scrolls are found all around the Middle Sea. Trade with the Seven Cities has grown greatly, goods now arrive from [[The Crossroads]] and the Magdar Kingdom, and the king's delegation has gone as far as [[The Northlands]]. Some sly merchants even trade with the Mharoti, though honest citizens look sideways at them for it.

Sandships ride just above the dunes on a pair of narrow pontoons, sailing the sand and silt as easily as a ship sails water. They come in all sizes, from the rowboat-sized sandskiffs of desert marauders to great cargo vessels.

## Religion

Nurians see the gods everywhere: in the god-kings risen to fight their enemies, in the gods themselves walking among them, and in the magic waters of the river. The cults of [[Anu-Akma]], god of the dead, [[Bastet]], goddess of cats, and [[Thoth-Hermes]], god of magic and learning and patron of the River Nuria, have been held highest for centuries. In recent years Aten, god of the sun, [[Ninkash]], goddess of ale and taverns, and Set, in his guise as Friend to Strangers, have risen in prominence. See also [[Major Gods of the Southlands]] and [[City Godlings of Nuria]].

The kingdom's towns, all under five thousand people, are Avaris, Banosh, Battari, Djanot, Herak, Highgate, Horem-gatra, Memphar, Nebr, Neffero, Per-Anu, and Ushanti.

### Nuria, City of the River

The capital sprawls for miles along the riverbank, a city of monuments, domed temples, palm-lined boulevards, small parks with pools and fountains, and thousands of houses of dried river mud. Its people are proud to live in the god-king's city and jealous of the greater fame they believe Per-Bastet, Per-Xor, and Siwal enjoy. In flood season the god-king uses his command of the river and the Angel's Road to turn the waters away from the city and onto the surrounding fields.

### Corremel, City of Ale

Corremel was founded by minotaurs as a fishing village in the river delta, and it still has a fine fishing fleet. When it turned out the delta soil could grow fine grain in huge quantities, the city turned to farming and brewing, and its beer now slakes the thirst of every Nurian in the delta, peasant and king alike. In summer the smell of fermenting ale fills the whole city. It is wealthy but not pretentious, and proud to have prospered without a reborn god-king to prop it up.

Rival breweries spare no expense stealing or fouling one another's recipes, and the church of Ninkash sometimes steps in and fines everyone involved when a feud turns violent. Subek brewers have multiplied in recent years, and nobody can copy their peaty ale. A sizable population of alehouse drakes lives in the city, drawn by the beer, though poaching has thinned their numbers lately and the city wants to know why they are disappearing.

### Laksor, City of the Twin Temples

Laksor is hailed as the birthplace of [[Lada]], goddess of healing and love. It has temples to all the great gods of Nuria Natal, a shrine to each of its minor gods, and many shrines and pantheist temples to the other gods of Midgard. Hundreds of spires, domes, and arches fill its skyline, lapis-lined fountains spray perfumed water into lotus pools, and its people call it the most beautiful and cosmopolitan city in the kingdom. As the city nearest the Middle Sea it sees many foreign visitors, and Governor Tau Tehuti Ghi makes sure they see it at its best, with armies of slaves keeping the streets clean and fines for landowners who let their property decay. Every twelve years the priests of Isis make a pilgrimage to her birthplace and the city overflows.

Its twin temples stand side by side. The Healing School of Isis is a spiraling tower hundreds of feet tall that sparkles like a unicorn's horn, where clerics under Her Holiness Yaminah Heppuru, the Smiling Matron of Teph-ha, train priests, doctors, midwives, and counselors, treat ills of the mind as seriously as ills of the body, and turn no one away. Beside it, the library-temple of Thoth-Hermes is the seminary of the priesthood, the cradle of the Scribes of Thoth-Hermes, and one of the largest stores of arcane and obscure lore in the [[Southlands]], guarded by shabti, gearforged, scroll mummies, and priests under Zehuti Kha-Se, Stern Patriarch of Memory. Hundreds of mendicant priests of Thoth-Hermes serve as scribes throughout the kingdom and carry its news.

### Per-Anu, City of Crimson Pillars

Per-Anu lies hidden in a sheltered hollow of the Mushmes Hills in the northeast, the city of death, the underworld, and the life beyond. It is warded against anyone whose work does not touch death in some way: priests of certain faiths, gravebinders, necromancers, butchers, healers, and killers. Few ordinary folk live there, but throngs of undead keep its streets busy. Thousands of red columns hold up the roofs of the Royal Embalmer's Hall and the Many-Pillared Palace and line the Red Market, where wizards hunt rare components. Three gates to [[Evermaw]], the Plane of Undeath, stand in the city, and wizards like to cast spells near them to see what goes wrong. The priests of Isis have abandoned their Temple of White Blossoms, once one of the safest places in the city, and rumor says the god-king sent people through a Red Portal. He will not say.

### Per-Bastet, City of the Cat

The nine districts of Per-Bastet sit along the river among ancient monolith-statues of the gods. Cats rule the city: countless cats, along with gnolls, basteti, and feline lycanthropes of every kind, under the gaze of Queen-Goddess Meskhenit and High Priestess Nafrini, a woman of surpassing beauty and gentleness whose word commands the city's gnoll armies. [[Humans]] say they are less welcome every year. The two rulers enjoy a small rivalry in feats of perfumery and magic against Nuria's enemies. The city has three great temples, to Anu-Akma, to Horus, and the greatest, the Dome of the Divine Face of Bastet. Its summers are cooler and its winters warmer than the rest of the river, which divinations suggest is the goddess's doing.

A moving river of sand flows into the city from the Sarklan Desert at fifteen miles an hour, under aging bridges and through the city's heart, and pours into a vast funnel-shaped crater called the Pit, where it vanishes. Things carried into the Pit sometimes turn up years or centuries later, hundreds of miles away in the desert. Below the surface of the sand river lies the Growling Sanctuary, a forgotten catfolk tomb named for the sounds the sand makes scraping past it, guarded by catfolk mummies and holding treasures of the cat god's wicked aspect.

### Per-Kush, City of Ivory

Oliphants, camelopards, hunting birds, and stranger beasts come north through Per-Kush, whose patron is [[The Hunter]]. The hunting of great beasts from the far south has made it rich, and if it keeps growing at this rate it will be larger than Nuria within two generations. Its camel caravans go daily to the Kingdoms of Gold and Salt, and its riverboats carry Nurian grain upriver. Abu, the elephant god of water, strength, and wealth, is gaining followers, as is [[Ptah]]. Its dwarves follow southern demons as well as Ptah, and one of them, Ashabtilach, the Shellwright, builds constructs of brass and iron and darker things, such as demonic retrievers, which so far serve the kingdom. Every summer the city holds games, and while winners are showered with gold, losers are sometimes hunted down by overzealous followers of the Hunter.

### Per-Set

A decade ago Per-Set was little more than a caravan stop at the ruins of a once-thriving city. Now pilgrims are pouring in to the Supreme Temple of the Wind Caller, Set's greatest temple, a structure of red clay blocks coiled with serpent statues whose jet, gold, and emerald were stripped long ago. The town is a patchwork of lean-tos, mended houses, and new buildings, and its busy souk is one of the largest in the Mushmes Hills. Set's priests wear dun robes hemmed in black. The undead in the temple's crypts will not touch Set's faithful but guard the place fiercely against everyone else. Angry Atenites roam the hills around Per-Set, beating pilgrims and burning their belongings, and a clash between the two faiths seems likely.

### Per-Xor, City of the Sun

At less than two hundred years old, Per-Xor is the youngest of the great cities and the heart of Aten's worship. Four brassbound Towers to the Sun, each with a lens at its peak, focus the god's rays on Martyr's Square, where the prophet Ra-Amon-Ra was burned by dragon fire fifty years ago. Aten has altered the towers so that the city is hotter than the land around it, a reminder of the prophet's sacrifice, and the people accept the heat as penance. The Tomb of the Prophet, of red granite and enchanted marble, has stood almost finished for five decades and draws thousands each year, most at the height of the rainy season, when the Xorians build an enormous bonfire of palm wood, myrrh, and oils outside it to light Aten's way. The square hosts the Blessing of the Children in late spring, the Barley Harvest in late summer, and the midsummer frenzy of the Height of Aten.

The city is ruled by the high priest and high priestess of Aten, Zuberi the Wise and Saint Hafsah, grandparents of an enormous family. They swear loyalty to the god-king, but their first loyalty is plainly to their faith, and they struggle to keep the cult of the Prophet from rising to equal Aten himself. The Prophet's most radical preachers talk of rebellion and a new holy dynasty, and the True Aten preaches openly against all other gods. The tension is rising, and those who can afford to are leaving before holy war breaks out.

### Tes-Luria

Tes-Luria was the capital of the old Kingdom of Luria, ruled by Bastet for centuries before she left for Per-Bastet, and once the greatest city of the Southlands, a hub of trade, learning, and rich harvests. It fell to ruin centuries ago. Some blame the water drying up, some say Bastet simply lost interest, and some say the ley lines shifted or were tampered with. The tombs of many of Nuria's richest godlings remain, guarded by mummified sphinxes, stone golems, and undead gnoll warriors. Some years ago a cult of catfolk and gnolls who worship Bastet's feral, lustful aspect took over the ruined Grand Fane. It has since split in two, the Slinking Sisterhood of the Untamed under Mau-tet Tivali and the gnolls of the Braying Band under Iwi Helwa, who keep a tense peace and never worship at the Fane at the same time.
