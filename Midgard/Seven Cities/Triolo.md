The corsairs of Triolo are feared from the White Sea to the Western Ocean. They raid everyone else's shipping, ransom captives from every nation, and make a fortune on spice and glass. The city runs on a mix of clever diplomacy, lawlessness, infernal bargains, and fortunes made and lost overnight, and it keeps ambassadors and spies in fifty cities. It has the greatest fleet in the [[Seven Cities]], and its Fleet Fair on Sphinx Island sells sailcloth, rope, glass, and spice.

A council of merchant families elects the First Duke-Admiral for life. Cadua is the first minotaur to hold the post. After a disaster in his youth he wandered from oracle to oracle and gave up a horn, an eye, and a hand for wisdom, and came home burning with the faith of Mavros. When [[Rumela|Illyria]] fell next door, he threw open the gates to the refugees and turned their fear into a war footing.

Triolo now stands on the front line against [[The Mharoti Empire]]. Its fleet helped destroy the Mharoti armada at the Golden Wave, its red-sailed corsairs harry the Dread Sultan's ships, and its land forces are Illyrian exiles, minotaurs from Kyprion, and dwarven mercenaries. With the dragons now holding a city on Kyprion, Cadua is torn. If he sends troops to help his own people, the dragons may come for Triolo instead.

## Captains of the Fleet

Triolo's government is a complex one: a Golden Council of two dozen merchant families, and a network of ambassadors and spies in fifty or more cities that keeps it better informed than any state but [[Kammae Straboli|Kammae]]. But Triolan policy is really steered by the officers of its fleets, in descending order the duke-admirals, admirals, captains, and commanders. The council elects the First Duke-Admiral, who serves for life or until abdicating, usually at sixty or seventy.

## Triolo, City and Harbor

Home to more than a hundred thousand people, Triolo is the heart of a web of sea routes reaching from [[Magocracy of Allain|Bemmea]] in the north to Harkesh and [[Nuria Natal]] in the south and east. Its harbor front is among the most cosmopolitan and outrageous stretches of flagstones anywhere. Displaced Illyrians have thrown themselves into the army, the navy, and the shipbuilding guilds to free their home. The Illyrian nobles are another matter. Some work for their homeland's restoration, while others wallow in despair or ruthlessly try to carve out a place for "nobles" in a Republic run by naval officers.

### The Armadas

Triolo's trade is funded, guarded, and secured by the state, with profits split among cartels of nobles and the duke. Its great merchant armadas, called "galleys," sail a dozen or two dozen ships together.

| Galley | Sailings |
| --- | --- |
| The Nurian Galley and the [[Capleon]] Galley (which also calls at Cindass) | Three times a year |
| The Achillon, Friulan, Harkesh, and Perzhan Galleys | Twice a year |
| The Orkasan Galley, within the [[Despotate of the Ruby Sea\|Ruby Sea]], also visiting Reth-Saal and Kaa'nesh | Once a year |
| The Barsellan Galley | Once a year, often canceled in years of bad storms or losses elsewhere |
| The Galley of Thorn | Roughly every third year. It no longer visits Thorn, but calls at Maillon, Bemmea, and Donnermark. |

Triolo also trades heavily north up the Glass Road to Zobeck and beyond, taking its fees in mercenaries and in captured Mharoti, who serve as galley slaves.

## Raguza, City of Pirates

Under the eye of a towering sea titan standing in its harbor, the old corsair haven of Raguza rings with shipbuilders' hammers, the shouts of drilling soldiers, and the screeches of returning hippogriff scouts. Half the Triolan fleet is stationed here, with more ships on the slips along the beach. The waters that once gave pirates easy access to merchant ships now let Raguzan privateers, holding Triolan letters of marque, raid Mharoti shipping. Under the Black Flag Accords signed by the Admiralty and the Duke of Raguza, any pirate willing to become a privateer was pardoned. Those who refused are banned from Raguza and have moved to a hidden cove near the ruins of Coron.

Raguza is ruled by Duke Legorio IV, scion of a thoroughly corrupt family, and his wife, the sorceress Biljana, a vile pair who govern by their own designs. The duke owes fealty to the Republic and under the Black Flag Accords must honor the requests of its local representative, Admiral Lucca Agnone. The two circle each other warily, neither willing to move while the Mharoti threat hangs overhead.

A letter of marque authorizes a privateer captain to attack the ships of particular nations and sell their cargoes in friendly ports without being called a thief, though any privateer captured by the enemy is treated as a pirate rather than a prisoner of war. Officially letters are granted after a careful review. The going rate is a pouch of 500 gp.

The Sky Riders of Illyria, sons and daughters of displaced Illyrian nobles who bond with a hippogriff or dragon mount in youth, fled to Raguza after the invasion and now serve as scouts, saboteurs, and couriers. Their able commander, Orania Oreste, a small, determined paladin of Mavros, leads every aerial charge on her hippogriff, Hunter.
