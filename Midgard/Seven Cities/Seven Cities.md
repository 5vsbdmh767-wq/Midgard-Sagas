In the Seven Cities, war is a season like planting or harvest. Every spring the city-states and the free companies they hire march out to take land, cattle, gold, and towns from one another, and every autumn they stop. Other lands think the [[Septimes]] are bloodthirsty, or at least calculating, and Septimes take that as a compliment. War is how rulers prove their wisdom, generals their cleverness, and everyone the blessing of [[Mavros]].

The fighting goes back to the Great Retreat. The elves ruled this warm, rich peninsula from Valera, and when they vanished overnight a dozen generals and mages each declared themselves emperor. The cities have been fighting over who inherited the "Southern Throne" ever since. There are rules to it: a season that runs from Sowing to the Harvest, six just causes for war, honorable surrenders before a siege, and prisoners held for ransom rather than killed. Anyone attacking after the harvest is just a bandit.

The last ten years have shaken all of that. The sea god Nethus was freed in Kammae and took [[Hecate]] as his wife. The [[The Mharoti Empire|Mharoti]] conquered Illyria next door (now [[Rumela]]), and a combined fleet from Capleon, Kammae, and Triolo, led by an avatar of Nethus, destroyed the Mharoti armada at the Battle of the Golden Wave. Kammae then seized the north of Capleon for dealing with the dragons, a sickness called the black strangles has hit Trombei's horses, and two months ago Mharoti dragons burned a city on Kyprion. Ambassadors from every city now sit in Valera talking, for the first time anyone can remember, about an alliance.

## A Short History

The elves shaped much of this rich, warm peninsula: its roads, great buildings, harbors, and monuments. Under the Ostrellian dynasty their empire reached out from the [[Arbonesse]] along the fey roads to the [[Despotate of the Ruby Sea|Ruby Sea]], to Valera, and over much of [[The Ironcrags]]. When Emperor Jorgyn stepped out of the world and called the Great Retreat, the empire vanished, and the Seven Cities suffered worse than most. The humans and others who had relied on elven armies, magic, and even food and water suddenly had no masters, resented but useful, and things fell apart fast around Valera.

Each city found its own way back to some kind of security, farming, and law, and each saw itself as the worthier heir. The elven governors had played the cities' rivalries skillfully, and with the elven peace gone, war ran riot. The worship of Mavros spread far beyond the soldiers and sentries who had always followed him. The wars have gone on for centuries. Peace treaties are signed when coin and supplies run low and fall apart with every fresh influx of money and mercenaries in spring. The hatreds run deep, and citizens of one city have little patience for the boasts of another. Yet when one city grows too strong, or an outside power threatens, the others find enough in common to unite and prove to Mavros and the world that no one is better at war.

## The Season of War

The Seven Cities revel in war and depend on it. Custom and law limit its destruction, give it a season, make it a source of status for generals and nobles, and make it a source of plunder and tribute from weaker states. Fighting a war properly means keeping to its forms: the mustering, the blessing of a company, the taking of plunder, the ransoming of hostages, and the limits of the season. None of this means anything to the Mharoti of Rumela, whose legions fight to win.

### The Six Just Causes

Every declaration of war cites one of six traditional causes, though in truth most wars grow from old feuds between neighbors:

1. To seize territory to which one has a proper claim.
2. To secure the blessings of [[Mavros]].
3. To avenge an insult to a ruler or a people.
4. To force payment of tribute, sometimes read as plunder.
5. To support an ally.
6. To punish a city for supporting the Mharoti. This cause is new, added in the ten years since Illyria fell.

### Free Companies

Free companies gather in Springmelt, or at the latest in Sowing. Dwarven companies from the Ironcrags muster early to march south, while minotaurs from the south wait until the muddiest roads are clear. Companies bound for Friula, Triolo, and Trombei assemble in the Canton of Melana, and those serving Capleon, Kammae, and Valera gather outside the walls of Valera near the Fist of Mavros, an auspicious place.

A company may be a dozen sell-swords with a few horses or hundreds strong. Most mercenaries join an established or newly forming company rather than dealing with employers directly, though a few specialists make a living alone. A legitimate company must draw up articles naming its company, rules, captain, division of spoils, cause, and length of service, and every member signs. Those who don't sign in spring get only half shares. Most then have a priest of Mavros bless the venture, though companies from Kammae ask Hecate, and dwarven companies ask [[Perun]], [[Volund]], or even [[Loki]]. Every company wears a badge so its members know one another.

Captains win status and wealth, and they also become targets for capture, assassination, bribery, and enchantment. A lieutenant or sergeant takes over if a captain falls, but many companies are held together by one strong leader, and the enemy knows it. After their first season, few mercenaries wonder why captains take twenty shares to their one. Triolo, Capleon, and Trombei raise sea companies on the same lines.

The season begins on the first day of Sowing or after the Rites of Spring, depending on weather and pay, and ends with the Harvest, when the levies must go home to bring in the crops. The faithful of Mavros call any attack after that pure banditry. A few companies keep fighting until First Fogs, a "bandit season" most nobles frown on, when companies that found no worthwhile plunder try to take something on the way home.

### Plunder, Ransom, and Surrender

Unlike the warriors of the [[Rothenian Plain]] or the goblins of the [[Western Wastes|Wastes]], Septime armies rarely kill civilians or prisoners, and they rarely hold prisoners long, since ransom or parole is quicker. Generals offer a city honorable surrender before a siege, and tribute can buy a treaty. The point of war is the advantage it wins, and if clever words and intimidation carry the day, the glory still counts. Wiping out a neighbor defeats the purpose, so surrender does not mean extinction, though every loss is a humiliation and may end a dynasty or begin years of tribute. Capturing a foe and extorting money from his city is better business than killing one more soldier.

The rules are not justice. Brutality happens. Prisoners are sometimes killed to make a point, usually those who refused to surrender, fought as partisans, or attacked villagers, and commanders rarely show mercy to a stubborn foe with little ransom value. Victorious commanders offer captured officers and nobles back to their families, cities, or lords, and the ransom goes to the company that caught them, shared like any spoils.

### The Campaign

After the mustering, armies gather supplies and march to the border. A declaration of war opens hostilities, and then comes one of four things:

| Action | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Small raid | A quick strike across the border. |
| Great raid | The cavalcade or chevauchée, the nearest the Septimes come to all-out war: an army devastates a region to force tribute or move a border, burning houses and mills, pillaging open villages, driving people from their homes, and destroying crops and orchards. Killing villagers breaks the traditions of Mavros, but armies do murder those who resist foraging or insult soldiers. |
| Siege | Needed for castles and walled towns, and fairly rare, since siege engines and magic are expensive, patience runs short, and disease and hunger kill many if a siege drags on. |
| Formal battle | The most common large engagement. Because musters are slow, wars are declared, and armies march by well-known roads, two armies always find each other. Battle opens with a challenge and retort between commanders and is fought by daylight, though night battles do happen. The winners take captives and the arms and armor of the dead. The losing commander concedes the field or surrenders, and his army may retreat intact to its camp while diplomats negotiate terms and exchange ransoms and hostages. |

### Alliances and Diplomats

Strength of arms wins the field, but only diplomacy secures what blood has gained. Septime diplomats are masters of careful phrasing, one-sided concessions, and subtle loopholes, and cities that cannot turn a victory into a political win soon fade. With aiding an ally a just cause for war, knowing whom to support, ignore, and betray, and when, matters greatly. No alliance lasts, except against a foreign invader or a city grown too strong. Only Friula tries to win more by diplomacy than by violence, and even Friulans go adventuring when it pays. No city wants peace in the sense of an end to all conflict. Priests of [[Lada]] and Ceres preach moderation, but the Order of Mavros always pushes for war, and the few who give their lives to peace are treated as children or harmless lunatics.

## Coins and Trade

Triolo does much of its trade north up the Glass Road to [[Zobeck]] and beyond, so the Triolan gold ducat is a standard coin far outside the Seven Cities. The Valeran ducat is much debased, and the silver mark and hackgold of the North are rare here. Rarer still, and worth twice a gold coin, are orichalcum coins, minted long ago by the elves and before them by the Ankeshelians. They are accepted everywhere, and are now minted only by the [[Grand Duchy of Dornig]] and, in some years, the Ironcrag cantons.
