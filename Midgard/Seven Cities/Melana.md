Melana sits in the forested foothills between [[The Ironcrags]], the [[Magdar Kingdom]], [[Verrayne]], and [[Triolo]], and holds the eastern gateway to the [[Seven Cities]], including the Lowland Road to [[Zobeck]]. It is the armory and quartermaster of the Seven Cities. Its archers and pikes are feared, its cavalry is not, and during its spring Armoring Fair the whole city smells of oil and iron.

Two censors rule, traditionally one human and one dwarf, chosen every five years by thirty of the richest citizens. The current pair want opposite things. The isolationist Massaro is negotiating with the Ironcrags, the [[Magdar]], and Verrayne to keep Melana out of foreign wars, while Voller has turned the forges to weapons, armor, and magic items stored in hidden vaults.

The reason is the Undercity beneath the streets, where Melana's dwarves live. They descend from monarchist outcasts of the free Ironcrag cantons and believe a dwarven king should rule the Ironcrags again. They see Voller as the king of prophecy, and they mean for him to lead them home. The human majority above ground is frightened of where that will lead.

## The Censors

Ancient prophecies of a king's return draw nearer to fulfillment, and Melana prepares for the greatness it believes is coming. Its usual grumbling has split into two camps since the dwarf Seppo Voller was elected censor. The dwarves and kobolds of the Undercity look to the Free Cantons as a kingdom to liberate, while the human majority above ground fights to stay free of foreign entanglements. Both sides know nothing will be the same.

The thirty electors are chosen by a complicated formula that favors the richest citizens, and merchants, bankers, artisans, and even mercenary captains compete fiercely for the seats, since the censors reward their supporters with rich contracts. The current pair could hardly be more different. The expansionist Voller, the Lord Smith, oversees a huge increase in the making of weapons, armor, and magic items, while the isolationist Jacopo Massaro bargains with ambassadors from the Free Cantons, the Magdar, and Verrayne. With the censors at odds, Melana is pulled two ways at once.

## Melana, City of Iron

The capital is an architectural marvel at the meeting of three trade roads in the Ironcrag foothills, with easy access to the Tijino Pass. Behind its massive wall, wide flagstone streets run to many markets and courtyards, the tall, slender towers of villas reach into the sky, and the domed, colonnaded Censors' Palace looks out over the Great Market and the entrance to the Undercity. The human majority, living mostly above ground, resents the ever-dominant dwarves and fears that Voller and the muttering of prophecies will drag the canton into a war only the Undercity wants. Many put their faith in Massaro to defend human interests.

### The Undercity

Beneath the paved streets lie the tunnels and halls of the dwarves. Mine shafts sunk deep into the earth give the city iron, copper, coal, and richer ores, and a maze of workshops, forges, homes, and mines runs for miles. Lately its forges have turned from tools and jewelry to weapons, armor, and powerful magic items, meant for the day the dwarves march to retake the Ironcrags.

The first dwarves of Melana were monarchist outcasts from the democratic Free Cantons of [[The Ironcrags]]. Their descendants still hold to the older traditions of lost [[Legendary Places of the North|Nordheim]] and believe a dwarven king should rule the Ironcrags, a view their free cousins find mad at best. Unlike some cantons, Melana values every dwarf's skill, and its women take on any role they choose. Its warmest friends among the cantons are Bareicks, Gunnacks, and Nordmansch. The jubilant dwarves see Voller's rise as prophecy fulfilled and look to the Lord Smith to lead them home. To most of the Undercity, the canton has only one leader, and it is Seppo Voller. The Silver Crown Tavern, deep in the Undercity, where no lamp has ever burned and few humans dare go, is the favorite haunt of monarchists and their kobold allies, who gather there to grumble about the humans and plan for the king's return.

### The King's Prophecy

When the deposed King Ruggeson the Golden fled the Free Cantons, he brought his blind sister, the dwarven oracle Relvina, author of the Prophecy of the King. Most scholars dismiss it as the ravings of a grief-stricken woman: a long, rambling jumble of half-told stories, odd observations, and nonsense, with brief flashes of prophecy scattered through it. The censors banned it long ago, and it now survives only in fragments passed from one fanatical follower to the next. Some 453 fragments are known. Among the most famous:

> The lizard dog sits upon the oversized throne, that be the first sign of fulfillment.
>
> In the halls of stone, the castoff leans northward and carries hope in his pouch.
>
> As the hammer's star rises, the city below gathers winter fruit for the spring march.

The monarchists say a tomb hidden in the hills of central Melana holds the remains of Ruggeson the Golden, the dwarf who should have ruled all the Ironcrags, and that his descendant, Seppo Voller, meets his most loyal followers there to consult the spirits and plan the campaign to retake the Ironcrags. The humans above would dearly like to know where it is.

### The Warren Guild

The kobolds live in warrens and natural caves the dwarves found while expanding the Undercity, and for many decades they were treated as little more than animals, set to digging in dangerous places, mucking out privies, and hauling ore. Ten years ago the kobold Vinzlo was elected censor and brought in a new respect for his people. Many still treat them unfairly, but Melana's kobolds have risen far, above all through the Warren Guild, a loose organization of spies, trap makers and finders, elite couriers, and now and then assassins. After his term Vinzlo took over the guild and was crowned archking, king-above-other-kings, by his people.

## Tolmezo and the Floating Roads

The busy town of Tolmezo stands at the edge of the marshes south of Melana and is the first stop on the Floating Roads, wooden causeways fixed by steel rings to stone pillars sunk in the swamp, which float and can be crossed in any season or weather into [[Trombei]] and along the Triolan border. After decades of decline, Melana has begun an ambitious project to restore them. Under Marsh Warden Sabine Felderol, teams of human and dwarven crafters work from platform to platform, slowed by the swamp itself and by hydras, lizardfolk, and the Ladies of the Wreck, a coven of cannibal hags.
