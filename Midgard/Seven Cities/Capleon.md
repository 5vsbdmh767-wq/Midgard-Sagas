Capleon grew rich on bankers, merchants, and the spice, salt, and tea trade, and once called itself the Barony of Capleon, Cindass, and [[Roshgazi]]. Its Baron's Trade Fair is famous for its spices, and for espionage and assassinations. Lately everything has gone wrong for it. Baron Cazagoza tried to buy protection from his rivals by courting [[The Mharoti Empire]], and after [[Rumela|Illyria]] fell and the dragons' armada was destroyed, it came out that Capleon's temple of the dragon god Seggotan had plotted against Nethus. Mobs drove the dragonkin out of the city, the Mharoti were furious, and [[Kammae Straboli]] marched in and took the northern half of the barony.

The baron throws festivals and collects artifacts as if nothing were wrong, while running the city councils through bribery, blackmail, and the dungeons of Lion's Rock. Most of the city, all the minotaurs, and the followers of Mavros oppose him, along with his own cousin Ines, who demands an inquiry into his dealings, and Johennes of Salzbach, who personally captured the sultan's admiral at the Golden Wave. Capleon is never far from a riot.

## The Baron and His Rivals

Baron Raúl Cazagoza, the "Southern Lion," shows the strain of age and worry, and seems mostly interested in pretending his fortunes are as bright as ever, throwing festivals, buying ancient artifacts, and filling his glittering court with scholars, bards, and adventurers. Behind the drowsy face he works furiously to hold the barony together and win back its lost lands. He still fears his talks with the Mharoti have gone too far to drop, while ambassadors from Triolo and Kammae warn Capleon against any deal with the dragons and heroes and bankers alike call for his removal.

## Capleon, City of Peace

Capleon is rich from trade, and trade has taught it sophisticated banking. Its bankers are shrewd and ruthless, though many buy private armies, spend fortunes on favor, or win and lose fortunes on risky ventures. The city has always called itself a safe place to do business, but that stability may be failing. Once nearly half its bankers came from the Mharoti Empire or southern lands like [[Nuria Natal]]. With the dragonkin driven out, new opportunities are opening for native Capleoni.

Its other towers hold the mint, a garrison of loyal troops, and the ambassadors of Nuria Natal, [[Trombei]], Valera, Triolo, and even distant Salzbach and [[Zobeck]]. |

It confounds visitors from simpler markets. The great trading families, the Caronova, Frulaneti, Legatay, Omjaya, and Ayeshi, commission captains to bring in particular goods or back luxuries on speculation, and the investors always make their profit even when the people they back do not. |
| Southern Quarter | When chaos struck the southern deserts, minotaurs fled to Capleon by the thousands, along with shaven-headed [[Nurians]] and the olive-skinned merchant women of Cindass. Their quarter is rich, complex, and many-tongued, and the temples of [[Thoth-Hermes]] and the Horned Moon Goddess hold sway. Blood and family count most, and gangs fight a shadowy war for its streets, led by the Nurian mage-assassin Nebit Kanika, the fanatical Grandmaster Hermano Garduna of [[Mammon]], and the kobold street king Quelleck Redhand. Only the minotaurs stay clean of it. When young hotheads get out of hand, heavy fines fall on their elders and their streets. |
| Teasellers' Row | The Guild of Poisoners, the weapon Capleon keeps sheathed and silent, which makes the barony a byword for smiling treachery and agonizing death. Bankers, failing captains, foreign spies, troublesome ambassadors, minotaur corsairs, and preachers of sedition have all met its poisons, its savage hunting dogs, or its mysteriously sinking ships, and it is whispered that nobles who support the baron too little meet them too. Its leader is Caliana the Teaseller, a woman long experienced in poison and death. Some say she is a witch, others that she has a pact with devils. |
| Blood of the Sea Arena | An amphitheater built over sea caves and blowholes that can be opened to wash salt water through it, used weekly and at festivals for bullfights, minotaur warrior rites, and combats between prisoners and the best of Capleon's legions. The next high tide washes away the mess. Its master of fighters is Captain Shushu, a braggart from Nuria Natal whose blade matches his boasts, and the wagering is often ruinous. The baron despises it as a waste but knows it lets humans and minotaurs blow off steam, and the guards deal harshly with any mob. |
| The Tumbles | The ruins of the Temple of Seggotan, the Mharoti water god, which once served sailors, fishers, dragonkin, and merfolk at the harbor's edge. Six years ago, as High Priest Jabara prepared to bless the ships sailing to meet the Mharoti armada, Nethus rose from the harbor, denounced the temple in a voice heard across the city for conspiring against him with the Mharoti, and threw down its green- and blue-veined marble towers. A shantytown of tents now hosts a thriving black market on the rubble, and below, in the surviving tunnels, Master Durant Grasa, the Lord of the Tumbles, holds his court of smugglers and rogues. |
| Temple of Night and Sea | Along a lovely canal rise the bones of a new temple to Nethus and [[Hecate]], which will be the largest in the [[Seven Cities]] when finished. Artisans, wizards, and priests build it side by side with tools, spells, and prayers. It is overseen by the minotaur High Elder Potnia Adrasta, leader of Capleon's minotaurs, who deals with obstacles by charging through them. |
