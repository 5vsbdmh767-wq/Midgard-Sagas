Valera was the southern capital of the elven empire, and it still thinks of itself as first among the [[Seven Cities]]. It is an empire in name only now, but it has old secrets, elven palaces, and the most devoted servants of Mavros anywhere outside [[Kammae Straboli|Kammae]]. Many families give a son to Mavros, to the priesthood or, for the poor, the legions.

The Imperial Senate officially governs, but young Emperor Loki V holds the real power. For fifteen years the city was ruled by his regent, Carridoc, the first man ever to call himself emperor after the elves left, who had hidden for centuries inside an elven mirror until Loki's father let him out. When Loki came of age he took the throne back in a counter-coup during a winter snowstorm. Carridoc kept his head and his post as Imperial Marshal and now schemes through the Senate.

Loki has made Valera the center of talks for a Septime alliance against [[The Mharoti Empire]]. He shelters the deposed Mharoti sultana in his Adelian Palace, with her dragon bodyguards sunning on the roof, and the two of them are courting [[Nuria Natal]] and the Wind Lords, the only powers ever to throw back the dragons. Free companies hired by Valera, Kammae, and [[Capleon]] muster each spring outside the city at the Fist of Mavros.

## The Emperor and the Marshal

Officially the Imperial Senate governs Valera. In truth Emperor Loki V holds the reins, and since his Winter Coup the Senate is little more than an advisory council. The army and the people are loyal to him, but he must still steer carefully around senators scheming to win back their lost prestige.

Chief among the embittered is the Imperial Marshal, General Carridoc Joderik Kastellan. More than 450 years ago Carridoc was the first human to declare himself emperor after the elves left, and when it all fell apart he escaped into a magical elven mirror. Generations later the brash, idle Loki IV, the current emperor's father, found the mirror and released him, and died of poison soon after. As Imperial Marshal and Regent, Carridoc truly ruled Valera for fifteen years, working the Senate through secret access to the Imperial Treasury and tricks of the palace enchantments, and he assumed it would go on after young Loki came of age. He was wrong. Soon after his crowning, Loki V, who had learned a great deal watching his regent, staged his own counter-coup in a late winter snowstorm and took full control. Carridoc avoided punishment, but he is now an adviser and castellan rather than a ruler. He still commands the legions, though in more than a decade he has never won their personal loyalty. Nearly all of them back the emperor by oath and by choice.

### The Alliance

The emperor's great work is an alliance of the Septime states against the Mharoti. He hosts ambassadors at the Adelian Palace, among them the deposed Sultana Casmara Azrabahir of [[The Mharoti Empire]], and together they are weaving a web of treaties between the feuding cities and gathering help, ordinary and magical, from far away. They are negotiating with envoys of the Wind Lords of the [[Dominion of the Wind Lords|Stone Desert]] and of [[Nuria Natal]], the only two nations ever to throw back the full force of the Dragon Empire. An accord settling the cities' age-old quarrels could see the Septime states turn their combined might on the Mharoti for the first time.

Ambassadors from the other Septime states, and even envoys from [[Zobeck]] and far [[Magocracy of Allain|Bemmea]], walk the palace halls. The strangest pair are the Wind Lords' envoy, the dark and brooding [[Tamasheq]] priest Izem ibn-Tariq, emissary of the Spider Prophet, and the Nurian ambassador, the ravenfolk lich sorcerer Akara Amasis, First Charioteer. The two are often seen walking and talking together, proof that the isolationist Wind Lords and the aggressive god-kings of Nuria can find common ground.

## The Adelian Palace

The two hundred rooms of the Adelian Palace are a fine example of early elven building, with enormous windows, open stairwells, mirrors, hidden shadow doors, and magical lights that follow anyone walking its halls at night. Its history of intrigue, seduction, and imperial power weighs on every room. The emperor has ordered a massive renovation that is still going on, with armies of builders, painters, and artists at work in all three wings while they are lived in.

Secret tunnels called the Ratlines, or the Silent Gate, link the palace to the Temple City, the headquarters of the legions, the docks on the River Roen, and several smaller passages. The warded ways open only to elves, the elfmarked, or magic. They are meant for the imperial family and their closest guards, but in practice the secret is known throughout the palace and beyond.

## The Legions

Valera's human legions, among them the Seventh, Ninth, and Eleventh, stay near the capital, with others on the borders of Trombei and Kammae. The human captains and soldiers carry on the elven traditions of strong infantry and archery, with ranks of spears and pikes and disciplined close formations, though some old elven arts, such as the "running archer" skirmisher, cannot be reproduced. It serves well when welded to the strength of Mavros and the zeal of the Returners.

The centaurs make the Valeran army truly formidable, helping it hold off both the larger forces of [[Trombei]] and the fanatical armies of [[Kammae Straboli|Kammae]], which press Valera between them. [[Centaurs|Centaur]] service to the emperor and the elves is one of the few threads that runs unbroken from the old empire, and the emperor's own centaur bodyguard, the Rhoetian Guard, is especially fearsome. The minotaurs are the finest infantry Valera can hire, for their strength, heavy armor, and double axes in the [[Kyprion]] tradition. The newest additions are dragonkin edjet heavy infantry and armored lizard riders lent by Sultana Casmara, who now train human levies in their swift striking tactics.

## Valera, the Temple City

The seat of imperial power rings with hammers and builders. Since taking the throne Loki has launched a great rebuilding, and new neighborhoods are rising from the ruins of the old. Yet most of the temples to a dozen great gods stand abandoned. The elven gods, [[Sarastra]] and the forest twins [[Yarila & Porevit]], have neither priests nor followers here, though now and then an offering turns up on a temple step. The great temple of Bright Honor of Valeresh, the elven war god, has no priesthood, though some soldiers pray there in memory of the elves. Many smaller temples of [[Baccholon]] and Holda have been looted or burned, and the shrines of the abandoned elven gods seem to frown down from the hilltop. The one exception is the temple of Nethus, which the sea god's followers are rebuilding.

The greatest temple in the city is the whitewashed, gold-leafed Golden Pantheon of [[Mavros]], where his wife, [[Marena]] the Red, is also worshiped. The High Pontifex Miter Konya Mellas leads the church of Mavros here, and every year he sends soldiers to escort pilgrims to the [[Western Wastes#The Seat of Mavros|Seat of Mavros]] in the Western Wastes. Young, bold adventurers can find work on these escorts and earn blessings for it. The Order of Mavros keeps its headquarters at the Fist of Mavros, where new officers receive their commissions and recruits are inducted into Valera's military traditions.

### Cults of the Return

Hundreds of years after the Great Retreat, many humans still believe their glorious, perfect, aristocratic elven masters will one day ride back down the Calloggian Way to restore the empire. The emperor discourages these cults of the Return, since they distract from the present. Some are led by charismatic figures such as Obertro Tollis, master of the Hands of [[Charun]], who are said to use their followers' faith for their own gain. Other Returners are sincere. They work to restore the city's imperial glory, half-worship the elfmarked, and fight in the front ranks of the legions to prove themselves worthy of the Valeran lords. Many stories tell of Returners who followed elven banshees or shadow fey sorcerers to unhappy ends. Most Valerans think the Returners more than a little mad, but their dream appeals to the poor and the hopeless. Most priests of Mavros pity them, and the two groups brawl from time to time.
