The Dragon Empire spreads across the Dragoncoil Mountains and the lowlands along the Middle Sea. About 350 years ago the red dragon [[Mharot]] grew tired of guarding his gold alone and talked the neighboring dragons into a pact, with the kobold tribes swearing themselves to the dragons in exchange for protection. It has been at war ever since. Every spring the sultan's decree sends the nobles out to conquer new lands, and the legions march with dragons overhead, fire giants and ogres in the ranks, and elementalist priests of the Four Elemental Temples behind them.

It is one of the few lands where humans are second-class. The scaled folk call everyone without scales jambuka, "jackals," and they farm, mine, serve, and fill the levies. Above them come the kobolds, then the dragonborn soldiers and gentry, then the drakes and lesser dragons, and at the top the nine great dragon lords, the morza, who each rule a province like a duke with a private army. The dragons never trusted one of their own with the throne, so they gave it to a human sultan or sultana instead. Harkesh, the capital, is huge, rich, and crowded, with a Grand Bazaar of a thousand stalls and ambassadors from a hundred nations.

The last few years have gone badly for the empire. Seven years ago it conquered Illyria and renamed it [[Rumela]], and killed the [[Magdar]] king at Marroc's Field. Then it lost an entire fleet to [[Triolo]] and [[Kyprion]] and an entire legion, dragons included, to [[Ishadia]] and Khandiria. Three generals overthrew the human Sultana Casmara Azrabahir, who fled to [[Valera]], and one of them, the dragonborn Ozmir Al-Stragul, came out of it as the Dread Sultan, the first dragonkin ever to hold the title. He is raising new armies, building new fleets, and taxing his people hard to pay for it. The empire is at war with the [[Magdar Kingdom]], [[Nuria Natal]], Ishadia, and Khandiria all at once, and few would bet against it.

The empire's symbol is a red dragon rampant on white, with a red crescent and two green stars. It trades in iron, carpets, silk, saffron, pepper, silver, jewels, red and blue dyes, perpetual lanterns, compasses, books and scrolls, ivory, pearls, hunting birds, lizards, and falcons. Its schools of learning are in many ways more advanced than those of the [[Seven Cities]] or the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]], its cities often outnumber whole northern nations, and its bazaars teem with thieves. Its people are proud, but there are signs of hubris, zealotry, and decay: cultists kidnap travelers as sacrifices to great fiery Baal or to local gods like V'ashra the Tormentor and Forty-Fingered [[Nakresh]], sects of every kind preach and brawl in the streets, and unrest in the priesthood has toppled more than one sultan. The worship of dark gods such as [[Mammon]] and [[Vardesain]] never quite goes away.

## The Dread Sultan
Ozmir Al-Stragul, the scarred, cunning Dread Sultan, is a fine general and an utterly ruthless politician; his titles are Lord of the Legions, Greatest of Warriors, Master of the Ocean Waves, Marshall of the Clouds and Breezes, and Keeper of the Empire's Secrets. After ousting the young Sultana Casmara Azrabahir (who escaped on dragonback to Marea and then to exile with the young Emperor Loki in Valera), his two fellow conspirators disappeared. He claims divine sanction and proclaims a new Age of Scales, while taking credit for Rumela, the conquest of the predecessor he said had lost the favor of Azuran. The sultan appoints judges, makes laws, grants and strips titles, collects taxes, distributes bread and tribute, raises the armies, and keeps the morza cooperating; his assassins threaten satraps who step out of line. Without him the empire would be eight dragon kingdoms at war. The dragons care little that the sultan is now dragonborn, since it still isn't one of them, but the jambuka find it chilling to see the scaled folk take yet another office, and the Kah-Vizier's heavy new taxes and the morza's demands for conscripts and labor are squeezing the lower classes.

He means to strike back. After a jambuka mob in [[Capleon]] killed or drove out its dragonkin and tore apart the temple of Seggotan, he invaded Kyprion and holds one town taken from the minotaurs, but the empire lacks the ships for more, for now.

In the empire you know where you stand, and people see it in your face.

The Mharoti use "dragonkin" for all the scaled races (kobolds, drakes, dragonborn, lizardfolk, even wyverns and dracotaurs) and "dragonborn" for the large scaled people who are its heavy troops, officers, and lesser nobles. Outsiders often confuse wind, silver, and gold dragons, or red and flame dragons. Mixed dragon and human blood is a matter of great delicacy: the poets sing of doomed love between dragon princes and hideous humans, but in reality half-blooded children are usually sacrificed or sent to hopeless regiments, though some are acknowledged by wind dragons in the more tolerant provinces of Betik, Marea, and Harkesh. The old sultana was of mixed blood, acknowledged by her draconic mother, and her heritage was used to justify her overthrow.

## The Legions
The empire speaks of its "20 victorious legions and 5 fleets," and the wise don't ask for details. The real count is 17 legions and 3 fleets: one legion was annihilated at the Battle of Wheeling Angels, when the ophanim of Ishadia struck down at least 4 dragons and 10,000 soldiers were paraded before the Great Raj of Khandiria; one was disbanded for excessive loyalty to the sultana; and one drowned at the Battle of Seggotan's Tears, which the Triolans call the Battle of the Golden Wave. A fourth fleet is already on the slips. A legion of 20,000 to 30,000 is two-thirds human and gnoll levies and kobold spears and archers, with dragonkin heavy infantry and cavalry, ogre shieldbreakers of Kaa'nesh, wyvern knights, oliphaunts, elementalists, and a flight of fire dragons. Armies live off herds driven along and seized, and dragons can always forage across the border. The empire has tried Magdar-style war wagons, called *arbasi*, roofed in tin, but its strength is light cavalry and skirmishers who can't use them well, and they take the wheels off at night.

## Dragoncoil Ley Magic
The ley lines of the Dragoncoil Mountains are elemental: fire lines and ice lines are common but fleeting, stone and ocean lines more enduring, and the most powerful peak nodes and coiling updraft lines are best left to those who can fly and cast at once. The Mharoti call shadow roads and fey roads the dragon roads, or the Gates of Veles.

## Seggotan's Demesne
The sea is the empire's tenth province, the domain of Seggotan and of the dragon turtle [[Storros]] the Sea Sage, leading admiral of the fleet, who prefers knowledge to valor; some of his children are more eager to fight. War galleys and barges carrying wind and fire dragons make up the rest of the navy. Storros is a gifted diviner and neutral counselor, consulted on fateful decisions, justice, and etiquette, who lairs in the Deep Blue Vault, an underwater library shelved with hundreds of sunken wrecks, and questions the carved figureheads in his Hall of Figureheads about their voyages. He pays well for unusual finds.

## The Nine Morza
The Dragon Lords hold most of the empire's wealth and think in years and decades; if they are displeased, the sultan may find himself dead or exiled. Mharot and his companions are survivors of the twelve who signed the original Compact over 300 years ago, plus newcomers. Poets praise every province, and the most beautiful province is always the one a traveler happens to be standing in.

### Mharot the Founder, Kalpostan
First Lord of the Empire, Duke of All Flames, Master of the Compact, and the empire's eldest and most powerful elementalist (see [[Mharot]]). He rarely leaves the old backwater of Kalpostan, his black wings streaked with grey and his belly scales gone glossy gold with age; kobolds and even dragonkin feel compelled to worship him in his presence. He sleeps most of the year in his Fire Seal Mountain cavern, waking once or twice to devour a herd, appoint emissaries, speak the Words of Transformation over new drakes, and advise the sultan each spring as the voice of the Compact. The runes of the Signer's Tower in his valley name the twelve founders, with each new morza carved below. While he sleeps, self-proclaimed dream oracles come and go and are usually executed; the one true interpreter is Enver Demir Al-Kaa'Nesh, the Firespeaker, a small man over 60, scarred by torture in his youth, who sees dragon dreams through lotus and poppy and lives in the Dreamer's Loft. He refuses apprentices, "waiting for a worthy shepherd girl," sometimes forgets he is human, and the morza worry what will happen when he dies.

Kalpostan is the rural, pastoral heartland of herds, shepherds' huts, snowy peaks, glacial lakes, superb soaring winds, and tiny mountain temples reachable only by air, where some say the dragonkin were first made. Its Shimmering Temple of Baal is so hot its heart is molten, ringed by boiling springs and served by 50 fire masters, each a child of Mharot.

### Satarah al-Beldestani, Harkesh
Keeper of the City and Castellan of Harkesh. Once a legendary dragonborn paladin of Seggotan, she climbed the Sky Stairs of [[Beldestan]] and came back a star drake, wilier and far more cynical; divination confirmed it was really her, returned with a mission from Seggotan and Veles-Beyond-the-Void. She founded the Cult (or Order) of the Empty Star, devoted to ancient knowledge and the "blood truth" of arcane heritage, which has replaced much of the old priesthood at the Portal of the Void and praises "the Prophet Satarah" in terms the other morza barely tolerate. She agitates for war with Nuria Natal, and her ties to void cultists and void dragons make her frightening for one so small.

Harkesh, the Golden City, spreads along the southern shore of the Mavressa Strait and must be fed by daily grain ships from Marea and Nuria and herds driven down from the mountains; even its water comes partly by aqueduct. Courtiers hold tiny lairs and walled gardens throughout it. Its harbor holds a thousand ships, where Rubeshi slavers, Friulan librarians, Khandirian priest-nobles, Bemmean apprentices, Nurian smiths, and Triolan merchants rub shoulders, along with the Seggosi, draconic sailors said never to set foot on land.

Other figures at court include Vorel, a silver dragon who is Mistress of the Royal Harem and organizes the sultan's celebrations; Aban Qalam, "Wyrm That Walks," the former ambassador to [[Magocracy of Allain|Bemmea]] who turned himself into a living swarm of roaches, beetles, and flies while experimenting with his draconic blood and is said to use a fey road between Bemmea and Harkesh; and Jabbir, the rakish faerie dragon herald and master illusionist.

### Ateshah, Gizmiri
Padashah of the Flaming City, Heart of the Desert, a dragon whose fiery presence creates the desert around him wherever he goes. He has not left the temple of Al-Ajach in Sarkland for decades, where only fiery creatures can survive him, and the Mharoti fear the prophesied day he will. He seeks ancient knowledge to extend his presence over all Midgard. Gizmiri is red sand and the bones of Khandirian armies, dotted with sacked Khandirian cities and the burial mounds of dragons slain by Khandirian sorcery. Sarkland, the City of Dust, sells everything to everyone from dragon-scaled bazaars, breeds war lizards and hunting lizards, and weaves the famous Sarklandi carpets, enchanted ones taking decades; rare war carpets are dyed with the blood of the fallen. Nearby are the ruins of Kukkutarma, haunted by wraiths, mummies, and werebats, and of Harrapo-Dar, now just wells and a fortress-like caravanserai, the Mouth of Maraut.

### Ibbalan the Illustrious, Mezar
Scribe of the Pact and Eldest Morza, Mharot's great rival and co-founder of the Compact, who led its first armies. The first Dragon Lord to die of age, he had prepared: through Baal's power he gave up his flesh and his rest, and now wears his hoard as a gem-studded golden hide over his bones, with an orichalcum-horned crown and gilded death mask. Reclusive, he rules through envoys, and his audiences are executions that feed his soul-fire. The jambuka of Mezar fear him as a devil; the dragonkin revere him as loyalty beyond death. Mezar borders Nuria Natal and stands ready for war with it, guarded by legions living and dead, and controls the east-west trade routes. Its cities include Irkaly, seat of Ibbalan, with the Great Souk and the Elemental Academy; Efesis, the winter capital, whose empty necropolis's dead were raised and marched against Nuria; Mezar, the summer capital, known for wine, horses, and a jade temple of Seggotan; the naval port of Prezhan, a former minotaur city with the Ageless Spire of Seggotan and a Festival of Shells when Storros visits; and Dao-Klarjeti on the Ruby Sea, with its twin monasteries of Azuran and Khespotan.

### Lashmaraq Talshah, Hariz
Queen of Fury and Marshall of Mayhem, who has spent decades building a lean, ferocious, superbly equipped army and tolerates no waste or incompetence. Her spies watch the other lords, who fear she may someday judge them unnecessary. She lairs in a cavern sinkhole deep in the Lumera Forest and guards the northern and eastern borders against Beldestan, the Khazzaki, and the wild wind dragons of the Qaen. Hariz is poor, isolated plains and forest of nomadic centaurs, humans, and gnolls. Tabur, its only real city, sends caravans east to Cathay and Sikkim and north over the Plain. Kazan is a fortress-ferry town on the Lumera with a magical wall against horse raiders, and the red-stone fortress of Pahrolendi, home of the blue dragon quartermaster Nerazi, is the staging point for legions marching on Khandiria.

### Parsis the Hidden, Betik
One of only two gold wind dragons in the empire, Master of the Fields and Clouds and a military genius who rules his province closely through his Golden Circle of advisers and wanders his cities in disguise as a scholar, lawyer, or alchemist, loving debate and the sound of his own voice. He runs the empire's spy network with the Kah-Vizier. Lately the sultan's assassins have murdered several of his councilors, and rumors of hidden dragon-blooded children make the other morza distrust him. Betik is the arcane heart of the empire: universities, observatories, stolen magic, invisible castles, cloudtop fortresses, and ley lines branching off the Scholar's Path. Its crown is Ashadar, a captured flying city of Sikkim, home to Parsis's hoard and the Hidden University, whose wizard collegium is restricted to the dragon-blooded but whose six other schools admit anyone who passes the scholar's exam. Its other cities include Qiresh, a great Ruby Sea port of indigo cloth and new warships, and Uqmal the Azure City, famous for fire dancers, war lizards, and tea. Ruins abound: Nurduran, burned in the first conquests; Siyaz, emptied by a kobold alchemist's vampiric mists; and Xirdalan, sacked by Khandiria and declared cursed afterward.

### Rüzgar, Zaldiri
Lord of Fog and Hungers, a cave dragon who loves the heights though his wings are bones and tatters and he cannot fly ("only the weak seek the sheltering sky"). He cares for little but battle and meat, rules a thinly settled warfront against the Magdar and [[Perunalia]], and resents that Rumela went to Glauvistus. The sultan finds his bloodlust congenial. Many believe he is secretly allied with the [[White Goddess]] or Vardesain's ghouls. His Hollow Palace overlooks Zaldiri, a wholly Mharoti city riddled with tunnels and cisterns and home to the crystal-encrusted Telluric Cathedral of Khespotan. Valogrod, a captured Magdar fortress, is the staging point for raids, called "the Hungry Castle" for its lack of food. The swampy ruins of Pharos hold dark dragons and darker ghosts, and at burned-out Tirovec, a clan of kobolds is quietly rebuilding the mills.

### Yiraz Azah, Marea and the Islands
The Cloud Chaser, a silver dragon from the far North and devotee of Azuran who preached peace from the day she arrived. The fire dragons assassinated her silver dragon host, and she surprised them by claiming his mantle. She recently lost most of a legion at sea against Kyprion and Triolo, an irony not lost on her, and believes Kyprion belongs in her claws. Marea, conquered 110 years ago, is mostly human, related to Illyrians and Triolans, speaks Common as much as Draconic, and keeps its old faiths of [[Rava]], Nethus, [[Charun]], Apollon (Khors), and Ceres; even her temple to [[Boreas (North Wind)|Boreas]] is nearly empty. Since Nethus returned, his priests have come out of hiding to preach in the harbors, and several have been martyred for blasphemy against the sultan. Its places include Achillon, a human city of sculptors and accountants with a rare gearforged community; the new capital Tyrku, with its lemon groves and red-coated oxen; the silk city of Mistras Marea; and the naval fortresses of Methony and Rados.
