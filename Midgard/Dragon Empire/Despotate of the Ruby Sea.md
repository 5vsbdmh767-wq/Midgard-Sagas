The Despotate holds a peninsula on the Ruby Sea, south of the [[Rothenian Plain]], and lives by slaving. Every summer the Rubeshi ride out with wagons, nets, and branding irons to take new slaves for their fields, mines, and the oars of their war galleys. They call them bondservants. They sell to anyone, and their red-and-black sails are dreaded by every fishing village on the sea, including those of [[Perunalia]].

Veltrin, the Glittering King, rules from a palace whose walls glitter with what he insists is gemstone. He hides his face behind a jeweled mithral mask, drinks, rages, and trusts no one. Nobles who opposed him were stitched into flesh golems and now serve as his bodyguards. His captains each command a ship bound to them by blood and demonic pacts, and his own flagship, the Golden Bird, carries a bound demon as its figurehead.

When truly threatened, the Despot offers blood to the White Goddess and turns his slaves into a horde of hulking, fanged, green-skinned warriors who fight like demons and then waste away within a month or two. The [[The Mharoti Empire|Mharoti]] could crush the Despotate if they cared to, and the Dread Sultan has lately demanded tribute. Veltrin has ignored him.

The Rubeshi are black-haired and green-eyed and sell to anyone: the gnomes, the [[Vidim|Tsar of Vidim]], the priests of [[Baal-Hotep|Baal]]. They trade in slaves, horses, amber, furs, mead, and gemstones (chalcedony, carnelian, sard). The Despotate was founded by a single man, Veltrin the First, and has held its peninsula against centaur and [[Khazzaki]] hordes. Besides Reth-Saal (14,400), it holds the towns of Monk's Harbor and Uratu and the castles of Tokmarcale and Zinjancale.

## The Glittering King
Veltrin is steeped in wine and luxury, a drunkard with a great temper and deep paranoia. He mastered the demon figurehead of the *Golden Bird* about 25 years ago and murdered his wife at the ship's launch. He hosts gnoll mercenaries and even children or simulacra of the Master of Demon Mountain at court, speaks highly of Eldara, one of [[Baba Yaga]]'s daughters, and raids the shores of the sea, including Orkasa and Sephaya, and sometimes Kaa'nesh, Cogelu, or [[Parthia]] just to exercise his soldiers. Some say that under his jeweled mask his flesh has been regenerated so often it looks like a troll's hide; those who say so within earshot lose their tongues.

During the Boyar's Rebellion, a dozen captains and a general plotted to kill him, but he was warned by the Master of Demon Mountain and called his ship's demon ashore, which summoned a dozen more. He executed the plotters and stitched them into the first of the Elect, flesh golem bodyguards of unshakeable loyalty. His most powerful rivals have been "promoted" to join them since; there are more than 30, perhaps 50, in armor and magic weapons, and no coup has been tried in over 20 years. Many believe the Grand Marshall Zaganos the Red, a tall, emaciated tiefling with a monstrous arm, a black bardiche, and a cape of human skin, was the one who betrayed the plot, though none dare say so; some think he only bides his time.

## The Army of Madness
When truly threatened, which has happened twice (once by the Mharoti and once by an alliance of windrunner elves and Khazzaki), Veltrin performs the Ritual of Ravening, offering blood to the White Goddess to turn his slaves into hulking, fanged, pallid green warriors as strong as centaurs. Within days he has an army that fights like demons. Within a month or two they grow listless and die choking as their flesh wastes away, which is why he cannot conquer his neighbors this way.

## Captains and Their Ships
Veltrin's favorite nobles, the captains, hold lands and villages and each commands ships of the red-sailed fleet, magically bound to their owners by blood, demonic command, or spells that only the captains know. The slaves say a ship dies with its captain, and when one does, its demon tears free from the bloodstained prow, howling, as it sinks. No expense is spared on the ships: the *Red Griffon* has a bound troll at its prow, fed the weakest rower before each voyage to encourage the rest, and the *Moaning Monk* has a dragonkin elementalist bound to its mast to summon winds at night while the slaves rest. Clever captains reward loyal thralls with food, clothes, authority, rest, or a chance to pray. A rower can be bought for a crust of fresh bread and a cup of wine, and the despot knows it.

The *Golden Bird*'s figurehead is a demon draped in rusted chains, found in a wreck by Veltrin the Elder, who asked Baba Yaga how to command it, then went to the Master of Demon Mountain for a demon of his own and paid in blood and flesh for a hundred years. With a brass compass that finds other ships and a demon leading his boarders, he became the terror of the sea, and Reth-Saal was built on the labor of the slaves he took. The demon hates the despot and the Master, and takes its rage out on the Mharoti. The captains believe a single word from Veltrin can loose it entirely; whether a word exists to chain it again, they are less sure. A daughter of the Master of Demon Mountain is said to serve as Mistress of Coin, and chests of gold go north every spring.

## Reth-Saal, the City of Jewels
A city of pillars and whitewashed walls inside a stout wall of earth and timber with stone towers, surrounded by tended fields and siege-proof marsh, with a fine harbor and the lighthouse called Veltrin's Eye. The Carnidine Palace glitters with cut glass the despot calls gemstones. The Keepers of Order, a militia grown into guards, informers, and spies, tolerate no dissent. Its slave market supplies the Mharoti nobility, and the breaking of new slaves (branding, lashing, the cutting of toes and tongues) is a public spectacle on the waterfront, where opium and brandy are sold openly. Endless toasts to the king are the price of doing business, and failing to toast often enough is noted. It is impossible to be excessive; a common one runs, "To King Veltrin, may he live a thousand years and a thousand more!"

## Border Raids
The Rubeshi scout Khazzaki camps near the sea and along the great rivers for slaves. When the tribes are angry enough, they call on their centaur friends and fall on the Despotate in force in late summer and fall, bottling up its castles, burning villages and granaries, and driving off thousands of animals. Sometimes the Khazzaki hint to Perunalia that it's a good time to send a ship from Sephaya to free captives. The worst raids lose surprise, and the raiders end up as slaves themselves.
