Beldestan is a high, dry country of goats, ruins, and very valuable mines of mithral and steel. It was once the heart of a dwarven kingdom, but banditry and dark magic drove the dwarves out, and human hill tribes took the mines by making deals with darker powers. The noble houses feud and betray each other for sport, and slaving, kidnapping, bride-theft, and goat-stealing are ordinary life. Travelers are considered free labor.

Beldestan worships its dark gods openly and proudly. Human sacrifice is routine, and the priests of the Void, Sabateus, and stranger things expect blood and treasure for their blessings. The weak end up in the mines until they die, and the strong take what they can and leave.

The Sky Stairs of Beldestan are an ancient pilgrimage site, a monumental stair said to climb from the dust to the heavens. Prayers at the bottom are said to work. Very few climb further, past the giant eagles, the howling winds, and the undead.

The Satrapy's arms are three golden eyes on blue. It was once the heart of the dwarven Trakhan Kingdom; its ruling human houses are Bastun, Yllomir, Kluchiss, and Plendremin, who prefer to let slaves and hired gnoll and dwarf slavers work the mines. Those who can't protect their families are mocked as cowards. Darakhul monks and dhampir priests are rare but not unknown. Hundreds of tiny villages and crossroads towns guard their road taxes and odd traditions, from raising ebon frogs to trepanning blasphemers.

## Galna
The capital (population 10,200) holds the enigmatic Knotted Ley Line of Galna, gambling halls in the Cloud Temple of Azuran, the Blood Caverns of Vardesain, the Echoing Cathedral of Veles (whose [[The Mharoti Empire|Mharoti]] visitors leave quickly), and the Horned Temple of the Ebon Moon, where the monthly public rites of the Goat of the Woods always leave a few participants missing. At its heart stands the Seat of Beldes Wisdom, the marble palace of the autarch and his harem of 33 men, women, catfolk, gnolls, and (it is said) doppelgangers, some wives of status, some gifts from as far as [[Nuria Natal]], some unlucky travelers who caught his eye. Woven through it are the Indigo Halls of Sabateus, the Void-ridden god of stars and magic, home to more than 400 priests under Khatun Bibi Kaftar, sometimes called the Empress of Beldestan, who rules the realm's spiritual life with a stern love of order. The priests move through Living Doorways and mirrored halls under ceilings of gems and lapis, watched by the ghosts of priests buried in the walls.

## The Towers of Khubara
On Beldestan's northern border, the dwarven descendants of Trakhan live in a city of tall towers high in the steppes, paying nominal tribute to the Satrapy. Height of dwelling is status, and bridges let you cross the city without touching the ground. Its artisans breed the swiftest falcons in the world, small and red-breasted, and work turquoise, iron, mithral, and ada, the metal of the rare wave-washed steel called adanisk, light, flexible, and strong, with a rippling pattern; weapons of it can shatter part of a foe's armor or shield.
