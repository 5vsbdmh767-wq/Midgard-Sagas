South of the Pytonne Mountains live the last of the giants who were once slaves of the magocracies. They are long-lived and cannot rest when they die. Their ancestors' ghosts follow them, draining their strength and urging them to put their bodies back in the earth or under the balanced rock cairns that mark giant tombs. Every giant is possessed now and then, waking weeks later far from home in the middle of some forgotten task. The hauntings leave them pale, sullen, and milky-eyed, and travelers often mistake them for undead. More and more giant corpses are also rising from the ground and walking off on errands of their own.

Only the frost giants who came down from the north have learned to command the ghosts of their dead, but there are hardly any of them left. The old giant cities, Great Andarre and Cassilon, are dust-choked ruins full of hovels, where giants pick through the rubble for some clue to end the hauntings. They have not forgotten who enslaved them, and Bemmean wizards are not welcome. To the south, the great Wall holds back the [[Gardens of Carnessa]].

The giants trade in stone and antiquities. Their tombs date from when they came here out of the Ghostlight Forest, only to see their kin die over centuries of enslavement. Some giants reach adulthood before an ancestor arrives to haunt them; others are haunted by half a dozen before they can walk. Giants of the Haunted Lands are joyless at best and feral at worst, and some have turned to the [[Black Goat|Black Goat]] in hope of quieting their dead.

## The Great Ruined Cities

| City | Description |
|---|---|
| Andarre (about 70) | The old seat of stone giant sorcery, whose people once spoke to the stone and coaxed it into towers and homes. The few who remain wander its massive towers listening for voices long gone from the rock, and the most hopeful patiently wear away the bedrock to restore the glyphs that once woke the stone. Blind oracles offer help laying ancestors to rest, and the only trade is in stone talismans against haunting. Tunnels below are said to hold tombs, workshops, caverns, and, in one collapsed vault, a shoggoth buried since the Mage Wars. |
| Cassilon (about 200) | Once the jewel of hill giant woodwork, every beam and doorway carved with runes. Most of it burned in the chaos when the hauntings began, and now ragged transient giants live in squalor among the husks, ruled by rival gangs of hill giants and ogres led by those who have laid their ancestors to rest. Frost giant leaders like Brotnoth the Uncaring use shamans and rune-stones to dull the spirits' hold and trade that relief for brutal service. Visitors should be careful. |

## Landmarks of the Haunted Lands

| Landmark | Description |
|---|---|
| The Black Cypress | An ancient cypress in a gulf-coast marsh, hung with shrunken heads left by lizardfolk who worship it as a god. Sometimes it opens yellow reptilian eyes to look at those bringing offerings. |
| Ebon Tombs of the Stone Sorcerers | The obsidian-studded sarcophagi of ancient stone-sorcerer kings who refused death and counseled their descendants through reflections in the obsidian. Hidden during the enslavement, they are sought again now that the ghosts walk. |
| The Lonely Canyon | A hidden Pytonne valley where a mad chieftain broke the stone bridge to his tribe's cliff caves and left them to starve; bones litter the canyon floor below. |
| The Malachite Barrow | The mound of a hill giant king who defied the mages and whose whole clan was destroyed. With no descendants to haunt, their spirits vent their rage on travelers while their corpses hunt the land. |
| Sorrowsown | A small village of hill and stone giants who have laid their ancestors to rest, or secretly cremated them against tradition, and rediscovered their old stone and wood crafts, which they sell in [[Trenorra]]. |
| Standing Dead of Balinor | Statues of gaunt giants scattered across the desert, in fact withered giants held in place by bony hands from the dunes until they starved. Some say this awaits all who ignore their ancestors' call. |
| The Stone Father | A colossal granite giant carved into a Pytonne peak that crumbles through the day and is whole again each morning, hands held out palms up. Three gold rings adorn its fingers; legend says that when the other five are found and replaced, the giant will step from the rock and free his people. |
