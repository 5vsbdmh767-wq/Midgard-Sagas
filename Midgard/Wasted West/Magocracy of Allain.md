When the Mage Wars ended, only Allain was left standing among the magocracies, and somehow (most suspect treachery) it ended up holding the great convergences of ley lines that once ran across the West. Travelers say you can smell the magic in the air like a coming storm. Arcanists of every kind fill the land, some to learn from the finest teachers in Midgard and some to steal power for themselves.

Allain's cities each have their own character. Bemmea, on its granite peninsula in the north, is the capital and seat of more than twenty colleges of magic, chief among them the Academy of Bemmea (see [[Magic & Scholarship]]). Maillon is the city of alchemists, with its Bottle Market and pelican-sealed potions. Tintager trains war mages and warlocks to hold back the fey wilderness on its border, and Cassadega was founded on newly found Ankeshelian ruins. Even the common folk of Allain know a surprising amount about magic and meet the unknown with curiosity. Some of them have clawlike nails or don't burn, and people say there is demon blood in the old families. Invisible hounds bay in the distance, hunting lawbreakers and children with a talent for magic who need training.

Nine archmages rule from the Spire Perilous in Bemmea. Five seats belong to Bemmea, one each to Tintager, Maillon, Cassadega, and the wilds, and one empty seat is kept for long-dead Vael Turog. Over them all looms Exarch Vermes II, said to be the last pure-blooded Ankeshelian or a true half-demon of [[Caelmaran]] royalty. The White Knights of [[Bourgund]] have had a pact with the mages of Bemmea ever since the [[Western Wastes|Dread Walkers]] were stopped.

The magocracy's symbol is an intricate circle glyph orbited by nine stars. Its trade goods are alchemical woods, cold iron weapons, potions and other magic items, vril technology, and light stones. Remote behind ocean, wastes, and forest, it still draws merchants for discounted (and often flawed) apprentice magic, Tintager's cold iron, Bemmean scrolls, and Maillon's elixirs and hardwoods. Everything has a price, though the haggling is not always over gold.

## The Ninemage Council
The council meets in the Spire Perilous, which still houses the Council of Caelmarath, never renamed after the empire fell, atop the Bemmean highlands at the end of the long Mage Road. There the mages scheme in an ancient dance of strange rules, obscure transgressions, and bizarre rituals.

Exarch Vermes II, one of the most mysterious figures in a city full of mysteries, has shaped Bemmea for centuries. Old stories say a writhing, inhuman form lies under his face-shrouding robes. No one disputes his power, and he played a vital part in the magocracy's recovery after the Mage Wars; the [[Wasted West]] would be far more horrific without him.

## Law and Life in Allain
Outside the cities, life is much like anywhere else in Midgard. The land west of the Leukos River is fertile farmland. Most villages have an appointed overseer, a spellcaster of real talent with a few guards, and traveling courts with skilled diviners arrive each season to try cases, swiftly and severely. Punishment is harsh: imprisonment is common, and the living are gibbeted in cold iron cages at major crossroads on the Mage Road. Spellcasters convicted of serious crimes serve among Tintager's Feywardens, and young apprentices may be forced to pledge their freedom to [[Bourgund]] among the armor bonded.

### Laws of the Magocracy
Allain has a saying: "No law is ever unwritten." Centuries of pacts, martial codes, constitutions, ordinances, and civil codes are still on the books for the clever to use or abuse. Frivolous laws are rarely enforced, but careless travelers, especially in the big cities, can be arrested for drinking curative potions in the gray light of dusk or dawn (Code Exarch IV.XI.VV) or using palindromes in common speech (Third Prime Ordinance XVI.V). Enforcing these old codes is a cottage industry in Bemmea and Maillon among mages who failed at everything else, and the fines and bribes make a decent living.

The common laws can be just as dangerous. A non-spellcaster who assaults an arcane spellcaster can be sentenced to up to a year of servitude to the victim, enforced by *geas*. Since the law says only the living can defend themselves in such cases, non-spellcasters should finish what they start.

### Mages and Magic
No one takes magic more seriously than the mages of Allain, yet outsiders notice little at first: a faint scent of ozone, and hooded figures beckoning from dark corners of inns. The mundane curtain pulls back only in places like Maillon's Bottle Market, Bemmea's Academies of High Arcana, the vril artifacts in Cassadega's shops, and the humming standing stones ringed with cold iron golems on Tintager's border.

The wizards of Allain walk unseen, because years of practice over so many ley lines have changed them. Older arcanists barely look human. Many carry a residual taint of the Realms Beyond from the days when they trafficked with beings from that now-forbidden realm; others carry demon or devil blood, or deal with twisted beings who sell power for a true love's whisper or the memory of fresh-cut grass. Some hide behind illusion, many behind deep hoods that conceal too many eyes or hair of puckered tentacles. They can be cruel and callous, and Bemmean wizards are shunned in most foreign lands for the devastation they and their colleagues caused. Still, some welcome them for their patronage, and caution is advised for anyone offered a bargain, if they have a choice.

### Forbidden Arts
Ink magic has been outlawed for some 90 years, since the "Hakren affair," when a cabal of demon-influenced scribes called the Verses strangled their master with enchanted ink and words, then served rival councilors in a campaign of glyph magic that killed more than half the old council. The exarch's divinations exposed them, and they burned in arcane fire. Scriveners, as ink mages are called, are spoken of only in whispers, and evidence of a thriving cult of Totivillus, the Archduke of Scribes, suggests ink magic's grip is wider than anyone thought.

Necromancy is not illegal, but it is neither condoned nor protected by law, and many mages scorn its practitioners even as they deal with devils. Undead are ignored rather than accepted, and more than one councilor has used magic that preserves the soul while desiccating the flesh.

## Bemmea
Bemmea stands on an isolated granite base joined to the mainland at low tide by a long sliver of land. Its mages don't say when the tide will turn, since they command the elements, and entry by land is at their whim. The peninsula is all that remains of a far larger ancient city smashed by the Isonade, and the remains of towers still jut from the waves around it, some still used by colleges and reached by bridge or magic. The city is small around but rises to near-impossible heights, its winding streets climbing past fraternal houses, academies, dormitories, private tutors, lodges, and laboratories. Only from far above can you see that the curving streets form glyphs that anchor the city's ley lines.

Bemmea is a cloistered community of masters, apprentices, and their servants. Visitors are discouraged, many natives look down on non-spellcasters, and colleges such as the Academies Arcana and the Librarium Caelmarath are closed to all but teachers, lecturers, and students. Knowledge and secrets are its real currencies. There is no city guard, because there doesn't need to be: water elementals rise from fountains and gutters and cobblestone elementals from the streets to subdue criminals, and visitors say "every torch is watching." Otherwise the city is not obviously fantastic. Its soaring bridges between spires-within-spires are architecture, not magic, and the occasional animated carriage, flying carpet, or shield guardian is as much as most mages show off. The ozone smell of Allain is strongest here.

Commerce mostly serves the academies. There is no vast magic market, but cheap magic can be had by those who know where to look: apprentice work, class projects, and poorly graded experiments. Rumors tell of dark markets in the city's flooded depths selling experimental cast-offs, stolen magic, and mostly functioning golems. Travelers can find rooms and food at the taverns Elves-in-Irons and the Skull Bowl, and safe lodging at the Great Old Oven and the Mortar 'n' Pestle, though they should choose carefully to avoid the wrong attention or a bitter fraternity feud.

### The Academies
Bemmea holds dozens of academies, from the sprawling council-run Academies Arcana to small private classrooms and single tutors such as those of Blackspike or Hightower. Students usually begin between 12 and 14 and may apprentice for up to a decade, starting with years of chores before the bright ones move on to private study. Students often change tutors to find a mentor in their specialty. Life is mostly quiet libraries and musty tomes. Because Bemmea's ley lines are so powerful, student spellcasting is closely watched, public displays of power are discouraged, and rivalries demand discretion. The council would rather see a promising child dead than expelled with dangerous, half-finished knowledge. Jacen the Paper Master is a famous exception, lecturing on busy street corners with lifelike paper constructs.

Many stay on as masters, researchers, or artificers, drawn to places like the Planteria Observatorium, a huge indoor orrery that tracks the shifting constellations; the Invisible Halls, an academy of glamours open only to those given charms by its dean; and the Librarium Caelmarath, which always needs scribes and translators. Its social season is busy with lodges, fraternities, and secret societies, including the mysterious Obscured, the Guild of Honest Inkers and Sanctioned Sigilists, the bookish Sons of Vael Turog (obsessed with the magical plagues of the Mage Wars), the Church Arcane and Universal, and the pranksters of the Affiliation of Unaffiliated Wizards. Graduates become master mages (or journeyman mages, if they keep studying) in about a decade, usually by 25, and are sent off with a handshake. Offers to return rarely last more than a few years, since no one in Bemmea wants to encourage competition.

## Tintager, the Iron Metropolis
Tintager stands on the last surviving Feyward, one of a line of ancient human watch posts along the Leukos River guarding the magocracies against the elves of the [[Arbonesse]]. Its whole purpose is that guard, though its walls of cold iron and stone have not seen an elven army in more than 400 years. From a distance, the city inside its concentric walls looks green with trees and gardens. Up close, not one seed grows in its hard soil: the greenery is cold iron feyward trees, verdigrised imitations of Arbonesse oaks that are said to counter elven enchantments and animate against the fey.

Tintager is the dark twin of Bourgund. Its rough eldritch warriors and sorcerers, trained by sergeant-at-arms Feywarden Marriot Splitleaf, have no use for their southern cousins' perfumes and banners. It accepts anyone who will serve, including many of Allain's devil-blooded tieflings, fleshwarpers, outcast summoners, pardoned ink mages, and failed Bemmean apprentices. Cold iron gleams everywhere. For years the Feywardens raided the Arbonesse for bounties, to test its defenses, or to hunt, and sometimes cut down whole groves and shipped the trunks to Maillon, yet still cast the elves as the aggressors. Many suspect a dark influence urging them on, perhaps something within the Arbonesse that Tintager's wards were holding back.

A few months ago the wards protecting Tintager from the forest, and the elves' ancient wards against the magocracy, were both damaged, and a new Dread Walker, Y'gurdraketh, appeared on the forest's edge. Mages who have dared approach think it a servant of the [[Black Goat|Goat of the Woods]], perhaps one of her Dark Young. Both the Feywardens and the elves blame each other, and skirmishes have multiplied. The Walker wandered northwest along the Leukos toward Cassadega, then looped back south to a grove around an ancient mossy monolith, where it has waited for weeks after annihilating two parties of elven scouts and a cadre of tiefling eldritch knights. Cultists have already begun to offer it sacrifices.

## Maillon
Before the Mage Wars, Bemmea had a famous alchemical market, and its stench hung over the streets. During the war the council banned the trade and teleported the entire alchemical district, buildings, alchemists, and all, into the nearby marshes. The alchemists shrugged and got back to work, and found the swamp full of exotic ingredients. The old stone center has since sunk, its flooded lower streets a haven for smugglers, fugitives, outcasts, and monsters, and the city has spread in a chaos of stilt shacks, boardwalks, boats, gondolas, and grounded ships turned into labs and homes.

The alchemists are prosperous and reckless. Chemists stride the swamps on tall stilts collecting ingredients, potion-mills belch poison, and waste poured into the water has soured the swamp and bred alchemical oozes and warped beasts, including Orogoth, a webbed, bulbous-eyed hill giant whose intellect has doubled and who now casts sorcerer spells. Over it all presides Andress the Ageless, head of the Guild of Master Chemysts, who looks like a child prodigy though he has held the post for 50 years. Some think he found eternal youth and fear he is slowly turning into something inhuman.

### The Bottle Market
"Let the buyer beware" was never truer than in the Bottle Market's crowded stalls on sprawling boardwalks, where reliable brewers sell beside dabblers, scoundrels, and snake-oil peddlers, and the aging Market Warden Gray Sorock keeps a lax eye. Maillon potions have an uneven reputation, partly because pure water, made only in the giant alembics hovering over the old city center, is a luxury most brewers can't afford. A few brewers have excellent names: Channy Four-Fires, who makes healing potions as flavored lozenges; Blightward's Smorgasbord, where the gnome Listra Sparks sells guaranteed taint-free potions at a premium; the Starbrow Brewers, whose healing potions always heal the most they can; and the Changelings, known for long-lasting potions of diminution and growth.

## Cassadega
The Ankeshelians were the first human civilization, raised up by their aboleth masters, who gave them the strange science-magic called vril and glyph magic. When the Ankeshelians turned from their masters to the gods, the aboleths summoned the Isonade, which smashed their island nation beneath its tail. Over 90 years ago, shifting waters exposed drowned ruins of Ankeshel near the mouth of the Leukos, and the scholars who quietly settled there under the Feywardens' eye built Cassadega.

Soon after, diggers found the treasure-filled tomb of the priest-king Thalassos IV and the first working vril battery seen in centuries, and the town exploded into a boom town of explorers and thieves. The coastal merfolk sabotaged dig sites and killed looters until even they were won over by a trade treaty. The Arbonesse elves raided to keep vril out of careless human hands, and stopped only when the ancient vril arc-cannons were restored and the Feywardens came to guard the city. Cassadega's riches include working vril weapons and batteries, glyph magic, gold jewelry, orichalcum, and the most valuable prize of all, the key to the indecipherable Ankeshelian language.

## The Ghostlight Forest
A druidic sect of giants once tended this forest, until the fall of Ankeshel loosed thousands of will-o'-wisps, which the Ankeshelians had bred like roses in a hundred shades and kept in blown-glass globes. The wisps overwhelmed the giants and drove them out to Balinor, where they built [[Haunted Lands of the Giants|Andarre]] and Cassilon. Now the wisps flicker near injury and tragedy and feed on suffering. Gouged trees warn of green dragons and drakes, swarms of sluagh and vampiric mists prey on camps, and man-eating plants grow along the trails. Caravans between Maillon and [[Barsella]] keep to the coast road, whatever lights beckon. Alchemists report spectral battles between ancient giants and swarms of glowing motes, and few survive when those haunts turn on the watchers.
