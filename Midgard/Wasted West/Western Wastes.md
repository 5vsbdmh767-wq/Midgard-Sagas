At the heart of the [[Wasted West]] lie grey dunes, ruined towns, and the Dread Walkers. These are the colossal alien things the wizards summoned in the last desperate years of the Mage Wars, frozen in place by a great working of the surviving mages some 370 years ago. They hang over the horizon like statues of rotting flesh, moving so slowly you can barely see it, and they cannot be killed, banished, or reasoned with. There are no natural ley lines left here, so the weather is wrong and the storms are unnatural. The stars change near the Walkers, and there are fields of doors standing alone in the sand with light leaking around their frames.

The ruins are full of horrors: aberrations, voidlings, shoggoths, packs of ghouls, and the hordes of mad necromancers. Between them, giant scorpions and bulettes hunt the dunes.

Goblins came up out of the dark after the Mage Wars, fleeing the dwarves, and made the Wastes their own. Some dust goblin tribes worship the Walkers, building tent-and-wagon towns in their shadows, digging burrows in their footprints, or lashing whole shanty fortresses to their legs, and reading omens in every twitch. Others are scavengers who dig the ruins for relics and fight each other over the old arcane academies. A warlord named Braagezz has recently set herself up as ruler of all the goblins, which worries everyone nearby, [[Verrayne]] most of all.

Dust goblin symbols vary by tribe, often skulls or tattered cloaks; Braagezz's banner shows two crossed red vril weapons on black. The tribes trade in vril artifacts, fused glass, bones, carved amulets, wasteland herbs, and leystones.

## The Land
Though the Great Slumber is some 370 years past, the Walkers' corruption is still fresh. Dune-choked ruins hide broken walls, cracked spires, dark stairways to dungeons and sewers, and statues of forgotten archmages, haunted by roachlings and the degenerate descendants of masterless familiars. Some places hold fields of doors, all that remains of vanished towns, with strange light leaking from behind them; rumor says they open onto other times or other realms. Elsewhere twin spires of stone arc with giant sparks after dark that strike different peoples at different hours, as if aimed. At the "crimson lakes," visible rips in the sky hundreds of feet up drip a bloodlike fluid into pools below that, left alone long enough, rises as sentient creatures; some goblin tribes hold them sacred. The constellations shift or vanish across the West, especially near the Walkers.

Mostly, though, there are miles of lonely grey dunes. Gibbering mouthers, gugs, mordant snares, strangling watchers, voidlings, and shoggoths infest the ruins, with ghouls and the mindless undead of mad necromancers, while giant scorpions, bulettes, and angler worms hunt between them.

## Cursed Lines and Arcane Weather
With no ley lines left, magic here is unreliable. Whole stretches of dunes kill all magic one day and amplify it the next, spells fizzle or surge for no reason, and teleportation and shadow roads are especially risky: without ley lines to guide them, travelers can go nowhere, arrive somewhere unintended, or end up stranded between worlds.

*Optional, from the Midgard Worldbook.* Right after a character casts a spell of 1st level or higher in the Wastes, the player rolls a d10 (a d8 for *dimension door*, *teleport*, and other transportation spells). On a 2 or higher, the spell works normally. On a 1, the spell slot is spent and the GM rolls on the table below.

| d100 | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1–50 | The spell sputters and fails. |
| 51–60 | The caster is shifted to a random location within 1d20 miles. |
| 61–70 | The spell triggers a supernatural storm (1d6: 1 boneshard sleet, 2 gravity quake, 3 heavy air, 4 magnetic storm, 5 time storm, 6 zombie fog). |
| 71–80 | The spell is warped to include the caster in its area, or to exclude the caster from protective or helpful magic. |
| 81–90 | Unusual noise or light from the spell attracts a wandering creature. |
| 91–100 | The spell does maximum damage, lasts twice as long, and/or has its save DC increased by 2. |

## The Dust Goblins
Before the Mage Wars, goblins lived in the dark places under the Pytonnes and the western [[The Ironcrags|Ironcrags]]. Fleeing the dwarves' push west, they came up into the ruin of the Wastes and made it home. Dust goblins are stoic, with a severe, upright bearing unlike their mountain cousins. They are quick to avenge any slight but, approached with respect, make knowledgeable and dependable guides and, once their trust is earned, invaluable companions. They have a knack for waking ancient technology and relics that stay inert even for gifted spellcasters, and the ambitious find good work in [[Magocracy of Allain|Cassadega]]. Underestimating a chieftain is a good way to become powder or a petrified statue.

The most devout tribes worship the Walkers, live in tent-and-wagon towns, burrows in their footprints, or shanty fortresses lashed to their legs, and are ruled by matriarchs and oracles who read omens in every twitch; they take slaves to "enlighten" them before the alien gods, and most go mad. Others scavenge the ruins and war over the old academies. Some settled ruins welcome trade, though weakness can bring a whole gang down on a careless visitor.

### Rise of the Beloved Leader
The tribes have had no single ruler since Sun Duke Karlott Martenne of Bourgund defeated their last king, Dizzerax. Then Braagezz of the Dust Diggers became chieftain when her predecessor and half his bodyguards died in an accident with a newly found cache of vril weapons. Indiscreet Dust Diggers whisper that she found the cache first, sabotaged it, and reburied it where he would find it; others point out that she lost part of her face and right arm in the blast. She then broke the Bloody Tusk (killing their giant chieftain Cragmaw), freed the Sand Bird's Disciples from their shamans, and burned the mummified king Dizzerax, who still led the cannibal Bonewraiths, and offered each defeated tribe a choice: join her or die slowly. Most joined.

Braagezz looks half goblin and half machine: most of her nose and her right ear gone, her face burn-scarred, her right arm replaced by a grafted vril weapon that blasts a cone of fire, an ancient powered Ankeshelian bronze breastplate that shocks anyone who comes close, and Cragmaw's orichalcum battleaxe in her left hand. She has no time for Walker-worship; the goblins should look up to her. From the dungeons under the ruined Mercurial Tower, guarded by a dozen qwyllion mercenaries, she plans to take the Ghost Heads next, then turn west against the remaining tribes, while keeping a nervous eye on [[Verrayne]].

## The Seat of Mavros
Perhaps the only true oasis in the Wastes, the Seat of [[Mavros]] is the spiritual center of the war god's worship, and the faithful hope to make the pilgrimage once in their lives. Priests strip pilgrims of every mark of rank and past glory, and they must survive the goblins of the Bone Road, the mad druids of the Roatgard, the Court of Webs, and the Wastes themselves on skill and comradeship alone. The taciturn Order of Mavros watches the route without guarding it, recovering and burying the fallen, giving minimal supplies (sometimes making the weary fight for a swallow of water), warning once against the Roatgard and the Silk Thicket, and steering pilgrims away from the Walkers.

A vast tent city surrounds the Seat, full of the faithful and those who prey on them: sellers of false relics, gambling, arenas, cockfights, duel pits, brothels, and grizzled commanders teaching tactics and trading war stories. A sour lake gives foul but drinkable water. Through the Victor's Gate stands the Seat itself, a huge throne carved from the desert bedrock and covered in generations of prayers, bearing a giant iron crucible of everburning sacrificial fire. The devout climb a ladder to feed it the broken shields, spears, and bones of their enemies. Priests sometimes forge the slag into holy symbols and weapons for worthy pilgrims, the faithful say its warmth mends their armor, and weapons brought before it are said to gain sacred fire or lightning.
