Many tales are told of the bony-legged fey witch Baba Yaga, and most contradict each other. It's hard to get a sense of Baba Yaga, and that's exactly how she likes it. Mortal rulers, the lords of Celestia and the Eleven Hells, and the gods leave Grandmother alone, seemingly out of fear.

Baba Yaga is a trader of secrets. It is said there is nothing she doesn't know, and the brave, the desperate, and the foolish make pilgrimages to seek her out. Those who find her risk being eaten or set to humiliating tasks. When she does part with her knowledge, her price is high: perhaps the memory of a first kiss, a final breath, or a forgotten artifact.

When she is not riding through the woods on an enormous sow or flying in her iron mortar, steering with a pestle and sweeping away her tracks with a broom, she can most often be found in the heart of the [[The Margreve|Margreve]] or out on the [[Rothenian Plain]].

==**The Dancing Hut**==
Her hut walks on two enormous chicken legs and is much bigger inside than out. Accounts of it vary: sometimes a plain log hut with no windows and a single door, sometimes a cabin of several stories, sometimes a house built of bones. Around it runs a fence capped with humanoid skulls, lit from within so light shines from the eye sockets. Visitors swear there are always exactly enough empty posts for their own heads.

The hut turns its back on intruders unless it is addressed properly, and politely: *"Turn your back to the forest, your front to me."* Even then, entry isn't guaranteed.

Baba Yaga is the sworn enemy of the gnomes of [[Niemheim]].

## A Trader of Secrets
Grandmother makes it her business to know everything: the last thoughts of the dying star Tovaya, the way to Buyan, the island of the dead, the life of the Forgotten Queen and why she was erased from history, the Words of Unfounding that could unseat the gods, the true names of every creature never born, the secret byways that cross reality, and the circumstances that will end the world. She is rarely found when she doesn't want to be, and those who find her often regret it, pressed into service as a beast of burden or a tool, or eaten. Now and then she seeks out someone in desperate need, when it suits her. For those clever enough to trick her and escape, all knowledge is in reach, and those who know something she wants hold rare sway over her. But she does not haggle, she never forgets a slight, and many who tried to best her have been erased from history. She is a schemer a hundred steps ahead of everyone, and her secrets could unravel the world, or so she claims. The wise let her be. The foolish seek out her hut.

## The Dancing Huts
Grandmother's hut is said to hold 20 or 40 rooms, a whole mansion of kitchens, cages, and kettles. It shifts its location constantly, admits only those who address it politely, and though it cannot fly, it makes mile-long leaps several times a day; seeing it bound across the horizon is a good reason to go indoors. Her daughters have huts of their own, newer and smaller, with legs only as tall as a horse but still with half a dozen rooms, a cellar, and feather beds. The huts go where their owners command: down from the Cloudwall valleys, across the [[Rothenian Plain]], through the deepest glades of the [[The Margreve|Margreve]], along the River Tanais, and even among the Riphean Mountains of the North. They don't fight, but they kick hard when attacked, they are warded with alarms, and they cross almost any terrain and swim churning rivers easily.

## The Inconstant and Timeless Witch
No one understands Baba Yaga's motives, not even her most devoted mystics, and the stories about her are one part warning and one part nonsense. Scribes who write about her sometimes find their words gone the next day, or turned into earthworms in their scroll cases. A duke once commissioned her portrait; it became a window to the Outer Realms, and he and his family went mad looking through it. Before it could be burned, it sprouted legs and ran into a river.

She enjoys bold company, and seekers who find her hut are often fed. Survivors say she is so full of knowledge that she carries on two conversations in one breath, and her guests must sort out which thread is which. Her hut is full of those who failed her, turned into household objects and animals but keeping all their memories. Choose your seat carefully: it may be a forgotten king. Their rage and sorrow can sometimes be turned against her.

She seems able to be in several places at once. Some scholars say she has many twin sisters, all named Baba Yaga and all doing her bidding; others that she is free of time and wanders from moment to moment, leaving echoes of herself behind. Perhaps, like nesting dolls, her many selves are one and endless.

Now and then a story spreads of her death. Witnesses describe the same thing: she appears somewhere unexpected at an odd hour, recites a little song, and through a series of incomprehensible actions gets herself killed, never speaking to the crowds who gather. She has been burned, crushed, drowned, stung, eaten by wolves, and flung at castle walls. One of her mystics says the deaths have to do with her walks through time.

## Her Daughters
Seekers sometimes find her with her daughters, the beautiful fey called vila. Vila don't age and don't have children, so it seems unlikely they are truly hers, and yet she treats them with a kindness she shows no one else. One, Eldara, is a favorite of the Despot of the [[Despotate of the Ruby Sea|Ruby Sea]]; others are seen in the Margreve, at the [[Magdar]] palace, or dining with the Tsar of [[Vidim]]. The vila can grant near-divine boons, such as freedom from hunger and thirst, resistance to exhaustion, and truesight, to those who serve their interests, though some scholars call these inverted witch-hexes and others see [[Loki]]'s hand in them. The daughters are not of one mind. Some help Grandmother eat her guests; some pity travelers and help them escape.

## Her Mystics
Few who meet her are the same afterward. Some go mad, some vanish, and some give up their lives to chase her secrets. These mystics of Baba Yaga roam Midgard reciting portents and finding meaning in every coincidence, convinced she leaves signs wherever she goes, and she goes everywhere. Now and then they gather for weeks to pile meaning on each other's stories.

## The Witch's Garden
In a valley between serpent-infested hills lies Baba Yaga's garden, where she is said to grow seeds gathered across time and worlds, including a herb that can heal any affliction, even one laid by the gods. The snakes' venom kills within hours, and some victims rise again as undead. The garden lies in caves lit by blue fungi and green fireflies, fed by an underground stream, hidden from mortal eyes and scrying and tended by mindless thralls who angered or failed her. An elemental being called the Keeper oversees it; the local animals have grown intelligent and worship it, and its earth elementals bore through the hills as scouts and guards and sometimes travel far to gather rare plants for the crone.

## Koschei the Deathless
Her most important servant is Koschei the Deathless, whose origin is lost: his defenders say he fell through tragedy and betrayal, others that his own wickedness damned him. Most believe his soul is hidden in an egg, in a duck, in a hare, in a goat once tied to a great oak on the island of Buyan, and that only destroying the egg can destroy him. Baba Yaga is believed to control the goat, and so the egg, and so Koschei. He has no love for her but obeys without question. He is a gaunt, pale shadow of the man he was, an incarnation of death that some call an avatar or herald of Chernobog or [[Perun]], and he rides an equally gaunt, intelligent black mare said to be among the fastest steeds anywhere. He seldom shows emotion, except a glimmer of pleasure in slaughter. He sometimes attends [[King Lucan]]'s court in Bratislor, always with a different terrified young woman on his arm (see [[Blood Kingdom]]).
