Centaurs roam the [[Rothenian Plain]] east of the [[The Margreve|Margreve]] and the forest's southeastern reaches. Bands of them occasionally make their way through the forest to raid [[Zobeck]]'s fields and outlying villages, and centaur bandits have been known to claim the old griffon towers. As the war god, [[Perun]] is popular among them.

Every year more centaurs abandon the isolation of their ancestors for the trade towns. Not so at Braidwood Village in the southeastern Margreve, where tradition and the [[The Old Ways|Old Ways]] thrive. The Braidwood centaurs are legendary hunters and warriors. They challenge nearby [[Alseid|alseid]] tribes over hunting grounds and water, and defend their territory from Rothenian centaurs looking for wood and game. Every structure at Braidwood is grown, not built: the centaurs graft, prune, and braid living trees into homes and sculpture.

## Rothenian Centaurs
On the Plain, centaur clans are small, sometimes a dozen members, and owe fealty only to their own chiefs and khans. They think humans fools for living in wood and stone; humans fear their violent tempers and drunken passions. They are split between the free clans of the eastern grasslands and those who hunt and fight as mercenaries in human lands, and their society is a meritocracy that elects its chiefs and war leaders and can depose a king by the will of the horde. The clans number roughly 120,000, perhaps as many as 200,000. They worship [[Perun]] as patron, with the Twins of Wine ([[Yarila & Porevit]]), Khors, [[Lada]], [[Boreas (North Wind)|Boreas]], and [[The Hunter|the Hunter]], and trade leather, bows, weavings, grass rope, and bone whistles. Their symbols vary by clan, often fur banners or colored streamers.

Centaurs keep a story to themselves: Perun first made humans, but they were too small and slow; then horses, but they were herd-followers with no cunning or clever hands. Mocked by his wife for failing twice, the Thunderer joined human hands to equine speed and made the centaurs, the race meant to rule the grasslands.

They measure wealth in goats and sheep, for leather, wool, meat, panniers, blankets, and tents. A woolen tent marks adulthood: a colt or filly "leaves the tent" on coming of age, and two centaurs who marry "join tents." They practice druidic magic. Most males are warriors and females are rarely seen outside the clan. They summer in the hills and high meadows and winter lower, returning to the same few camps year after year.

### Raids
Centaurs take what others don't protect well enough: livestock, grain, cloth, coin, and especially wine and brewers' shipments. A raid begins before dawn, with silenced arrows for the sentries and two groups, one a diversion. Some fire great flaming arrows from up to 900 feet to burn huts and granaries, and they brew a poison so slow-acting that [[Kariv]] and [[Khazzaki]] take it for a fever. Rarely, perhaps twice a generation, a summer gathering decides a clan must be avenged, and the whole centaur nation sweeps off the Plain to burn the offender's borders. The [[The Mharoti Empire|Mharoti]], the slavers of [[Despotate of the Ruby Sea|Reth-Saal]], the king of [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau]], and the [[Vidim|Tsar of Vidim]] have all learned this lesson.

### The Long-Teeth
The black strangles has reached the centaurs. It starts as a blackening of hair and skin around the hooves, then rot that makes standing agony, and as the victim rests, the disease takes hold: the teeth grow to twice their size and yellow, sunlight burns, thoughts darken, and they hunger to bite other centaurs. In packs that mock their old clans, the Long-Teeth hunt healthy centaurs by night, and in the end become mindless cannibals that prey even on each other. Each side blames the other for bringing it: the Plain blames a traveler from [[Trombei]], and Trombei blames the Plain. Chieftain Paloth of the Yengde, working with the khan and the Grassweavers of Perun, has found a poultice that stops the final stage and seeks a remedy rumored in Trombei.

### Centaurs and Others
Kariv smiths shoe centaurs for free, for luck, and centaurs scout for and defend Kariv caravans; centaur bandits never rob the Kariv, since robbing the cursed is bad luck. Most centaurs enjoy skewering the infernal gnomes of [[Niemheim]], though some clans have made a truce and serve as guards in the Wormwood. The Dargit and Yengde send out the most mercenaries, sometimes gone for years, and they are welcomed home with celebration. The centaurs were once close to the windrunner elves, but the elves have recently retreated east and abandoned their sacred sites, and not even the centaurs know why.
