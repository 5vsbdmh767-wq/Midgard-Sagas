The ratatosk are squirrel-folk native to [[Yggdrasil]], the World Tree, and to its many branches. They act as guides, messengers, and guardians of its planar roads, and they are insatiable gossips and tricksters whose joy in gathering secrets is outdone only by their delight in spreading them in mischievous ways. They make it very clear that they are not beastkin but divine messengers of the gods.

The ratatosk believe they descend from Ratatosk the Clever, the first of their kind, who stole the seed of the World Tree from the gods and buried it at the very center of the multiverse, where it grew large enough to hold the planes in its branches.

## Ekorre and Tradvakt

Most ratatosk are ekorre, the sleek-furred squirrel-folk most people picture, often red or brown with small tusks. They make up about seven in ten of any community, and they are the clever, curious, egotistical gossips who carry messages across the planes. About a quarter are tradvakt, the larger ratatosk sometimes called warlords, who see themselves as the guardians of Yggdrasil. They train in weapons and tactics from a young age, keep order in their communities, and guard travelers. The rest belong to a few lesser kinds that outsiders seldom see.

## Scurries and Drays

Ratatosk live in close matriarchal family groups called scurries, and every ratatosk can proudly recite their scurry's tales all the way back to Ratatosk the Clever. A dray is a settlement of about a dozen scurries, hidden in some nook up or down the length of the World Tree behind clever camouflage and a stout wall of woven vines and branches. The eldest female of each scurry sits on the dray's council, which keeps order and settles disputes, and now and then appoints a warleader to deal with a threat. The council's most important duty is hosting the sakmoot, a daily gathering, usually in the morning, where everyone trades news and gossip.

Visitors to a dray can expect a barrage of nosy questions. Older ratatosk try not to "borrow" things from guests; the young ones may not be so restrained. They fully intend to return what they take, if they don't get distracted.

## Grenstad

The great city of the ratatosk is Grenstad, the city of the five branchings, home to more than 4,000 ratatosk and smaller numbers of aasimar, tieflings, ravenfolk, and planar humans. Three of its five branches lead to Midgard: Alvatrad, the Witch Tree, and [[Wotan]]'s Tree. One leads to the [[Court of Golden Oaks|Court of the Golden Oak]] in the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]], and the last to Revelport in the Dreamer's Islands, which makes Grenstad a vital hub of trade and news. Among the other drays, the most famous are Acorndray, Conedray, and Snagdray.

Clarahekkarina, queen of the ratatosk of Yggdrasil, is the hostess of the Ekollon Halle, a feast hall woven from the living branches of Yggdrasil, roofed with green-and-silver leaves and warmed by enchanted blue and yellow fires that cannot burn wood. Three times a month, following the moon, it fills with chittering crowds and with friends, allies, merchants, and travelers, for the queen lets anyone attend who fits through the door. The ratatosk eat their gryta stew there with great abandon.

## In Midgard

Ratatosk are seen in the [[Northlands]], where they have real numbers but are too insular to shape events. They have a small presence everywhere the World Tree's branches reach, including the Shadow Realm, where they seem to enjoy the company of elves, bearfolk, and gnomes. Among the old elven courts there is said to be a Squirrel Court of Yggdrasil, though that may be an elven joke.
