Sapopova are an amphibious people with pronounced frog or toad features. Some believe they are divinely descended from the animals they resemble; many others believe frogs and toads descend from them. These small folk build their homes by ponds, rivers, and other waters, where they can slip away from predators that can't hunt both on land and in the water. They lay their eggs in long strands or clusters in their spawning grounds, and they return to those waters at the end of their lives.

Young sapopova are often impulsive, always testing how far they can go, and that thrill-seeking leads many into adventure, whether to help their community or to win wealth and power. Those who survive usually come home as adults, though some make new lives elsewhere. When they return they share their stories with the community, adding them to a collective memory that is passed on to the tadpoles in turn, which is why sapopova so often surprise others with obscure knowledge or profound wisdom.

## In Midgard

In much of Midgard, sapopova are called grippli. Scattered grippli tribes share the Broken Reeds, the drifting reed-island marshes north of [[Trombei]], with lizardfolk.

The Broken Reeds are the best-known home of the grippli, but not the only one. Sapopova villages dot the slow rivers and marshes of the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]] and the swamps of the [[Southlands]], usually out of sight of the roads. Most outsiders only meet them as river guides and ferrymen, or as the odd adventurous youngster who wandered too far from the spawning grounds and decided to keep going.

Sapopova who come home from their travels are expected to tell everything, and a village's eldest storytellers can recite tales that are generations old. Merchants who need to know what happened on a stretch of river fifty years ago sometimes find it worth their while to ask.
