Born between civilization and the wilds, beastkin are as varied as the animal kingdom itself. Sometimes called wildkin, they share the two-legged form of humans, elves, or dwarves, but carry the clear traits of a particular beast: hawk wings, a scorpion's tail, rabbit ears, owl eyes, a crab-like carapace, a shark's razor teeth, or bony ox horns. Some show only slight bestial features, while others look like their animal kin walking upright.

Some beastkin believe they are related to the intelligent beasts that hold court in the fey realms. Others claim descent from lycanthropes or other magically transformed beings. Whatever the truth, every beastkin has a tie to the natural world, and many choose to live alongside the animals they resemble. Young beastkin are often driven by curiosity to seek out other peoples and far-flung places. All beastkin wrestle with primal urges, but they are quick to form unshakable bonds with anyone, humanoid or beast, who shows them compassion and loyalty.

## In the North

Beastkin are few in the [[Northlands]]. Most live in [[Björnrike]], in [[Skaldholm]], and, perhaps oddly, in [[Thursrike]], where they have carved out a place among the giants. In Skaldholm, the City of Bards, the skaldic school called Rjodr's Grove blends natural sounds with music and draws many beastkin and werekin students. Its master, Cyra Rjodr, is a shy, reserved wren beastkin druid. [[Bearfolk]] say they are not beastkin, whatever outsiders assume.

## On the World Tree

Avian beastkin, mostly ravenfolk, are among the most numerous people of [[Yggdrasil]] after the ratatosk, living in ramshackle rookeries of nests, shops, and shrines strung together with bridges and ladders. The [[Ratatosk|ratatosk]], for their part, insist they are not beastkin at all.
