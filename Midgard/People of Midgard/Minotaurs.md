The bull-folk share many traits with the bulls they resemble. Both men and women have horned heads covered in shaggy hair, and warriors braid their hair with teeth or other tokens of fallen enemies. Their thick coats run from bright white through red-brown to dark brown and black, and many minotaurs shave or dye the fur in patterns that show their allegiances and beliefs. Brands, ritual scars, and gilded or carved horns are common too.

Adult males stand 6½ to 7 feet tall, with females about 3 inches shorter, and both carry a great deal of muscle even for their size. Strength counts for a lot in minotaur society. They live about as long as humans but grow up faster: childhood ends around 10 and adulthood is celebrated at 15, though most don't start families until 25 or later. They spend the years between proving themselves to their elders.

Minotaurs eat enormous amounts of meat and greens alike, and great banquets mark every important social and religious occasion. Regions compete fiercely over their cuisine, sometimes violently. The host of a feast before a great ceremony or contest can earn nearly as much honor as the champion, and a host who fails is deeply shamed.

## Horns

A minotaur's horns are its most valued possession. Both sexes grow them, and they show an individual's status and strength as well as serving as a weapon. A minotaur who loses part or all of a horn carries a heavy stigma and has to keep proving their worth; "brokehorn" is a fighting insult. Some temples will regrow a damaged horn for a minotaur who completes a quest or a series of trials, but a few famous warriors have gone through life broken as a mark of pride, and they tend to be the most driven of all.

Many minotaurs carve their victories into their horns. Warriors mark the foes they have beaten, artisans the honors their work has won, and some earn markings for the great mazes they have solved. A minotaur who honors their heritage keeps their horns polished, and some gild them to draw the eye to their deeds.

Minotaurs are masters of labyrinthine magic: mazes, confusing charms and glamours, trap-spells, and wards. They keep it to themselves and never teach it to outsiders.

## The Seven Cities

The island of [[Kyprion]] has been the minotaurs' homeland since the dragons burned their southern cities, and its queen rules from the Palace of the Bull at the heart of the Great Labyrinth. Minotaurs also live in large numbers in [[Capleon]] and [[Triolo]], where the First Duke-Admiral is a minotaur and minotaurs from Kyprion fight alongside [[Rumela|Illyrian]] exiles against [[The Mharoti Empire]].

## The Moon Kingdom

About 1,500 years ago, minotaurs from the eastern end of the Middle Sea crossed to the Lands of Wind and Sand and founded the cities of [[Roshgazi]], Cindass, Derrada, and Kadralhu, which they shared with their human allies, the [[Qamari]]. Some 460 years ago those western cities broke from the Queen of the Minotaurs in Kyprion and declared the Moon Kingdom of Tes-Qamar. The Queen in the East tried to win them back peacefully, and gave up when the dragons began to threaten her.

The Moon Kingdom prospered until the Mharoti attacked 300 years ago. The dragons razed Derrada and Kadralhu and wrecked much of Roshgazi and Cindass, and the minotaurs fled across the sea to Capleon, Triolo, and Kyprion. The Lost Fleet of Roshgazi vanished in the mists on the way, and whole clans were given up for dead.

Now the tide has turned. With Kyprion under threat, minotaurs are returning to Cindass, which Emir Palana Tellisha is rebuilding with a new harbor, new walls, and a new labyrinth; about half its 15,000 people are minotaurs. Four ships of the Lost Fleet have made landfall at Roshgazi, three centuries late, their crews unaged and with no memory of the years between. See [[Dominion of the Wind Lords]].

## In the Southlands

The largest minotaur populations in the [[Southlands]] are in [[Nuria Natal]] and in Sudvall in the far south, where many minotaurs are knights who balance chivalry with meditative spiritualism. Southland minotaurs often serve on sandships and airships as sailors, guards, or good-luck charms. Their patron there is [[Sabateus]]. All minotaurs are wary of the ravening disease that has plagued their people in the past.
