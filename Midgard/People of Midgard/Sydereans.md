Sydereans are born of creatures or powers from beyond the mortal world. Some call them starborn or plane-touched, and their origins are as varied as the cosmic forces that shaped them. A few are the children of a mortal and an outsider such as a celestial or a fiend, but such pairings are rare and wrapped in portents or conspiracy. More often a syderean is born to an unsuspecting mortal family, through some magical upheaval, a trait sleeping in the bloodline, or a soul returned by some unknown design. Now and then a grown adult is transformed into a syderean in a flash of light or a burst of smoke.

Every syderean lives between two worlds, that of their mortal peers and the one tied to the magic inside them. Whatever their parentage, they are not bound by the cosmic laws that govern their sires, and they are free to embrace, reject, or ignore the struggles of their immortal kin. Many gravitate toward large cities, where they draw less scrutiny than in a village.

In Midgard, sydereans of celestial descent are called aasimar, and those of fiendish descent are called tieflings.

## Celestial Sydereans (Aasimar)

The great Phoenix Throne of [[Ishadia]] is still a shining beacon in poetry and song, but the truth is that [[The Mharoti Empire]] is slowly devouring the aasimar, and they struggle to win a victory each time the dragons send an army. Most aasimar live in Ishadia and Aksaba, and their glory days are long behind them. They can no longer pass through the Portal of the Heavens in the holy city of Shuruppak to visit their celestial ancestors, and the doors to the heavens seem firmly shut, so the power of the angelic folk dwindles. Pilgrims and paladins still bring coin and prayers to their lands, and there are plenty of valiant paladins and devout clerics among the aasimar themselves. Give them a sword and a righteous cause, and they can still strike terror into the cruel, the greedy, and the faithless.

In the [[Southlands]], aasimar are among the typical worshippers of Isis. In the [[Northlands]] they are rare, or at least rarely seen.

## Fiendish Sydereans (Tieflings)

Tieflings were once widespread in the human magocracies of the West, and for a time they dominated the noble classes of Caelmarath and Vael Turog. Those great public families are gone. Tieflings are now an exiled people, found in families and pairs but rarely in larger numbers. In most places they are reviled as the demonmarked or the hellborn and driven out, and many still blame them for the destruction of the human lands of the West and the retreat of the elves.

Only in Bemmea and Tintager do the demonmarked live in significant numbers, and even there they seem eager to prove they can be trusted. Shadow elves, gnolls, and gnomes enjoy their company, as do some dragonkin; elves, humans, and halflings rarely abide them.

In the Southlands tieflings are found everywhere, and the largest number live in Kush, where they often hold positions of power. Many also live in the Fallen Kingdom of [[Aksaba]].

## Beyond Midgard

Aasimar and tieflings travel the planes. Smaller numbers of both live among the ratatosk in Grenstad on [[Yggdrasil]], and in the [[The Shadow Realm|Shadow Realm]] they are known as planar travelers, always considered strangers and never natives.

## Aberrant Sydereans

The aberrant sydereans have no old name in Midgard, and most people who see one assume they are looking at a monster. Many are born in the West, in families that still live in sight of the ruins of the Mage Wars, where the doorways to the Realms Beyond were opened and never properly closed. Others are born to the Void Speakers, those few souls drawn to the Void who claim to see the strands of fate. Some dust goblin tribes in the [[Western Wastes]] treat an aberrant child as an omen of the Dread Walkers and raise it with something close to reverence. Everywhere else, aberrant sydereans learn early to hide what they are.

## Spellborn

Spellborn sydereans carry the raw power of arcane magic in their bodies. Most are born near the great conjunctions of the [[Ley Lines & Shadow Roads|ley lines]], or to families who have spent generations working magic, and the [[Magocracy of Allain]] has more of them than anywhere else. The mages of Bemmea test every spellborn child they can find, and a spellborn of Allain rarely gets to choose whether to study magic. Those who slip away often end up in [[Zobeck]], where the [[Arcane Collegium]] asks fewer questions.
