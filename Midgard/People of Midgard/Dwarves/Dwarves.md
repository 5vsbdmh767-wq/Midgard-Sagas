The dwarves of Midgard live in three great realms: the Northern clan halls such as [[Tanserhall]] and [[Wolfheim]], the 
free cantons of the [[The Ironcrags|Ironcrags]], and the southern realms of [[Nuria Natal]] and [[Sebbek Sobor]]. The dwarves of the three realms are distinct in their culture, strengths, and style.

[[Northlands|The Northlands]] dwarves are keepers of the old ways and the old gods, smiths and warriors, farmers and traders, stout and strong and able to down a barrel of ale at one sitting. They keep busy fighting the giants or the werewolves and worgs in the North, but sometimes the Northern dwarves take to the sea in their longships and raid the coasts, from [[Vidim]] through the [[Donnermark]] and [[Krakovar]] to northern [[Grand Duchy of Dornig|Dornig]] territories, but bypassing the [[Magocracy of Allain]]. The Northern dwarves are especially accomplished at ring-making, smithwork, and (for some reason) berserk shapeshifting, including a society of especially fearsome bear-shifting berserkers.

**The cantonal** dwarves are makers, miners, and smiths, digging deep into the [[The Ironcrags|Ironcrags]] for iron and gold and forging items of great wonder; primarily exceptional spears, crossbows, and arrows, but also great artistic works. Singular items are a lifetime’s masterwork: a clockwork steed or wagon, an airship, a returning hammer, or a suit of invulnerable armor. None of these are beyond the grasp of a cantonal smith. The cantonal dwarves serve no kings, choosing instead to rule themselves. They frequently serve as mercenaries in the [[Seven Cities]], among the nobles of the Grand Duchy, and against the [[The Mharoti Empire|Mharoti Empire]], side by side with the [[Magdar]] knights. They have a deep hatred of the undead of the [[Blood Kingdom]].

**The Southlands** dwarves are distant relatives at best, with different language, magic, and style. In the desert heat the Southern dwarves shave their heads and wrap their beards tightly in gold or copper wire; sometimes these beards are forked or braided as well. They serve a male mask of [[Rava]] named [[Ptah]], and they build clockwork bodies they call shabti, or servants. Their skin is dark as ebony, and—when not shorn—their hair runs to pure black, gold, or (strangely) red. In the middle of it is an island, on which the dwarves have carved a fortress from the living rock. Known as Borgstadr, the fortress is home to three fireforge clans: Firefall, Hamarfall, and Smokebeard.

## Dwarves of the North

Dwarves are among the most numerous peoples of the [[Northlands]], perhaps second only to humans, and they take on every kind of work there, from wheat farming to fine metal and leather craft. Over the centuries, place and circumstance have changed some of them.

The baugsmidr, called ring dwarves by outsiders for their knack with magical rings, are a small, lesser known people found at the heart of dwarven holds across the North. Long ago [[Gullveig]], an obscure deity of magic and gold, lived for a time among one isolated community of dwarves and taught them the divine art of binding magical runes into precious metals. It brought them great renown and power, and also the jealousy and greed of others. After raids nearly destroyed them, they retreated deep underground into hidden enclaves, and their descendants still treat the locations of their homes as a secret shared only with those they trust completely. They are leaner than other dwarves, with gold or silver hair and large, pale eyes, and many become spellcasters. The few who leave usually go looking for magical lore or lost artifacts.

Fjord dwarves long ago gave up the halls and mines of their ancestors to sail the fjords and live in rough coastal settlements across the North, and many of them find the old halls a bit claustrophobic. They much prefer sea air and a deck underfoot. Like other Northern dwarves they are often menacing raiders and fierce warriors, but they can be deeply honorable and generous to their friends and allies.

## Duergar

Duergar are among the primary worshippers of [[Addrikah]], along with derro and deranged dwarves and humans. A copper statue of a seated dwarf that duplicates itself into a full crew of oarsmen is counted a duergar marvel in the North.
