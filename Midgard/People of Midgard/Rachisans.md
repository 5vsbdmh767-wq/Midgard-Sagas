Rachisans are small humanoids with the features of ground-grown plants such as alliums, brassicas, and nightshades. People often assume they are related to treants or to plant-like fey such as dryads, but rachisans are a people of their own, as varied as the vegetables they resemble.

Scholars argue over where rachisans came from. Many rachisans believe the fey made them, though they disagree about whether it was on purpose, a happy accident, or a bit of both. However it began, rachisans now raise families of their own: two or more parents produce seeds, plant them, and tend them until an infant sprouts. All that variety gives rachisans a fluid view of gender, diet, and family that makes perfect sense to them and baffles more animal-like folk.

Rachisans do well in many cultures but prefer rural communities where their gift for growing things is useful. Those who take up adventuring usually do so because their home was suddenly and violently overturned, and they had to take drastic steps to protect their friends and family.

## In Midgard

Most rachisans who know any story of their beginnings tell the same one: they first sprouted in the Summer Gardens of the Gennecka Forest, where the elves' Golden Court once stood. Whether some elven gardener meant to make them or the gardens simply did it on their own, nobody now alive can say, and the rachisans tell it both ways depending on who is listening.

When the elves left in the Great Retreat, the rachisans were left behind with the gardens, much as the gnomes and halflings who had served the elves were. The Summer Gardens went wild, and the rachisans scattered into the farmland around them. Today the largest rachisan communities are in the fields of [[Perunalia]], whose scholars prize botany and agronomy and are glad of neighbors who can coax a crop out of anything. Rachisan families also turn up in the herb-growing villages of [[The Margreve]], where mandrake and moonberry are grown for the alchemists of [[Zobeck]], and along the farms of the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]].

Many rachisans keep small shrines to [[Yarila & Porevit|Yarila]], the goddess of spring, and a rachisan family planting its seeds will often wait for the first warm rain of the year to do it.
