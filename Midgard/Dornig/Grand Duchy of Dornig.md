For centuries nobody doubted that the Grand Duchy of Dornig was the true heir of the elven empire. It kept the shadow roads, the lost lore of the elves, and, above all, its ruler. When the elves sounded the Last Horn and left, one of the great elves of the Archons' Court stayed behind for love and duty. She married the human heir of the little barony of Dornig, and over the centuries she became duchess, grand duchess, and finally the Imperatrix. Every noble house in Dornig, great or petty, claims descent from her through her elfmarked children, by blood, by marriage, or by creative family trees.

Dornig looks peaceful from outside, but it has always seethed with intrigue. Its map is a jigsaw of kingdoms, baronies, free cities, church lands, and principalities, many of them not even connected, constantly redrawn by court politics (the smallest independent state is a single tavern in Salzbach). Three great houses descended from the Imperatrix compete for her ear: Hirsh-Dammung, the soldiers, at Hirschberg; Aldous-Donner, the mages, at Reywald on the edge of the Arbonesse; and vann Rottsten, in the uplands around Bad Solitz. Dornig has also always taken in refugees, from the Wastes, from [[Blood Kingdom|Morgau]], and lately from fallen [[Krakovar|Krakova]], whose exiled Queen Urzula now lives at court.

Four years ago the Imperatrix fell ill with something no alchemy, herbcraft, or magic can touch, and now lies in a coma, neither dead nor undead. With no clear heir, the realm is starting to crack. The three houses, the church of Yarila and Porevit under Saintmistress Rowanmantle, and the Black Knight of the shadow fey all circle the empty Copper Sphinx Throne. Two years ago an elf calling herself Kalvora Moonsong arrived claiming to be the Imperatrix's pure-blooded granddaughter, and the Imperatrix's regalia, which had rejected every other pretender, reached out and recognized her. No one knows what she wants.

The duchy's fey roads, kept by the elves and secured in their keeps, still make it the swiftest realm in Midgard to cross (see [[Ley Lines & Shadow Roads]]). Half its land is primeval forest held in trust for the elves: the [[Arbonesse]] in the west, and the smaller and more dangerous Tomierran, which hides the ruins of Thorn.

The duchy's arms are a black eagle on a red and yellow field, counterchanged. Besides its three provincial capitals (Hirschberg, Reywald, Bad Solitz) and the River Court of the [[Arbonesse]], it has three crown cities that answer directly to the throne (Courlandia, Salzbach, and Donnermark), dozens of vassal holdings, and the great castles of Arching Castle, Castle Reln, and Ransom Siege.

## A Chronicle of Dornig
When the elves ruled, the lands along the Great Sea and the Nieder Straits were deep forest. The old elven songs say the elves taught the gnomes to weave magic, the halflings to serve, the dwarves to craft, and the humans to dream. They ruled for a thousand years unchallenged and five hundred more against the rising human Young Kingdoms, whose taste for dark magic troubled the Archons, until the Archons' Court sounded the Last Horn and the elves withdrew along the shadow roads to the [[Silendora|Summer Lands]].

Regia Kalthania stayed. The Company of the Blessed, the adventuring band she traveled with, called her Moonthorn; it was led by Reln vann Dornig, second son of the little barony of Dornig. The court bards say she stayed for love and for duty, and that she wept on the last morning as the crystal-armored knights rode out of Thorn. In the chaos of the Mage Wars that followed, diabolists killed Reln's parents and elder brother to seize the barony as a base. Reln, Moonthorn, and their allies destroyed them, and Reln took the crown and Moonthorn as his consort. Dornig became a bastion of safety, absorbed its neighbors as vassals, and grew from barony to duchy to grand duchy. When peace was made with the surviving Arbonesse and the Ironcrags, she became the Imperatrix.

By then Reln had died, his life stretched as far as elven magic allowed, and their eldest daughter had perished when Lost Arbonesse sank beneath the waves. Rather than let a younger child take the Copper Sphinx Throne (a gift of the Ironcrag dwarves), the Imperatrix took the reins herself and has held them since, outliving her children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren. Her reign was mostly peaceful. Donnermark's founding turned Northern plunderers into rapacious merchants, though abominations still come out of the Roatgard Forest, reavers raid across the Straits, and 200 years ago the dwarves of [[The Ironcrags#Grisal, the Black Canton|Grisal]] took the mountainous southern border and its human population. Relations with [[Magocracy of Allain|Allain]] and the Roatgard druids are more brittle than they were, especially since a new Dread Walker appeared.

In 480 AR the Imperatrix fell ill with something no alchemy, herbcraft, or magic could touch. The three great houses, the church of Yarila and Porevit, and two true elves with claims of their own, the Black Knight of the shadow fey and Kalvora Moonsong, now circle the throne.

## The Imperial Court
For four centuries, since the Imperatrix's first Jubilee, the court had no fixed home. Every three years, after the harvest, the Great Procession carried the Imperatrix, her retinue, some 400 courtiers, and their households to the next of the three great cities, an echo of the old Archon Court's moves from Thorn to [[Valera]] to Liadmura to [[Perunalia|Sephaya]] to the Arbonesse that made humans think there were many elven nations. This Movable Feast kept the court in touch with the whole realm and let each city's resources recover, like crop rotation. Hosting it was a great honor and a great burden. After a cadet branch of the vann Rottstens attempted a coup, the Imperatrix skipped Bad Solitz for six extra years as punishment.

Now the Feast does not move. The Imperatrix lies in Castle Grauberg on the High Platz above Reywald, most of the court mills about Thunderstone Castle at the foot of the hill, and the city groans under the cost. Every servant at court is paid by at least one of the three great houses, which is expected and tolerated. Taking gold from Morgau, the River Court, Allain, Grisal, the other cantons, or [[Zobeck]] is not, and risks the traitor's death: flaying and fire.

### The Imperatrix's Slumber
The Beloved Imperatrix is an ancient elf with a long, narrow face, ears that almost meet behind her head, and huge black eyes. Her long white hair is still braided every morning, and she wears a simple copper circlet, a gift from Reln. Even before her illness she had begun to feel her years, calling courtiers by their grandfathers' names, though anyone who took her for a frail old lady was quickly surprised. Four years ago, after a cotillion presenting the young women of Reywald to society, she told a handmaiden she felt exhausted, retired, and did not wake. Chirurgeons, priests, and the Arcane College have all failed. She breathes, does not eat, and does not waste away, and time slows slightly around her; no force, magical or otherwise, can reach her. Her chamberlain Thadia lets no one approach, while miracle workers and humbugs crowd the halls below claiming to know the cure. With no heir named, the realm is run by representatives of the three great houses and the Abbess of the Twinned Cathedral.

## Religion in the Grand Duchy
Dornig worships the gods of the [[The Crossroads|Crossroads]]: chiefly the twins [[Yarila & Porevit]] (called Freyja and Freyr in the Arbonesse), Mavros-Perun ([[Mavros]] in the west, [[Perun]] in the south, Valeresh to the elves), [[Lada]], [[Rava]], and [[Volund]]. The crown treats all gods as equal; the people don't. As the elves slowly return, the elven mother Holda is gaining followers, and her claim to rank above the Twins irritates the Twinned Cathedral. [[Baccholon|Baccho]] is popular with the young at court, and every poetry salon in Salzbach has a little shrine to him decorated with smashed glasses, though darker rumors link him to the newest Walker in the Wastes. The dark gods are banned, but Chernobog is worshipped by evil things in the wilds, Blood Lodges of [[The Hunter|the Hunter]] exist, and [[Marena]] and [[Mammon]] have cults. The greatest worry is [[Sarastra]]; the Lords Arcane fight both spellcasters who study with the shadow fey and elves and elfmarked who fall under her sway.

## Fey Roads of Dornig
Dornig is hard country to cross: two ancient forests, younger but no less deep woods, high moors, and forested valleys, with old dwarf-built roads and rivers that all run north to the reaver-haunted Straits. What holds it together is the fey roads. The roads of the Arbonesse still run clean to the Summer Lands, used by the River King's people, but the shadow fey have taken much of the rest of the network; those near Zobeck, for instance, don't reach the Summer Lands at all, which is why outsiders say "shadow road." Dornig's own roads bypass the Summer Lands and simply link the realm's towns. The Imperatrix struck a bargain with the shadow fey that they leave small groups (never more than 20) alone. The exception was the Great Procession, which she led along the ancient road called the Processional holding the Phial of Khors, keeping every shadow creature at bay by force of will. With her incapacitated, guides who know the paths are highly prized.

Every city has a working gate held by its ruling family, secured against anything that might come through. A gate can be a doorway, a grand arch, a hole at the foot of a tree, or a waterfall, and travelers don't always know they've entered one until it's too late. If you can't turn back, the best chance is to let the road take you where it will. Vassal towns are scattered across the realm and talk to their ruling city by fey road.

## The Great Houses and Cities

### Hirschberg
The largest city, on the west bank of the Rhogarn River where the roads from Zobeck and Salzbach meet, is a city of trade and industry whose riverside forges work night and day; its smiths fold steel to rival the dwarves'. Its buildings rise three and four stories over open undercrofts for shops and stables, the gentry live upstream among domed gardens, and the old Auldmines in the hills are home to dwarves, halflings, and worse. Uniforms are everywhere, since Hirschberg faces Grisal and now undead Krakovar. Across the river, three great spans reach Castle Reln, white-walled and blue-roofed on volcanic rock, the oldest and strongest castle in Dornig and Reln vann Dornig's original seat, kept clean and empty in trust for the Imperatrix. The modest Shrine of Blades of Donar, Perun, or Thor, roofed with shingles shaped like sword blades, draws the god's lightning away from the city and glows when struck; its Boltmaster, Jima Lanternhold, is coordinating relief for Krakovan refugees.

Visitors with noble letters stay two weeks with their hosts, neither too short nor too long. Those without find crowded, expensive inns. [[Elves]] and elfmarked are respected, dwarves are worthy foreigners, gnomes, halflings, and kobolds are assumed to be servants, and gearforged are treated as property: one without ownership papers can be declared abandoned and confiscated by the crown.

### Reywald
Reywald lies on the edge of the Arbonesse, the gateway for trade with the elves, a bright city with no walls built around a small, very deep lake. Instead of walls, seven four-story Rey Towers ring it, each with a distinctive bell that tolls the hours in turn and a fire brazier, staffed by temples, adventuring companies, military societies, and the Lord Arcane's spellcasters. More towers line the roads to Salzbach and the Tomierran and run along the Arbonflow into the forest. The seventh tower, the Hexenhold, has a bell of unbreakable crystal from the River King that sounds only in the gravest danger. At the center stands the Twinned Cathedral, the mightiest temple of Yarila and Porevit, built of nearly translucent gold marble with amber windows that glow for ten miles on a sunny day, its two spiked towers carved with angels and archons said to wake if the city is threatened. It sits on its own fey gate. The Arcane College works under the Treasury of Antiquities, which approves all investigations in the Arbonesse and may search unlicensed practitioners and confiscate their finds.

### Bad Solitz
Among the peaks of the Tonder Alps, Bad Solitz fills a broad, fertile, tiered valley that climbs to a never-conquered city-fortress, walled along the surrounding hills. It smells of sulfur and ash, from the volcanic vents and mud flats on the moors; the Archon Court once came here for the hot springs. Landslides and elemental incursions shake the region, though there has been no eruption near the city for the whole of the Imperatrix's reign. The valley's volcanic soil grows some of the finest wines in the north, plus orchards, wheat, and carp ponds, enough to outlast any siege. It is a well-run, open city, home to artists and mages who find Reywald too stiff and Hirschberg too mercantile, and to the most complete library in Dornig (the Great Library of [[Friula]] disputes that). Nobles keep apartments riddled with secret passages, and everyone knows as much about their neighbors as their neighbors know about them. The Tourmaline Palace on its crag stands empty unless the Imperatrix is in residence; its towers hold pampered prisoners, and a small temple of Veles on its grounds sees fey road arrivals. There is no curfew, but the red-armored Drake Guard is everywhere.

The vann Rottstens are the most volatile of the houses, recovering from a decade of decline. Their last head, Jodario, was last seen being dragged into the Lower Planes by devils he had summoned, having recognized his illegitimate son Dimitor as heir. Dimitor's elven mother, Demalla Ravensblood Olthania, served as regent until he came of age; the official story is that she is resting, on pilgrimage to [[Ishadia]], or adventuring, and gossip says she met a bad end. Cadet branches have plotted with Morgau, Grisal, and Krakova, and now eye the boy-prince. Dimitor is paranoid for good reason.

## A Season in Court
Court sits wherever the Imperatrix is, but the true social season, the Omessali or Winter Dances, comes in winter, when snow stops ordinary travel and the fey roads bring the families together in the great cities. It runs from Redleaf, after the harvest dances, until Springmelt and the Mustering, when promotions and postings are announced with a military review and parade. It includes Ghost Night and the Lantern Festival, when debutantes are presented to the Imperatrix, and the three-day Crown Festival of the Imperatrix's Birthday on the fifth of Snowfall, now muted by her condition. Evenings bring new plays, poetry, music, balls, and feasts, where your dance partner and your seat matter enormously; those "below the salt" are there to fill chairs. Duels are outlawed but happen, assassinations happen among the lesser houses (Rowanmantle stocks up on restorative magic for the season), and many weddings and many feuds are born here.

## Gaining a Barony
The great houses always want new blood, and a famous hero willing to swear fealty can win a barony with a keep or manor and a title. Candidates must be human, elf, or elfmarked; there are no halfling or dwarf barons, and tieflings, huginn, gnomes, minotaurs, gearforged, and other "minor races" are not considered suitable. They must be highly experienced, renowned for deeds in Dornig itself (fame elsewhere counts for little), favored by a house or the crown, and good-looking enough to suit the family trees. Money is not enough, and flaunting it is gauche. Baronies are granted to one person, who answers for the whole party.

A typical barony is small, its borders visible from the keep. The liege takes most of the income, a castellan appointed by the liege keeps the books and an eye on the new baron, and the town governs itself. Most baronies have a fey road gate, and the castellan can walk the roads to the great cities. Barons who fail to turn a profit, attend court, provide soldiers, or serve their liege lose their land, by magic, siege, or assassination if necessary, and traitors die the traditional Dornig death: flayed alive and burned. Oaths of fealty are magical, sworn in court season through the Lord Arcane, and bind much like a *geas*, as they did in the elven empire.

## The Tomierran Forest
The smaller and more dangerous of Dornig's great forests was abandoned completely; the call to retreat came from the Towers of Thorn, and within a day not one elf remained. Ancient trees shade weathered ruins and towns where time stopped, the food still warm on tables the elves rose from at the sound of the horn. Treants, dryads, moss lurkers, free-willed elementals, owlbears, perytons, and other leftovers of elven experiments guard its secrets. Once the Imperatrix's private demesne, it is now held by the church of Yarila and Porevit, and looters must surrender their finds and their knowledge. Church-sanctioned expeditions leave from Thornguard, shadier ones from Tomierau.
