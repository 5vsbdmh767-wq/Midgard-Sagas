The Arbonesse is one of the great forests of Midgard, bigger than any of the [[Seven Cities]]' lands: oak, hawthorn, and elm, open meadows with dolmens and faerie rings, and ferns and moss underfoot. It lies in the west of the [[Grand Duchy of Dornig]] and is held in trust by the throne for the elves. The border between this world and the [[Silendora|Summer Lands]] is thin here, and it is easy to slip from one side to the other without noticing.

Its people are the Arbonesse exiles, elves who never answered the Last Horn, and their descendants. They live in small, self-reliant villages, swear fealty to the Imperatrix, and now and then send troops to her wars. The old elven cities of the forest have been abandoned to wild animals, except for the River Court, a string of white citadels and vineyards along the Neurabon River, with the River King's castle standing in the middle of the stream. Its fey roads to the Elflands are the shortest and most direct there are, and the River King has made it a trading post between the two worlds. The Birch Queen's Fair is held somewhere on the Summer Lands side of the forest (see [[Festivals & Trade Fairs]]).

Not all of the forest is friendly. The shadow fey have seized parts of it, and a new Dread Walker is said to have come to its western edge. Over the past decade, new elven villages have also appeared in the Arbonesse almost overnight, their people acting as if they had always been there. These newcomers are stranger than the elves Midgard knows, and closer to the other fey. The elves, it seems, are coming back.

The River Court's arms are a green shield with a wavy silver divider, a golden sun rising from the river and a silver horn hanging beneath it. It trades in nuts, timber, elven silk, river wine, and brandy wine.

## The Exiles
Most elves met across Midgard are Arbonesse exiles, scattered from the drowned stumps of Lost Arbonesse to the edge of the Wastes, and most of them have never walked the fey roads. Their elders travel to the Summer Lands, but those born in Midgard mostly stay, whether by choice or by some command from the Archons' Court no one knows. The elder elves move between this world and the Bright Land without much caring which side of the veil they are on. The forest's old cities, all but the River Court, are abandoned to wild beasts; the exiles guard them from raiders and sometimes explore them for things to trade to Dornig's Lords Arcane. They shun the regions the shadow fey have taken.

## The River Court
The River Court has lost some luster but is still the nearest thing to a living elven city. It was not built on any older city's site, and its fey roads to the Summer Lands are the shortest there are. The eldest elves in Midgard (save the Imperatrix) live here among fey followers and creatures of the Elflands. Around its white citadels on the Neurabon lie vineyards, simple houses of a few rooms, and wide amphitheaters for ceremonies both courtly and religious. [[Elfmarked]] merchants from Reywald, wisely with elven escorts, come to trade with the far side of the fey roads, and merrow, sylphs, and other water fey come to deal with humans, preferring magic and memories to gold.

Ulorian the First rules more for his own interests than to command his kin. He can stir the waters of the Neurabon and does so more and more often. The court's power has slipped with the sudden influx of pure-blooded elves, and Ulorian wonders whether something in the Elflands is driving them back. He is especially worried about the growing power of the shadow fey and their Black Prince, and hasn't decided whether to resist or give way.

## The Court of the Summer Lands
The Summer Lands are where elven culture still thrives, a parallel world where the fey rule over gnome, goblin, and halfling servants (see [[Silendora]]). Its court has ruled for more than 400 years under His Royal and Merciful Majesty King Valeshi IX, called "Falcon" or "Grandfather" by his kin, and his beloved Queen Haldifelli III, called Sifsdottir for her love of the goddess of archery and ale and for the bow skill that won her husband's hand. The aging pair retire from the balls at midnight, leaving the younger generation to their revels: six children, led by the heir, Crown Prince Raymarran "Oak" Sommerrau, and nine grandchildren, among them the Imperatrix of Dornig, plus dozens of great-grandchildren serving as generals, priests, wardens, and ambassadors. One of them, the young wizard Dolpharran "Dagger" Sommerau, recently renounced his title and left, some say to join the shadow fey courts, others say to plot the return of direct elven rule to Dornig or [[Valera]]. Strangers without strong introductions, high status, or rich gifts are politely wined, dined, and ignored.
