How each Vault of Magic armor and weapon got its price. Done 22 Nov 2026. If a price feels wrong at the table, change it on the item page and the tables will pick it up the next time I rebuild them.

## How the prices were set

- **Permanent items** use the five-step method on [[Magic Item Pricing]]: start at the lowest price for the rarity (common 25, uncommon 500, rare 2,000, very rare 15,000, legendary 25,000 gp), double it if the item is mainly for combat, double it if its benefit lasts (always on while worn, or longer than 1 minute), double it if it can affect several creatures at once, then add 10 gp per circle of the best spell it casts, or 10% if it uses charges for other powers.
- An item that grants flight, invisibility, teleporting, a pocket dimension or an endless resource is priced one rarity higher.
- **Enchanted items** (a simple power that fits any weapon or armor of a kind, like extra fire damage) use the table on [[Using Magic Items#Item Type and Value|Using Magic Items]], plus the cost of the base weapon or armor.
- **Consumables** (ammunition and other one-use items) use the consumable table: priced like the spell they copy, or by rarity.
- **Cursed items** are listed at half price, your ruling. They carry a hidden note saying so.
- No setting adjustment. List prices are standard; [[Magic Item Prices]] has the regional rule.
- These are my readings of the rules. Lines marked **Judgment** are places where I stepped away from the plain formula; check those first.

## Worth knowing

- Almost every weapon or armor with a +1 and a special power comes out at 2,000 gp (uncommon), 8,000 gp (rare) or 60,000 gp (very rare). That matches how the official Tales of the Valiant items are priced.
- The formula can go past the top of a rarity range. Encouraging Armor (120,000 gp) and Chieftain’s Axe (120,000 gp) are very rare items priced like legendary ones, because their powers help every ally. Lower them if that feels wrong.
