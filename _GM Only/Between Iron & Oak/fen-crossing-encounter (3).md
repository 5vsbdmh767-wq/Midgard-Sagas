# THE FEN — twelve hours

**1 mile per hour. Twelve miles.** Two mapped encounters; everything else theatre of the mind.

> **Everything in a box like this is meant to be read out.** Skip, reorder, cut mid-sentence.

**Noise connects the land. Blood connects the water.**

---

## THE RESIDENTS

**GRAACK — the thorn pans.** Large grey bird on stilt legs, standing on the water. Looks like a heron. Wades out where it can be seen and waits.

**THE PHIBBIT — the deep pool.** Tiny frog-fey, vividly coloured, has decorated the whole pool with stolen shiny things. Pranks trespassers until they leave. Knows which pads are safe.

**PIRANHA LOTUSES — the deep pool.** Blue-green pads with glass-flower blooms. Three patches, one awake.

**MUDCRAWLERS — thorn and channels.** Horse-sized crayfish walking on the water. Territorial, disengage rather than die. **Spring — egg sacs and juveniles everywhere.**

**THE MUD GIANT — the dry banks.** Huge, unfinished, a potter. Kiln smoke over the water all day. **Speaks Giant and nothing else.**

None of them care about each other.

## THE CROSSING — read aloud

Connective tissue for the hours between beats. Read a paragraph, or one line, or nothing.

### Setting out — hours 0 to 2

> The water is the temperature of nothing. It takes a minute to notice you can't feel where it ends and your legs begin. Every step goes down further than you expect, finds something soft, and holds — and then you have to pull the foot back out, and the mud lets go with a sound.
>
> A hundred yards in, someone works out the arithmetic out loud, and after that nobody says much.

> The smell arrives all at once rather than gradually — algae and swamp gas and something older underneath, sweetish, like a cellar. Twenty minutes later you have stopped smelling it entirely, which is somehow worse.

> Mist lies on the water in a flat sheet about knee-high. You can see over it perfectly and you cannot see your own feet.

### Deep morning — hours 2 to 5

> The trees out here stand on their own roots, three and four feet clear of the water, as though the ground went away from underneath them a long time ago and they simply stayed. Between them there is nothing to steer by. You pick a trunk, you wade to it, you pick another.

> Duckweed lies over everything in a green skin. It closes behind you as you go. Look back and there is no line, no track, no evidence that anyone came this way — just green, unbroken, all the way to where you started.

> Something small and quick goes past your shin under the water and is gone before you can react. It happens again a hundred feet on. Nobody sees what they are.

> The trees have started to lean. Not toward you — *away*, all of them, arching back from the open pans as though the water is something they would rather not be standing in either.

### Midday — hours 5 to 7

> The mist burns off and the light comes down hard, and the water throws it back up at you. Everything is glare and green. Your eyes ache. The mosquitoes arrive in a body about ten minutes later and they do not leave.

> The heat gets into the water. It stops being cold and starts being warm, thick, and faintly greasy, and it is worse.

> You find a hummock with grass and standing room and you get out of the water for the first time in six hours, and the moment you do, your legs shake. Nobody mentions it. **Whoever pulls off a boot finds two leeches. They will not be the last.**

### Afternoon — hours 7 to 9

> Flies. Not the biting kind — the other kind, that go for the corners of your eyes and your mouth and will not be waved off. There is no dry cloth left to cover anything with.

> The bottom changes. For a while it's firm, almost sand, and the walking is easy enough to be suspicious of. Then it goes without warning and you are in to the hip and going down, and it takes two of you to get the third one out.

> Bubbles come up in a slow steady line thirty feet to your left and keep coming for a full minute. Nothing surfaces. The line does not move.

### Dusk — hours 9 to 12

> The light goes orange, then goes long, and every stump and root and floating branch acquires a shadow twice its length. For about twenty minutes the fen is the most beautiful thing any of you has seen in a week.

> Then it goes blue. The mist comes back up off the water in the cold. The frogs start — thousands of them, all at once, so loud you have to raise your voice — and then, at some point you can't identify, they stop.

> You have been in water since morning. You cannot remember the specific sensation of dry. Your hands have gone soft and white and strange, and the skin on them has started to lift.

### If they push into the dark

> The water is black and the sky is black and the only difference is that the sky has nothing floating in it. You are feeling for the bottom with each foot before you commit to it, and the pace has halved again, and the numbers everyone did this morning are worthless now.

> The lights come out over the water. They keep their distance. They keep pace.

### One-liners — drop anywhere

> Something bumps your leg and doesn't move away.

> The water here tastes of iron. You did not mean to taste it.

> Sixty feet on, a dragonfly the length of your hand hangs completely motionless, then is somewhere else.

> There is a beaten path through the reeds. Something uses it regularly. It is much too wide.

> A single boot, upright in the mud, filled with water and duckweed.

> The reeds ahead have all been bent the same way, in a swathe forty feet across.

> You have walked past this tree. You are almost sure you have walked past this tree.

> Someone's stomach growls and three people flinch.

> A frog the size of a dinner plate watches you from a root, turns its head to keep watching, and does not move otherwise.

> Bones in the shallows — a deer, picked clean, articulated, all of it still lying in the shape of a deer.

> The mud gives up a smell so bad that everyone stops walking at the same moment.

> For about a hundred feet the water is completely clear and you can see the peat below, and the leaves in it, and how far down it goes.

### The channels

> You hear it first — moving water, which you have not heard all day. The channel is twenty feet across and the current in it is fast enough to be white at the edges. The bottom drops away.

> **Msshka goes in over his head.** So does Chairo, unless somebody has her.

> On the far side everyone stands in the shallows getting their breath, and behind you the channel goes on doing what it does, and there are more of them ahead.

### Looking back

> The Court is somewhere north and up, and you cannot see it, and you have been able to see nothing but water and trunks for nine hours. The only reason you know it exists is that one of you climbed for it this morning.

> Behind you the fen goes flat and grey and unbroken all the way to the horizon, and somewhere out in it the tomb is under the water again.

## TotM BEATS — pick, drop, reorder

### The pots

> A fired clay pot stands on a hummock ahead of you, upright and whole, glazed a dull ochre. Fifty feet on there's another. Then another. They go on ahead of you in a line as far as you can see through the mist, evenly spaced, all upright.

*Nobody is around. They're good pots. **Taking one matters later** — it's the phibbit's price.*

### The nursery

> The water ahead is boiling. Not with heat — with bodies. Thousands of them, each the size of a palm, pale and half-transparent, crawling over each other in the shallows. As you watch, one seizes another and begins eating it, and nothing around it reacts.

> Then the reeds part on the far side and something the size of a cart pushes into the light. Under its shell, slung between the legs, is a sac the size of a barrel.

*She will not let them near. **Going around costs an hour.** She won't pursue.*

### The cart

> A broken axle stands out of the water at an angle, one warped wheel still on it. The rest of the wagon is down in the peat — you can see the shape of it under the surface, dark and whole and further down than seems right.

*Whatever it carried is preserved. **Digging is loud.***

### Black timber

> Something has surfaced in the channel ahead — a tree trunk, thicker than any of you, standing out of the fast water at an angle. It is black. Not rotted, not burned. Black all the way through, hard as iron under your hand, and polished smooth by the current.

*Bog oak. **Thousands of years down there.** A haft or a shaft, with tools and hours.*

### The rust

> The water in this pan has a skin on it — orange, metallic, breaking into plates like oil where you step. Underneath, in the silt, there are lumps.

*Bog iron. **Rozen or Msshka knows it on sight** — it's how half the villages in the region get their metal. Rakeable by hand. Heavy.*

### The hand

> Where the peat was disturbed, something has come up. A hand, then an arm, then the rest of him — brown as saddle leather, entirely whole, the hair still on his head and the eyelids smooth and closed. There's a rope around his throat, drawn tight and knotted at the side.
>
> He is not decayed. He has been kept.

*He is not undead. **He is just extremely well preserved.** There's a knife on him, and something in his fist. Taking either is a decision, not a roll.*

### The rider

> The canopy opens overhead and the griffon is right there — low, close enough that you can see the rider look down and find each of your faces in turn. She doesn't slow. She doesn't signal. She banks left and is gone over the trees.

*She has been counting them. **She does not speak.***

## THINGS THAT HAPPEN — pick freely

Bigger than the d12, smaller than the set pieces. Each has something attached.

### Hazards

**Soft peat.** Whoever is heaviest — Msshka, or anyone in metal.
> The bottom stops being a bottom. You are in to the waist before you register it and still going, and the mud has your legs the way a fist has a rope.
*DC 13 STR (Athletics) to get out alone, or an ally with a hand and a check. **Failure costs ten minutes and whatever was in the low pockets.** No damage. Real fright.*

**The leech ground.** A slow warm stretch of black water.
> You are two hundred feet into it before anyone looks down.
*Everyone who wades it: 1d4 leeches. Removing them takes a rest and a flame. **Leaving them on is worse.** No mechanical damage unless you want it — the horror is entirely in the description.*

**Gas pocket.** Only if someone is carrying open flame.
> The bubbles have been coming up in a line to your left for a while now.
*Open flame within ten feet: the surface catches. A sheet of pale blue fire runs across the water for six seconds and goes out. **2d6 fire in a 15-ft line, DC 13 DEX for half** — and every bird in a quarter mile leaves at once, which is its own problem.*

**The cold.** Hour eight onward, if they're still in the water.
> You have been wet since dawn and the light is going and something has changed about how your hands work.
*DC 12 CON or a level of exhaustion, separate from the march. **A fire and an hour clears it.** There is nowhere to build a fire.*

### Finds

**The drowned shrine.**
> A wooden shrine stands out of the water on a post, the size of a birdhouse, its little peaked roof still holding shingles. There is a painted figure inside it. The face has gone — not broken, just worn away by weather to a pale oval. There are offerings on the shelf in front of it. They are recent.
*Coins, a twist of bread, a carved bird. **Somebody comes out here to do this.** Taking anything is a choice; leaving something is also a choice, and the fen is watching either way.*

**Stepping stones.**
> Flat stones just under the surface, spaced a stride apart, running away to the north-east. Somebody placed them. They are not going where you are going.
*Following them costs two hours and leads to the shrine, or the giant's kiln, or a dry island — **your pick.** Ignoring them is fine and slightly haunting.*

**The punt.**
> A flat-bottomed boat is drawn up on a hummock, upside down, and it is sound. The pole is under it. The rope is tied off to a root with a knot somebody meant to come back to.
*Halves their travel time for as long as the water is deep enough. **It belongs to somebody.** The phibbit knows whose. So does the giant.*

**The trapline.**
> Snares — a dozen of them, set in the reeds on the near bank at knee height, well made, with fresh cord. One of them has something in it that has been dead about a day.
*Somebody works this marsh. **Nobody has come for the catch.** That could be nothing. It could be that the graack moved in three days ago.*

**The old fire ring.**
> Stones in a circle on a dry hummock, and charcoal inside them, and the charcoal is not old. There is room here for four or five people to sleep. Somebody chose this spot because it is the only spot.
*The obvious camp. **Also the obvious place for anything that hunts people to check.***

**The dead griffon.**
> Bones in the shallows, big ones, and a great many grey and tawny feathers still caught in the reeds around them. The skull is a beak. The forelimbs end in something between a claw and a hand.
*Been here a season or more. **A Court griffon, this far out, dead.** The rider overhead may care a great deal about that, and it is not the party's fault.*

### Unsettling, no explanation offered

**The stag in the tree.**
> There is a stag caught in the fork of an alder about ten feet up. Whole, dead, not long. Nothing you can think of puts a stag ten feet up a tree.
*Do not explain this. **Ever.** If a player insists, let another player theorise and move on.*

**The bell.**
> A small iron bell hangs from a low branch out over the water, green with age, and a rope runs down from its clapper into the water and disappears.
*Ringing it: nothing happens for four minutes. Then, distantly, something rings back. **That is all that ever happens.** Whatever it means, they will not find out today.*

**The watchers.**
> Every bird in the tree ahead is facing you. Not calling, not moving — a couple of dozen of them, all turned the same way, and they track you as you pass.
*The Margreve, noticing. **No roll, no consequence.** Lesshy and Rozen might read it as neither hostile nor friendly, which is accurate.*

**The voice.**
> Somebody is singing. A long way off, across the water, a woman's voice and a real song with words you almost recognise. It stops the moment anyone calls out.
*It does not come back. It is not the phibbit — that's later and it's worse at impressions. **Leave it unresolved.***

**The eel run.** Night, or heavy overcast.
> The wet grass on the hummock is moving. All of it. Eels — hundreds, going overland in the same direction, over the moss and through the reeds and back into the water on the far side.
*Harmless. Takes twenty minutes to pass. **Nobody who sees it forgets it.***

### Choices

**The long way round.**
> The reeds close up completely ahead. Going through means cutting. Going round means an hour, and the ground looks worse.
*Cutting is fast and **costs Margreve status.** Rozen and Lesshy will know it does.*

**The carry.**
> Chairo cannot cross this one. Neither can Msshka, at speed.
*Somebody has to commit both hands. **Whoever does is out of the fight if one starts** — and one might.*

**The pot.**
> Somebody points out that the pots are good pots, and dry inside, and you are carrying wet everything.
*Taking one is free. **It is also the phibbit's price**, hours from now, and nobody will connect the two until it happens.*

# MAP 1 — THE THORN PANS · the graack

## The approach

> The thorn stands in the water here — low arching clumps of it, straw-coloured, thick with small magenta barbs, drowned to the waist and going on for a hundred yards. Between them the water lies open and pale and shallow. There is firm ground on the far side; you can see the moss on it from here.

**And out in the middle:**

> There's a heron standing in the open water. A big one, grey, up on one leg, perfectly still, with its reflection unbroken under it. It has been there since before you saw it.

*Let them pass it. **Let them decide it's a heron.***

## The bird

**AC 15 · HP 126 · Large · speed 30, fly 30 · Perception 17 by sight · Vulnerable prone**

- **Stilt-Legged** — body ten feet up. Ignores plant difficult terrain, fits 5-ft gaps. **Melee from the water reaches only its legs.**
- **Mostly Unsinkable** — walks the surface at half speed or less
- **Paralyzing Shriek** (bonus) — 15-ft cone, DC 16 CON, **deafened + paralyzed** until its next turn
- **Beak** ×3, +8, reach 10, 15 dmg
- **Seize** — replaces a Beak. Escape DC 15. Half speed and one Beak/turn while carrying.
- **Take to Higher Ground** (reaction) — saves against prone, flies off

## Read

**The turn:**
> The heron's head comes round. Its beak opens — and it keeps opening, far past where a beak should stop, and the inside of it is full of small sharp teeth.

**The shriek:**
> The sound goes through your chest rather than your ears. Your jaw locks. Your hands stop being yours.

**Seize:**
> It takes you across the middle in one bite and lifts, and your feet leave the bottom.

**Carrying you off:**
> It is walking. On the water. Not through it — on it, unhurried, at a heron's pace, and the channel is coming up.

**Toppled:**
> One leg goes and the whole thing comes apart in the air — wings, neck, a shriek cut in half — and the surface that was holding it stops holding it.

## Running it

**The answer is the legs.** Prone drops it in and it sinks. **Don't say so.** Its reaction gets it out of the first attempt.

**Terrain:** water 2 ft, difficult, −2 Acro/Stealth · channels 4 ft, DC 17 Athletics or prone, Small must swim · briar difficult, total cover · NE and SW banks are the only firm ground.

**Briar is regard, not hazard.** Passes for Lesshy and Sidious. Catches Msshka and Allia — 1 piercing per 10 ft, DC 10 DEX. **Anyone who cuts takes a Margreve status hit, and the rider is watching.**

**Solo. Past deadly for five at level 4.** It leaves if hunting turns bad.

# MAP 2 — THE DEEP POOL · the crossing

**125 × 150 ft.** A fallen log spans the pool north to south. **It is the only footing.** Everything either side is swimming water.

> **Everything in a box like this is meant to be read out.** Skip, reorder, cut mid-sentence.

---

## THE GEOGRAPHY

**The log.** Five feet wide, slick, ~130 ft end to end.
- **Half speed (15 ft) — safe, no check**
- **Full speed — DC 12 Acrobatics or fall in.** Dash is DC 15.
- **Passing each other** — both to half speed, both DC 10 Acrobatics

**The water.** Swim speed, or Athletics at half speed. **Medium or heavy armour: DC 10 Athletics each round or sink and start drowning.**

**The banks.** Firm ground north and south. **South bank ends everything.**

---

## THE LOTUSES — placement

**Three patches of blue-green pads in the pool. Only ONE is awake.**

- **Patch A — awake.** West side, adjacent to the middle third of the log. This is the statblock.
- **Patch B — dormant.** East side, near the south end.
- **Patch C — dormant.** West side, far north, behind them once they've started.

The dormant two are scenery. **Don't stat them, don't roll for them.** They exist so the safe route has to thread between three things instead of going round one — which is what makes the phibbit's directions worth buying.

**Blood wakes them.** Canon: they detect blood in the water and follow the trail. **If the fight drags, describe Patch B stirring.** That's a warning to the players, not a second encounter. Do not actually run two. The intended effect is that they run.

---

## ARRIVAL

> The trees open and there is water — real water, deep and dark and still, a hundred feet across, the first thing all day that has a bottom you cannot see. Lily pads lie flat over most of it. And across the whole span, from your bank to the far one, lies a fallen tree, thick as a cart, its bark black and wet and just about wide enough to walk.
>
> After twelve hours of wading, it looks like a gift.

**Then, before anyone steps on:**

> Someone has driven a knife into the bark of the log. Not thrown, not dropped — pushed in at an angle, hilt out, about a third of the way along. It has been there a while. It is not in anyone's way. It is just there, at that angle, and something about the placement is deliberate.

## THE WRONG PADS

> Most of the lily pads out there are green — flat, dull, ordinary. But there are patches that aren't. Bluer. And out of those, thin stems stand straight up out of the water, each one ending in a wide open flower, and the petals catch the light like they have been cut out of coloured glass.

*Nature or Perception, passive is fine, for:* **those aren't flowering the way the others are.**

## THE PHIBBIT

**CR 4 · AC 14 · HP 85 · speed 30, swim 40 · Perception 16 · Stealth 15 · immune poison · Common and Sylvan · CHA +5**

- *Invisibility* (self) and *silent image* 3/day · *major image* 1/day
- **Hypnotic Stare** (bonus) — DC 13 WIS within 30 ft or **charmed 10 min**, save each turn
- **Reactive Leap** (reaction) — hops 10 ft when attacked; out of range means **the attack fails**

### The reveal

> Something is sitting on the log. It is the size of a cat and it is the colour of a stained-glass window — wet emerald down the back, cobalt across the shoulders, a throat like a hot coal. A frog, more or less, sitting upright with its long fingers folded. Its eyes are gold with sideways pupils, and they have been on you for some time.
>
> It looks at your boots. Then your pack. Then, at some length, at whichever of you is wearing the most colour. It does not appear to be afraid of you at all.

### Voice

**Vain, territorial, cowardly, and an aesthete.** It appraises everything they own out loud and it is not kind about it. It refers to the pool as *mine* constantly. It wants them gone and would honestly rather be paid than crowded.

**It has a name and will give it unprompted, at length, and be annoyed if it is shortened.** Pick one: *Ilvenet-Who-Keeps-the-Bright-Water* · *Ossilio the Third of the Green Pool* · *Ashgrave-Brilliant* (it chose this itself and is very pleased with it).

### Opening lines — pull from

> "No."

> "You have been walking through mud for a day and now you want to walk through *my* pool. Look at you."

> "Turn around. There's another way. There isn't, but turn around."

> "That coat used to be a colour. Do you know that?"

> "You can't cross. Well — you can. You'll die. So: no."

> "Is that pot glazed? Let me see the pot."

> "You've brought a *child* through the fen. I'm not going to say anything about that. I am going to think it very loudly."

## THE PRANKS

**It is trying to make them leave. None of it is lethal. Escalate.**

**1 · The second log** *(silent image)*
> Twenty feet to your left, another tree lies across the water — lower, wider, easier. You are fairly sure it was not there a moment ago, but then you have been tired for hours.

*Stepping onto it: they fall in. No damage, just deep water and everything wet.*

**2 · The figure** *(silent image)*
> Someone is standing on the far bank waving both arms over their head. Too far to make out a face. They want you to hurry.

*Look away and back: gone.*

**3 · The rot** *(major image, its best one)*
> The log gives. Not all at once — a long splintering crack that runs the length of it, and the whole span sags a foot toward the water, and the bark under your boots goes soft and wet and black and begins to come away in sheets.

*Nothing is happening. An Investigation check, or simply continuing, reveals it.*

**4 · Hypnotic Stare** — on whoever is loudest.
> Its eyes flash — actually flash, colour rippling across them in bands — and for a moment everything it has said sounds extremely reasonable.

*DC 13 WIS or charmed 10 minutes. A charmed character wants to do what it says, and what it says is go back.*

**If they push through all of it, it changes tack and negotiates.** It is not stupid.

---

## THE DEAL

**It wants colour.** Not value — *brightness*. It has coins. It has no glazed pot, no dyed cloth, nothing amber, nothing that catches light in a way it hasn't got.

**If anyone took one of the giant's pots that morning, that's the trade.**

### What it says to offers

**Money:** > "Coins. I have coins. Look at the reeds, they're *full* of coins. Nobody looks at coins."

**Something dull but valuable:** > "It's very well made. It's the colour of a puddle."

**A weapon:** > "Where would I put it. Where. Would I put it."

**The pot, or anything bright:** > "...Give me that. Give me that and I will tell you where to put your feet, and I will not watch you drown, which is a thing I do enjoy, so you are getting a great deal."

**A story, told well:** *(if a player actually performs)* > "That was better than I expected. That was better than most of what happens here. Fine. Fine!"

### What a bargain buys

- **The route.** Which pads are safe, in order. **The pool crossed dry.**
- **One item from the decorations.** It picks. It will not be talked into a better one.
- **Gossip.** Below.

### The gossip — pull from

> "The bird. Grey thing, stands about, looks like a heron. It is not a heron. It comes here for my frogs and I have *told* it."

> "The big crawlers go round the north side in the spring. They're stupid but they're polite about it."

> "There's a grey one further in that makes pots. Enormous. Not clever. Best colours in the marsh, though — I've been trying to get one off it for eleven years."

> "Something drowned up that way a long time ago and the water kept him. He's still up there. He's very well preserved. I visit."

## IF THEY ATTACK IT

**Reactive Leap makes it maddening** — it hops ten feet and the attack simply fails. 85 hit points. It will not die fast.

**It doesn't fight back. It leaves.**

> It is not there. There is no splash and no sound, and the colour is gone off the reeds too, all of it, in the time it took you to look.

**Consequences:** no route, no decorations, and Patch A is between them and the far bank.

## IF THE LOTUSES GO OFF

### The reveal

> The blue pads move. Not drifting — *moving*, sliding across the water toward the log against the current, and the beautiful glass flowers on their stems are turning to face you as they come. Every one of them opens. Inside each is a ring of small wet teeth.

### Swarm of Piranha Lotuses — CR 5

# THE MUDCRAWLERS

**AC 15 · HP 80 · Large · speed 40, swim 60 · Water Walker · amphibious**
Charge (+5 dmg, DC 13 STR or prone) · Gore +5, 14, DC 13 CON or **speed halved** · Pincer +5, 10, **grapple, two targets** · Crush (bonus) DC 13 STR or 7

> Something is walking on the water. Not swimming — walking, on the surface, fast, and it is the size of a plough horse. It is a crayfish. The shell is furred green with moss and the antennae are longer than a man.

**They are territorial, not hungry.** They come to move the party off, and they **disengage into the muck** rather than die.

**They can be bought.** Villagers keep them fed at a perimeter as a deterrent, and a fed crawler learns who feeds it. **Don't offer this.** If someone throws rations in the water:

> It stops. Both antennae come round. It goes for the food and not for you, and afterwards it stays about forty feet off, keeping pace, watching.

**A bought crawler follows them for the rest of the day.** That is a real and slightly alarming asset.

**One is a scene. Two is a fight. Three would kill them.**

# THE MUD GIANT

**CR 7 · AC 13 · HP 173 · Huge · speed 40 · Vulnerable fire · Languages: Giant**

**Do not fight this. 27 a slam with a grapple attached.**

## It comes because of noise

> The reeds go over ahead of you, a whole stand of them, pushed flat — and something stands up out of the water that you had taken for a bank.
>
> It is grey and wet and enormous, and it is not finished. The arms are smooth where arms should have detail. The face is barely out of the clay — a suggestion of a brow, two hollows, a mouth that hasn't been opened yet. Wet clay runs off it in sheets and keeps running.
>
> It looks at you the way you would look at a bird that had come in a window.

## It cannot talk to them

**It speaks Giant. Nobody here does.** It tries anyway — slow, patient, repeating itself, gesturing with hands the size of doors.

**This is the scene.** It is not menacing them and it cannot explain itself, and every attempt makes it more insistent because it thinks it's being unclear.

## What it wants

**Kin to Rejects.** Mud giants read themselves as unfinished, and they collect orphans, exiles and wounded animals into an ersatz family.

> It goes very still. Then it crouches — the whole mass of it, down to your level, slowly — and it looks at the child. Then at the one with the growths coming out of his shoulder. Then back at the child.
>
> It puts one enormous open hand flat on the water in front of them, palm up, and waits.

**It is offering. It thinks it is being kind. It has no idea that it isn't.**

## How it goes

- **They refuse clearly** — it doesn't understand, and asks again, twice more. Then it withdraws, and it is visibly hurt, and it goes back to its kiln.
- **They stall or hesitate** — it waits. It has all day. It will follow at a distance.
- **They give it something** — a pot returned, a shown kindness, an animal. It takes that instead. **This is the way out** and it costs them nothing but a moment of attention.
- **They attack it** — they die, or they run. Fire *hardens* it before it crumbles it: AC up three, wounds that keep bleeding.
- **They flee** — it doesn't pursue. It stands in the water and watches them go.

## Afterwards

**It knows where they went.** Speed 40 and no reason to hurry.
