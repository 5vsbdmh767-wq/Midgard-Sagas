> [!tip] Challenges for the Well-Equipped Adventurer
> The more powerful the gear your PCs have, the harder it is to challenge them. What types of challenges should a GM throw at a tooled-up party?
>
> The obvious first step is to throw more challenging monsters at the party. But to get the most bang for your buck, you need to do more than just select some creature at the next CR up the scale. Play to your party’s strengths while targeting areas that may challenge them.
>
> **Take advantage of damage resistances.** Use monsters that are resistant (or immune) to nonmagical weapons.
>
> This encourages PCs to use new magic weapons and makes them feel like getting them was worthwhile. With stronger weapons, they can take down stronger foes. If the party has magic items that deal other damage types (especially elemental damage), consider using monsters with an array of vulnerabilities and resistances. Give your fighter with the flame tongue sword a fire-weak treant to take down occasionally. Then follow it up with a fireresistant glabrezu encounter to challenge the fighter to try new tactics.
>
> **Increase the number of monster actions.** Consider giving monsters more capital to spend in the action economy.
>
> Monsters with legendary actions are the built-in way to do this. To increase the challenge, add 1 to a monster’s standard legendary action count. You can also increase the number of attacks a monster can make with Multiattack or, for a bigger challenge bump, allow a monster to go twice in the initiative order. That’s not as powerful as having two of the monster, since it still only has one monster’s worth of hit points, but it will vastly change the encounter, and the PCs are likely to focus their attention on that opponent.
>
> **Shift the focus of the encounter.** Maybe the enemy isn’t a major threat to the party in straight combat. If it were just a standard fight, the outcome would be certain. But not all encounters are straight up throwdowns. Maybe the enemy has hostages, they’re terrorizing a town, or they’re attempting to complete a world-ending ritual. These kinds of encounters are all about speed and smart thinking for the party. The faster they take out the enemy, the more damage they can prevent.
