GM-only summaries of the twelve dread villains in *Night Hunters* chapters 3 to 6. Stat blocks, maps, and full scheme write-ups stay in the book; page numbers below are the book's printed pages. The book sets most of them on worlds linked by [[The Labyrinth]] rather than in Midgard. Anything under a "Midgard Fit" heading is my suggestion, not canon and not a ruling.

The party is 4th level (bible v5.26). Tier 1 villains are at the top of their range now. Tier 2 is the next fit.

## Shared Mechanics

Each villain has its own shadow-token hooks (extra ways to earn and spend tokens, on top of [[Shadow Tokens]]), a villainous agenda of schemes with complications, consequences, and clues, a realm with lair actions and regional effects, and a Rewards section with new magic items. Tier 3 and 4 villains add Benchmark Challenges tables for sizing the final fight.

## Eugene Mott, the Toymaker

The last scion of a rich merchant family. After his wife and two daughters died in a carriage accident, he called into the Labyrinth for a way to make new, perfect children, and the Ascendant One answered. He built the Factory in the pocket dimension Jollynook, turned his workers into toy servants, and opened identical Mr. Mott's Toys shops across the planes, each run by a clone sharing a limited hive mind. He picks adult customers as candidates (never children), has them abducted, and those who fail his tests become workerdolls or have their souls put into toys. No one has passed yet.

### In Play

He can be an early patron or friendly shopkeeper whose nature comes out later. Conflict comes through abductions, his minions (bantlings, gigglers, soulbound stuffies), and raids for magic items to fuel the Factory's furnace, as in the Requiem Trove scheme against a cathedral vault. Killing him only kills a clone. To end him, the PCs must sever his pact with the Ascendant One, which kills every clone; the patron rebuilds the Factory otherwise.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for discovering the Crystal Heart and the pact, freeing a bound soul, and reaching the Factory. They can be spent to choose a Factory portal's destination or to resist his Father's Tears and Puppeteering. Rewards include the *discombobulating monocle*, the *repair needle*, and Mott's Collection of toys. The Ascendant One may offer a PC a similar pact.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

A Mr. Mott's Toys in [[Zobeck]] would suit the clockwork city. The Labyrinth link already exists in canon.

## Garzea Family, Undying Nobles

During a plague centuries ago, patriarch Igon Garzea sealed his household in, then accepted a pact from a voice offering protection and eternal life for sacrificed souls. He murdered the servants to seal it. His eldest daughter Ariné hanged herself rather than live with it. The entity, Itzal ("the Savior"), is a faceless, tar-like Aberration (Outsider) drawn by the plague's despair. It mostly possesses Igon and feeds on the suffering around the family. The Garzeas move to new cities, build a respectable name, harvest victims, and retreat to their ancestral estate, Baseliza Itzali, until suspicion fades.

### In Play

The family can be a slow, creeping threat for months or a more aggressive one. Schemes include Doomed Betrothed (a child marries a rich, isolated noble who is later sacrificed) and a Grand Event ball. If exposed early, they use influence to push back, then flee. The final fight is with Itzal, who jumps between family hosts.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for attending the ball unarmed and unarmored, infiltrating as a servant, or visiting the estate alone as a betrothed. They can be spent to resist the Ties That Bind charm or to interrupt Itzal's Soul Siphon. Rewards include the *Garzea sacrificial dagger*, and the cleansed estate can become a PC base (base building).

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

Any southern coastal city with old vineyard nobility, such as one of the [[Seven Cities]]. Itzal is an Aberration drawn by despair. I'd keep it unconnected to the Void threads in the bible unless you want that link.

## Thornwick Hall, Murder Mansion

Baron Virgil Thornwick founded and enriched the town of Hargrove while secretly being a serial killer, hiding victims in the mansion's walls and foundations. After his death, his gentle son Viktor was driven mad by the victims' spirits and slaughtered the household, then killed himself. The massacre birthed a sentient entity that fused with the house. It manifests as the Caretaker, feeds on the trapped spirits, and lures new victims, most visibly through a woman in white with a ghostly lantern who leads entranced townsfolk inside (a will-o'-wisp puppet).

### In Play

The book calls it half creature, half dungeon, built as a horror opener for 1st to 3rd level. Warn the players beforehand that their PCs will be trapped inside for several sessions. The house can't be exited or damaged. The Caretaker escalates if the PCs resolve spirits' torments or explore the attic, the red room, or the mausoleum. There are two ways out. Defeating the Caretaker in its lair (the catacombs and mausoleum) opens a brief window, but it regenerates in 24 hours. Resolving the torment of three Thornwick Spirits destroys the house for good.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for resolving a spirit's torment, roleplaying the Haunting Visions, and surviving a night. They can be spent to ward a room for a short or long rest. Rewards include about 1,000 gp in valuables, a *skeleton key*, and a *spirit board*, which casts the *shadow board* ritual.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

Hargrove could be any declining timber town on a forest edge, such as the fringes of [[The Margreve]]. The hooks name the monster hunter, practical occultist, and spirit caller backgrounds.

## Alexander Morrello, Diabolic Artist

A child prodigy whose drawings come to life. His first was a six-clawed monster that killed his nanny for denying him dessert. Patrons who crossed him died, his parents vanished, and he ran away at 15. Now he drifts between cities on patrons' money, and his painted nightmares kill people through "accidents," disease, curses, or slaughter, sometimes after he has already moved on. His favorite creation is Scamp. He is charming, petty, and craves adoration.

### In Play

It plays as a mystery: track the nightmares back to the artist. His schemes are Patron's Favor (forged letters of introduction to win a rich sponsor), expanding his favor through gallery showings, and the Artist's Retribution against those who slight him. He flees rather than fights. Removing his gift takes repeated *remove curse* over several days or divine intervention. Whether the gift compelled him or he simply lacks restraint is left to you.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for attending his events, facing your own nightmare at a disadvantage, or sitting for a bound portrait. They can be spent to force a reroll of his Quick Nightmare recharge or to auto-succeed against his Devilish Charm. Rewards include the *shard of nightmares*, which drops from painted nightmares of CR 8 or higher.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

Any cosmopolitan city with an art scene and a corrupt council: [[Triolo]] or another of the [[Seven Cities]], or [[Zobeck]].

## Marquis Leopold Vollmond, Dread Werewolf

The hereditary Marquis of Dornmartsch, a poor, wolf-rich eastern borderland that guards its kingdom against eastern raiders. He is a prime werewolf, descended from the first werewolf. Plague, raids, and emigration have shrunk his people. With the alchemist Liola Krentz, he is dosing the town well of Fichtenstadt with a catalyst made from his blood and that of northern worgs. At the next full moon, the townsfolk will become dornwulves under his control. Outwardly he is a charming, generous host. Beneath that he is a paranoid xenophobe who sees his people as possessions.

### In Play

The opening incident: brothers Henry and Louis Krieger stole tainted whisky and have been changing into dornwulves at night, killing livestock. A day-by-day timeline runs from the PCs' arrival, with prices rising as the panic spreads. He invites the PCs to dinner, never actually commits to anything, pays them to deal with outside threats, and sends assassins once they become a problem. He flees at half his hit points and then goes after whatever the PCs care about.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for accepting false information, choosing to fail lycanthropy saves, freezing on learning the dornwulves are victims, and giving in to violence. They can be spent for immunity to the town water, to end the curse with a hit die, to find a willing NPC, or to bypass his nonmagical immunity. Rewards include the *Dornmartsch map*, *heart thorn (herzdorn)*, and *staff of the thornmarch*.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

The German names and the borderland march suit the [[Grand Duchy of Dornig]] or another Electoral Kingdom on the edge of [[The Margreve]]. The "great power growing in the mountains beyond" could be something in [[The Ironcrags]].

## Sardea the Vile, Ghoul Emperor

You ruled on October 19 that Sardea replaces Nicoforus the Pale as Ghoul Emperor of [[The Darakhul Imperium]]. The wiki now names Sardea as emperor, with the White City as the capital, and says Nicoforus ruled before. How and when Nicoforus fell is open; the wiki doesn't say. The book itself notes that its Imperium is loosely based on *Empire of the Ghouls* but meant to stand apart from Midgard, so the two can be combined.

Sardea woke as a ghoul, spent years looking for a way back to life, and came to see undeath as a blessing to share. From a throne of corpses in the Corpus Sanctum of the White City, he rules thousands of starving ghouls. He is eloquent, patient, and genuinely protective of his subjects, with spasms of hunger breaking through. He wants stability and a reliable supply of flesh. The Unsated Maw, a living cult that worships him as a god-sovereign, runs vassal villages that pay tribute in flesh.

### In Play

The Necrocracy of Zemmir Roet scheme is a good opener. Ghast emissaries offer a village protection from a goblin host in exchange for secret fealty and 5,000 pounds of flesh a month. Killing the emissaries brings a brutal reprisal. The PCs face the Unsated Maw first, then the Imperium's strength. Sardea favors hospitality and kept promises, and positions the PCs as tools. The endings are Regicide (often helped by would-be usurpers inside the city) or Covenant (an uneasy coexistence, with a coup against Sardea coming either way). If Sardea dies, the Imperium falls into civil war.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for concealing the Imperium's plans, defeating its enemies, aiding Sardea's agenda, or keeping pacts with its allies. They can be spent to act at 0 HP (by eating a bite of Humanoid flesh) or to befuddle low-CR Undead. Rewards include the *last words* boons (six varieties, whose curse lets Sardea track the bearer) and *nox virulus*. The White City's gates and the Corpus Sanctum carry the sigil of suffering hazard.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

The Imperium's tunnels run under [[The Ironcrags]] and [[The Margreve]] (per the wiki), so Sardea's agents can reach the campaign region directly.

"Unsated" is already a traditional darakhul surname ([[Peoples of Shadow#Darakhul]]), and the White City's "nameless gods of hunger" sit easily with [[Vardesain]], the darakhul's hunger god, so the Unsated Maw could be a Vardesain sect.

The book's coming coup echoes Vilmos Marquering's failed coup, which founded the [[Twilight Empire]] in the Shadow Realm, so the Twilight Emperor is a ready-made backer for a usurper.

The bible has nothing on the Imperium, so nothing here clashes with it.

## Cult of Umbral Harmony, Cosmic Harbingers

In the Labyrinth, the syderean sorcerer Quoden Panovar and his apprentice Roxif touched an entity pressing against the Labyrinth's veil, a being that watches the Material Plane from beyond reality. Roxif's flesh was remade into the Vestige, a rippling sphere of mirror-bright metal that houses a fragment of that entity. Quoden now worships it. The public face is a gentle religion called the Numinous Veil. The inner cult is the Umbral Harmony, led by the silent Scribes of Silence.

### In Play

The schemes run in order. In Enemy of My Prey, the cult wins over a desperate town by destroying its threat. In Realm of Our Own, it claims a Labyrinth passage. In Pierce the Veil, it tries to reach the entity again, which would warp everything nearby and threaten Labyrinth travel. The Vestige is apathetic and loses interest once Quoden dies. Corpses in its area become Aberrations. Quoden uses the archmage stat block with INT and CHA swapped.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for destroying the cult's Aberrations, pursuing them in the Labyrinth, and unmasking the Numinous Veil. They can be spent to sense the nearest Labyrinth passage or to banish an Aberration. Rewards include *spell scrolls* of 4th to 6th circle from grateful faiths, and the fabled *ontolic mirror*.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

The Labyrinth is canon and in the bible. The entity sits beyond the veil in the Void ("Ginnungagap" in Midgard canon). The bible's Void threads (Derende, the Weft, Family Starless) are central to the campaign, so I'd keep this cult unconnected to them, the way the bible keeps its other cults apart, unless you decide otherwise.

## Doctor Baila Luminada, Flesh Stitcher

An elf physician in the seaside town of Arastella, in the peninsula nation of Ibbresa. Her husband Jorme co-founded the Revolutionary Assembly. Her four sons (Ayram, Edouard, Casim, Efran) fought for it and all died in the war's last battle, while she ran a neutral field hospital and invented a solution for reattaching limbs and organs. Commander Arvid Bazmonde now rules Ibbresa as dictator from Arastella. With her daughter-in-law Zopia (Edouard's widow), she has finished one war golem from Edouard's remains, has twenty flesh golems in her surgery's cellar, and keeps a clone of herself. She blames Bazmonde for her sons' deaths. Few healers capable of resurrection survived the war's purges, which is why she chose golems.

### In Play

She starts as a helpful doctor. Zopia, grieving and uneasy, is the PCs' way in. In Bring Her Children Back, she makes a golem from each remaining son, three days apart. In Seek Justice, once she has two golems, she attacks Bazmonde. If cornered, she moves into her clone, and at the end into Mother Grief, a construct body of spare parts, and tries to raze Arastella. She eventually tries to turn Zopia into a golem too. Uses the master alchemist stat block in Humanoid form.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for letting her replace a limb, capturing a son-golem instead of destroying it, killing Bazmonde, or turning Zopia. They can be spent to bypass Construct immunities, to find a consumable item, or to read a Construct's immunities and hit points. Rewards include the *heart surge* and *limblife solution*.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

A small republic recovering from civil war fits the [[Seven Cities]], whose city-states war often. The book's shadow-token text for this villain says "Ibaila and the fallen angels" by mistake.

## Masika the Golden Goddess, Dread Mummy

Thousands of years ago, Masika was a priestess of the River Avaret who sacrificed herself to guide Pharaoh Ketheses through the afterlife toward his promised godhood and golden age. She has woken as a mummy lord in his Golden Pyramid to find his bones dust, his line out of power, his palace now the Grand Library of Hut-Avaret, and the river failing. She means to bring the golden age herself and to wipe out the Ketheses bloodline. She hides her undead face with magic and secretly wants to be worshiped as the savior.

### In Play

The PCs first meet her as a sweet young woman studying in the library archives. In Golden Age, she uses the *Idol of Keth* to work three miracles, which the idol's curse corrupts into disasters because she isn't of the bloodline. The first is a flood that strands fish and then keeps rising. The other schemes are Revenge on the Ketheses Bloodline and Amass Peacekeepers, an undead army from the necropolis. She reforms if destroyed while her holy symbol survives. Her right hand is Sullah the Devourer.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for not intervening, destroying risen townsfolk, or using a Ketheses descendant as bait. They can be spent for mummy rot immunity, radiant weapon damage, or reading an Undead's defenses. Rewards include the *blighted axe*.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

A river-kingdom desert city with pyramids and pharaohs fits [[Nuria Natal]] in the Southlands.

## Boyaryna Vieira Krovivna, Dread Vampire

When Boyar Nikolai Orlyk and his wife Natalia died, the valley's customs forced their eldest daughter Vieira either to marry or to pass the title to a distant male cousin. Instead, she and her sisters Nytsaya and Voronika turned to sorcery and drank the cousin's blood in a ritual that made them vampires. They renamed themselves Krovivna and have ruled Kazhangrad for over a century, keeping its people enchanted and ignorant. Vieira keeps her parents' bones in a box under her bed and talks to them.

### In Play

The schemes run in order. In New Blood, vampire emissaries (Aleera Ulrich, Harlan Amaliyev, Ivar Yurnayev) recruit merchants and settlers with charm, bribes, and lavish parties. In Army of the Dead, undead raiding grows. In the Ritual of the Black Sun, Vieira gathers artifacts for a ritual that would let her line walk in daylight. The final fight is at Castle Kazhangrad during the ritual. Her sisters can be slain earlier. If she falls, the valley is freed but leaderless.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for letting a vampire feed as a sacrifice, destroying her agents, and collecting ritual items. They can be spent against vampire charm and Legendary Resistance. Rewards include a large treasury and the sentient *Orlyk reliquary*, which holds her parents' bones and spirits.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

A Slavic mountain valley ruled by vampires suits the highlands of [[Krakovar]], as an independent power the [[Blood Kingdom]] tolerates or courts. The "Black Sun" ritual should stay unconnected to the bible's GM-only Chernobog cult, in keeping with how the bible keeps its cults apart.

## Elerezhe, Draconic Dread Lich

Son of the ancient blue dragon Qaivezhe and a human mother. After he won the *Abundant Cauldron* from the archmage Larzus, his father tried to kill him. Elerezhe survived, turned the cauldron into the *Threnodic Well*, learned to bind dead dragons, and became a lich. His five phylacteries are reanimated dragons called Avatars of Avarice, the first being his father. He secretly rules the mountain kingdom of Vultarrais through King Dyer Narlund, the Temple of the Ascendant Dragon, and a web of agencies (the Ministry of Unity, Exchequer's Office, Harrier League, Electrum Guard, and Correctional Academies). He means to bind or kill every dragon, slay the dragon gods, and become the one true Dragon God.

### In Play

The PCs are first hired, through his paymaster Aggre or Harrier League leader Norriq Gyre, to kill a pair of copper dragons. His twin schemes, Dominate or Destroy Dragons and Amass Magical Power, run together, followed by the rituals of Become the Dragon God. Missing dragons have caused a surge of monsters, and Scalesworn missionaries preach the new faith. The finale is in the Temple of the Ascendant Dragon as Vultris becomes a necropolis. He returns in 1d10 days unless every Avatar is destroyed. His arrogance can leave an Avatar alive.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for turning populations from his faith, freeing or defeating dominated dragons, realizing the Avatars are phylacteries, and slaying his named associates. They can be spent to resist dragons and energy types, to read a Dragon's or Undead's defenses, or to grow wings. Rewards include a large hoard and several legendary items.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

Dragon politics suits the borders of [[The Mharoti Empire]], or a lich-ruled kingdom suits the [[Wasted West]]. A would-be dragon god would draw the dragon empire's attention.

## Ibaila, Fallen Angel

Ibaila was an archangel of the Thrice (the sun gods Qoien, Ophlir, and Lese) on the Labyrinth world Pharisos. Adventurers serving [[Sabateus]] destroyed the Thrice and absorbed their domain. Ibaila and its siblings, Thrice Ignored and Thrice Forgotten, avenged them, then lost their purpose and fell. Now they believe the future is only safe when no mind marks the passing of the day. They wage holy war on populous worlds, especially cities with a temple of Sabateus. Ibaila shifts form each dawn between Sunrise, Midday, and Dusk. Pharisos's sun is frozen in the sky, and Ibaila still drags it across the dead world.

### In Play

The Fallen announce themselves openly at sunrise, then work a ritual at each of the next three sunrises. The first is Locus of Corruption: Thrice Ignored spreads disease from inside the city, with a *divine ebullition* as backup. They spare beaten PCs and kill only those who trap or obstruct them. The book suggests the optional Doom rules from *Monster Vault* for them. Ibaila can drag a grappled PC into the Deep Void.

### Shadow Tokens and Rewards

Tokens are earned for exploring a world they destroyed, failing or stopping a ritual, and long talks about the Thrice. They can be spent to survive a Celestial's killing blow (with a price) or for a vision of the current ritual. Rewards include the *blessed pinion*, *mender's symbol*, and *oil of cleansing*.

### Midgard Fit (suggestion)

Sabateus is already on the wiki. The book says any sun-related god can stand in for him.
