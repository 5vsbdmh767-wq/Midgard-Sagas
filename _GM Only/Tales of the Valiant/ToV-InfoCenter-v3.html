Lineages & Heritage
ToV replaces "Race" with Lineage + Heritage. Lineage = biological traits. Heritage = cultural upbringing. Source: PG
ToV Lineage/Heritage System In Tales of the Valiant, character ancestry is split into two components: Lineage (physical and innate traits — replaces race) and Heritage (cultural background and upbringing — distinct from background). This allows mixing a human lineage with a dwarvish heritage, for example.

Combat Rules Reference
Key combat mechanics from Tales of the Valiant, including ToV-specific rules. Source: PG
Core Combat Flow
On your turn: Move (up to speed) + Action + Bonus Action (if available) + Free Object Interaction. Reaction available once per round on any turn.
Attack · Cast a Spell · Dash · Disengage · Dodge · Help · Hide · Ready · Search · Use an Object · Improvised actions (GM discretion)
Luck Points are a unique ToV resource. Characters typically begin with 0 Luck and accumulate it through play. Luck Points can be spent to reroll a d20, grant advantage, or trigger class/heritage abilities. Max Luck tracked on character sheet (default 3). This is the primary mechanical divergence from standard 5e. In Foundry, map to resources.luck.
1: Disadvantage on Ability Checks · 2: Speed halved · 3: Disadvantage on Attack rolls and Saving Throws · 4: HP maximum halved · 5: Speed reduced to 0 · 6: Death. Remove one level per long rest (with food/water).
3 Successes = stabilize. 3 Failures = death. Roll d20 at start of each turn at 0 HP. Natural 20 = regain 1 HP. Natural 1 = 2 failures. Taking damage at 0 HP = 1 failure (2 if critical). Tracked on character sheet under Death Saves.

Field Guide to Monster Harvesting — Vol. 1 & 2
Optional rules for harvesting monster parts, crafting unique materials, and monstrous magic items. Compatible with 5e and ToV. Sources: FGH1 FGH2
About FGH Vol. 1 Published by Kobold Press (Open Design LLC, ©2025). Designers: JB Little, Meagan Maricle, Mike Welham. Introduces the complete monster harvesting system: proficiency requirements, harvesting checks, material categories, 40+ general unique materials, monster-specific materials from Monster Vault 1 & 2, and a full monstrous magic items catalog. Vol. 2 expands this framework with Creature Codex and Tome of Beasts 1–3 materials.
Who Can Harvest
Qualifying Proficiencies A PC must have proficiency in one of the following to attempt harvesting: Survival skill (for most creatures), Trapper Tools (for most creatures), Arcana (GM discretion — for noncorporeal or uniquely magical creatures), Tinker Tools (GM discretion — for Constructs or machine-like creatures). Roll a WIS ability check using the qualifying proficiency against the creature's Harvesting DC.
Harvesting Process
Collecting 1 Harvest takes approximately 1 hour. Total time scales with creature size: a Tiny creature (½ Harvest) takes ~30 min; a Gargantuan creature (8 Harvest) takes up to 8 hours to fully harvest. Best performed outside of perilous locales when time is not a factor.
The PC gains 1 Harvest from one material category of their choice, AND the creature's total available Harvest is reduced by 1.
The PC gains NO Harvest during the attempt, AND the creature's total available Harvest is still reduced by 1 — the failed attempt ruins some material.
If a PC has proficiency in BOTH Survival and Trapper Tools, AND exceeds the Harvesting DC by 5 or more: the PC gains an extra 1 Harvest of any category, even beyond the creature's normal maximum. This bonus can only be gained once per creature, regardless of how many successful attempts are made.
Some materials can be harvested without killing the creature. A living creature that has been harvested can produce that same harvest again after 1d6+1 days as the material regrows. The GM may adjust this timeframe based on the material and creature. This does not change the creature's total Harvest maximum.
Unless overruled by the GM, harvesting Humanoids is an expressly evil act rejected by all societies. NPCs who witness (or suspect) this become immediately hostile. Materials from Humanoids cannot be sold, purchased, or used to craft items. GMs should establish at session zero whether humanoid-adjacent creatures (centaurs, satyrs, etc.) fall under this prohibition. Note: this hostility only applies to lethal harvesting — a creature that freely gifts part of its body is exempt.

Market Conditions The GM has final say on value. Common materials (deer hide in rural communities) may be worth only copper. Rare magical materials in low-magic settings may require finding a specialist buyer via Carousing downtime.
Crafting with Harvested Materials
Nonmagical Items (Crafting Downtime) When the harvested material is clearly relevant to the item being crafted, each Harvest expended reduces the gold cost to craft by 25%. Minimum cost floor: 25% of original value (cannot be reduced further, no matter how many Harvests are spent). Time to craft is NOT reduced. Example: plate armor normally costs 750 gp to craft → 1 Harvest of dragon hide reduces to 563 gp → maximum 4 Harvests cannot drop below 187 gp.
Consumable Magic Items (Potion Making Downtime) When clearly relevant, 1 Harvest of material reduces the consumable magic item crafting cost by 25%. This reduction can only be applied once — additional Harvests beyond the first have no effect. Time to craft is NOT reduced.
Spell Components 1 Harvest can replace a material spell component with a listed gp value of up to 10 × the creature's CR (minimum CR 1 for this calculation). The Harvest is always consumed by the spell, even if the spell normally doesn't consume components. GM may rule that thematically inappropriate substitutions (e.g., 100 wolf hides replacing a 1,000 gp diamond for resurrection) don't qualify.
Optional Rule: Decaying Harvest
Standard Campaigns: No tracking needed. Skilled harvesters are assumed to properly preserve materials. Survival Campaigns: Harvested materials decay, evaporate, or become unusable after 7 days unless preserved (e.g., gentle repose spell) or incorporated into a crafted item. Particularly rare or unique materials may decay sooner or last longer at GM's discretion.
Six Material Categories
🦴
Bone (Bo)
Skeleton, claws, fangs, spines, spikes, horns. Used to craft armor, shields, weapons. Smaller bones as decorative adornments. Bone powder/marrow for potions.
🐾
Hide (Hi)
Skin, scales, outer layer. Used to craft armor, blankets, clothing.
🩸
Fluid (Fl)
Blood, venom, other liquids. Used to craft potions, oils, pastes, antitoxins, unguents, poultices. Harvested poisons are injury type with one use per Harvest — failing to harvest subjects the harvester to the poison's effects.
🧠
Viscera (Vi)
Muscle tissue, organs, internal parts. Used to craft foods, potions, containers. 1 Harvest of edible viscera sustains two Small/Medium Humanoids for one day.
🪶
Hair (Ha)
Hair, fur, whiskers, feathers, fibrous material that isn't hide. Used to craft clothing, dolls, thread, decorative adornments.
🦑
Appendage (Ap)
Extra limbs not fitting other categories: tentacles, antennae, etc. Used to craft armor, weapons, decorative adornments, specialized equipment (e.g., hollowed eyestalk → spyglass).
Category Assignment The GM determines which categories a creature produces. Many creatures can produce materials in almost all categories; some produce only a few. Not every creature that meets a material's listed criteria necessarily produces it (e.g., a bloodless creature can't produce blood-based materials).

Overview
Inspired by the Norse Poetic Edda, the ratatosk are celestial humanoid squirrels who dwell on Yggdrasil. Insatiable gossips and tricksters whose joy in gathering secrets is only eclipsed by their delight in spreading those morsels mischievously. They build fortified towers and sprawling tree cities in secret nooks or suspended beneath the vast branches of Yggdrasil.

Culture & Society
AspectDetails
SakmootDaily morning gathering to exchange news and gossip. Most important community event. "The softest whisper in the farthest nook of the endless void will eventually be repeated in the smallest sakmoot."
Personal PropertyTreated as a vague guideline rather than law. "Borrowing" shiny or interesting items with full intent to return — unless distracted.
SecretsNo secrets exist among ratatosk. Exchange of gossip is routine and expected before any business is conducted.
SettlementsExterior buildings of World Tree bark in branch crooks (DC 15 Perception to spot). Network of halls, burrows, and workshops carved into Yggdrasil's outermost bark. Each dray has a feasting hall and a shrine to Ratatosk the Clever.
WanderlustYoung ratatosk exhibit wanderlust upon reaching adulthood. Most explore Yggdrasil gathering gossip from Vedfolnir, Nidhogg, or travelers.
FoodPrimarily vegetarian. Main dish: gryta (rich nut stew from World Tree acorns with honey and spices). Also berries and flower bulbs from vertical gardens. Love Midgard sweets.

Holidays & Religion
Every single day of the ratatosk seasonal calendar is marked by a holiday. Reasons include: important anniversaries, heroes/legends, the first day of the month, Fur Brushing Day, arrival of visitors, the last day of the month, a favorite food, celebrating the color green (d8 table, p.5).
Major CelebrationDetails
ViskarmootFirst day of Harvest Tide: each community sends its strongest/cleverest to the Aerie, Nidhogg's den, or the planes for 4 months. On last day of Snowfall, they gather in Grenstad to publicly present secrets to the Squirrel Court.
Fallskarm CelebrationFirst week of High Summer. Commemorates Churr Scurida who leaped off the World Tree with a cure. Participants don winged garments and hurl themselves from Yggdrasil. Winner receives rare magic items or the right to ask Vedfolnir (the all-seeing hawk) one question.

Planar Guides — Fees & Rules
Ratatosk often act as planar guides. No formal guild, but most follow unwritten rules: state destination before departure; half-pay up front (or geas-backed promissory); quickest and safest route; no petting the guide; no squirrel jokes. Preferred payment: rare lore, juicy gossip, magical items, or shiny things. Some accept coin.

Labyrinth Worldbook — Overview
The space between the worlds. A campaign setting of interconnected worlds, factions, portals, and the ever-hungry Void. Lead Designers: Wolfgang Baur, Celeste Conowitch, Richard Green. Publisher: Kobold Press. ©2025. Source: LAB
What is the Labyrinth? The Labyrinth is the liminal "space between worlds" — a tangle of tunnels, bridges, waterways, and stranger pathways connecting all worlds, bright and dark alike. It is not an outer plane or an inner plane. It is a road, not a world, sprinkled with pocket demiplanes, bottled cities, and the ruins of ancient civilizations. It is suffused by the Ethereal Plane and connected to the Astral. All travel between one world and another passes through the Labyrinth at some point.

Seven Secrets of the Labyrinth
SecretDescription
Worlds Can Be Created and DestroyedEntire worlds can be born and die in days; others last timeless ages. Worlds can be grown from seeds, constructed, or dreamed into existence, or arise from the Astral Sea.
The Labyrinth Is Not a World, but a RoadThe Great Maze is a set of passages, tunnels, rivers, roads, and connections sprinkled with small demiplanes. It predates the minotaurs, though their explorations awakened it. Connects all mazes on all worlds.
Five ConnectionsThe five connectors: (1) pathways (tunnels/caverns) — the greatest; (2) River Styx; (3) branches of Yggdrasil; (4) the Astral Sea; (5) the Void (most dangerous, via void rifts).
All Roads Go Through the LabyrinthAll travel between worlds passes through the Labyrinth at some point. Shortcuts dip in and back out via portals and rifts.
Mortal Worlds Stand AloneInhabited worlds are larger and more populated than the Labyrinth itself. Most inhabitants are ignorant of the Labyrinth. Only fiends, celestials, voidlings, and wayfarers know how to enter it.
Walkers Create Paths, Paths Create RoadsThe Labyrinth grows by absorbing power from all four magic sources (Arcane, Divine, Primordial, Wyrd). The atmosphere of a section changes based on who travels or settles there.
The Void's Hunger Can't Be SatisfiedThe Void devours and destroys worlds. Its hunger never ends. No world is rich enough and no bargain fine enough to make the Void stop its corruption.

Time in the Labyrinth
Time is measured in torches (1 hour — the time a torch or light spell lasts) and days (24 torches). "Week" and "month" are not consistently used; stretches of days up to 91 are a "season." Four seasons make a year.
Calendar ElementDetails
Hub CalendarThe standard for all factions. Year 1 = roughly 5,100 years ago, when Arvannos the minotaur stumbled into the Great Maze. 364 days per year + one non-calendrical New Year's Day (all work stops; remembrances and celebrations). Current year: 5124.
Four Ages(1) Age of Minotaurs / Age of the Great Ring: years 1–1252. (2) Age of the Celestials: years 1253–3731. (3) Age of Portals: years 3732–4590. (4) Age of Dreams: year 4590 (Cartokk the Dreaming God awoke) to present (5124).
Old ReckoningCalendar of the Ancients, used in their buildings, stelae, and runestones. Stretches 4,000+ years before the First Labyrinth. Uses nested 91-day seasons, each with 7 long counts of 13 runs. 23-season great counts (~5.5 years). Only the Keepers seem to understand it.
HolidaysWorld Tree Festival (spring): first leaf buds on Yggdrasil; celebrated with clever masks, music, dancing, "Tree Knot" cake, nut cakes for ratatosk. Feast of Arvannos (summer): solemn remembrance of the Labyrinth's founding; procession to graveyards/memorials. First Pathway Feast (fall): celebrates explorers; raucous with beer, wine, and spiced snail sausage; new explorers given a first torch and led ceremonially around their settlement. New Year's Day: oaths to discover new places or defend old ones; bard performs tales of community successes.

Void Taint (Chapter 7, p.227)
Void Taint is a measure of how much the Void's influence has seeped into a creature's essence. Measured in levels. Creatures native to the Void plane and those already suffused with void influence are immune. Many spells, items, and creatures in this book inflict void taint levels.

Not using Void Taint? Replace levels of void taint with levels of exhaustion. If an effect would cause multiple levels, just inflict one level of exhaustion instead.
RuleDetails
ThresholdA creature can safely accumulate taint levels up to their CHA modifier (minimum 2) before suffering penalties. This is the creature's threshold. When taint exceeds the threshold by at least 1 level, the creature immediately gains an Indefinite Dread effect and taint resets to 0.
Indefinite DreadRoll d10 for a roleplaying flaw that lasts until cured: (1) See secret messages in random objects. (2) Hear alien voices whispering near corners. (3) All memories of a specific event or person are suddenly gone. (4) Constantly scrawl strange glyphs (sometimes cutting them into skin). (5) When excited or upset, some words come out in an unknown language. (6) Symbols of a certain deity cause great pain to see or touch. (7) Ordered collections of objects are fascinating and must be counted. (8) Collect strange or disgusting trinkets (creature teeth, dirt scrapings). (9) Music is always present and must be let out. (10) Hear scratching from the other side of interior walls.
Flesh Warp (optional)Instead of a dread effect, a creature can choose a physical change that lasts until cured. Examples: (1) Barbed Hide — spiny barbs deal 2d4 piercing to creatures that hit you in melee or grapple you; disadvantage on DEX checks/saves in medium or heavy armor. (Other warps detailed in Worldbook pp.228–229.) Creatures with a flesh warp have disadvantage on CHA checks to influence creatures without the Void tag.
Curing Void TaintReduce taint by 1 level per week spent outside the Void plane (restart timer if any taint-save trigger occurs during recovery). A 5th-circle or higher spell (e.g., greater restoration): automatically reduces taint by 1 level; may also end 1 Indefinite Dread effect (DC = 10 + current taint level, CHA save). Flesh warps require 7th-circle+ magic (e.g., regenerate ritual).
Void ExposureChapter 7 includes a Void Exposure table listing specific triggers that force void taint saves (entering Void terrain, using void magic, being touched by void creatures, etc.). Full table at p.230.
Void HazardsSpecific environmental hazards found in void-corrupted areas are detailed at p.230: purple mire (terrain produced by collapsed void foldings), void dust, etc.
Faction Status System (Chapter 7, p.223)
The status system tracks the party's relationship with important factions. Positive status grants access to new quests, favors, and influence. Negative status leads to being inconvenienced, stonewalled, or attacked. Status levels: Welcoming (+5) → Friendly (+3) → Intrigued (+1) → Indifferent (0) → Vexed (−1) → Unfriendly (−3) → Hostile (−5). Full rules in the Game Master's Guide.

Portal Rules Summary (Chapter 7, p.220)
Portal TypeDescriptionNotes
GatesPortals created by spells (e.g., gate, plane shift, teleport). Short-term, predictable, safe.Expensive, short duration, easily detected and warded against.
Physical PortalsFixed geographical crossing points that can last days to centuries. Marked by physical signs. Can appear naturally or be constructed by arcanists and mechanists.Easily guarded, manipulated, or trapped. Hard to recognize. Keepers are the primary experts. Ruins of the Ancients contain many, mostly non-functional.
Portal KeysMagic objects tied to a specific physical portal. Activate or deactivate it when in proximity. Most allow a single round trip; reattunement by a portal wright restores magic. Ancient portal keys may allow unlimited travel.Extremely valuable. Falls into wrong hands = catastrophe. Ancient keys can lead to undocumented worlds.
Portal SealsMaster portal wrights (Keepers only) can seal a portal completely (huge magic/time/cost). A Great Portal Seal deactivates every physical portal connecting a world to the Labyrinth — effectively cutting it off.Last resort only. A Great Portal Seal is an extraordinarily drastic measure.
RiftsUnstable, volatile portals formed when the fabric of reality is shredded. May appear spontaneously (especially from 5th-circle+ Wyrd spells). Void servants intentionally create these.Generate magical backlash; permanently damage nearby terrain and creatures. Temporary by nature.
Four Questions When Describing Any Portal: (1) Appearance — How can creatures perceive it? Does it smell, sound, taste, or feel a certain way? Perceivable only by magic? (2) Activation — How is it used? Simple walk-through? Ritual required? Certain times only? (3) Stability — How long does it remain? Disappear after a set time? After a certain number of uses? (4) Destination — Where does it lead? Multiple places? Can travelers tell before using it?
