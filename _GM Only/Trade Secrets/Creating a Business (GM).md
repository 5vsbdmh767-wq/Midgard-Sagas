## NPC Businesses

The part of an NPC business a GM needs to detail depends entirely on what role it plays in the adventure. Each of the four types of inventory can be important, but it’s rare for all of them to be.

***Capital.*** In an RPG, capital is most often experienced as the location where a scene happens. Warehouses, ship decks, storefronts, and the like can be the stage for investigations, social encounters, and combat. Other kinds of capital decorate the scene with furniture, animals, tools, or machinery that the PCs and NPCs can interact with during these encounters.

***Goods and Services.*** This is the beating heart of most NPC shops. In most game sessions, PCs interact with a business to buy or sell merchandise, and the only concerns are what the shop has on hand and what price they are charging. This may be different when the PCs are in a remote location where only a handful of merchants operate at all. In this situation, the GM can create a limited list of available goods and services and may even limit how much gold these shops have to buy spoils from PCs.

When the PCs are in a city, things improve markedly for commerce. Numerous shops offer wares important to dungeoneering or magical studies. Unless what the heroes want is exceedingly rare and exotic, it’s more a question of where they can find something or a matter of price (ice in the tropics may still be a luxury). The GM needs to decide only whether a good is available, and if it is, NPCs can direct the player characters to whatever shop the GM wants them to visit. Don’t spend time developing detailed inventories for every merchant in the city when your players are unlikely to visit them.

***Labor.*** If you are roleplaying at a location, it’s best to have a defined NPC for the players to interact with. That’s covered in more detail in the **Make a Merchant** section in this chapter. For combat scenarios, any staff details are best prepared in advance, especially if they can fight effectively. The commoner stat block from *Monster Vault* is enough for most workers and staff.

***Supplies.*** Only worry about those supplies that are intrinsically valuable if the PCs might loot the place. Goldsmiths, jewelers, bladesmiths, and magic shops all invest in security for just this reason.

### Goods and Services

GMs can also take a hybrid approach and combine their own fiat decisions with partial goods lists, random chance, or limits. Here are a few approaches.

***Aspirational Items.*** The GM makes a list of valuable and useful items the PCs either can’t yet afford or can only barely afford. This usually includes magic items or items crafted from rare materials. While these are tracked individually, the mundane items in the shop’s category of goods are freely available.

***Price Ceiling.*** Most items are priced such that the more powerful they are, the more they cost. GMs can set a ceiling to the value of items available in local shops and assume they have everything below that price.

***Chance.*** Call for a die roll to see what fortune has to say about a shop’s inventory when a PC wants for something beyond standard wares.

#### Stock Check Table

When characters visit an NPC-run business, use this **Stock Check** table to determine randomly if an item is in stock, either at a given store or within a given city. Roll a d8 for an uncommon item. For rare items, roll two dice and take the lower roll. For more common items, roll two dice and take the higher roll. Roll additional dice to further weight the odds toward finding or not finding the item.

**Stock Check**

| d8 | Is It In Stock? |
|---|---|
| 1 | No, I heard a Kraken ate the last shipment. |
| 2 | No, we don’t have anything like that. |
| 3 | No, but it’s in a caravan headed our way. |
| 4 | No, but we have something almost as good as that; let me show you. |
| 5 | Yes, but we just have one, and it’s at a premium price. |
| 6 | Yes, you are in luck, we have it. |
| 7 | Yes, and I’ll give you a good deal on it. |
| 8 | Yes, I’ve got it, but let me show you something even better! |

#### Magical Goods

Consider these three key questions when deciding whether magic items are available somewhere in a campaign.

- What is the setting of the adventure and specifically, how common are magic items in that time and place?
- How might those magic items affect the PCs’ power level compared to the challenges they will face?
- What role are magic items supposed to serve in the campaign?

***Setting.*** The *Player’s Guide* discusses the basics of high and low-magic settings and how that informs this decision. If your world is filled with magic, then it’s far more likely magic items are on sale for a reasonable price. If magic is the domain of a unique few, you won’t find a flaming sword at the local pawn shop.

Beyond the world itself, consider the specific location. Small rural villages are unlikely to have magic items an adventurer would be interested in, while a major metropolis that is home to a wizard’s college and ten temples teeming with clerics would almost certainly trade in magic items.

It’s also worth considering what kinds of laws are in place regarding the sale and use of magic and magic items. Magic is powerful, and anything powerful is likely to have authorities trying to control it for their own benefit through the law.

***Balance.*** The magic item attunement rule does most of the heavy lifting for balance concerns, but GMs should always be thoughtful about which magic items they introduce to their campaigns and how they reduce the challenges the GM poses. Fortunately, it’s easy to make encounters a little more challenging for a party that is clever and well equipped.

Another balance consideration is making sure that if magic items are available to buy, all the players in your game can find something that appeals to them and works well with their character. Making items available that specifically target a PC who has had poor luck gives everyone some shiny toys to play with.

***Role.*** Magic items can play several roles in both storytelling and game play. These roles can inform whether they should be freely available for purchase.

If magic items are meant to feel like a big deal, something special and unique that motivates heroes to risk their lives, then having them appear in shops everywhere erodes this feeling quickly. Anything bought and sold in the market can lose its luster and romance quickly. Traditionally, magic items are a reward for either overcoming powerful foes or cunningly discovering some secret cache. If they can be bought for cash, they won’t feel as valuable and thus lessens the sense of reward.

However, if magic items are a core aspect of character advancement, then having them in shops is an important way to ensure the PCs aren’t relying entirely on chance to arm themselves with the tools they need to keep up with the challenge of encounters. This helps PCs find items that are an especially good fit for them rather than having to make do with equipment outside their mechanical specialties.

Magic items often become either a plot point or story element. This role neither encourages nor discourages them from being available for sale, but trade in magic items does open new story opportunities. Buying an interesting item in a shop is a great way to kick off a story around the item, and it easily could be the start of an entire campaign. A shop looking to acquire specific items and willing to hire adventurers to go get them is a quick and easy hook to get the PCs into nearly any sort of adventure.

## Make a Merchant

Merchant NPCs with colorful personalities provide a staple for player interaction. They are central figures in town because everyone interacts with them and knows them. Being a hub of activity makes merchants bountiful sources of gossip and local information, especially if they run an inn or tavern. That said, even a launderer or the village smith knows a lot about their local community and what’s happening in it.

### Types of Merchants

The two primary types of merchants are those who own a business and those who work running a business for a distant or silent partner. Entrepreneurial merchants are highly ambitious and driven people who are always looking to expand and improve their businesses, at least until their next grand ambition comes along. Merchants working for others have more diverse motivations and interests. They could be highly driven, but they don’t need to be; they only need to be good at their work.

Different merchants play different roles in a business. Merchants buy, sell, and trade, always looking to make a profit. Beyond that, they keep accounts, track and move inventory, promote their wares, seek out new buyers and new supplies, hire security, and arrange to produce and improve their goods.

Adventurers usually interact with shopkeepers and caravanners rather than bookkeepers or shipping clerks. Adventurers need adventuring equipment, magical services, and shelter, and shopkeepers and innkeepers provide them. After a successful haul, adventurers also need merchants who buy recovered loot and treasures. Caravanners employ mercenaries and adventurers to guard their caravans against bandits and monsters. Even if there is no dungeon to delve or kingdom to save, merchants still need security on the road. It’s a great partnership.

### Example Merchants

The following merchant profiles are NPCs you can drop into your campaigns, contacts for player characters, and inspiration for your own colorful characters.

#### Joyfer Amendes of Joyfer’s Elven Silks

Joyfer Amendes is a human gentleman trader of middling age, well known among the towns he visits, both for his wares and his personal charm. Joyfer sells silk clothing he claims is made by elven tailors and weavers. While Joyfer sells fine clothes for both men and women, he is best known for selling silk nightclothes, which he touts as the most comfortable and luxurious found anywhere. A few customers doubt the clothing’s elven origin, especially elven customers, but all agree they are exceptionally comfortable, durable, and well made.

Joyfer has a handsome face, charming voice, and incredible persistence in making a sale. His salt-and-pepper hair and beard are well groomed and stylishly decorated. He consistently dresses in his own wares, along with fashionable leather boots and a fine feathered felt hat. The merchant is unfailingly polite but can have a cheeky sense of humor with customers who enjoy a laugh. It’s difficult to speak with him without taking home at least a pair of silk stockings or gloves.

Joyfer drives a convertible wagon he sets up as a covered market stall; it packs up easily and is pulled by Joyfer’s two horses, Montvan and Bannet. Joyfer keeps a strict schedule, selling his wares in a series of cities and towns, typically completing two circuits a year. In the winter, Joyfer travels south to stock up on new wares and visit family, though he is cagy about exactly where he goes. He wants to keep his family safe and his supplier a secret.

Joyfer often sets up his stall in lower-income districts of cities rather than in the upper-class districts where most silk merchants trade. While his prices are those of luxury goods, he charges considerably lower than most high-end tailor shops. If asked about this, Joyfer gives a small speech: “Comfort and luxury should not belong only to the upper crusts,” and sometimes, “Hard-working people deserve a chance to feel smooth elven silk against their skin when dressing for an important day.” This has won him many devoted customers in the places he visits; some save all year for his arrival.

#### Elise Kaltana of the Oathsworn Alliance

Elise Kaltana has had a long and storied career as a privateer, pirate, and merchant captain, culminating in the formation of her trade guild, the Oathsworn Alliance. Elise’s beastkin lineage blessed her with her patagium wings and navigational senses, which provide her with advantages as a naval marine and ship’s hand. As a fierce and cunning warrior, she quickly rose through the ranks to become a privateer captain for the Dunlan Empire, guarding flotillas and hunting down pirates. She became known as the Flying Fox, a title uttered with both respect and fear.

After a bloody coup, Elise found herself on the wrong side of the new leadership and branded as a traitor and criminal. Elise and her crew took up a pirate’s banner, growing rich by capturing any trading vessels the Dunlan Empire dared to send her way. After a decade of piracy, Elise’s pirate fleet grew from two ships to a host of thirty, and she had effective military control of the Sunlit Sea as well as multiple ports of call in independent city states. The Dunlan Empire became a shell of its former glory and was divided into a handful of feuding factions.

Weary of bloodshed, and with plunder drying up, Elise shifted her pirate empire into a militant merchant guild, trading legitimate goods across the Sunlit Sea and hunting down competing pirates or governments who dared contest her monopoly over their trading routes or ports of call. The city-states, now largely under her protection, prospered greatly from the reliable access to markets throughout the region while Elise and her Oathsworn captains became enormously wealthy.

As of late, Elise feels her age catching up with her, and she worries that when she passes, the Alliance could fracture due to the ambition of some of her fleet captains. She has struggled to find a candidate with both the cunning to lead the group’s trading activities and enough support among the other captains to maintain unity. Some whisper this preoccupation has led her to lose her tight grip on regional politics and fleet operations, which has only advanced the ambitions of younger captains eager to claim control of the Oathsworn.

#### Gronthic Varsnarl of the Sovereign Snail

The orcish merchant Gronthic Varsnarl and his trading house, the Sovereign Snail, is a local legend among adventurers and traders. Located in the port district, the Sovereign Snail is more of a warehouse than a shop. Its vast and largely unadorned building is absolutely crammed full of boxes, barrels, crates, pottery, and bags filled with everything Gronthic has ever accumulated in his forty years of trading. His business slogan, “buy anything, sell everything,” is one he takes rather literally.

The Sovereign Snail is also full of wooden ramps that give Gronthic full access to his inventory as he moves about the warehouse in his “wheeled throne of glory.” According to Gronthic, he lost both his legs to an ancient red dragon—which swallowed them whole—back in his adventuring days. His throne of glory includes metal prosthetic legs; these are only decorative and are permanently affixed to the elaborate clockwork wheelchair.

Gronthic operates the Sovereign Snail with the help of an old and mute necromancer named Tonette who maintains a host of zombie workers that double as security. To limit the odor of decay, the zombies have been coated in pine tar, which covers their usual scent but makes the whole warehouse smell like a skunky pine forest. Otherwise, Gronthic is the sole proprietor who personally makes every deal. Customers bring whatever they want to sell to the warehouse, and they must cart away anything they buy. Somehow, Gronthic remembers every item in his vast warehouse, knowing exactly what he’s got, where it can be found, and exactly how much he wants for it.

Adventurers come to the Sovereign Snail to sell unusual treasures like cursed magic items, statues of ancient and terrible gods, and other random junk that no one else wants. Merchants come looking for hard-to-find commodities and rarities that Gronthic squirreled away months or years ago.

Gronthic takes great delight in the art of haggling and seems to engage in what he calls “retail combat,” more for sport than to maximize his profits. He gets rather grouchy when customers don’t even try to bargain with him. Customers are sometimes caught off guard when he shouts, “That’s no bargain—offer me less!”

#### Patty Bonfire of Patty’s Portage

Patty Bonfire is one of the most beloved and trusted caravan operators in the Wild Coast region, a part of the world renowned for the many dangers overland travelers encounter on a daily basis. Patty’s key to success in the caravan business isn’t one that most people would guess at: fantastic camp food.

Patty was born into the Hearthfire clan of dwarves nearly two centuries ago. From an early age, she showed skill in gathering cave fauna and cooking alongside her culinary father. The position of chief chef was a prestigious office among her people.

Unfortunately for Patty, when she came of age, she faced a small army of stubbornly determined dwarven suitors vying for her hand in marriage. With no interest in a domestic life with any of them, Patty renounced her clan ties and fled the mountain hold for a life on the surface. Patty wandered widely, exploring the surface world, tasting the foods and ingredients of whatever local cuisine she found. She paid for her lodging and food by cooking and sharing her father’s recipes.

Eventually Patty began carrying foodstuffs and spices from town to town, trading them and teaching the locals how to use them. It was easy to convince adventurers and mercenary companies to travel with her and provide security while she cooked fantastic meals for them on the road. A few became permanent companions just to enjoy her hospitality on a nightly basis. Patty referred to these regular guardians as “her boys and girls,” and they, in turn, called her “mum.” Bandits and outlaws soon learned that Patty Bonfire’s caravans were defended by fanatical and dangerous adventurers.

Before long, merchants and nobles sought Patty’s company to carry their most important parcels and precious goods, and in time this replaced her trade in foodstuffs. Despite this, Patty always carries more than enough provisions to feed her guardians and delights the locals wherever she goes.

### Personality of a Shopkeeper

Personality combines a person’s history, motivations, life circumstances, heritage, culture, mental health, attitudes, relationships, and a whole lot more. That said, some aspects are more important for roleplaying than others.

While attitudes can change, most people have a default they present on first meeting others. Those default attitudes move along different levels on an axis. For instance, folks can run from overflowing warmth to outright hatred. Others can be wildly gregarious or completely withdrawn. You don’t need to decide exactly where an NPC lands on these ranges, but give some thought to general tendencies.

Motivations define what an NPC is likely to say and do. Typically, people have many different motivations, some immediate, some long-term, and some overarching life goals. With NPCs, stick with one or two initially, then as the story progresses and if the NPC’s role is significant, you can add more immediate motivations based on their over-arching ones. The key is to have the NPC’s words and deeds align with achieving their goals whenever possible. Most merchants have at least one goal related to their business. The most common is making a living or getting rich, but some are simply passionate about the goods or services they trade in. That said, not every merchant is always focused on their work, and they may have personal motivations that override everything else, such as caring for their family or feeding a gambling addiction.

Once you have a sense of their general attitude and have worked out their motivations, add a good finishing touch, one or more personality quirks to spice them up. These can be as simple as an accent, the clothes they like to wear, or a favorite word or phrase. Whatever it is, it should be something obvious that is likely to come up in meeting or talking to the NPC.

## Five Ready-to-Use Shops

Here are five shops ready to drop into your campaign.

### Jenny’s Leathers

Jenny’s Leathers is a tannery and leather working business on the outskirts of the large port city. In the tanning yard, a team of tanners transform hides into a range of leathers. A good portion of the leathers are sold to other merchants, but the finest are kept for Jenny and her apprentices for the other half of the enterprise.

Jenny and her apprentices have a workshop adjacent to the tannery where they turn finished leathers into a range of high-end goods, each stamped with her unique maker’s mark. These shoes, belts, hats, gloves, and in rare cases, armor, are sold in high priced boutiques and shops in many cities. They often become family heirlooms or are enchanted as magical equipment.

Jenny has been known to sell products directly from the workshop to those who seek her out, but most of her goods are bought by merchants who then resell them. The stench of the tannery tends to keep the public far away and Jenny doesn’t make any effort to market her own wares, preferring to focus on the craft and the training of apprentices to someday carry on her work and techniques.

Jenny’s Leathers does sometimes hire adventurers and hunters to acquire rare hides for her special projects. These transactions are generally handled by one of Jenny’s apprentices, but she takes a personal interest if there are special instructions or requirements for the hides she needs. Many have been known to take on the job, not for gold, but for the chance to have something custom crafted by Jenny’s hands from the trophy.

#### Owner and Shopkeeper: Jenny Izthitch

Most people are surprised to learn that Jenny is a lizardfolk. Tall, even for one of her kind, her blue green scales are striking, and her Common is thickly accented. Those who ask quickly discover she has no interest in sharing her life story. She’s only interested in her current work and business.

Her clothing reflects this focus. Rather than her own fashions, she wears a basic, stained work apron. Those who look past her general appearance can see that each of Jenny’s claws have been carefully shaped to serve as individual leather working tools.

***Location:*** Large port city.

***Category:*** Manufacturing and consumer goods.

***Inventory:*** Finished leathers, luxury leather clothing and accessories.

***Security:*** The stench of the tannery and a pair of old watch dogs.

***Interior:*** Most of the tannery is outdoors. The workshop is a messy and crowded space in a wooden longhouse.

***Quirks:*** Jenny offers items for sale to visitors even though she doesn’t run a retail shop.

### Hammer and Dove

Hammer and Dove is one of the largest and best-known smithies along the coast. In business for nearly 150 years, it has greatly expanded since it was founded by the gnomish smith, Striker Wiggins the Elder. While the elder Wiggins passed long ago, his children and grandchildren still run the smithy and the attached shop.

What started as forge in a corner market stall now occupies an imposing three-story brick building. Inside are three hearths and forges, a storage warehouse, and a storefront that sells wares and takes custom orders. Massive, polished brass icons of a Hammer and Dove adorn the shop’s street facing corner and have become an icon in the neighborhood over the years. The shop even sells small cast-bronze versions as good luck tokens, and the symbol is etched into all its wares.

The workshop is open enough that the public can view much of the work at the forges. Crowds often gather to observe the “Wigginses” hammering away. The workshop has been fitted with clockwork apparati including a massive auto-hammer that makes a deafening noise. The devices and the noise routinely draw onlookers.

The forge is prolific and produces masses of good quality armor, weapons, and tools for sale and for export to armies, mercenary companies, and traders throughout the coast. While not know for masterwork quality, their products are reliable and trusted.

#### Owner: The Lily Society

The public assumes that the Hammer and Dove is owned by the Wiggins clan, but the original Wiggins had a serious gambling problem and was forced to sell his business to the Lily Society, a criminal syndicate specializing in espionage and blackmail. The Lily Society has pulled the strings of the Wiggins clan ever since. They not only produce considerable income for the gang, but their weapons trade leads to valuable intelligence.

#### Shopkeeper: Young Winder Wiggins

Young Winder Wiggins, second son of Striker Wiggins the Elder, is the current master smith at the Hammer and Dove. Winder is responsible for the smithy’s clockwork components, a passion that was instrumental in the smithy’s expansion.

Young Winder is entirely focused on the forge and has little interest in customers; that is done by his sister and children. Young Winder detests the Lily Society, but he recognizes they their claws are in far too deeply to try breaking away.

***Location:*** Frontier city.

***Category:*** Consumer goods and retail.

***Inventory:*** An extensive selection of iron and steel tools, supplies, weapons, and armor.

***Security:*** One private night watchman guards the shop at night. During the day, a Lily Society agent is often present, concealed by an *invisibility* spell to monitor the business and keep an ear out for anything useful in the crowd.

***Interior:*** The Hammer and Dove has three floors. The ground floor houses the smithy shop and its extensive inventory along with the forge, which extends up into the second floor to accommodate the clockworks. The rest of the second floor contains offices and rooms where family members rest between shifts. The top floor is a warehouse for raw materials and finished goods awaiting shipment. An open freight elevator connects the forge to the warehouse. Goods are hoisted by a rattling pully and counterweight all day long.

***Quirks:*** The door handles, windows, furniture and other features are all sized for smallfolk rather than bigfolk, though the doors are tall enough for both.

### The Pickled Porcupine

The Pickled Porcupine is a classic roadside inn and tavern located along a major road and trade route between the coastal cities of the kingdom and its mountainous interior. The inn’s name is based on a literal whole pickled porcupine in an enormous glass jar that sits on bar of the inn’s tavern. The inn has a running challenge for anyone who dares to try and eat the entire beast.

The Pickled Porcupine is actually a series of buildings that includes a hearthside tavern, ample stables, camping sites, a small smithy, a bathhouse, and lodgings that range from common bunkhouses to private multi-room cabins. Surrounding all this is a wooden fortification with a full gatehouse and guard towers. The fortifications are staffed by a cadre of Duke Zaffir’s guards who also have a guardhouse inside the fort and their own private stables.

The Pickled Porcupine is always full of travelers and merchants who stop for rest, supplies, security, and trade. Many mercenaries and adventurers call it home when traveling to the kingdom’s interior wilderness, pursuing bounties, or seeking work as caravan guards. The inn has a dozen staff as well as their children, plus cats, dogs, and a donkey named Daisy. When the inn is full and noisy, it has the rambunctious feel of a small town during high summer.

#### Owner: Duke Magnus Zaffir

The land the Pickled Porcupine sits on belongs to the rural lord, Duke Magnus Zaffir and his family. The duke commissioned the fortifications to secure and patrol the stretch of road. The innkeeper pays a tax to the duke and maintains the inn and stables with whatever remains. Other than collecting his rents, the duke takes little interest in the Pickled Porcupine.

#### Innkeeper: Big Paul

Big Paul works the tavern as the bartender and host most evenings. True to his name, Paul is a massive bear of a human male. Unlike many innkeepers, Paul isn’t known for his warm personality and misers his words as if each one he utters might cost him a gold piece. His face is typically a stony grimace. Nonetheless, he’s known for fairness and an even temper. New patrons are often surprised to learn he is beloved by his regulars and his staff.

***Location:*** Wilderness outpost.

***Category:*** Hospitality.

***Inventory:*** Ample food stores, fresh water, lodgings for up to fifty people and thirty animals, equestrian supplies.

***Security:*** Between six (winter) and twenty (high summer) of the duke’s guardsmen, along with half a dozen or so adventurers and mercenaries. A wooden palisade wall, 15 feet tall with 20-foot-tall guard towers and gate house.

***Interior:*** The tavern is the largest building with a large feast hall seating up to thirty customers. The stone base supports log timber walls and wooden plank floors; its grand hearth offers heat during the winter. Appointments are rustic in style but well-built and cared for. Lodgings vary from a sparse bunkhouse to a private room fit for traveling princes and earls.

***Quirks:*** Travelers can pay 50 gp to try and eat the Pickled Porcupine. No use of magic is allowed and anyone larger than Big Paul must eat it whole in one bite. Smaller contestants must eat everything but the bones and quills, and they have one hour to do so with no vomiting allowed. To win the contest a character must succeed at both a DC 15 WIS save (for the willpower to eat the nasty thing) and a DC 20 CON save (to avoid vomiting). Winners are entitled to a growing jackpot (currently 750 gp) and to add their name to a bronze plaque on the hearth. Losers get a beer in a special mug that proudly states “I puked a porc!” in painted letters.

### Pets Peculiar

Among the wonders of a grand city is a remarkable shop, Pets Peculiar. It sells exotic plants and animals from all over the known world. Everything about the shop, from its construction, its proprietor, and its wares is unique, exquisite, and wondrous.

The shop itself was not so much built as grown from a massive tree, or more specifically, a collection of trees woven together into a single trunk, rising amid cobblestone streets and ordinary buildings of brick and stone.

The grand entry doors and stained-glass windows are integrated into the building’s natural shapes. Small living branches in the upper heights bear leaves and flowers that change with the seasons. Inside, the space resembles an open garden, and its center is open to the sky above.

The shop is the creation of the enigmatic master druid Obera. Its wares are living creatures the druid has collected from far afield. The plants grow in the interior garden or in pots arranged through the shop. Many of the animals roam freely in the interior: birds perching on the tree’s interior, beasts climbing its wooden walls, and fish and turtles swimming in the central pond. A few creatures are kept in special enclosures, ensorcelled to create the conditions needed for their comfort: elementals, certain deep ocean fish, and the like.

#### Owner and Shopkeeper: Keeper Obera

Obera is a person of striking physical and emotional grace. Obera is Syderean, just under 7 feet tall, with a porcelain complexion swimming with shifting blue patterns framed by long, gold-spun hair. Dressed in flowing silks, they glide through Pets Peculiar as if floating on aether. Obera’s voice is nearly as enigmatic, like a clear whisper that is both calming and commanding.

Obera is a potent druid and uses their talents to speak with the animals in the shop so that Obera can not only find a good pet for a given customer but ensures that the animal has an affinity for their new owner. Obera won’t sell an animal to a customer whom the animal instinctively distrusts. This practice has caused some clients to leave Pets Peculiar in a self-righteous rage, but most appreciate this matchmaking process.

***Location:*** Metropolitan capital.

***Category:*** Retail.

***Inventory:*** Exotic animals, magical creatures, and rare plants.

***Security:*** Obera is a 15th-level druid and can handle most threats. Some animals in the shop are dangerous enough to serve as a deterrent, especially when Obera directs them. While the shop appears to have no ceiling, a dome of magical force akin to a *wall of force* spell seals the area tightly. Only the large oak double doors offer entrance or exit from the shop. These doors are enchanted with an *arcane lock* spell that only opens to people who carry a special invitation stone. In addition, the doors are barred after hours.

***Interior:*** The interior of Pets Peculiar is all one large space about 40 feet in diameter and approximately 60 feet high. The walls are living wood, and the floor is earth and soil with paths made from paving stones winding around the central garden pond. Various platforms can be found at different elevations, most of which are perches for animals, but the highest is Obera’s own modest bedroom.

***Quirks:*** Pets Peculiar is an invitation-only shop. Customers can only shop there by receiving an invitation stone from Obera or one of their druid apprentices. Most customers come to the shop and request a stone from a druid stationed just outside the main doors each morning. A potential customer must answer a few questions regarding what kind of pet they seek and why. If the druid deems the request worthy, they receive a stone good for entry that day.

Depending on how busy the shop is, the druid at the door might offer stones to people who are interested in viewing the animals, who are curious about the shop, or who just happen to be passing by.

On rare occasions, customers are invited to the shop without a request. These invitation stones are good for however long the person needs to make their way to Pets Peculiar. Stories say that sometimes these invitations are delivered by animal messengers to people living in faraway lands who have never heard of the shop. In these instances, a note from Obera is included with the stone.

### Escante’s Arcane Emporium

High in the upper city district, Escante’s Arcane Emporium is a thriving shop catering to elite and wealthy customers who wish to buy or sell magical artifacts. While the shop is only some thirty years old, it has established a strong reputation for being the place to buy and sell magical wares. Though it’s also known you won’t find any grand bargains. The wizard Vivarus has a reputation as a polite but ruthless negotiator.

While the shop is tastefully decorated with inlaid furnishings and colorful tapestries, the emporium is built like a fortress. The walls are of thick and enchanted stone with no windows; only a single entrance exists, with a gatehouse where guests must store weapons and magic items they don’t intend to sell. The shop is entirely lit by magical lights.

The shop caters to a specific clientele, so prospective shoppers must demonstrate to the guards at the gate that they have something of value to trade with or prove that they are a person of high status who can afford to make a purchase. While the clientele includes upper crust nobles and merchants, the emporium is also visited by rough-and-tumble adventurers looking to trade the spoils of their adventures.

#### Owner: Durgin Blackscale

Durgin is a kobold and the secret master of Escante’s Arcane Emporium. Over the years, Durgin and his Blackscale clan acquired a respectable collection of treasures through excavation, exploration, and trade.

Durgin excels at trade, but as a kobold, he finds it difficult to arrange favorable trades with the kind of people that could afford to collect high end magical treasures. Escante’s Arcane Emporium was his solution, creating a front by employing a well-respected elven arcanist and historian while he supplied the stock and the money to back it.

Durgin and other members of the Blackscale clan often work in the shop, secretly directing Vivarius in terms of sales and trades while pretending to be his faithful servants. While Durgin trusts Vivarius, his negotiation instincts are second to none, and his keen nose for a good deal is key to the shop’s financial success, always getting the better part of any exchange. Durgin and his clan have plenty of money already his real goal is to assemble the greatest collection of magical artifacts the world has ever known.

#### Shopkeeper: Vivarius Escante III

The shop is named after its shopkeeper, Vivarius Escante III, an elven man of considerable age and a wizard of some skill. Vivarius dresses in elegant but highly traditional upper-class fashions. His aged skin has a texture like wrinkled papyrus, and he seems a delicate, slightly frail creature of skin and bone with long flossy white hair. Vivarius is renowned for exacting manners and decorum as well as his incredible knowledge of the goods in the Emporium.

While most customers assume that Vivarius is the shop’s owner, and he does nothing to discourage this thought, he’s merely Durgin’s loyal, well-paid employee.

***Location:*** Metropolis.

***Category:*** Retail goods.

***Inventory:*** All magical items from simple potions to fantastic artifacts.

***Security:*** The security at the emporium is extensive. Two mercenary guards stand in the gate house which has a heavy wooden door (often barred) as well as a locking portcullis. Thick stone walls are lined with enchanted lead panels that block teleportation, scrying, and dimensional travel into or out of the shop. Every door, cabinet, display case, and other portal is sealed with *arcane lock* and keyed to Vivarius and the Blackscales. Permanent *alarm* spells protect all private areas of the store, and an overnight *alarm* is active on the only entrance after closing time. Two polished iron golems stand in the interior of the shop. They attack any unrecognized intruder when the shop is closed and anyone the proprietors direct them to attack when it’s open.

Physical and magical traps protect the cases of the most valuable items. They’re armed any time Vivarius or Durgin are not present. Finally, Vivarius performs divinations nightly to forewarn of existing plots to rob or burgle the Emporium so that he and Durgin can take additional precautions to thwart schemes.

Due to this extensive security, only two items have ever been stolen from Escante’s Arcane Emporium. A somber memorial tablet (labeled “Unwanted Guests”) commemorates the names of twenty-two would-be thieves who died trying to defeat the Emporium’s countermeasures.

***Interior:*** The interior space of the emporium is rectangular, 120 feet by 60 feet and 30 feet tall. A 20-foot-wide balcony encircles the room, accessible to a stairwell to either side of the entrance. One wall features a massive steel and lead vault that stores cash and items not currently for sale. Numerous glass cabinets in the interior display items for sale. The central lounge has comfortable seating and a tea table for refreshments.

***Quirks:*** The shop’s lounge serves to entertain customers, conduct negotiations, and allow examination of merchandise. A permanent *zone of truth* is in place here, which can take customers by surprise. The zone has led to some surprising discussions among patrons.
