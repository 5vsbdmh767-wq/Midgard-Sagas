*From Trade Secrets, chapter 3. GM material: trade events that shift market prices, and random goods tables. The player-facing parts of this chapter are on [[Trade Goods & Money]] and [[Batch Crafting]].*

## Trade Events

Once you determine beginning market modifiers, use the **Trade Events** and **Wartime Trade Events** tables to change them over time. Trade events can create new opportunities for players with businesses or change the value of the treasures that adventurers want to buy or sell.

Roll on the tables or select events that interest you. You can use the **Trade Events** table at any time, while the **Wartime Trade Events** table can generate events specifically for a nation engaged in war (which often impacts markets, sometimes dramatically). The results of each event apply a market modifier to set of trade goods. You can also apply these modifiers to other trade goods at your discretion.

**Trade Events**

| d20 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Bountiful Harvest.** The harvest is excellent, pushing down prices for all food and drink (−0.3 market modifier). |
| 2 | **Celestial Alignment.** An unusual event in the heavens drives up demand for arcane inks and spell components (+0.3 market modifier). |
| 3 | **Cold Snap.** Cold weather hurts crop production, driving up prices for grains and plant fibers (+0.2 market modifier). It’s also led to a run on rugs and tapestries (+0.4 market modifier). |
| 4 | **Death of a Leader.** A notable leader died, creating uncertainty about the future and falling prices for all goods (−0.1 market modifier). |
| 5 | **Drought Conditions.** A lack of rainfall creates drought conditions driving up prices for livestock, grain, and drinks (+0.4 market modifier). |
| 6 | **Earthquake.** A significant earthquake destroyed many buildings leading to a jump in demand for lumber, plant fibers, tools, and stone (+0.4 market modifier). |
| 7 | **Falling Morale.** The nation is in a poor mood. Overall demand for all goods except drinks is down (−0.2 market modifier). |
| 8 | **Fashion Trend.** Everyone wants a particular item of clothing (+1 market modifier) and isn’t interested in any other clothing (−0.2 market modifier). |
| 9 | **Flooding.** Local flooding causes a run on clay and pottery as people rush to protect their possessions from the rising waters (+0.3 market modifier). |
| 10 | **Hysteria.** Everyone is afraid of a dark prophecy. People panic-buy preserves and weapons (+0.6 market modifier). |
| 11 | **Ideal Weather.** Fine weather has people out of their homes and in the mood for shopping. Demand for household goods drives up prices slightly (+0.1 market modifier). |
| 12 | **Import Shortage.** The supply of foreign goods is cut off by distant events. The price for porcelain and spices soars (+1 market modifier). |
| 13 | **Mining Boom.** Rich veins of ore open up, increasing supply and driving down prices (−0.3 market modifier). |
| 14 | **Monster Incursion.** Monsters are raiding the area, driving up prices for animal fibers and livestock (+0.2 market modifier), while lowering prices for monster parts (−0.3 market modifier). |
| 15 | **Plague.** Sickness sweeps across the land driving medicine prices sky high (+1 market modifier). |
| 16 | **Public Works.** A building boom drives up the prices for clay, hides, lumber, ingots, and stone (+0.3 market modifier). |
| 17 | **Religious Festival.** An important festival drives up prices for spices, incense, and wine (+0.2 market modifier). |
| 18 | **Rising Morale.** Good times raise demand and prices for all goods (+0.2 market modifier). |
| 19 | **Rumors of War.** Troops mustering for war lead to price speculation. Prices for martial goods rise (+0.2 market modifier). |
| 20 | **Wildfire.** A campfire starts a major fire, wiping out fields and woodlands. Prices for grains, plant fibers, timber, and lumber rise (+0.3 market modifier). |

**Wartime Trade Events**

| d10 | Wartime Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Conscription.** The nation is short of troops and is conscripting civilians. Demand for household goods falls (−0.4 market modifier). |
| 2 | **Contracts.** The nation opens the coffers to buy food and weapons. Demand for martial goods, iron ingots, timber, and livestock rises (+0.3 market modifier). |
| 3 | **Decisive Victory.** A major battle was won, raising morale and leading to celebration. Consumer demand rises sharply for all goods (+0.2 market modifier). |
| 4 | **Major Defeat.** The military was crushed and retreated from the enemy. People are buying essentials and little else. Demand for preserves and livestock rises (+0.3 market modifier), while demand for everything else falls (−0.4 market modifier). |
| 5 | **Minor Defeat.** News from the front lines is not good. People are in a dour mood, lowering all goods prices (−0.1 market modifier). |
| 6 | **Minor Victory.** News from the front lines is encouraging! People celebrate with demand for beer, spirits, and wine (+0.1 market modifier). |
| 7 | **Refugees.** An influx of refugees into the cities causes demand for grain and eggs to spike (+0.5 market modifier) |
| 8 | **Shortages.** War disrupts production of nearly everything. Prices for all goods rise dramatically (+0.5 market modifier). |
| 9 | **Siege.** The local area is under siege! Importing is impossible. Prices for grain, eggs, beer, and medicine skyrocket (+2 market modifier) while everything else plummets (−1 market modifier). |
| 10 | **Spoils.** Spoils of war are flowing into the area creating excessive supply and lowering the price on everything (−0.2 market modifier). |

## Random Goods Tables

Use the tables in this section to help determine on the fly what goods are being transported in a ship, what’s stored in a warehouse, or a good whose market price is in flux. Roll on the **Goods Category** table, then on the table for that category, then on the d6 list for the trade good.

**Goods Category**

| d6 | Category |
|---|---|
| 1 | Raw Materials |
| 2–3 | Refined Goods |
| 4–6 | Finished Goods |

**Raw Materials**

| d20 | Category | Trade Good (d6) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Animal Fibers | 1–2: Furs; 3: Hair; 4: Silk; 5–6: Wool |
