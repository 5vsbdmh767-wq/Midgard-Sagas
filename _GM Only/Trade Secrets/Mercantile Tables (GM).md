*From Trade Secrets, appendix. Quick d20 tables for markets, merchants, and trade.*

**Suspiciously Cheap Items**

| d20 | Item |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Ornate Lute.** Strung with real hair and plays perfectly well, but one string audibly weeps when plucked. |
| 2 | **Gilded Goblet.** Tarnishes overnight, no matter how often it’s polished. |
| 3 | **Beautifully Tooled Leather Satchel.** Complete with masterwork stitching, but it growls when left empty. |
| 4 | **Fine Silk Cloak.** Repels water but smells faintly of brimstone. |
| 5 | **Cracked Lantern**. Flickers when lit, and its shadows linger unnaturally. |
| 6 | **Jeweled Dagger**. Looks and feels like steel but disintegrates like paper when it gets wet. |
| 7 | **Velvet Pouch of Gold Coins.** Coins covered in real gold but their chocolate-like cores melt in warm hands. |
| 8 | **Merchant’s Ledger.** Written in a meticulous hand, but the numbers add up to odd sums. Whoever studies it risks obsession. |
| 9 | **Goose Quill Pen.** Writes in shimmering gold ink but reads your words out loud as you write them. |
| 10 | **Small Velvet-lined Jewelry Box.** Anything put inside disappears permanently. |
| 11 | **Steel Manacles.** Unbreakable by mortal strength, but the key is always missing. (Knock spell works normally.) |
| 12 | **Brass Spyglass.** Functions normally but sometimes shows ships or cities that don’t exist. |
| 13 | **Ornate Shield**. Painted with a roaring lion crest, but splinters like driftwood on the first critical hit. |
| 14 | **Intricately Carved Ivory Comb.** Slowly makes your hair fall out. |
| 15 | **Fine Leather Boots.** These boots never scuff or wear, but they squeak loudly at the worst times. |
| 16 | **Impressive Battle Horn.** When blown, it releases only harmless bubbles. |
| 17 | **Deck of Playing Cards.** Completely normal, but the ink bleeds off and stains the fingers of anyone cheating with this deck. |
| 18 | **Worn Scroll**. The magical text is faded and barely visible, and important parts of the incantation are missing. |
| 19 | **Wooden Figurine**. Fine craftsmanship, but it moves on its own when unobserved. |
| 20 | **Fashionable Hat.** Made of silk and feathers. Animals are inexplicably drawn to it. |

**Profitable Rumors**

| d20 | Rumor |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Spice Caravan**. Rumored to be transporting a new herb that doubles the potency of any potion. The wagon is only guarded by two humans. |
| 2 | **New Mint.** The ruler’s mint makes a coin so pure that wealthy collectors pay triple its face value on the black market. |
| 3 | **Rare Silks.** A local guild master is hoarding rare silks, to crash the market for his competitors. |
| 4 | **Traveling Alchemist.** Trades in “golden dust” that turns base metals into slightly more valuable ones (or so it’s claimed). |
| 5 | **Cursed Gem Hoard.** On the black market, this collection would be worth a fortune (if the curse could be lifted). |
| 6 | **Growing Demand.** A smuggler claims to have a new wine that intoxicates without a hangover. Demand is growing fast. |
| 7 | **Shipment of Dragon Scales.** Reportedly arriving at a nearby port and valued for weapons, armor, and magical crafting. |
| 8 | **New Sea Route.** A new trade route has opened with a distant archipelago. The first traders will make fortunes if they can survive the journey. |
| 9 | **Timber Monopoly.** Suddenly, logs are selling for triple the price; a merchant may have a monopoly on timber from the nearby forest. |
| 10 | **Shipwreck.** A trade ship has gone missingoff the coast. It was carrying barrels of enchanted wine that grant luck to the drinker. |
| 11 | **Secret Recipe.** A tavern sells a stew said to cure ailments. A local black-market broker will pay top coin for the recipe. |
| 12 | **Secret Wine Cellar.** A priceless collection has been locked up on a noble’s estate. His stable boy seeks to sell a key to the highest bidder. |
| 13 | **Shortcut.** A smuggler claims to have discovered a shortcut trade route; it’s fast and could be highly profitable, but it is guarded by a band of outlaws. |
| 14 | **Royal Favor.** A noble seeks rare imports to impress a visiting dignitary. Generous payment to those who brave the treacherous trade route to deliver the goods. |
| 15 | **Missing Goats.** A shepherd’s herd of magical frost goats has gone missing. Their magical milk is very valuable. |
| 16 | **Moonfruit Orchard.** An orchard outside of town produces a rare moonfruit. Its caretaker insists it only ripens and can be harvested under the light of the moon. |
| 17 | **Noble’s Warehouse.** A wealthy noble keeps a nearby warehouse filled with stolen goods. Word is that the goods are moving to a new location within a fortnight. |
| 18 | **Land for Sale.** A merchant port is selling cheap plots after a mudslide or flood deterred previous investors. |
| 19 | **Haunted Market Stall.** Goods from this stall sell for triple—if buyers are brave enough to handle the “haunted” items. |
| 20 | **Monopolized Mine.** A dwarf-run mine has struck a vein of gemstones. Townsfolk might be working with guards to seize access. |

**Marketplace NPCs**

| d20 | NPC |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Masked Perfumer.** A robed figure with dozens of masks dangling from their belt sells perfumes that evoke vivid memories, but not always the buyer’s own. |
| 2 | **Goblin Auctioneer.** Standing atop a crate, this fast-talking goblin auctions “cursed goods” at suspiciously low prices. Every third item explodes in sparkles. |
| 3 | **One-Eyed Astrologer.** A blindfolded elf offers trade forecasts and shipping predictions, claiming to read the stars even in broad daylight. |
| 4 | **Swamp Peddler.** A frogperson sells miniature gourds filled with “swamp potions.” They insist each cures something different. |
| 5 | **Tattooed Fortune-Broker.** A dwarf whose tattoos shift and change offers to sell prophecies like stock tips: “Invest in salt, avoid pearls this season!” |
| 6 | **Grinning Knife-Juggler.** A toothy kobold flips daggers while selling “self-sharpening cutlery.” |
| 7 | **The Pigeon Whisperer.** A ragged man covered in pigeons trades messages, rumors, and letters. You must pay with seed. |
| 8 | **The Wandering Bard-Banker.** A lute-strumming elf who doubles as a moneychanger. He insists all deposits must be sung aloud before he’ll accept them. |
| 9 | **The Ragpicker Prophet.** A filthy beggar who collects scraps of cloth claims to weave together the fates of those who touch his patchwork cloak. |
| 10 | **The Sleepy Camel-Trader.** A merchant dozing atop a massive, six-eyed camel claims to sell mounts that never tire, if you can wake him to negotiate. |
| 11 | **The Singing Smith.** A burly orc hammers out coins and jewelry while singing loudly; each crafted piece hums faintly with his song. |
| 12 | **The Talking Ravenfolk Merchant.** A large black ravenfolk in a tiny hat sells shiny trinkets from a velvet-lined stall. It speaks in riddles and demands payment in worms or secrets. |
| 13 | **The Smoke-Seller.** A sorcerer bartering jars filled with “bottled smoke” from famous battles, each with ghostly whispers inside. |
| 14 | **The Web-Clad Seamstress.** An old woman, covered in docile spiders, sells garments woven from their shimmering silk. |
| 15 | **The Rag-And-Bone Knight.** A beggar in a dented helm drags a cart of scrap, claiming to sell fragments of “fallen heroes’ armor.” |
| 16 | **The Book-Crier.** A loud human hawks books that scream their stories aloud when opened. |
| 17 | **The Mirror-Mask Merchant.** This eerie vendor wears a mirror for a face and sells small hand mirrors. Each one shows the buyer’s reflection slightly older each time they look. |
| 18 | **The Palmy Chef.** A red-scaled lizardfolk cooks food directly in its fiery hands; the dishes always taste faintly of brimstone. |
| 19 | **The Honey-Tongued Priest.** A bearfolk priest with bees crawling over his robes sells honeycombs, said to heal wounds and affect dreams. |
| 20 | **Wid-Haired Gnome.** This smallfolk sells a motley assortment of pact boon implements for warlocks: daggers, find familiar scrolls, and eldritch grimoires. Surely that's not a profitable business. A mysterious shadow demon named Othsgoroth provides unsubtle security. |

**Hazards of Trade and Travel**

| d20 | Hazard |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Bandit Ambush**. A well-organized gang strikes a caravan, demanding coin or blood. |
| 2 | **Flooded Roadways**. Seasonal rains turn the main trade route into a swamp, miring wagons and carts in impassable mud. |
| 3 | **Unnatural Storms**. Wild magical weather descends: raining frogs, lightning that whispers, or fire snow. |
| 4 | **Highway Tolls**. A local lord (or enterprising thug) demands an unofficial tax for safe passage. |
| 5 | **Haunted Pass.** Always the fastest path, but ghosts of slaughtered merchants attack anyone traveling the cursed road. |
| 6 | **Border Wars.** A sudden dispute closes borders, trapping you on the wrong side of the conflict. |
| 7 | **Divine Embargo.** A local temple declares your goods unholy and forbids their trade under divine punishment. |
| 8 | **Currency Collapse.** The coins you were paid recently lose value overnight due to a royal decree about a new mint. |
| 9 | **Pickpocket Gangs.** Swarms of nimble thieves target local merchants, lifting purses and even small goods. |
| 10 | **Cargo Mix-Up**. Barrels or crates are mysteriously switched—rare goods vanish, and worthless junk takes their place. |
| 11 | **Stolen Identities.** A “merchant” using your name and seal is posing as you and has already undercut your profits. |
| 12 | **Festival Tax**. Local authorities demand a new levy to fund festival celebrations, hitting visitors and merchants hardest. |
| 13 | **Extortionate Lodging.** The localinns and warehouses charge double or triple the price for outsiders. |
| 14 | **Collapsed Bridge**. A key bridge has fallen, stranding caravans until a solution is found. |
| 15 | **Broken Wagon.** A stranded merchant asks for help repairing or relocating a wagon blocking the road. It is a bandit trap. |
| 16 | **Monster Stalking.** Signs of claw marks, broken trees, and eerie howls suggest a predator stalks this trade road. Gnawed and cracked mule bones back up the story. |
| 17 | **Injured Caravan**. Another merchant caravan lies in ruins, its guards wounded and its cargo scattered. They request your help. |
| 18 | **Fire in the Market**. A cooking accident, magical misfire, or spark from a careless wizard ignites nearby stalls. |
| 19 | **Port or Market Quarantine**. Disease or pest outbreaks require a lockdown of warehouses, docks, or marketplaces in the town you are traveling to. |
| 20 | **Guild Strikes**. Local guilds halt production, delaying shipments and increasing prices. |
